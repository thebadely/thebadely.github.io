Midlands clubs Coventry and Worcester Warriors have both confirmed their interest in taking up the potential offer of a place in the Prem - but not if it puts their clubs at any sort of financial risk.

Prem Rugby last week opened a registration process for teams to apply for the 2029-30 season, with the aim of expanding the league back up from the current 10 clubs to 12.

The lack of depth in English top-flight club rugby has been a concern ever since London Irish followed Worcester and Wasps in folding at the end of the 2022-23 season.

Wasps, originally a London club who played at Sudbury before moving to football club Queens Park Rangers' Loftus Road home and then on to Adams Park, Wycombe, were based in Coventry, at the CBS Arena, when they went out of business. 

They announced in 2024 that they intended to return to the south east and were looking for a new home in Kent - and last week were reported to have put in a planning application for a new community training ground at Swanley.

Worcester are into their second season in the rebranded Champ following their reformation, while Coventry, one of the powerhouses of English rugby union in the days before the sport went fully professional in 1996, have not played in the top flight since the first season of the Courage League in 1987-88, when they were relegated.  

"The Prem are right in trying to expand," Cov executive chairman Jon Sharp told BBC Sport. 

"It's very hard for their clubs only having nine home games each.   

"And we would love to go to the Prem. We owe it to our history. We owe it to our supporters. And I'm prepared to spend money sensibly on the extension of our facilities. 

"I'm happy to spend some millions to do that, but to spend Â£20m to buy a  P-share (the ownership stake held by each Prem member club)? No. I've made it clear that I'm not doing that."

Top of the list of the Prem's requirements for any team is a minimum stadium capacity of 10,001. Cov's Butts Park Arena, since their old, traditional 10,000-capacity Coundon Road was torn down in 2004 for housing redevelopment, is only 5,250.

"The right thing for the Prem to do is relax the minimum operating standards they introduced and do away with the need for a P-share," added Sharp. "Or at least make it a lot easier to acquire one and restore promotion and relegation.

"They started the process back when they were reduced to 10 clubs by the collapse of Wasps, Worcester and London Irish and then started approaching clubs individually. 

"They approached Cov because of our history, and because our stadium is not far off.  But we'd still need about Â£25m to meet that minimum capacity of 10,000."

On the weekend when Coventry and Worcester met at the Butts Park Arena, the hosts went level on points with their opponents in the Champ thanks to a 57-42 win in a thrilling game of 14 tries. 

But, while Warriors conceded over 50 points on the ground for the second year running, the two clubs are at least on the same side as regards their demands.

"Yes, we're interested," Warriors chief executive Stephen Vaughan told BBC Hereford & Worcester. "Yes, we will be kicking the tyres on the request for information. 

"We aspire to play in the highest possible place that we can, but we will not jeopardise the future of the club. 

"I can give every Warriors fan out there every reassurance that we will investigate every possible route to it. 

"We are very, very sought after within the Prem. But, at the moment, and we've been very clear on this, the financial situation is so penal that we would jeopardise the very future of the club if we were to go up on the current criteria."
