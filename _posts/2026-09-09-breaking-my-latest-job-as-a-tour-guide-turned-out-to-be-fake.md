Let's dive into the details: Ella was hired on the spot by Haggis Waggon Tours after going to a job fair in Paisley

When Ella was offered a job as a tour guide, it seemed like an exciting opportunity.

The 23-year-old was hired on the spot by Haggis Waggon Tours boss Darryl Smith after meeting him at a job fair in Paisley.

Just a week later, Ella was listed as a "guest favourite" on the group's website, despite never having done a tour for them or meeting any customers.

She has since discovered its "head office" was an empty property and many of the claims on its website were false.

Ella says she first met Darryl Smith in June and was hired without a formal interview, along with several others.

A week later, she received an email, apparently from the HR department, asking for her address, right to work documents, and bank details.

Darryl Smith responded over email to some of the allegations put to him by BBC Scotland News.

In his email responses, he said there was no formal interview for candidates as training was offered instead - "giving potential employees the opportunity to see if it is the role for them".

Darryl Smith is described as the founder and chief executive of Haggis Waggon Tours

On 9 July, the group of new employees were invited to walk around Paisley as a "training exercise".

Ella said something "felt off" when they couldn't see the bus that Darryl Smith said he had brought.

They stopped outside what he described as the "Haggis Waggon Tours' head office" on Paisley High Street. The building was vacant and locked up.

The same address was listed on the business' website under the "contact us" section, but the landlord of the building confirmed to the BBC that Darryl Smith does not hold a lease there. The contact address has now been changed to say "Edinburgh".

Darryl Smith told the BBC he had built the website himself and mentioning that address had been a mistake.

"That spot was only ever used as a meeting point for new tours leaving from Paisley," he said.

Have you been involved with Haggis Waggon Tours?

Despite the "red flags", Ella decided to give Darryl Smith the benefit of the doubt and agreed to shadow a Harry Potter themed tour on 13 July.

She was due to be picked up on one of the Haggis Waggon Tours buses in Paisley at 08:00, along with three others.

But after four hours, no vehicle had arrived.

Ella says Darryl Smith assured the group there had been a mix-up and claimed to be dealing with domestic issues.

"When the original concern with the buses was brought up and it was pointed out that the pictures that he was using on his social media and on the website were AI, he had given us the excuse that he hadn't had all the buses wrapped yet," she said.

"I think to this day none of us have actually seen the buses. They don't exist, do they?"

Darry Smith says the coaches "do in fact exist".

"Images online of tour buses are AI as [the] company is relatively new and we are in the process of getting coaches wrapped with our 'Haggis Waggon' design," he said.

The website features images which it claims are of the tour group's buses

Ella says she spent weeks researching Scottish history and witch trials without any payment in preparation for the role, but she'll now need to return to searching for jobs.

"I'd never thought about being a tour guide before, but when I actually was standing there that day and I put some thought into it and I would love to do that job," she said.

"It leaves you feeling a bit helpless. What am I supposed to do now?"

Another member of the group, who we're calling Jennifer, agreed to speak anonymously.

She also met Darryl Smith at the Paisley jobs fair in June, which was organised by Invest in Renfrewshire and the Department of Work and Pensions (DWP).

"[He] seemed dead friendly," she said.

"When we approached him, he was wearing a kilt and even had a polo shirt with the Haggis Waggons Tour logo printed on it, so it looked legit at the time."

She first realised she'd been recruited without interview when she was added to a group chat on WhatsApp.

The premises which were listed on the website are empty and locked up

Like Ella, her picture was also added to the Haggis Waggon Tours website.

But after waiting with Ella for the bus that didn't turn up, she asked for her information to be removed.

Jennifer doesn't think the group will ever get answers about Darryl Smith's intentions.

She's now applying for Universal Credit and has been left angered by the situation.

"What really annoys me the most is just the fact that I don't know why," she said.

"I feel disheartened. I thought I was getting a job, and it was a decent job, a good job, a fun job and something I could take pride in about my own country.

"You put your trust in someone and then they sort of rip it from underneath you.

"And it just really concerns me that he has personal details of me, and I don't want that coming back on me."
