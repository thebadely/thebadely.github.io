Let's dive into the details: He has been in big-budget blockbusters like Twilight, Tenet and The Batman, as well as a string of quirky arthouse films - so what clues can Robert Pattinson's past roles give us about what he might be like as James Bond?

Every actor creates a new James Bond, and Pattinson is one of the best in the business at crafting a memorable character.

The British actor can be an action man or an anti-hero, a romantic lead or a comic fool. He can be granite-faced or goofy, and everything in between.

"I was thinking about what the aspects of James Bond are, and he's done all of those throughout his career," says Paul Tweddle, host of the Heavy Spoilers film podcast, external.

He cites Pattinson's performance as a brooding heartthrob in teen vampire franchise Twilight, his suave and quick-witted secret agent in Christopher Nolan's Tenet, the withdrawn and focused title character in The Batman, and the ruthless and manipulative Antinous in Nolan's The Odyssey.

Pattinson has "often been drawn to really dark fare", says Anna Smith, film critic and host of the Girls on Film podcast, external.

"Wouldn't it be interesting to see Bond go a little bit darker?" she suggested to the BBC's World Service. "But also what he's got, and I'd like him to do more of, is comedy. And goodness knows, we need a Bond who can handle humour."

Unusually for a James Bond actor, one word that has cropped up frequently is "weird".

Earlier this month, Pattinson was described as "the weirdest guy in Hollywood" by the Celebrating Cinema podcast, external.

"Pattinson is an actor who makes bold, interesting, risky choices. For such a handsome guy, he can bring the weird, and then some," wrote Chris Hewitt in Empire, external.

"About 0.07 seconds after the announcement, people were already anticipating the weird with his Bond. And they may well get their wish."

Pattinson (right) starred with Willem Dafoe in The Lighthouse in 2019

Variety's chief critic Guy Lodge said, external Pattinson's reputation as an actor "has been considerably enriched by his predilection for playing, frankly, losers and weirdos", such as in more offbeat films like Good Time, The Lighthouse, Primetime and Mickey 17.

"Even his blockbuster assignments are more interesting when he lets his inner freak out to play," Lodge added. "His big, heightened, leeringly villainous turn in The Odyssey is off on its own beam entirely."

Associated Press film critic Jake Coyle said, external smaller films like The Lighthouse and High Life show "Pattinson's proclivity for pushing boundaries".

"He's certainly the funniest and quickest to laugh James Bond we've had in decades," Coyle continued. "It will be interesting to see how much of his personality bleeds into Bond, or how much the tux stifles it."

Robert Pattinson: How the Twilight star became 007 

So what do Pattinson's performances in some of his biggest films tell us about what to expect?

Pattinson in Tenet with Himesh Patel (left) and John David Washington (right)

Pattinson plays a dapper and enigmatic British agent in Christopher Nolan's time-bending 2020 thriller, as adept at bungeeing off an arms dealer's tower as he is delivering some deliciously dry wit.

"He's very suave, he's thinking on his feet," Tweddle says. "He's someone who's holding his cards close to his chest and not necessarily giving the game away.

"Tenet is the obvious one where Nolan's basically doing a kind of James Bond movie."

The Times' chief critic Kevin Maher agrees, external: "Pattinson has effectively played Bond already, when he effortlessly inhabited the role of the suave yet nonetheless lethal British spy Neil in Tenet.

"For many observers (including this one) that was the role. That was the audition. There was no turning back after Neil."

New York Times critic Alissa Wilkinson noted that Pattinson has picked up other "Bond-adjacent" roles. "The most obvious is Bruce Wayne, a.k.a. Batman, the wealthy playboy with a lair of tech toys and his own gussied-up automobile," she wrote, external.

"Wayne and Bond have a lot in common on paper, but in Pattinson's hands Wayne becomes emo, reclusive, awkward, with the hair of a '90s grunge rocker."

The Guardian's Peter Bradshaw also pointed out, external parallels with how Pattinson played "the darkly conflicted caped crusader Batman in Matt Reeves's 2022 film, lurking athletically in the shadows - like Bond, used to working in secret".

Like Tenet, The Batman proved Pattinson is an expert at action scenes, whether that be jumping off a building or being involved in an explosive car chase with The Penguin.

"Batman is a really good example of where he's a withdrawn figure, someone who has his mind focused solely on a task, and I think that's a big part of what James Bond needs to be," Tweddle says.

However, Pattinson's 007 should be a little less brooding and aloof than his stony-faced Batman and Bruce Wayne.

Although Pattinson plays the villain in Nolan's Greek epic, Antinous's menace and ruthlessness could come in handy when playing Bond, according to Tweddle.

"I think Robert Pattinson also has quite a psychotic side to him, which a lot of the James Bond books delve into - how he's a killer, and he needs to have that side to him," he says.

"What he's done in The Odyssey shows he can be manipulative, and connive and make certain plans, which is what a spy needs to do."

Collider's Collier Jennings named, external The Odyssey, along with Tenet and The Batman, as films that show Pattinson is "the perfect" fit for Bond.

"All three roles carry an element that makes James Bond work," Jennings wrote.

"Batman has the combat skills and subterfuge you'd need to be a spy of Bond's caliber, not to mention the cutting edge hardware.

"Neil has a great grasp on the intricacies of pulling off complex covert operations. 

"Antonius walks with a swagger and arrogance that either makes you love him or hate him."

Pattinson's current film sees him play real-life NBC journalist Chris Hansen, who carried out sting operations on suspected paedophiles for TV show To Catch a Predator.

It again demonstrates Pattinson's range as an actor and his ability to take on complex roles - he balances the portrayal of Hansen not as a villain but equally not as a hero.

"He's very much face-to-face with the predators in the room, and he's controlling the situation," Tweddle explains.

"It's very confrontational and demands a certain amount of power. Being able to control the room like that is also quite transferable to James Bond, and interrogations are a big part of what he does."

Pattinson will next be seen playing shape-shifting villain Scytale in the forthcoming third instalment of the epic sci-fi series - directed by Denis Villeneuve. 

The pair's reunion for the next Bond movie is not a coincidence. Villeneuve's experience of working with Pattinson on the film may have been the deciding factor when casting James Bond.

"The thing I adore with Robert Pattinson is that when you roll cameras, there's always a certain moment where you have no idea what will happen. He is an explosion in front of the camera," the director told Time, external before the Bond casting was announced.

"He has an incredible imagination and is a very playful actor, and a very creative actor, always trying to bring new ways of expressing himself and always trying to surprise me."

Surprising, bold, weird? One thing is for certain, Pattinson's James Bond is going to be interesting.
