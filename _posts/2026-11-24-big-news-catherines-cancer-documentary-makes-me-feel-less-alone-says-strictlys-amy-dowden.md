Strictly Come Dancing's Amy Dowden says the upcoming documentary from Catherine, the Princess of Wales, about her cancer journey will help many people, including herself, "not feel so alone".

Dowden praised the documentary and said it confirmed how "anyone can be dealt the devastating blow of a cancer diagnosis". 

Dowden, Strictly's first Welsh professional dancer, was diagnosed with stage 3 breast cancer at 32 and has undergone chemotherapy and two mastectomies.

Catherine's BBC documentary, Heal, will reveal how cancer has impacted her outlook on life, as well as the people, places and experiences that have helped her.

Dowden told ITV's Lorraine she believed the documentary would be "quite raw" and emotional, but "so important" in showing the "truth and effects of cancer". 

The dancer said she had a lot of admiration for the princess, adding: "Thank you Princess Catherine for doing this."

In the trailer for her documentary, Catherine is candid about life-altering effect of the disease, saying: "Cancer does change you."

"It changes your body, it changes you physically... but there is a much, much deeper part to what a cancer diagnosis shifts in you."

Dowden has previously echoed this feeling, saying that after a diagnosis, "in a click of the fingers, your life changes". 

"You have everything stripped away from you," she said.

Heal will follow Catherine as she does the National Three Peaks challenge, which involves climbing the highest mountains in England, Scotland, and Wales within 24 hours. 

Catherine completed the challenge in June to raise funds for the Royal Marsden Cancer Charity, the hospital where she underwent treatment, and to "explore life beyond diagnosis".

When the princess revealed she had cancer, Dowden shared her sympathy on Instagram. 

Since her own diagnosis in 2023, the dancer has shared her experience of breast cancer online and in the BBC documentary Strictly Amy: Cancer and Me.

She said her decision to let a camera crew film her cancer journey was scary, but she felt a duty to raise awareness.

"I felt straight away, well maybe I could make a difference."

Like Catherine, Dowden's cancer is in remission, and she has been performing in the current series of Strictly.

"It is often through pain that we realise there is a greater beauty that lies beyond that," Catherine says in her new documentary

In Heal, Catherine will discuss how the diagnosis made her "confront her own vulnerabilities".

"When you're faced with your own mortality, you have to accept that life is not infinite," she shared in a clip from the documentary. 

The princess first revealed her diagnosis of cancer in March 2024 and announced she was in remission in January 2025, after chemotherapy.

She has since begun a return to royal duties.

However, she has said she wants to dispel the idea that people can immediately return to how they felt before illness.

Heal will be shown on BBC iPlayer from 12:00 GMT and BBC One at 18:40 GMT on 29 November.
