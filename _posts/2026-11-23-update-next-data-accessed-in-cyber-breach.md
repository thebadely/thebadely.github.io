Next has said that some of its data has been accessed in a cyber breach affecting a third-party online supplier.

The high street retailer said there was no evidence that customers' personal data had been compromised.

The firm - which has its headquarters in Enderby, Leicestershire - said it had been informed by one of its suppliers about the incident, which led to "unauthorised access to certain benign data relating to Next".

"We took immediate steps to investigate the nature and scope of the incident with the supplier," the company told investors.

"Our investigations are ongoing. However, current evidence indicates that no customer personal data has been compromised.

"The information affected is limited in nature and, based on our current assessment, cannot be used to facilitate fraud against our customers or Next."

The company added it was treating the situation "with the utmost seriousness", and working with the supplier as well as the police and regulators.

Next is the latest retailer to be caught up in a cyber breach.

In October, Asos said an "unauthorised party" had gained access to an employee's account, leading customers to receive a phone alert saying that the retailer had been hacked.

The firm said the attacker gained access to some personal data, including names and contact details belonging to customers.

In 2025 Marks & Spencer suffered from a major cyber-attack, which forced it to stop all online sales for about six weeks and led to some empty shelves in shops.
