A man whose unprovoked knife attack on a 17-year-old boy caused the victim to lose his leg has been sentenced to 10-and-a-half years.

Idris Ahmed, 19, from Riverside in Cardiff, attacked the teenager on Barry Island beach on bank holiday Monday, on 25 May. He previously denied grievous bodily harm with intent, and possessing a knife, but changed his plea to guilty during the trial.

The victim, who cannot be named because of a court order relating to his age, had to have his leg amputated below the knee after the wound became infected.

In a statement read at Newport Crown Court, the victim's mother said she continued to have nightmares, and often ran into her son's room to check on him.

Appearing in the dock wearing a grey top and grey trousers, Ahmed was told by Judge Daniel Williams he would be sentenced to a decade in a young offender institution, because his "violence" had led to the 17-year-old losing his leg.

"The consequences of your action can only be understood by [the victim] and his family," the judge said.

Angharad Jones, prosecuting, said the victim suffered life-changing injuries after Ahmed attacked him "without warning". 

She said the victim had acted "as a peacemaker" between the group Ahmed was socialising with and those in his own party after some friction broke out between them.

The victim previously told the jury he had bled heavily and fallen in and out of consciousness immediately after the stabbing.

The 17-year-old fell to the ground immediately after the attack and looked pale, saying: "I am going. I feel myself going", the court previously heard.

He was airlifted to hospital where he underwent four surgeries, but despite several transfusions, doctors noticed that oxygen was not flowing properly to the lower leg.

The jury heard it was determined the teenager needed an amputation of the lower half of his leg.

Following the stabbing, Ahmed fled the scene and discarded the knife close by, an act seen by an eyewitness who later identified him in an identity parade.

He was also captured fleeing the scene by a woman who was taking selfies on the beach with her partner.

Before he threw the knife, Ahmed was heard saying: "I need to dash this."

The incident took place on Barry Island on bank holiday Monday on 25 May

In her impact statement, read in court, the victim's mother said the incident had devastated the family and changed their lives.

She said she continues to have nightmares and panic attacks, and often runs into her son's room to check on his wellbeing.

"I will always be extremely grateful that [my son] survived, but surviving isn't the same as being OK. We are not OK," she said.

Speaking outside the court after Ahmed was sentenced, she also discussed the impact of knife crime.

"I really wish people would know the effect that it has," she said, visibly emotional.

"The family is who gets left with it. The impact of trying to get our lives back together.

"There is never going to be anything that we can do to make his life better."

"People need to understand that when you pick up that knife, in that moment of time, you're destroying somebody's life forever. There is no turning back," she said.

The victim's sister said she had taken her brother to the beach that day and "never imagined anything could happen".

"They were laughing, singing and joking in the car. A few hours later our lives were shattered," she said.

A letter written by Ahmed was read in court, stating he accepted "full responsibility" and recognised he had "a real impact on someone's life".

"I have thought of [the victim] and his family every day, and the suffering they have been through because of my actions," he said, adding: "Since the offence I have completely changed my thinking."

Det Insp Emma Hampton, from South Wales Police, said the "tragic case" highlighted the "devastating and far-reaching consequences of knife crime", which "remains a key policing focus nationally".

She said the force was committed to tackling the issue by preventing young people from carrying them and "robustly dealing with those we find involved in such activity".
