Here’s what you need to know: As a player, Sam Burgess won a premiership in the NRL with South Sydney Rabbitohs - but will his first success in the northern hemisphere come as a coach?

Britain did not have a single motorway in 1955.

We were still five years out from the MOT being introduced and cars did not require a seatbelt.

There was one television channel, provided by the BBC.

Capital punishment was still in place.

The humble postcode had not yet been rolled out across the land.

And rationing was still very much in the national memory, having only been phased out a year beforehand.

All of this paints a picture as to what life was like when Warrington last won a league championship.

On Saturday they stand 80 minutes away from ending a 71-year wait for another title and their boss Sam Burgess has the chance to lay down a marker as a coach just as he did during an illustrious playing career.

But can Super League's perennial nearly side finally get over the line and finally earn a league triumph in the modern era?

Former England captain Burgess began his career as Warrington head coach in earnest ahead of the 2024 season - off the back of Daryl Powell's uncompetitive stint in charge of the Wire.

A legend of the game as a player, it was his first top job as a coach, five years after he retired following his enviable stint both on the pitch and as assistant coach down under with South Sydney Rabbitohs.

A first Wembley appearance came shortly after, as Wolves reached that season's Challenge Cup final, when they were undone by a Wigan Warriors side on the way to a quadruple.

Twelve months later, treble-winners-in-waiting Hull KR beat them again in the final.

Burgess' side eventually missed out on a play-off spot altogether in 2025, finishing eighth, but, in contrast, they have put together a consistent campaign in 2026 to finish third in the table, behind second-placed Leeds on points difference.

So you might expect Burgess to be feeling rather pleased with himself, and his side, as they prepare for the first Grand Final of his tenure and a meeting with Powell, who has inspired Wakefield's ascent from the Championship to the top of the sport after leaving the Wire.

Well, not quite.

"Not really," Burgess replied when asked in his pre-match media commitments whether it gave him extra satisfaction that his side had proved him right by emerging as challengers after their disappointing 2025.

"I'm pretty satisfied to be here. I guess I'll find out how satisfied I am on Saturday at about eight o'clock."

Warrington reach Grand Final with Leeds win

It took 14 years into the Super League era before Warrington reached their first Grand Final and this year is the fifth time they have reached Old Trafford, following previous finals in 2012, 2013, 2016 and 2018.

Tony Smith's impressive Wire side came undone against Leeds first and then again at the hands of Wigan in 2013 and 2016 - with Steve Price last leading the club to a Grand Final in 2018 when they were defeated by the Warriors for a third time in a row.

Since that point, the Primrose and Blue have failed to trouble the upper echelons of the sport, with St Helens' dominant spell starting the following year while Wigan and Hull KR have both taken turns at having a commanding stint of their own.

So far this year, they have narrowly missed out on second place in the regular season and dispatched reigning champions Hull KR and Leeds in the play-offs.

Yet when times were tough in 2025, Burgess never gave up and said that it was a belief in his side and the knowledge that Warrington had a long-term plan that kept his camp motivated.

"I could see a lot of green shoots last year where we're going," he added.

"You've got to be able to believe it to do it, and I believed in the club, I believed in the players we had, and I believed in the philosophy that we've got, so I guess we've been very consistent all year without jumping out at anyone, which is a good sign.

"We've had a lot of challenges throughout the year, we've managed them well as a club, which has been important."

Steve McNamara (right) led Catalans Dragons to two Super League Grand Finals before he joined Sam Burgess' (left) coaching staff at Warrington

Ask those in the know at the beginning of the 2026 season which two teams would contest the Grand Final and not many would have picked out Warrington or Wakefield.

"It's Warrington's year" has become something of a meme in the northern hemisphere's rugby league fandom in the past few years - describing how one of the league's best-funded and well-supported teams always looks the part, but falls at the final hurdle.

Former England coach Steve McNamara arrived at the club as an assistant coach last October following his departure from a spell at Catalans, who he guided to the Challenge Cup and two Super League Grand Finals.

He is set to join Hull FC for next season but Burgess believes their previous failures have not happened in a vacuum - but have shaped the team they have become this season.

"We've played a couple of big games over the last three years, so we've got a bit of experience," said Burgess.

"We have matured as a group and as a club. The club has matured with us, which is important. Whether it counts as much, on Saturday, we'll see."

Of course, Burgess' time as a player can be summed up by a stunning 2014 season which culminated in a victorious NRL Grand Final with the Rabbitohs, when he played on with a broken cheekbone and also won the Clive Churchill Medal as man of the match.

Asked about how he reflected on that moment ahead of his first Grand Final as a coach, Burgess said: "It's much easier as a player.

"Any experience is experience whether it's as a player, coach, staff member, assistant, whatever. We've got a bit of experience in the room. 

"Steve McNamara brought a lot of experience to us this year as well, which is going to help for something.

"We've got a lot of things in our corner. Again, when the whistle blows at six o'clock, you've got to do the right jobs and get the performance on the field."
