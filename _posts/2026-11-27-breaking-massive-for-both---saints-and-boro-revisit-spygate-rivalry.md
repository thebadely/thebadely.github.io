Let's dive into the details: With a win tonight, Tonda Eckert's Southampton would move to within four points of Kim Hellberg's Championship-leading Middlesbrough side

"I don't want to shut out the noise, we just need to know that it's coming and it's going to be electric." 

The "noise" Southampton head coach Tonda Eckert refers to has been building over recent weeks, with 'Spygate' returning to the collective consciousness as Saints and Middlesbrough meet on Friday (20:00 GMT) for the first time since the scandal.

The most recent time they came face-to-face was in last season's Championship play-off semi-finals.

Saints ended up winning over the two legs, but were then expelled from the final having being caught spying on a Boro training session before the first match at the Riverside. 

It turned into a five-month saga which started with a Saints performance analyst being photographed from behind a tree, and ended with Eckert being handed a suspended ban and fined Â£28,000.

The German head coach signed a new deal at Saints exactly a week ago and previously admitted the experience of Spygate had changed his life.

Now St Mary's awaits as the two sides will re-ignite their rivalry after a tough end to the last campaign for both.

Saints players felt aggrieved for not being allowed to feature in the play-off final, while Boro suffered the heartbreak of losing at Wembley, as Hull City won promotion to the Premier League.

"It's a massive game for both teams," former Nottingham Forest and Ipswich defender Luke Chambers told the 72+ podcast.

"Middlesbrough have bounced back tremendously well from the play-offs, the ups and downs, they didn't know if they were coming or going, and to go through that heartache of making the final and not getting over the line - credit to them. 

"It will be an electric atmosphere and there will be added needle on the pitch, especially with some of the things that happened in the games between the two sides last season when the news broke out."

Boro boss Kim Hellberg and Eckert both arrived at their respective clubs in November 2025 and have enjoyed plenty of success on the pitch over the past 12 months.

But following the 2-1 defeat in May's play-off semi-final second leg at St Mary's, Hellberg described it as "one of the toughest days" in his coaching career.

Although Friday's game promises to be filled with emotion on the pitch and in the stands, the two head coaches claim to have made up since the events of last season after Eckert called Hellberg to apologise over the summer. 

"We spoke and he called me. I've always been a person who wants to give people a second chance," Hellberg told BBC Radio Tees.

"He has also gone though some things that have been very tough for him in terms of this which is something you don't wish anyone.

"I think in general for me it's overplayed. We have talked about it, everything has been sorted and now I'm looking forward to playing against a good side with a good manager and see how good we can be, and I'm really looking forward to it."

After Eckert took responsibility and apologised over the summer, both he and the club also expressed a desire to move on after the backing of the Saints board and owner Dragan Solak. 

Some members of the Middlesbrough press have been robust and consistent in their strong criticism of Southampton and Eckert since Spygate first emerged.

Eckert had walked out of media conferences during the play-off semi-final following some stern questioning of him, while a piece was published on Thursday which questioned the German's character.

On Friday, Southampton published their own video, external on club channels, emotionally recalling the play-off campaign and how success was "ours... until it wasn't" before Boro "got another go", followed by footage of Oli McBurnie's winner for Hull at Wembley.

"People talk about last season, but we're focused on this one," narrates Southampton-based streamer Tyrone, over clips of goals from this season. "And now, here we are again; new hope, more reasons to believe, the same leader. We know all eyes are on us, and we're ready." 

Southampton fan Rob Maddox echoed the emotions of both managers and felt that the majority of both fanbases want to now put the previous distaste behind them.

"There has been so much football since the play-off semi-final that I think both clubs are ready to get on with it now," he said. 

"Some might look to hype the game up as a rivalry but I think the game should be looked at as two very good footballing sides going head to head. 

"Of course there is going to be that little bit of spice and I'm sure we'll see it in the stands, but I hope that after the game we're talking about both teams showing off what they can do on the pitch."

Boro fan Jack McBride agreed that "Spygate's over and done with now" but admitted there was still a feeling of "bitterness" within the fanbases.

"I've seen some back and forth which is fun and all part of being a football fan, but there's also the other side of it which is bitter, twisted and they're still going on about it in a negative way," he told BBC Radio Tees.

"We had our chance and we didn't take it. Saints have had a four-point deduction, Tonda's been punished, we move on. Was it enough? Personally, I don't think that it was but we move on from it.

"This is a game we've all been looking to, and it was probably the first fixture that Boro and Saints fans were looking for.

"I think it's going to be quite a hostile atmosphere. I think Saints will be booing constantly and Boro fans will bring the noise as much as they can."

The sides' latest showdown is not only a tasty encounter because of the fallout from Spygate, but also because two of the division's best teams will be going toe-to-toe.

Boro are top of the table and have the Championship's leading goalscorer in Will Lankshear, who has scored 14 times in 15 appearances.

The 21-year-old joined the club from Tottenham Hotspur over the summer for a fee that could rise to as high as Â£20m, following an impressive loan spell at Oxford United.

Meanwhile, Saints forward Cyle Larin has also been enjoying something of a prolific campaign himself, netting eight times in his 13 matches.

Eckert's side are sixth, but would be fourth and three points off Boro if not for the four-point deduction handed to them because of Spygate.

The two sides have the best home records so far this season and have both scored more than 30 goals each across their 17 matches played.

"Friday's a massive game. A massive football game before anything else," Boro midfielder Myles Peart-Harris told BBC Radio Tees.

"The rivalry we now have is even better so the boys are looking forward to Friday. Hopefully we play our football, stick to what we know and get a result, we're ready. 

"We're more excited about the game of football because we know they like to play football, we like to play football, so I think tactically it's going to be a great time for everyone to watch and most importantly we can go there and get a win."

Former Reading and Leyton Orient midfielder Jobi McAnuff told the 72+ podcast: "What it boils down to is two of the best teams in the league this year. 

"I think it'll be a cracking atmosphere and there will still be needle there because it was such a huge story, it was massive.

"The implications and fallout went on for months and I think that will add to it. It will be a fantastic spectacle and I hope this season we are talking about what happens on the pitch rather than what's happened off it."
