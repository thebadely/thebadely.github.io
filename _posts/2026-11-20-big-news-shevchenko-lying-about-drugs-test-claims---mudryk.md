Mykhailo Mudryk has accused Ukraine legend Andriy Shevchenko of lying about the circumstances surrounding the winger's positive drugs test as his dispute with the country's football federation continues.

Tottenham Hotspur winger Mudryk, who is on a season-long loan from Chelsea, was suspended from November 2024 to July 2026 after testing positive for the banned substance meldonium.

In an interview on Wednesday, Mudryk said he blames the Ukrainian Association of Football (UAF) for his failed drugs test and subsequent ban, believing it entered his system while on international duty.

In response, Shevchenko, the UAF president, accused Mudryk's representatives of repeatedly asking for a UAF employee to "falsely" take the blame for his failed test.

Former Chelsea and AC Milan striker Shevchenko also said Mudryk's comments amounted to a "baseless slander against our people, who do their work honestly and professionally", and called on the player to retract his words.

In a statement on Friday, Mudryk refuted Shevchenko's accusation, saying it was a "lie" and that "neither I nor my representatives made any such request".

Mudryk has also accused the UAF of attempting to mislead his team's investigation into the reasons behind his failed test, and claims to have evidence proving it.

"My team and I repeatedly asked the UAF to help establish how I might have been exposed to meldonium and who, if anyone, might have been responsible," said Mudryk. 

"In several letters to the UAF at the time, my lawyers made clear that we were seeking truthful information that could be independently verified. One of those letters was leaked to the media by the UAF earlier today and clearly shows that my lawyers wanted 'true, verifiable and reliable information'.

"Further, the UAF were repeatedly unhelpful and even attempted to mislead my team in order to distance themselves from the case. For example, UAF staff testified at my hearing that I was not using the UAF's supplements and Mr Shevchenko has repeated that assertion in his statement - but I have WhatsApp messages that prove that I was using their products during their training camps. That is, I have evidence to prove that their repeated assertions are wrong."

Mudryk also said the UAF was "repeatedly slow" to respond to requests from his team and "withheld important information and documents" from his lawyers.

BBC Sport has contacted the UAF for comment.
