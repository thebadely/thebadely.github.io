According to new developments, Andy Farrell's Ireland beat South Africa on Saturday

After Ireland's win over world champions South Africa on Saturday, Andy Farrell had one eye on the past, and another on what is to come.

It was a result that had left Ireland's head coach brimming with pride, as they ended a run of six straight defeats against either the Springboks, New Zealand or France. 

With the World Cup now just 10 months away, it is all part of the bigger picture as Ireland aim to progress past the quarter-finals for the first time, with the ultimate goal of lifting the Webb Ellis Cup.

"Whether we lose by three or win by three, you understand how you apply yourself in a big game like that, and it doesn't get any bigger," Farrell said after the 20-17 win.

"You're always trying to kick on to the next level, and sometimes you don't know what that level is because you've never been there as a group.

"But this group is like that. We're trying to do new things together, and it sets us up nicely to keep kicking on."

Farrell looked back to the opening three matches of the Nations Championship in July, when he handed a number of players minutes at international level against Australia and Japan - such as Nick Timoney, Tom Stewart and Tom Clarkson.

Those players played a key role in downing the Springboks in Dublin as second-half replacements.

"We chose to take players to the Southern Hemisphere who had not had that type of experience.

"It's never going to be smelling of roses in that regard [performances over 80 minutes], so you end up where you're at, but we grew as a group, so therefore it's priceless for us."

Given how the tables were weighted, Ireland would have topped the southern hemisphere table by two points.

But the northern hemisphere's domination of the inaugural competition meant a more condensed table, and a fifth-place play-off against Australia will be their reward.

But Friday will be another tough test against a Wallabies side who were one missed penalty away from beating Ireland in Sydney in July.

Speaking before he knew who Ireland would be playing, and when, Farrell denied that playing for fifth place would be an anti-climax.

"You can only deal with what's in front of you," he said.

"We've got another week of international rugby together. We're in the green shirt, and that's special and it's a privilege, so we'll look forward to that."

Ireland's team selection to face South Africa had a surprise as captain Dan Sheehan, who usually plays at hooker, was named to start at number eight.

It was only his second professional start in the back row, with his first outing coming for Leinster against Munster before the international window.

Farrell's decision was certainly justified as Sheehan more than played his role in a scrum that held its own against the world-leading Springbok pack. 

"He's so calm, isn't he? I spoke to him over the last couple of weeks about all sorts of things, about number eight, but he wasn't worried and it doesn't bother him in the slightest," said Farrell.

"It's like, 'why are you asking me that question?'. It's almost like an insult. He's so calm, he's such a gifted rugby player.

"That's his character. He just sees it for what it is and he gets on with it. That calm and influence has a knock-on effect that goes through the team."

Another standout for Ireland was Jamison Gibson-Park, whose experience and guile helped the hosts get over the line as the Boks piled on late pressure.

One clearing kick, from his own try line to the halfway line, was a particular standout in a game filled with classy moments.

Farrell described the 34-year-old's kicking as "immense", and said he is getting better with each passing year.

"He got us out of trouble so many times. You almost expect it from him, but you shouldn't," said Farrell.

"He is growing the whole time. I've never seen him in this good of nick, it just shows the hunger that he's got.

"That's what we've got within our group as well, we've got senior players who are constantly working so hard to continue to get better."
