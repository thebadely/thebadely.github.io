Here’s what you need to know: Ceri Holland has not played for Liverpool since mid-September

Ceri Holland has been named in the Wales squad for next month's Women's World Cup play-off final against Norway despite being out since September through injury.

Olivia Clark, Hayley Ladd, Rhiannon Roberts and Ella Powell are also included after fitness issues, but Ffion Morgan, Laura Hughes and Scarlett Hill are among those who miss out with injuries.

The versatile Holland, arguably Wales' most influential player, has been out with a muscle problem, though she is thought to be close to returning to action for club side Liverpool.

Her involvement is a major boost for Wales as they attempt to reach the Women's World Cup for the first time.

Rhian Wilkinson's side face Norway at the Cardiff City Stadium on Tuesday, 1 December, before the return leg in Oslo four days later.

Leicester goalkeeper Clark and Sunderland defender Roberts are also included even though they have not played since September, but the loss of promising Manchester United defender Hill is a blow given her impressive displays against Albania.

Crystal Palace's Ladd and Powell, of Bristol City, have played for their clubs since missing Wales' World Cup play-off semi-final games against the Albanians last month.

"For the play-off finals, just to have those experienced players back in the squad and on the pitch, I am very pleased," said Wales head coach Wilkinson. 

"I think the blend of our young players and that experience is critical. The [young players] are almost naive, not understanding the play-off final pressure - it doesn't mean much to them. That's fantastic - that brings something to the group.

"Then we have the experienced heads who understand that the first leg is half-time, that a ranking is a ranking, that opposition hype is often merited, but equally they are just another team in our way."

With 204 caps between them, Hayley Ladd (left) and Rhiannon Roberts (right) bring welcome experience to Wales' squad 

Winger Morgan had returned to action for West Ham after not being involved against Albania, but she is back on the sidelines after suffering a knee injury while playing for the Hammers last weekend.

Wilkinson said it is down to Morgan's club to reveal the extent of her injury. 

"I haven't spoken directly to her, I've sent her a message," Wilkinson added.

"I think obviously when you pick up an injury and she's so proud of being Welsh and loves coming into camp... it's a double disappointment. 

"But I'll speak to her later. Just give her some time."

Wolves midfielder Chloe Williams keeps her place having been a late call-up for the semi-final, while the only uncapped player in the squad is third-choice goalkeeper Soffia Kelly.

Wales are hoping to cause an upset as they bid to reach the Women's World Cup for the first time, though beating former champions Norway may not be enough to guarantee a place in Brazil next summer.

The complex nature of the qualification process means that Wales may have to negotiate inter-confederation play-offs next February to secure a World Cup place, even if they triumph over Norway.
