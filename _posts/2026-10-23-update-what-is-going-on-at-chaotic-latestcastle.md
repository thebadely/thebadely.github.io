That sequence includes back-to-back 4-1 away defeats at Leeds United on 14 September and Coventry City on 12 October.

"The pressure will seriously be on if they don't get a result at Palace," Charlotte Robson from the True Faith Newcastle United podcast says.

Expectations were high when the former Red Bull Salzburg boss arrived on Tyneside shortly before the start of the season with a strong record of success at his previous clubs in Saudi Arabia and Austria. 

Yet Jaissle, 38, is very much unproven in the Premier League.

Seventy-nine days into his reign, it is fair to say he is still trying to put his own stamp on the team after Howe's long tenure.

Jaissle has issues to solve at both ends of the pitch.

Newcastle are without a home clean sheet in each of their last 16 games in all competitions - the longest run in their history. They are leaking an average of two goals per game in the Premier League - only Crystal Palace (17) and Ipswich (15) have conceded more than Newcastle's 14.

Meanwhile, they registered one attempt on target against Villa - their least in a Premier League home match since August 2024. Worryingly, that solo attempt came from defender Sven Botman, while Jaissle used centre-back Dan Burn, a substitute, up front when he was sent on.

There is an argument Newcastle are even yet to adequately replace forward Callum Wilson, who left the same summer as Isak after scoring 47 times in 113 Premier League appearances. Yoane Wissa was technically his replacement with Nick Woltemade the Isak replacement.

Robson believes patience is needed after a summer of upheaval.

There is certainly no panic inside the club right now, with David Hopkinson, the CEO, and Ross Wilson, the sporting director, both applying an air of calm.

But for how much longer?

It has by no means been a terrible start by Jaissle, who is expected to be given time to get it right. That said, fans want to see an improvement in performances - starting on Sunday.

After the weekend, Newcastle visit Everton in the last-16 of the Carabao Cup next Thursday, host Everton in the Premier League on 2 November and then visit Fulham five days later before the next international break.

Jaissle will not want to go into that without a win in six league and cup games.

"There are more questions being asked now," adds Robson.

"At the start of the season it was made clear in interviews we wanted to be an aggressive attacking team. We are not there yet, we're still working it out."
