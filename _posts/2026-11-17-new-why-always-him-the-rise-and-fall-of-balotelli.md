Let's dive into the details: Why always him? The rise and fall of Balotelli

Maverick. Loose cannon. Unmanageable.

Mario Balotelli heard it all.

Perhaps better known for off-field antics than his undeniable - yet arguably wasted - talent with the ball at his feet, the Italian has always attracted headlines.

Fifteen years have passed since his famous 'Why always me?' Manchester derby T-shirt celebration, which came just days after a firework was set off in his own bathroom, causing a house fire.

The 36-year-old is still playing, now in the United Arab Emirates first division with Al-Ittifaq alongside his biological brother, Enock Barwuah.

Here, BBC Sport looks at a career that has included 13 clubs, six countries - and an assist for arguably the most famous goal in Premier League history.

Typical of his unconventional footballing life, Balotelli's career did not begin in an elite club's academy.

Instead, by his mid-teens he was turning out for Italian third-tier side Lumezzane, close to the family home in Concesio.

A trial at Barcelona did not lead to a long-term stay, and instead he was picked up by Inter Milan in 2006.

It was at Inter that Balotelli first came across his great mentor - Roberto Mancini.

The relationship between the two has been likened to that of a father and son. Under the Italian coach's tutelage, Balotelli flourished.

Aged 17, Balotelli helped Inter win the 2007-08 Serie A title and reach the Coppa Italia final, memorably scoring twice against Juventus to send his side into the semi-finals.

Life changed for Balotelli with the arrival of Jose Mourinho in the weeks after that campaign ended.

There would be disagreements, disciplinary action and accusations that Balotelli's attitude was not up to scratch. Mourinho said Balotelli was "unmanageable" and described one performance as "close to a zero rating".

The relationship with Mourinho was fractious - a rare occasion when the Special One was confounded by a player.

Balotelli still won three Serie A titles and one Champions League with Inter, but his reputation as a supposed loose cannon was already beginning to overshadow his feats as a player.

Balotelli played for Mancini at Inter and Manchester City - and was briefly recalled for Italy by his old boss in 2018

Logic dictated that Balotelli's next step should be to reunite with mentor Mancini. That happened in August 2010 when the 20-year-old joined Manchester City.

Mancini set out to show that the unpredictable ways of Balotelli could be harnessed for the team's good, aiming to get more out of him than Mourinho could.

A hardline stance on discipline often appeared to soften when Mancini dealt with Balotelli, who by spring 2011 had garnered a reputation in England as a fantastic footballer who could not stay away from controversy.

Having already been sent off twice for City and been involved in a car crash - not in his famous camouflage Bentley but an Audi R8 - the striker threw a dart at a youth-team player, an act that showed a level of immaturity.

After helping City to win the FA Cup in 2011, ending their 35-year major trophy drought, Balotelli came under scrutiny after attempting a backheel trick shot when through on goal during a pre-season friendly against LA Galaxy.

Mancini swiftly hooked him, later questioning his mentality by saying: "If you are serious, you can play 90 minutes. If not, you can come and sit by me on the bench."

Sometimes the audacity paid off - like the famous shoulder goal and subsequent nonchalant celebration against Norwich.

On other occasions, not so much.

In October 2011, emergency services attended his Cheshire home to deal with a firework lit in his bathroom. Balotelli said a friend had been responsible.

BBC Sport reported at the time that four firefighters used two hose reel water jets to douse the flames, with a large fan deployed to clear smoke.

Mario Balotelli scored 30 goals in 80 games for Manchester City

Days later he was in the spotlight for the right reasons as he scored City's opener in a 6-1 win over Manchester United, celebrating with the 'Why always me?' T-shirt.

There were other stories - that he had given a homeless person Â£1,000, that he had turned up to a young boy's school to confront the headmaster about the child being bullied, that he drove around Manchester handing out presents while dressed as Santa.

True? Perhaps only Balotelli knows and he has often denied such rumours. But such was his reputation at the time that the stories became plausible.

A chant City fans coined made reference to his ironic allergy to grass - which kept him out of a Europa League match in 2011 - and driving around Moss Side with "a wallet full of cash".

On the pitch, Balotelli was sent off against Arsenal in April 2012 in what, at the time, seemed like a season-defining moment that could end Manchester City's title bid.

But one month later he assisted Sergio Aguero's last-gasp goal against Queens Park Rangers that gave City a first Premier League title. It was his only Premier League assist that season.

Micah Richards, who was a team-mate and friend to Balotelli at City, said: "He was crazy in a good way. He was brilliant to have around the squad - a cult hero not just for the nonsense he did off the field but on it as well.

"The best thing about him is he's a lovely guy - he's not arrogant or how the papers try to perceive him, he's just a bit mad."

But did the moments of brilliance make the "nonsense" worth it?

Despite once insisting his patience with Balotelli was "limitless", Mancini ultimately had a rethink.

The final straw came in January 2013 when the pair had an argument at the training ground. Balotelli left for AC Milan later that month.

Mancini said: "He is not a bad guy, and a fantastic player, but I'm very sorry for him as he continues to lose his talent and his quality. I don't have any words for his behaviour.

"I hope for him he can understand he is in a bad way for his future."

Affection remained on both sides, but the relationship had become too fraught.

"Once upon a time, Mario did genuinely believe he was one of the best players in the world," said Nedum Onuoha, another former City team-mate.

"There are lots of stories about him from his time at City, but the one I remember is at our old training ground in Carrington when someone was talking about Lionel Messi and Cristiano Ronaldo, and Mario said he was as good as them - and he was deadly serious.

"It obviously hasn't worked out that way, but he still has done so much more with his career than most players could even dream of."

Balotelli never threatened to have an international career to rival Ronaldo or Messi.

But there were still moments that linger in the memory.

He became the first black player to feature for Italy at a major tournament when he starred at Euro 2012.

And, more than that, he hit three goals to finish as the tournament's joint top scorer - with five others - as Italy lost to Spain in the final.

An abiding image is that of a shirtless Balotelli flexing his muscles after scoring the goal that sent the Azzurri to the final, with a 2-1 win against Germany.

Balotelli's celebration against Germany in the Euro 2012 semi-finals became an iconic image of the tournament

Yet that tournament proved to be the peak of Balotelli's international career.

Italy's national team took a downward turn, and so too did his club career. By 2015, Balotelli had more or less became an Azzurri afterthought.

While Balotelli enjoyed some big moments in an Italy shirt, he did not always feel the love reciprocated from his country of birth.

Before Euro 2012 he threatened to leave the pitch if he was subjected to racist abuse and spoke openly about the vitriol he suffered while playing for Inter.

In fact, racist abuse has followed Balotelli throughout his career and he has commendably refused to tolerate it.

Born Mario Barwuah in Palermo to Ghanaian parents, Balotelli was placed into foster care aged three.

Initially, he stayed with his foster parents - Silvia and Francesco Balotelli - in Brescia during the week, then returned to his biological parents at weekends. He was eventually fostered on a full-time basis and took on the Balotelli surname.

Balotelli identifies with both parts of his heritage - Ghana and Italy - and he struggled to reconcile his love for a nation with the people who aimed verbal abuse his way.

The good times were all too brief: 36 caps and 14 goals has been his tally since 2018. He wanted more and has often made it known.

Onuoha said: "Playing for his country was always so important to him and that's another really positive thing about him, that he had that drive and ambition."

Mancini told AC Milan that Balotelli could become one of the world's best while in Serie A.

A glut of goals followed the striker's return to the San Siro in January 2013, before a surprising move back to the Premier League followed 18 months later.

Liverpool had been impressed, but Balotelli's spell on Merseyside would prove forgettable for all. One goal in 16 Premier League games under Brendan Rodgers was not what was expected from the Â£16m buy.

The failed transfer sparked a loan move back to Milan, where injuries hampered his progress. When it was time for Balotelli to return to Merseyside, Jurgen Klopp had arrived and said the striker would not be part of his Liverpool plans.

Balotelli later called the move to Anfield "the worst mistake of my life".

Was it that Liverpool move that sent Balotelli's career spiralling into freefall?

Spells in Nice, Marseille, Brescia and Monza followed - each to varying degrees of success but never with a feeling of stability.

Then came Turkish club Adana Demirspor, Swiss outfit Sion and Italian side Genoa. He spent no more than one season at a time with any of these clubs, although he did return to Demirspor for a second stint.

And so to the present day, with Balotelli aged 36 and plying his trade in the UAE.

It is easy to wonder what might have been had Balotelli stayed in Manchester, had he managed to settle enough to entirely fulfil his potential.

But, after so much media attention and a career that has spanned more continents and leagues than most, it is perhaps also understandable that Balotelli has settled on fulfilment through simply playing the game he loves with his brother.

"Playing with a family member like he is will give him that happiness," said Onuoha.

"And yes, it is not Serie A, La Liga or the Premier League, but Mario is on a field sharing it with his brother - for him that will mean everything.

"At this point there aren't many people that would hold much against him. I think when he was younger, some people would.

"But now I think it is more a case of almost allowing him this happiness, and enjoying seeing him finish off the last days of his career in a place that makes him happy.

"So many people end their careers on a sour note, but for him he is going to be ending it doing exactly what he wants to do."
