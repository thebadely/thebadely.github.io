Rangers manager Derek McInnes says his team's naivety and complacency made their task more difficult in their thrilling 3-2 win over Aberdeen, but hailed their ability to get the job done.

The Ibrox outfit had to navigate a tense finale as they held off a defiant Aberdeen side, just three days after their dramatic late win at Motherwell, and are now just a point behind Celtic at the Scottish Premiership summit.

Rangers had roared into a 3-1 lead after a stunning 11-minute burst at the start of the second half overwhelmed the visitors, who led at the break through Brad Lyons' header. 

However, the Dons pulled one back through vibrant youngster Findlay Marshall and  both sides had opportunities to add to the scoreline in a gripping closing passage.

"We put ourselves in the mire there, we were so naive at times," McInnes told Sky Sports.

"The biggest part for me is how we lose the initiative by just being too complacent and that has to change because we won't always outscore opponents.

"Once we got ourselves in front you think 'just keep the shoulder to the wheel'. We hit them like a train and they were 3-1 down because of the speed, intensity, intelligence and then we started to play backwards. 

"We played more backwards passes than I can remember in a long time, we need to show more control."

McInnes' displeasure at the first-half display manifested itself in a half-time triple substitution as Findlay Curtis, Tuur Rommens and Kim Min-Su were hurled into the action.

Rangers were a different team in the 11 minutes that followed, with goals from Ryan Naderi, Djeidi Gassama and Nico Raskin turning the game on its head and flipping the mood of the Ibrox faithful. 

"For us it was all about winning, we've won the game and we'll dust ourselves down," McInnes added after a 14th straight game without defeat. 

"It was three good goals and I'm delighted by the impact of the subs and we dug it out, but we made it harder for ourselves."

That ability to grind out wins was evident at Fir Park on Saturday and was apparent again at Ibrox. Belief is growing among Rangers supporters, who are longing for a first league title in six years.

They have won six points from losing positions this season, a tally bettered only by St Johnstone and Celtic, who have seven apiece.

A win over Falkirk on Saturday, before Celtic travel to face Hearts on Sunday, would put Rangers top of the table for just the second time this season.

For the umpteenth time this season, Rangers were indebted to in-form striker Naderi.

The 23-year-old struggled for a consistent run of games in the early stages of his Ibrox career, but has more shown his worth with Youssef Chermiti and Lawrence Shankland sidelined through injury.

He has 13 goal contributions in 13 league appearances - eight goals and five assists - and also fired Rangers into the Premier Sports Cup final with his winner against Aberdeen at Hampden earlier this month.

"A lot of players have had plaudits recently, but where would Rangers be without Naderi?," former Rangers striker Kris Boyd asked on Sky Sports.

"He's scored big goals for Rangers this season. His goal return has been outstanding and they're big, big goals for Rangers."

Naderi, who featured twice for the Czech Republic during the international break, has the Scottish Premiership's highest expected goals (xG) and xG per 90 minutes.

His perfectly weighted assist for Gassama showed the touch of a player full of confidence and playing at the peak of his powers.

After weathering an early Rangers storm, Aberdeen took the lead and looked well worth their advantage at half-time.

For all of their endeavour and late surge, they failed to recover from their 11-minute capitulation. Still, Aberdeen legend Willie Miller was heartened by the performance under new head coach Mario Frick.

"It's been a long time since I've seen a performance as good as that from an Aberdeen side, albeit it was a defeat," Miller said.

"They pushed Rangers all the way. It will be a learning process for them, you'd certainly hope that would be the case."
