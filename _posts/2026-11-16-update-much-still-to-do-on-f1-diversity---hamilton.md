Lewis Hamilton says there is "much still to do" when it comes to increasing diversity in Formula 1.

The seven-time champion has used a report published on the five-year anniversary of establishing his Mission 44 charity, external to demand three key steps from the sport.

The campaign seeks to increase the representation of black people, women and people from lower socio-economic backgrounds in F1.

The report recognises that all parts of the industry have made steps to increase diversity.

But it said the sport did not have the data to show whether these efforts had "changed who enters, progresses and stays in motorsport".

Hamilton said: "When I walk through the paddock now, I see more women, more Black and Brown faces, more people who look like the fans in the grandstands than at any point since I started. That is heartening, and the people behind it deserve credit.

"And yet, there is much still to do. We have opened the door, but we have not yet learned to count how many people walk through it, or how many stay.

"The sport's first social impact report shows how much is happening. This report asks what it adds up to.  This is a sport that measures performance to the thousandth of a second. We can measure this too."

The Mission 44 report makes three recommendations:

Measure who joins, who is promoted and who stays, and let the best practice be recognised.

Take investment in people out of the F1 cost cap and put money behind the pipeline collectively.

Publish the paid places being offered every year.

An F1 spokesperson said: "We are proud of the programmes and initiatives which help inspire the next generation of Formula 1 and how they continue to increase access and opportunity across our sport.

"Following recommendations from the Hamilton Commission, the sport signed its first ever diversity and inclusion charter in 2024, and in 2025 conducted its first ever sport-wide data gathering exercise to capture the global reach of our social impact programmes, external.

"This showed that in 2025 season alone, more than 500,000 young people engaged in education, STEM and inspiration programmes globally and over 1,000 structured skills and talent development opportunities across our sport.

"As F1 continues to grow on the global stage, so too does our ambition to deepen and evolve our social impact. We are committed, alongside the F1 teams, the FIA and broader members of our sport to continue build on this foundation and look forward to sharing the next phase of our work."

The Mission 44 report proposes that F1, governing body the FIA, the teams and the sport's main sponsors "invest at least Â£50m of new money over the next five years into supporting young people from underrepresented backgrounds into motorsport careers, and supporting them to stay".

It said it was not able to say whether activity related to diversity was succeeding in increasing numbers because there was "no collated data on the entire motorsports workforce, only on the 'activity and reach' of social impact programmes".

It added: "Figures are self-reported through a structured data request.

"However, we cannot see who is recruited as a result of the activity, who stays or who progresses in their career.

"The data does not record demographic details of who participated in the programmes, and teams define underrepresented groups differently, so even where numbers exist, they cannot be compared.

"The original question of the Hamilton Commission was how many Black people worked in motorsport. Five years on, the sport cannot say exactly how that number has changed."

The report recognised examples of improvement in diversity at teams who reported figures. Not all did.

Mercedes set itself a target in 2020 to improve representation of underrepresented groups in its workforce after data showed only 3% of were from minority ethnic backgrounds, and 12% were women.

After implementing training, recruitment and development processes for hiring, by 2025 Mercedes reported 20% of employees were women, 10% were from minority ethnic backgrounds, and 37% of new joiners in the first half of that year were from underrepresented groups.

McLaren publishes detailed workforce data every year and its 2025 sustainability report showed that 36% of staff identified with one or more underrepresented characteristics, against a 2030 target of 40%, and 44.8% of new starters in 2025 were from underrepresented groups.

Women make up 22% of McLaren F1's workforce and 10% of technical functions, with that latter number up from 7% the year before.

All the F1 teams, F1 itself and governing body the FIA have signed up to a diversity and inclusion charter, external.
