Christian Wade became just the second player to score 100 Prem Rugby tries after getting a hat-trick in Newcastle Red Bulls' 34-28 defeat at Exeter.

Wade, 35, went over twice in the first half - his second giving the Red Bulls a two-point half-time lead - before pouncing on a Henry Slade error to race in for a third early in the second half.

Newcastle's former England winger is now just one try off the all-time Prem try record of 101 set by Chris Ashton at Exeter in April 2023.

Wade, who spent four years trying to break into the NFL from 2018, returned to the Prem in 2024 with Gloucester after two years at French side Racing 92, last year moving to Newcastle after a brief spell at rugby league's Wigan Warriors.

Exeter's Ross Vintcent marked his return from injury with a try after just 38 seconds as the two sides shared five tries in the opening quarter that left the hosts 19-14 up. 

While the game did not go on to match Saturday's 18-try, 119-point thriller between Northampton and Bath, it did see eight tries as Slade's 64th-minute penalty and a late Bachuki Tchumbadze try ensured a first win of the season for Exeter. 

The bonus-point win keeps Exeter in eighth place in the Prem but closes the gap with leaders Gloucester to four points while winless Newcastle are second-from-bottom having got two losing bonus points.

Exeter director of rugby Rob Baxter told BBC Sport:

"That part of it I was quite pleased with but when we had the ball the other way around, for some reason we were just lacking in energy, drive and speed off the floor, speed to go forward and quality of tackles.

"Fair play to Newcastle, I thought they exploited that quite well because they kept the ball up and alive. They got the ball into space and scored some good tries - that flip-flopping of the score almost wouldn't let us settle."

Newcastle head coach Dan McFarland told BBC Sport:

"Every time you lose, you're disappointed.

"Both weeks I've been proud of a lot of what we've done, very proud of the effort that we put in there, very proud that we went out and played the brand of rugby that we want to play going forward.
