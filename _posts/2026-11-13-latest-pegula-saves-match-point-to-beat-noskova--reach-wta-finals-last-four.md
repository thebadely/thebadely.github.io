A gritty Jessica Pegula saved a match point to beat Wimbledon champion Linda Noskova and reach the WTA Finals last four at the expense of Coco Gauff.

Pegula knew she would have to beat Noskova to advance after Gauff's victory over Marta Kostyuk earlier on Thursday.

The American showed great mental resilience throughout a fluctuating match to win 7-6 (7-2) 4-6 7-5.

Noskova had gone a break up in the third set and held a match point on Pegula's serve at 5-4, but could not take it.

Pegula served 16 aces - the most she has produced in a WTA Tour match - and returned brilliantly to wrong-foot Noskova.

The Czech was below her best on her own serve, hitting 15 double faults to nine aces.

"I've learned that sometimes the serve feels good and sometimes it doesn't, and how to manage the days when it doesn't and to ride it when you are serving well," said Pegula. 

"Sometimes there's no rhyme or reason."

Noskova had already secured her place in the semi-finals and will face either Elina Svitolina or Aryna Sabalenka on Saturday.

Pegula, meanwhile, will face French Open champion Mirra Andreeva for the first time. 

Before the tournament began, the Women's Tennis Association chair apologised to Pegula after private comments she made to her coach about Andreeva during a practice session were live-streamed. 

"It's the match people wanted, well now you are going to get it," Pegula told reporters after her victory. 

Coco Gauff won the WTA Finals title in 2024

Earlier, Gauff had shown all of her battling qualities as she fought through injury to beat Kostyuk.

The American received treatment on her lower back midway through the match and soon found herself 5-1 down in the second set.

However, the world number four rallied and reeled off six games in a row to complete a 6-4 7-5 win.

"Physically, I wasn't at my best, which I'm not really used to," Gauff told Sky Sports.

"It was like a mental challenge, learning how to play through that."

The long tennis season has taken a toll on a number of players, with Ukraine's Kostyuk also receiving treatment on a wrist injury that has been hampering her since August.

It is the second time that Gauff has overturned a 5-1 deficit at this tournament, having done the same to beat Pegula on Sunday.

However, the 22-year-old admitted she was not expecting it to happen again, and was already preparing herself for a third set.

"I asked my physio at 5-2 down, what can I do on the bathroom break [between sets] to try and loosen up," Gauff said.

"And then there was no bathroom break."

Gauff struggled with her serve throughout, producing nine double faults to one ace, but said afterwards she struggled to get through her motion without feeling pain.

Kostyuk - a late call-up to the season-ending finals after Elena Rybakina's withdrawal - also struggled to generate pace and spin because of her wrist problem.

World number four Gauff came back from an early break down to take the first set, but immediately ran into problems in the second.

She called for the trainer after going 3-0 down but soon trailed by a double break, with Kostyuk saving three break points in between.

However, with her movement hampered and the crowd behind her, Gauff was able to swing hard and ultimately outlast Kostyuk.

"It's the last tournament of the season so I just wanted to finish it on a high note and give myself the best chance to play the semi-finals. Either way, I didn't want to be sad walking off the court," said Gauff.
