It has been recently reported that Lark Atkin-Davies played in all six games of England's victorious World Cup campaign in 2025

Winning a World Cup on home soil is up there among the pinnacle of things an athlete can achieve in their career. 

Lark Atkin-Davies was part of the England Red Roses team that did just that in September 2025, clinching the World Cup at Allianz Stadium, Twickenham.

That match was also the hooker's final game until last month, when she returned to the pitch with Bristol following the birth of her daughter. 

After achieving a goal so monumental, it would be understandable if it took some athletes a little while to find new motivation and targets going forward in their sporting career. 

But for Atkin-Davies, who describes herself as a "naturally very driven and motivated person", it did not prove tricky after a year away from the sport to come back and aim for even more. 

"I'd love to win a PWR (Premiership Women's Rugby) title with Bears. I've said it since being here that I think we've got everything we need to be able to do that," she told BBC Sport. 

"And I truly believe that we can go and do that this season. 

"Then personally, I'd love to put my hand up for selection for the Six Nations. To get back into an England shirt is a real goal of mine. And then obviously next year looking at the [British and Irish] Lions as well, I'd love to put myself in contention for that."

'Mums thank me for changing the narrative': How motherhood in rugby has evolved

Atkin-Davies returns to rugby after birth of daughter

Atkin-Davies is one of the most experienced front-row players in England. 

The 31-year-old has 74 caps and has been a mainstay of the Red Roses since making her debut in 2015. 

She has also played across the PWR, starting her career at Worcester before joining Loughborough and then Bristol in 2022.

But Atkin-Davies says that before she dreamt of being a professional rugby player, she wanted to be a mother and since welcoming her daughter Olive this summer her perspective on her career has changed.

"Going through pregnancy and then having a daughter especially, has just brought this new motivation and new drive," she said.

Atkin-Davies is one of four Red Roses players who were away from the sport last season through pregnancy, with Abbie Ward, Zoe Stratford and Rosie Gallagher also welcoming children in recent months.

She has previously cited her Bristol team-mate Ward as being a huge part in showing her that rugby and motherhood can co-exist. 

Lock Ward became the first centrally contracted England player to return to the sport and use the Rugby Football Union's maternity policy following the birth of her first child in 2024.

"I always speak about how much of an inspiration Abbie is and was with having Hallie (her first daughter) and then going on to win a World Cup," Atkin-Davies said.

"Seeing her with Hallie at that final whistle, carrying the World Cup around, I'd love to potentially have a moment like that.

"If Olive can grow up seeing me in an environment where I'm being challenged but where I'm doing something I love, that's a really big motivator."

Atkin-Davies will co-captain Bristol Bears this season along with Sarah Bern

Such is the regard Atkin-Davies is held in at Bristol, she has been named co-captain along with England team-mate Sarah Bern, for the new season. 

After a disappointing campaign in 2025-26 when the Bears finished eighth in the table, they start away at Harlequins on Sunday, 22 November boosted further by the signing of England star Ellie Kildunne. 

The Bears were beaten PWR finalists in 2024 and reached the semi-finals in 2025 but have never won the title. 

Atkin-Davies believes with the squad they have, that could change this season. 

"I've always truly believed that we could win the PWR title," she added.

"I said that at the beginning of the season last year - but we've got so many incredible individuals and, as a group, I just think we've always had that potential."

As for her own performances, Atkin-Davies believes the best is still to come and motherhood is to thank for that.

"Post-World Cup, as soon as I found out I was pregnant, it was like, 'OK, first and foremost, I want to make sure that I'm doing everything I can to have a really healthy pregnancy and to have Olive'," she said.

"Then also 'what can I be doing whilst I'm out of the game that can continue to keep pushing me forward?'"

She continued: "I said early on coming back to training at Bears that there's almost this new level that's been unlocked for me. 

"I find that a really exciting place to be in because I think I'll be a better player for it. 

"We're just at the start of the journey in terms of that return and being better than I was before."
