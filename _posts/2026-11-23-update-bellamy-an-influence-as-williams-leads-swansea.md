Here’s what you need to know: Ashley Williams and Craig Bellamy have been reunited having previously played international football together 

Ashley Williams says he has been in regular contact with Wales boss Craig Bellamy since taking temporary charge of Swansea City.

Bellamy was at the Swansea.com Stadium on Saturday to see the Swans draw 2-2 with West Bromwich Albion, in their first game since the shock departure of Vitor Matos.

Williams, who leads Swansea for a second time at Middlesbrough on Tuesday, says Bellamy is one of a number of managers he has consulted since taking the reins at his former club.

"I spoke to Craig [before returning to Swansea] and I've spoken to him most days as well which isn't unusual," Williams said.

"I've got a good relationship with Craig. Anything that I do, I would consult people that I look up to and he's definitely somebody that I look up to and lean on."

Williams became part of Bellamy's Wales staff in September, joining as an assistant coach alongside former Swansea head coach Alan Sheehan.

That was a first move into a senior coaching set-up for ex-Wales captain Williams, who has largely focused on punditry work since retiring as a player in 2021.

The 42-year-old says Bellamy is one of a number of managers influencing his approach to leading the Championship club.

"I've been fortunate to play under Roberto [Martinez], Ronald Koeman, Brendan [Rodgers], Chris Coleman, Michael Laudrup, so many really, really big names," Williams said.

"You take a little bit from all of them - you leave the bits that you don't like, and you take the bits that you do like."

Former Swansea and Everton defender Williams added: "I've been really impressed with Craig. We have long days on international [camp], he can be demanding but it's Craig and you'd expect nothing less.

"It doesn't become a chore because it's so fun and again just like now [at Swansea], I'm working with a friend - I consider Craig one of my really good friends.

"If anything, in terms of my first job to go in properly, the bar's been set so high now of how I see things working on a day-to-day basis, because I think he's one of the best."

Williams and Sheehan were brought in by Wales following the departures of James Rowberry and Piet Cremers.

The Football Association of Wales did not specify how long the duo would be involved, but Williams says he expects to be part of the national side's staff for next March's Nations League play-off against Slovenia.
