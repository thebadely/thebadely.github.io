A cyber attack on a police force in Wales may have resulted in staff information being "accessed or compromised".

Dyfed-Powys Police said the hack had caused disruption to some non-emergency systems but no personal data relating to members of the public was believed to have been accessed.

The force said the incident was identified on 14 September, and an investigation is under way.

The Information Commissioner's Office (ICO) has been notified.

Dyfed-Powys Police said it remained fully operational and its response to emergency incidents had not been affected.

It said online and email contact services were unavailable for a period following the attack, but these had now been restored and 999 and 101 service had not been affected.

The force said it was receiving support from cyber-security specialists and its systems had been subject to "precautionary measures" while specialist teams investigated the incident and worked to restore services safely.

A spokesperson said: "At this stage, our investigation has found no evidence that members of the public's personal data has been accessed or compromised as a result of this incident.

"We are, however, continuing to investigate whether any information relating to our staff may have been accessed or compromised, and are taking all appropriate steps to protect that information, and will provide appropriate advice to colleagues if required."

They added they "understood the potential concern this may cause" and wanted to "reassure the public that we have and will continue to take all necessary steps to protect our information and maintain the security of our systems".

The force said it would provide further updates when it was in a position to do so.

The area served by Dyfed-Powys Police includes Carmarthenshire, Ceredigion, Pembrokeshire and Powys, and equates to more than 500,000 people - a number that rises significantly with tourists each year.

The force has 1,349 police officers, as well as 1,063 police staff, police community support officers (PCSO) and special constables, according to an information request in March 2026.
