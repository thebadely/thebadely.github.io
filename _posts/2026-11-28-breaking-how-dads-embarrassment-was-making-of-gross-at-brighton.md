Many people will have parents to thank for shaping them into who they are today, but for Pascal Gross it was his dad's embarrassment when watching him play that turned out to be the making of him at Brighton.

It was not that Gross senior was not proud of his son, but he was unhappy with how he acted on the pitch in one of his first games with the Seagulls after signing for them in the summer of 2017.

"He went to the game. He went to every game," Gross said in The Football Interview.

"It was Leicester away and I was on the floor constantly like a little child crying for free-kicks and did nothing.

"I think my dad must've spoken to a couple fans. He said he was my dad and stuff. But then on the way back [from the game], he said he covered himself with the programme. He said it was embarrassing the way I played.

"He said 'if you keep playing like that, you will quite fast go back to Germany'.

"It was bad, but I learned from it."

Gross' dad urged his son to not look for free-kicks and instead play "faster" to adapt to the Premier League.

It was to prove important advice as Gross has become a hugely popular and influential player at Brighton.

Across his two spells at the club - having returned this summer following a two-year stint at Borussia Dortmund - Gross has contributed to 87 Premier League goals, scoring 35 times and providing 52 assists, the joint-highest total of goal involvements by a German player in the competition's history.

'Gross will be remembered as one of the best Albion players of the modern age'

Gross' dad was not just a parent keen to see his son make the best of his talent, but he had also been a respected football coach.

A former player, Stephan Gross built up decades of experience of coaching at youth level.

In the early 2000s he was youth team coach at VfL Neckarau, where he coached his son and helped him and several others develop impressively enough to catch the eye of professional clubs.

"He coached us from maybe from age 10 to 15," Gross said.

"But he prepared us for what it takes on a professional level - that the mental side is really important and you have to be resilient to pressure.

"Technical game, behaviour, all of those things I think he coached into not just me, but there's a lot of players [he coached] who also went on to play in the Bundesliga, or other levels."

Gross, Manuel Gulde, Robin Szarka and Marco Terrazzino all moved to TSG 1899 Hoffenheim as a result of Gross' coaching.

As is often the case, Gross has had to evolve as a player with age, and by his own admission he is a different type of player now than he was in his first stint at Brighton.

The 35-year-old is still chipping in with the goals and assists, with four of each in his 11 Premier League games so far, but now finds himself as much an on-field coach as a player.

"I try to put the gameplan on the pitch and try to drive and coach and improve my team-mates around me," he said.

"I see in other teams where players on the pitch organise the press and I think it's really important and I try to just put the gaffer's idea as a player on the pitch."

To still be going at the age he is and performing to the level he is, it is clear Gross loves football, despite having an issue that some might have thought would have curtailed his career early on - an allergy to grass.

"I have had this allergy since I was a child, but I can handle it. It's not that bad," he said.

"My mum always texts me [when there's a reaction]. She sees it straight away."
