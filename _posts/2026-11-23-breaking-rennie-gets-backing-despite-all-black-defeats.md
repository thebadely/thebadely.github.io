Here’s what you need to know: Dave Rennie took the All Blacks role following the departure of Scott Robertson

All Blacks head coach Dave Rennie has received the unequivocal backing of his bosses at New Zealand Rugby to lead the team through to next year's World Cup.

Rennie's side have suffered back-to-back defeats in the November leg of the Nations Championship and have lost five times in the calendar year for the first time since 1998.

"We have complete confidence in Rens," NZR chief executive Steve Lancaster said.

"His position is not in question. He will take the team to the World Cup. That is not going to change."

Rennie was appointed in March and started his All Blacks tenure in style, dispatching France and Ireland in the July part of the Nations Championship before a memorable victory over world champions South Africa in the Springboks' spiritual home of Ellis Park.

But New Zealand lost the next three Tests against the Boks to fall 3-1 in the 'Rugby's Greatest Rivalry' [RGR] series, and, although two convincing wins over Australia retained the Bledisloe Cup, the All Blacks have endured a difficult tour of Europe.

A first loss to Wales in 73 years was followed by an English rout in Twickenham, while the All Blacks have also been hit by the tragic death of their long-serving head of security Logan Cadwallader.

The emotional toll of Cadwaller's passing along with the arduous schedule this year are thought to be two mitigating factors behind the dip in form, and New Zealand's administrators will review the impact of the RGR series when planning its return in 2030.

Lancaster added that the union will look to make a call in the middle of next year as to whether Rennie takes charge of the team beyond the 2027 World Cup.

"We will conduct a full campaign and season review following this year, as we always do," he said.

"That will inform how we approach next year, but it will also inform our discussions around what the contract situation looks like beyond next year.

"Our intention is to make all those decisions and have clarity before we go into the World Cup. Nothing has changed from when we appointed Rens earlier this year.

"He is a world-class coach. We went through the most rigorous appointment process that I have been involved in, which included spending time observing him coaching in Japan with his team and in his environment."
