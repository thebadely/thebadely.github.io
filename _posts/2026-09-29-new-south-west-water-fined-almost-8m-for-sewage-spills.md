Sentencing, Judge Jo Matson described the pollution as "horrendous" and "very significant".

The water company said in a statement after sentencing: "We apologise for what happened in the past."

Judge Matson said she had made clear in that case "incidents of pollution by water companies will not be tolerated and must be taken very seriously by these courts". 

"I have taken into account that these incidents pre-date that sentence and I have also taken into account the changes that South West Water have made since then," she said.

"Only time will tell if those changes are successful, I certainly hope that will be the case."

She noted the water company had adopted wording that said "one pollution is too many".

"It goes without saying that must be the case," she said.

"However, in this case there were hundreds of incidents of sewage spills on to our beaches and into our waterways and sea and untreated sewage gushed through the harbour wall into the harbour at Polperro on a bank holiday weekend."

The firm needed to make sure "horrendous, very significant incidents of pollution such as these never happen again", she added.

Clarissa Newell, from the Environment Agency, welcomed the fine imposed on South West Water

Clarissa Newell, from the Environment Agency, said after sentencing it had "taken a long time for these offences to come to court" due to the nature of the investigation.

"We will get challenged by South West Water every step of the way so every piece of science we use has to be done with calibrated instruments and with qualified people," she said.

"Today with modern technology we know so much more than we ever did about the environment and quality of our water."

South West Water said the case related to "historic matters between 2015 and 2021" and the majority of spills involved "the use of overflows in storm conditions".

"We're investing Â£760m to tackle storm overflows and have reduced spills by 17% in the last year alone, with spill duration down by 25%," the company said.

It added "significant improvements" had been made at the sites where pollution occurred.

"We remain focused on the future as we deliver record levels of investment to achieve cleaner rivers and seas across the South West by 2040, a decade ahead of national targets," it said.
