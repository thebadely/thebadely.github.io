It has been recently reported that Wes Burns earned the last of his 10 Wales caps in October 2024

It is usually the manager who pulls a player to one side before or after training to offer a word of advice or appreciation.

But it was the other way round with Craig Bellamy and Wes Burns.

Wales winger Burns says he made a point of pulling Bellamy aside to thank him for the "massive" support he gave him after a career-threatening knee injury that ended his Premier League dream in 2025. 

Without Bellamy's backing, Burns said, his Wales career might have gone the same way.

"The world of football forgets you very, very fast when you're not playing," said the 31-year-old, who's back with the national team for the first time in two years.

"But when someone of Craig Bellamy's stature, the head coach of Wales, is calling me every month, or once every two weeks, to make sure I'm doing well... it's a massive, massive plus.

"Rather than feeling a million miles away, it always made me feel I was in touching distance of getting back in."

You can understand why Burns initially feared the worst. 

He was out for almost a year after suffering a torn anterior cruciate ligament during Ipswich Town's defeat at Liverpool in January 2025.

By the time he returned, 11 months later, Ipswich were back in the Championship, and Burns was off Wales' radar as they prepared for their ultimately unsuccessful bid to reach the 2026 World Cup via the play-offs.

The Cardiff-born winger was never completely off Bellamy's mind, though, and now he wants to make up for lost international minutes by playing a part in the Nations League campaign that began with Thursday's 1-0 defeat in Portugal.

Wes Burns missed Wales' World Cup qualifying campaign due to his knee injury 

Burns did not make it off the subs' bench in Lisbon, but hopes to get some minutes in Wales' second Nations League tie against Denmark in Copenhagen on Sunday, 27 October. 

After the injury trauma that cut short his fairytale journey from League One to the Premier League with Ipswich, though, he is just pleased to be back on the international scene at all.

"You just have to remember you're only one second away from disaster, really," said Burns.

"That's how I felt when the injury happened. You feel that could be the end of your career because you never know how bad the injury is initially.

"And when you've had surgery, and your knee's like a balloon, you're thinking 'how on earth am I even going to walk again, never mind play'.

"Everything heals in time, though, and it was just about making sure I got the rehab right. The phone calls from Craig, knowing he had a similar injury when he was younger as a player, meant so much to me."

Burns could have been playing Premier League football again this season, having helped Ipswich bounce straight back to the top flight in the 2025-26 campaign.

But the former Fleetwood winger chose instead to drop two divisions to join Leicester City, who have struggled to adapt to life in League One under former Swansea City boss Russell Martin.

"I could've done another year at Ipswich, and I was weighing up whether to finish the story of playing a full season in the Premier League (which injury deprived him of the previous time) - a level I'd worked so hard to get back to," said Burns. 

"But I also had to think about the longevity of my career, and in the end I took the option of a longer contract at Leicester and possibly recreating what I'd already done at Ipswich (back-to-back promotions)."

Such a journey might seem remote right now, with Leicester sitting 13th in League One with just two wins from their opening seven games, but Burns insists the Foxes will improve as the season goes on.

"We all know the story - Leicester, Premier League champions 10 years ago, amazing Champions League run, FA Cup winners and now sort of sitting mid-table in League One," he said. 

"But it feels like they're very much in the same spot as when I went into Ipswich.  

"It's a chance to recreate something that I've done in the past with an amazing manager (Martin) who obviously gets a lot of stick.

"He has a style of play and a philosophy that's very, very football-based and high risk but when that works it's amazing.

"Trust me - I've played against very good Russell Martin teams and they're very hard to play against, but it's a process and processes take a long time.

"I've got no doubt it's going to pick up, for sure."

Burns hopes to feel the benefit of new Wales coaches like Ashley Williams 

Burns' last outing for Leicester was a 30-minute cameo off the bench at Barnsley on  19 September.

One week on, he hopes to step back onto the international stage for the first time since starting Wales' 1-0 win over Montenegro in Nations League B in October 2024.

From Oakwell in Barnsley to Copenhagen in Denmark, followed by a Cardiff City Stadium meeting with Erling Haaland's Norway on Thursday, 1 October.

"Going from the weekly level of football I'm now at to possibly playing against Norway, Denmark or Portugal (at home next month)... obviously the gap in quality is going to be massive," said Burns.

"But I've had experience of playing in the Premier League and against very good international teams.

"The one that stands out for me is when we played Netherlands away in the Nations League (in 2022), and I was on the same pitch as (midfielder) Frenkie de Jong - still to this day the best player I've ever played against or witnessed on a football field.

"So just drawing on experiences like that, and big games I've played in, will definitely help if I do get on the pitch against Denmark and Norway.

"It's just so exciting to be involved in games like these."
