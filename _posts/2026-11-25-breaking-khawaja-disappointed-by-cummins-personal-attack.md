Usman Khawaja says he is "disappointed" by the personal "attack" he faced from Pat Cummins in response to his claims about the 2018 ball-tampering scandal, stating he has "never seen that side" of Australia's Test captain.

Retired batter Khawaja reignited debate around 'sandpapergate' in his autobiography Sabr, claiming the entire Australia team knew about the plan to tamper with the ball in the third Test of their series against South Africa in Cape Town.

The incident was widely condemned and led to bans for captain Steve Smith, vice-captain David Warner, and fellow batter Cameron Bancroft.

Cummins has since dismissed claims the bowlers were involved as "utter nonsense" during a remarkable 68-minute press conference and accused Khawaja of being "casual and flippant" with his friends' reputations and careers.

Khawaja responded on The Grade Cricketer podcast, insisting it was "never about me versus Pat" as he reiterated his support for the 33-year-old to remain as Test captain - but expressed his surprised at Cummins' reaction to his testimony.

"I've always loved him as a person and a leader. I was never expecting him to attack me personally like he did," said Khawaja, who played 36 Tests under Cummins.

"Personally, the way he did it and kept going and kept going, I've never seen that side of Pat before. That disappointed me."

Reiterating his version of events, Khawaja added: "I can sleep very easily at night. I have told the truth."

Prior to Khawaja's book, Cummins and the other frontline bowlers from Cape Town - Josh Hazlewood, Mitchell Starc, and Nathan Lyon - had always denied knowledge of the plan to use sandpaper.

In a 2021 statement, the quartet asked for an end to the "rumour-mongering and innuendo", reaffirming their stance that they "did not know a foreign substance was taken onto the field" until images appeared on the ground's big screen.

While neither supporting nor dismissing their position, Khawaja said the bowlers are "pegged" to their words from five years ago and their position created a divide in the Australia dressing room.

"It's not like they told us they were going to do it," added Khawaja. "They just did it.

"They separated themselves from the rest of the team at that point."

Khawaja also revealed he has written an apology statement on behalf of the team and sent it to Cricket Australia chief executive Todd Greenberg in an attempt to find a way to "meet in the middle".

The statement read: "We accept and acknowledge as a team that we knew we were ball-tampering by artificially altering the condition of the ball in South Africa [in] 2018. Whether it was sandpaper or by any other means is irrelevant.

"We knew what we were doing and we take full responsibility as a team and a squad for our actions, something we should have done eight years ago.

"Furthermore, we would like to acknowledge that Steven (Smith), Cameron (Bancroft), and David (Warner) paid an enormous price for the sake of the team, for which we are sorry and eternally grateful."

While Greenberg replied to say he did not think the statement was "gonna fly", Khawaja said the pair are confident of bringing the saga to an end.

"I've still got this pipe dream of accountability," said Khawaja. "He said, 'Look, no matter what it was at least we can look each other in the eye and say that we are trying everything possible to try to move Australian cricket forward.'

"The really good thing that came out is Todd Greenberg said everyone pretty much has immunity. 

"I reckon we're in a really good spot now where it's a little bit freer, where we can actually talk about this."
