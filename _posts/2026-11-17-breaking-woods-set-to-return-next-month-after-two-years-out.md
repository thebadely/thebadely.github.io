Tiger Woods is set to return to competitive golf in December for the first time in more than two years.

Woods, 50, has not played in a traditional ranking event since the 2024 Open Championship at Royal Troon amid a series of off-course issues.

The 15-time major champion has endured a challenging few years with a number of injuries and turmoil in his personal life.

Woods will host next month's Hero World Challenge - an event which is part of the PGA Tour schedule but does not carry FedEx Cup points - and has been announced as part of the 20-player field.

The American is one of three exemptions for the tournament in the Bahamas along with compatriots Rickie Fowler and Gary Woodland.

Speaking to SiriusXM PGA Tour Radio in a video posted last week, Woods said: "I'm feeling better, no doubt about that.

"I've been training quite a bit. I will be at Hero either way, so I'll see you there."

Woods' long-awaited return comes after he accepted a five-year driving ban in September following a plea agreement in his reckless driving case.

In March, Woods had been arrested and charged with driving under the influence after clipping a truck and rolling his car in Florida.

Woods was also found with two white pills in his pocket, which were identified as hydrocodone - an opioid used to treat pain.

He subsequently stepped away from golf to seek treatment in what was described by his attorney as an "intensive, highly individualised and medically integrated programme".

Woods has endured a succession of debilitating injuries, including multiple back surgeries, serious fractures to his right leg following a car crash in 2021 and a ruptured Achilles tendon in 2025.

Woods also suffered the death of his mother, Kultida, in February 2025, while his girlfriend Vanessa Trump was diagnosed with breast cancer earlier this year.

Trump, the former daughter-in-law of US President Donald Trump, said last month that she is now cancer free.
