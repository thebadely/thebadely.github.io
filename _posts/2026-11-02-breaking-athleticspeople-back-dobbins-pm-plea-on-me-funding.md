More than 350 sportspeople - including Olympians Dina Asher-Smith and Tom Daley - have signed a letter to the prime minister calling on him to meet former sprinter Beth Dobbin and commit to increased funding towards ME research.

Dobbin, 32, recently revealed in an interview with BBC Sport she had been diagnosed with "life-decimating" ME (myalgic encephalomyelitis) and as a result spends more than 22 hours a day in bed.

ME is a chronic, fluctuating disease, causing symptoms such as sleep problems, issues with thinking and memory, pain, and severe persistent fatigue that does not improve with rest, and more. It has no known cure or specific treatment. 

More than 400,000 people in the UK are thought to have ME, though the true figure could exceed one million, with severity ranging from people able to work or study to those requiring round-the-clock care and tube feeding.

On 21 October, Dobbin wrote to the prime minister but did not receive a response - and she has now been backed by athletes across sport.

"For many of my team-mates, I dropped off the face of the earth four years ago when I developed severe ME," she said.

"I've been taken aback and extremely touched by how many of them - and so many others in the sports world - have jumped at the chance to help by signing this letter. It means so much to me. 

"I hope that, with all of them behind me, Andy Burnham is listening and will commit to doing what's needed to deliver real change for a community that has been neglected for so long."

Among the 360 sporting figures to have signed the letter are Everton manager David Moyes, his United States women's counterpart Emma Hayes, and former Chelsea and England centre-back Millie Bright. Athletes Laura Muir, Eilish McColgan and Adam Gemili are among 114 Olympians and Paralympians to have added their support.

The letter calls on Burnham to "level the playing field" for funding into ME research with that for other illnesses "to give hope to the many affected that something is being done about this life-destroying, much-ignored illness and its spiralling impact".

Dobbin chose to speak out about her illness despite knowing doing the interview could trigger a brutal deterioration in her health.

ME patients often rely on off-label medication to manage a condition that can cause more than 200 symptoms, including nausea, headaches, poor cognitive function and brain fog.

A conversation, or even something as simple as a light too bright or a sound too loud, can cause a "crash" that lasts 24, 48 hours, or in the worst cases, months.

Since the interview, she has experienced several "bad days" and has only had the physical strength to take a bath once a week.

"I've not gone into like a full-blown crash as of yet which is really good but it is the nature of the illness that at any point you can just be tipped over," she said.

"It's been so much mentally and cognitively that I've had to really pull back physically, so I'm in bed even more hours of the day."

Dobbin has been overwhelmed by the reaction - the size of which she had never anticipated.

"I've had thousands and thousands of messages of support," she said.

"It's really shocked me how much it's spread worldwide. I've had messages from America, Canada, Australia, all around Europe. It really has gone massive."

Karen Hargrave, co-founder of the charity ThereForME, said: "Beth's advocacy has been extraordinary, and today's letter, with the incredible support behind it, will mean so much to so many people with ME across the UK.
