It is the rarest of sights.

Three Britons on a skiing podium, the union jack emblazoned across their race bibs as they lift their skis in celebration.

And then you learn they all have the same surname.

At a slalom race in New Zealand in September, Luca, Freddy and Zak Carrick-Smith completed a sibling clean sweep of the medals, something no three brothers are believed to have ever done before in the history of FIS alpine skiing.

"It was super special," Freddy tells BBC Sport. "The goal is to do the same thing at a World Cup or the Olympics now."

Their success came at the start of a winter season in which the brothers hope to solidify their burgeoning potential as British alpine skiing's next big things.

And could they follow in the grooves of Olympians Graham and Martin Bell, and Alain and Noel Baxter, to become GB's next family skiing dynasty?

Freddy, 19, is on GB's World Cup squad, while his twin Zak and older brother Luca, 21, are on the second-tier Europa Cup team.

And with their parents as their coaches, it is a full family affair.

The boys' mother Emma Carrick-Anderson was a four-time Winter Olympian for Team GB, while father Phil Smith founded Snoworks ski courses, which operate across Europe.

"They know us best, so they know us much better than just a random coach would," says Zak.

"And then as brothers, me and Freddy have been racing our whole lives together, pushing each other, and then Luca ahead pushing us too. I think that's why we've got so far."

Luca adds: "The best part of it probably, for us, is the competition, and I always want to be the best in everything, especially when we grew up.

"Everything you did, you'd always be pushing harder and harder. We all get along really well, and it's just like being with your best friends all the time.

"It's always been pretty incredible, but it has been difficult at times."

While Luca (back right) was born in Scotland, twins Freddy (front left) and Zak (front centre) were born in France and the trio were raised in the Alps

Emma is matter-of-fact about that challenge. Asked what was behind the decision to go it alone as a family, she said: "Basically we didn't have a choice. Ski racing is incredibly expensive.

"When the boys were young, it was all just fun. I don't think we really planned that we were going to take this journey to where we are now, knocking on the World Cup door.

"We would never have been able to afford to put them in an academy. So I think we were lucky, I was an ex-ski racer, Phil was a ski racer, we were also ski coaches, so we could do it ourselves. Not all families are quite as lucky."

Emma Carrick-Anderson competed at the 1992, 1994, 1998 and 2002 Winter Olympics for Great Britain

Coaches, ski techs, fitness instructors, course-setters, drivers, cooks - they have done plenty of jobs on their sons' journeys up the rankings.

Emma describes them as completely different personalities, and yet "all incredibly determined in their own way". But managing the emotions of her three sons as they learn to navigate the ups and downs of professional sport has been the parents' hardest role.

"They're our kids first, so they're humans first before athletes. And I think that's so important because that will help you ride out the tough times," says Emma.

"I was an athlete myself, and it's very difficult sometimes to separate Emma from the ski racer.

"That is something I know a lot of people struggle with, but it's something that we've been really working on with our guys. You know, if you have a bad result, it doesn't matter. You're not a bad person."

Freddy Carrick-Smith celebrates after his second run in Solden

Thankfully, there hasn't been much in the way of bad results for the Carrick-Smiths so far this season - and Freddy in particular has enjoyed a dream start to the winter.

In October, in the first giant slalom World Cup of the season in Solden, Austria, he finished 12th - the best-ever result by a British man in the discipline at that level.

After the first run, he was in 21st place - and so hard were his celebrations at that point that he snapped a pole.

Zak and Luca were watching his second run from the finish area, while Emma was watching from the wings and Phil from the start gate. Freddy recorded the fifth-fastest time of the day to rise nine places in the standings.

"It gives me a lot of confidence," he said. "I think I knew I had it in me, but after last year's performance in the World Cup [when he failed to qualify for the second run], I wasn't so sure.

"But I was feeling good going into the race, and I thought I could get into the top 30 and have a good second run, so I thought it was possible."

"We just keep going. You keep going, keep being positive," says Emma. "Things like Freddy's results. it just makes you realise how incredibly far we've come."

And yet they hope there is further to go. Asked about their ultimate goal, twin telepathy comes to the fore.

"We want to be the best in the world," Freddy and Zak say in unison.
