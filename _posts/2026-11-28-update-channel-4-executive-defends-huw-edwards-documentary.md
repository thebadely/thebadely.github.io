Let's dive into the details: The disgraced former BBC newsreader has given his first interviews since being convicted of making indecent images of children 

A senior Channel 4 executive has said there is "clear public interest in confronting" Huw Edwards after it received criticism over an upcoming documentary about the former BBC newsreader. 

The 65-year-old, who pleaded guilty to making indecent images of children in 2024, has spoken to Channel 4 as part of a documentary which follows the disgraced broadcaster after his conviction. The programme is due to air on Tuesday. 

Louisa Compton, director of news, said "nothing about making this film diminishes the seriousness" of the harm caused by Edwards' offending.

It comes after Downing Street said "we have no interest in hearing" from Edwards and that broadcasters must "act responsibly".

Victims campaigners, politicians and members of the public have criticised the decision to allow Edwards to give an account of his actions.

The Observer newspaper and Tortoise Media also interviewed Edwards for a podcast series to be released in the coming days.

It will be the first time Edwards has been publicly questioned about the events which led to him leaving the BBC and later receiving a suspended jail sentence.

Channel 4's Compton said Edwards "is not being allowed to tell his story unchallenged or on his own terms", adding he was not paid and had no control over the content of the film or questions he was asked.

She said he had "never publicly faced detailed questioning about his criminal behaviour or the complete collapse of his career and reputation", adding: "There is a clear public interest in confronting him with those questions rather than allowing them to remain unanswered."

Edwards, who was the BBC's most senior news presenter, pleaded guilty in July 2024 to three counts of making indecent images of children and was given a suspended six-month prison sentence.

Compton said: "Nothing about making this film diminishes the seriousness of that harm, nor does Edwards' participation entitle him to sympathy, redemption or rehabilitation."

Duncan Craig, head of We Are Survivors, which supports male survivors of sexual abuse and rape, accused the media outlets who have interviewed Edwards of "not thinking about the victims". 

He said giving a platform to sexual offenders "gives them the ability to nail themselves to the cross".

Former safeguarding minister Jess Phillips criticised Channel 4 for giving Edwards a "platform".

"Have they interviewed any of the thousands of other men who have been convicted of this crime to hear their side of the story or is it just interesting because he was famous?" she said to the Sun on Sunday.

"They should admit that the reason they picked Huw Edwards not any of the thousands of other men who have done this is because it sells, it's not about confronting the issue.

"Unless they had the express permission of his victims and they were part of the process it's just more platforming his voice while theirs remains silent."

The interviews have also been criticised by three people who previously alleged Edwards behaved inappropriately towards them.

One anonymous accuser, who said in 2023 that Edwards had sent them threatening messages, said it was "incredibly bizarre that anyone, big names like Channel 4, are platforming him when he's a convicted sex offender".

Another who claimed Edwards sent them suggestive messages, while they were working at the BBC, said they were "disgusted", while a former junior BBC employee who previously said they had received inappropriate messages from him called it "strange and irresponsible".
