Britain's Oliver Bearman will continue to race for the Haas team in Formula 1 in 2027.

The US-based team announced the 21-year-old's new deal on Thursday, at the start of the Qatar Grand Prix weekend.

It marks Bearman's third full season with Haas, for whom he first raced for two one-off events in 2024 before becoming a full-time driver in 2025.

The announcement ends a protracted negotiation period in which Bearman's management were seeking reassurances about his future from Ferrari.

Bearman is a member of the Italian team's driver academy and has ambitions to race for Ferrari in the future.

It was not immediately clear what reassurances Bearman had been given about his future by Ferrari.

Haas team principal Ayao Komatsu said: "Continuing with Ollie into 2027 was a natural decision for us.

"We've seen his growth, his commitment, and the quality he brings to the team, both on and off the track.

"He has a tremendous work ethic and a strong desire to develop both himself and the team, and there is no better person, in my opinion, to lead this team forward."

Bearman added: "It's no secret that I'm delighted to be continuing with Haas into 2027.

"This team has played an integral role in my development, and I've felt a huge amount of belief and support from everyone here.

"We've got big ambitions to keep pushing forward, and I'm excited to lead that work, building on what we've achieved, and striving for even more together â we have unfinished business."

Bearman will be partnered at Haas next season with Brazilian Rafael Camara, who is also a member of the Ferrari driver academy.

Camara has started racing for Haas at the end of this season, following the team's sacking of Frenchman Esteban Ocon midway through the Mexico City Grand Prix weekend.

Bearman's concerns about his future had been rooted in the fact that there might not be a seat available at Ferrari for some considerable time.

Charles Leclerc is on a long-term contract there, and while Lewis Hamilton's deal is believed to end after 2027, the seven-time champion is keen to continue in F1 for a number more years.

Bearman, who said at last weekend's Las Vegas Grand Prix, that he was "quite disappointed" his new contract had not yet been sorted so late in the season, is believed to have been exploring his options.

His management is said to have been asking Ferrari for reassurances about his future, either about the possibility of racing for the main team, or if that is not an option, being allowed to pursue a career at a more competitive team than Haas.
