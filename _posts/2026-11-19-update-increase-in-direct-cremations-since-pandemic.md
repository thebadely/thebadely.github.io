It has been recently reported that SunLife's 2026 Cost of Dying Report found direct cremation was now chosen for around one in five funerals

Funeral directors say there has been an increase in direct cremations, with no mourners in attendance, since the Covid-19 pandemic.

Brian Ugalde, whose firm of funeral directors has worked in Cornwall for 135 years, said non-attendance had become more acceptable since the lockdowns when families were not always allowed to attend.

He said: "A funeral is a ceremony...a farewell, a celebration... it is for for those left behind," he said. But, he said, direct cremation came after Covid  when "the model became acceptable and practical", as well as being cheaper.

SunLife's 2026 Cost of Dying Report found direct cremation was now chosen for around one in five funerals in the UK.

The report said, in the majority of cases, the deceased had requested or arranged a direct cremation themselves before their death. 

In 86% of those cases, families decided to hold their own celebration of their loved one's life at a later occasion.

The Park has lowered the price of direct cremation

John Ware from Walter C Parsons funeral directors in Plympton suggested people talked to loved ones before buying an unattended cremation plan so their family knew what to expect.

"If they talk to their local funeral director there is probably an option that sits somewhere between a direct cremation and a completely traditional funeral which would suit the need of both parties, in terms of keeping the cost lower, but also having a service that the family can attend to reflect on that person's life."

Plymouth's The Park crematorium, which opened in 2024, has recently reduced the cost of direct cremation, citing lower prices offered by other crematoria and national companies.

Graham Smith, head of bereavement, registration and coroner services at Plymouth City Council, said national providers could use crematoria in other parts of the country meaning not everyone was cremated in their home city.

"In my view if you live in Plymouth, if you work in Plymouth, why would you use a national provider?"

The cost of traditional funeral services has increased

VAT, the cost of wood for coffins and fuel price increases have all added to the cost of traditional funeral services. But people the BBC spoke to in Plymouth had mixed views on direct cremation.

Andrew Statton said he had not previously heard of direct cremation but added: "My wife died seven years ago of cancer and we had the ashes and then had a memorial service afterwards, so it makes no difference does it?"

Ann Roberts said she would consider a direct cremation because it would save her family money and they could "decide what they want to do". She added: "If you don't talk to your family, they won't know what you want."

But Pam Brinkhurst said she preferred a more tradition funeral. "When you get cremated or buried the family are there. It's a much better send off."

Phoenix Elson agreed: "I would rather pay the extra money and for my son to actually be there to celebrate my life".

Simplicity is one of the national companies offering direct cremation and said "our nationwide network of crematoria helps us keep fees competitive". 

They added: "Giving customers more choice, including the option to be cremated locally rather than having their loved one transported across the country to secure the lowest fee, is a positive development for consumers and the sector as a whole in the Plymouth area."
