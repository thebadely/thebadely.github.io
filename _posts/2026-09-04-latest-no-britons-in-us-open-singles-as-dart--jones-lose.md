Harriet Dart and Francesca Jones fell to seeded players as British interest in the US Open singles ended in the second round.

Dart said she felt "under the weather" during her 6-2 6-2 defeat by Czech 25th seed Marie Bouzkova as temperatures reached 30C in New York.

Her loss meant Jones was the last Briton standing in either singles draw, and she acquitted herself well before falling 6-4 6-4 to 14th seed and rising American star Iva Jovic.

Nine British players competed in the US Open main draws - the most since 1981 - with four coming through qualifying.

Five of them reached the second round, but Jacob Fearnley was the only player to win a set as they all fell to seeded players.

Lucky loser Fearnley fell in four sets to 27th seed Tomas Martin Etcheverry, while qualifier Toby Samuel was beaten by 18th seed Jiri Lehecka.

Katie Boulter lost to Wimbledon runner-up Karolina Muchova in only 58 minutes on Wednesday.

In the men's draw, Cameron Norrie, Arthur Fery, Harry Wendelken and Jan Choinski exited in the first round.

Jones, ranked 90 places lower than Jovic, made her work for one hour 54 minutes to reach the third round.

Humid conditions meant both players frequently dashed to the back of the court between points to dry their hands and face.

It was 18-year-old Jovic who started on the front foot, opening up a double break and a 3-0 lead as she controlled the rallies with deep hitting from the baseline.

Jones snatched a break back and kept in touch with Jovic, but could not make the most of a break point as Jovic served for the set.

After wrapping up the opener, Jovic raced into a 4-1 lead in the second, only to once again be dragged back by Jones.

Jones saved three match points on her own serve but Jovic produced one of her best service games to seal victory and set up a meeting with friend Alexandra Eala, from the Philippines.

"It's not like I was outclassed. You're talking about someone who has gone deep in the slams," Jones said of Jovic.

"It was just half-chances. It's frustrating, but it's pretty clear my level is there."

Dart, who is ranked 131st in the world, struggled on serve against Bouzkova, committing eight double faults and landing only 45% of her first serves.

Dart draped ice towels around her neck and used cold-air hoses at the changeovers in an attempt to cool down.

"I have been a bit under the weather. It is what it is, sadly," Dart said.

"It was quite warm. I had a temperature; I was trying to cool down. I've played many weeks in a row. 

"I am not used to air conditioning being pumped everywhere. We go from really hot outside to freezing inside.

"All credit to my opponent - she is an incredibly tough player - so even if I had been at full capacity, I don't know."

Bouzkova took command almost straight away, winning seven games in a row to clinch the first set and go a double break up in the second.

Dart was unable to recover as Bouzkova saw out the rest of the match to reach the third round, where she will face Polish eighth seed Iga Swiatek.
