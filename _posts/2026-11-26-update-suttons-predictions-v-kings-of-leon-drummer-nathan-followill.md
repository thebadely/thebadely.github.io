It has been recently reported that Arsenal co-chairman Josh Kroenke should probably turn his phone off if the Gunners fall behind against Manchester City on Sunday.

Chris Sutton's predictions guest this week, Kings of Leon drummer and City fan Nathan Followill, is good friends with Kroenke - and says he will be in touch if the game is going City's way.

"I'm super excited for this weekend and I've got my smack talk ready for Josh already," Followill told BBC Sport.

"I've heard about the City fans with their inflatable bananas and I need to get myself one. My wife is going to be like 'er, an inflatable banana just arrived in the mail - do you know anything about that?'

"We are going to win 4-2 so I will be texting Josh throughout the game... and, after Erling Haaland's second goal, I will send him a picture of me kissing the banana!"

Followill got a behind the scenes tour of Etihad Stadium and City's training complex when Kings of Leon played next door at Co-op Live arena in 2024. "It's a beautiful stadium, and an amazing set-up," he said. "Their practice pitches are better than most college pitches in the US."

Followill has supported City for more than 20 years, external and his children have followed in his footsteps by becoming Blues, despite the best efforts of his next-door neighbour in Nashville, as well as Kroenke.

"When my kids were born, they both received a full Arsenal kit - head to toe - in the mail from Josh," he explained.

"Both times, they were returned to sender - instantly.

"My son and daughter are both City fans now, but my neighbour, Jeremy Connor, is Tottenham, and I mean flying the flag full on.

"When they lose, you can hear him screaming - and when they win, you can hear him screaming too.

"His son is trying to convert my son to Spurs and I am like 'that is never going to happen in this house!'"

Chris Sutton and Nathan Followill were speaking to BBC Sport's Chris Bevan.

Followill says it was eye-opening to watch City's boys and girls' teams play when he visited their training campus. "I had no idea how much they put into developing kids from the youngest ages all the way up," he said. "That was probably the most impressive thing for me - to see how many young kids were out there, with the ultimate dream of pulling on that blue shirt one day."

After drawing a blank since week two, Chris finally picked up another win in week 11 - and it was his spot-on prediction for Liverpool to thump Manchester United 4-0 that sealed the deal.

That meant he got three correct results, including two exact scores - at Anfield, and Tottenham's 2-0 win over Ipswich - giving him a total of 90 points.

In a tricky week for predictions, where Liverpool were the only team to start the weekend in the Premier League's top six who won, it was those exact scores that made all the difference.

Chris' guest, undisputed world champion boxer Lauren Price, the BBC readers and AI also all got three correct results.

But the BBC readers were the only ones to also get an exact score - in the Spurs game - giving them 60 points.

Lauren and AI both ended up on 30 points - AI did call Newcastle's draw with Arsenal correctly, but it was wrong about Bournemouth's win over Nottingham Forest, which everyone else got right.

Not many of you got that Liverpool-United scoreline correct, either.

From around 27,000 predictions, the most popular scoreline picked was a 2-1 home win and only 570 of you went for 4-0.

"When has AI ever gone for a 4-0?" said Sutton. "That's what the crowd wants. The crowd don't want AI - who wants mathematics?

"The crowd want gut-feeling and instinct. I think I should get 1,000 points for that prediction, because I was brave enough to go for it."

For the first time this season, there were SIX games where no-one made a correct prediction - who thought it was easy?
