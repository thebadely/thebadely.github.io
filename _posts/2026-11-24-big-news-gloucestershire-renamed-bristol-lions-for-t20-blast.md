Let's dive into the details: Gloucestershire will play under 'Bristol Lions' in the Blast while their county crest has been redesigned

Gloucestershire will rebrand as Bristol Lions for the T20 Blast from the 2027 season.

The club revealed their intentions earlier this month to change their identity for the  format to include Bristol, the city where they play home matches at the Seat Unique Stadium, as part of a strategy to "reach a new audience". 

The club, which was founded in 1870, will continue to play County Championship and One-Day Cup matches under the Gloucestershire banner, however, their county crest has also been redesigned as part of the change.

"Gloucestershire County Cricket Club remains unchanged at our core, anchored in over 150 years of heritage," said chief executive officer Neil Priscott.

"Unveiling the Bristol Lions T20 identity is a proactive, strategic step to widen our supporter base across this vibrant city. 

"By establishing a modern connection for short-form cricket, we aim to fill stadium seats, engage younger and more diverse audiences, and build financial equity to compete at the highest domestic levels. 

"This approach safeguards our history while driving sustainable growth for generations to come."

Both the men's and women's teams will compete under the Bristol Lions name in the T20 Blast campaigns.

The men have won the title just once in their history - in 2024 when they beat West Country rivals Somerset.

They won seven of their 12 T20 matches in the 2026 season and reached the knockout stage of the tournament, while the women's team have competed in Tier 2 of the competition since it was restructured two years ago.

Since being relegated to Division Two of the County Championship in 2022, Gloucestershire have finished bottom in two of the past four seasons, including this summer when they won three of 14 matches.

"We want to consistently compete as a top eight county across all formats," said director of cricket Jon Lewis. 

"The T20 format demands intensity, energy, modern presentation and a powerful connection with the crowd. 

"Establishing the Bristol Lions identity gives our players a powerful platform to build on, exciting current fans while inspiring a new generation of players and supporters across the region."

The Seat Unique Stadium in Bristol, formerly the County Ground, has been home to Gloucestershire since 1889

A club statement said that the rebrand to Bristol Lions would help "accelerate commercial revenues" and "secure long-term financial sustainability, to directly reinvest back into cricket across all formats".

Warwickshire previously rebranded to Birmingham Bears for the Blast in 2014, also targeting a new audience.

While they said they saw attendances grow during that time, the decision was taken before the start of the 2026 season to reverse the change and return to Warwickshire. 

"With our long-term future in mind, and following feedback from members through the members' committee, now is the right time for change," said chief executive Stuart Cain.

"We represent the county, and that's what returning to Warwickshire Bears is about."

Gloucestershire have played at the historic Bristol County Ground in Ashley Down, Bristol, for more than a century, with the first match held on Nevil Road in 1889.

In December 2023 it was confirmed the club were exploring options to relocate to a new site on the edge of the city.

And in April last year they signed a non-binding head of terms agreement to explore a new site in Swanmoor Stoke, situated in the north west outskirts of Bristol near the M5 and M49 motorways.
