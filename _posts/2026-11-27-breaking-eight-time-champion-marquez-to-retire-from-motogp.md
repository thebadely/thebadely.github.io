MotoGP champion Marc Marquez is to retire from the sport - days after clinching a record-equalling eighth title.

The 33-year-old Spaniard retained the world championship in Portugal last weekend, putting him level with Giacomo Agostini and one clear of modern great Valentino Rossi.

The Ducati rider had extended his contract until 2028 in June but will now end his professional career after the final race of the season in Valencia on Sunday.

Instead of making the announcement during a news conference, he surprised the live broadcast of the first practice session, holding a series of flash cards while sat in the garage - in the style of a scene from the 2003 film Love Actually.

They read: "Thank you to everyone who's been part of the journey. I hope you've enjoyed it as much as I have.

"This will be my last race in MotoGP. We made history."

Marquez won his first title in 2013 and remains the only rider to win a MotoGP championship in his rookie year.

He began his MotoGP career with Honda, winning six titles in seven seasons up to 2019.

After joining Gresini Racing - the satellite Ducati team - for 2024, he spent the past two seasons with the Italian outfit's factory team.

He won the championship in both, joining Rossi as the only riders to win at least two world titles with two different manufacturers.

In winning his eighth premier class championship on Sunday, Marquez overcame the biggest points deficit in MotoGP history to take the title - 102 points.

It was his 10th world title overall, having previously been champion in 125cc (2010) and Moto2 (2012).

"It is official," read a team statement. "Marc Marquez has decided to retire from MotoGP, and the 2026 Valencia Grand Prix will be his final race as a professional rider and with the Ducati Lenovo team."

Marquez's younger brother Alex also races in MotoGP and finished second to him in the overall standings last year.

Italian great Agostini won his eight titles between 1966 and 1975, with his compatriot Rossi dominating between 2001 and 2009.

Rossi won nine world titles across all classes while Marquez has now won 10. Rossi claimed a record 89 victories in the premier class and Marquez has 80, seven of them coming this season. 

Last year he won his first title in six years, a period in which he overcame 109 crashes, multiple injuries including a broken arm, ankle, ribs and fingers, and a severe concussion.

His latest championship came despite another injury and crash-blighted campaign, in which he had to wait until the eighth race in Hungary before claiming his first win.

He suffered a flat tyre in the season-opener in Thailand in March, fell at the Spanish GP in Jerez and again at the French GP - where he picked up a foot fracture which ruled him out of the following race, the Catalan GP.

After having foot and shoulder surgery, he returned at the Italian GP in Mugello and trailed Marco Bezzecchi by 102 points in early June.

But Marquez won seven of the next 13 GP races, twice missing out on the chance to seal the title, in Qatar and Saturday's sprint race in the Algarve, before clinching it with one race weekend to come in his homeland.

Is Marquez MotoGP's greatest?

This is the end of an era. The whole motorsport world is in shock.

This was not the news that we expected during the final round of the season in Valencia.

Marquez still has two years left of his Ducati contract but has decided to announce his retirement.

The MotoGP grid for 2027 was already complete, with Marquez set to pair up with Pedro Acosta, but now that will change.

Marquez did something he always wanted to achieve, to become a 10-time world champion. He has done that and in doing so bettered the MotoGP record of the legendary Valentino Rossi.

He has had one hell of a journey too.

He dominated the sport from 2013 to 2019 and overcame some dark periods between 2000 and 2023, where he experienced more than 100 crashes.

Even in 2023 he told BBC Sport that he was not ready to win again. At the end of that year he made the shock decision to leave Honda, ending one of sport's most successful partnerships to join Gresini Racing, and then made the move to the factory Ducati team.

In 2025 he was the comeback king. After five years of heartache, he was able to defy the odds and become a champion once again. When I caught up with him after that seventh title he was still in disbelief. A year later he rewrote MotoGP history - but no-one expected the news that was to follow.

Marquez's body has been through a lot - even this year he had his seventh surgery on his right shoulder after a crash at Le Mans. Now it appears he has achieved everything he ever wanted.

I think many motorsport fans would have liked him to have stayed until the end of his contract with Ducati, with the aim of trying to earn a record-breaking ninth premier class title to overtake Giacomo Agostini.

But whatever the future now holds for Marquez, he retires firmly in the knowledge that he has established himself as a true legend of motorcycle racing.
