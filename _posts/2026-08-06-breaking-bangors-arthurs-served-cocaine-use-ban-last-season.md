Bangor striker Ben Arthurs has completed a three-month ban after testing positive for cocaine following an Irish Premiership game in March.

The 28-year-old was subject to an anti-doping test after his side faced Glentoran on 10 March and a statement from UK Anti-Doping [UKAD] on Thursday said Arthurs admitted to "ingesting cocaine for recreational purposes within a social setting".

Under UKAD rules, athletes can be issued reduced three-month suspensions "if they can establish that they used the substance out-of-competition and in a context unrelated to sport performance".

An independent expert concluded that the use of the drug "most likely" took place before the day of the game, which Glentoran won 4-0.

Arthurs was suspended by Bangor in April and at the time it was said the Seasiders were "unable to provide further details" while an investigation took place.

With Arthurs' having been issued with a provisional suspension on 7 April, his ban has been served and, having already returned to pre-season training, he is eligible to face Cliftonville at Clandeboye Park when Lee Feeney's side begin their 2026-27 Irish Premiership campaign on 15 August. 

It will be Arthurs' ninth season at the club where he is the second top goalscorer in their history. 

Doping control was introduced in the Irish Premiership and Women's Premiership in 2024 in a move joint-funded by the Irish Football Association [IFA] and Northern Ireland Football League [NIFL].

BBC NI approached the IFA and NIFL for comment.
