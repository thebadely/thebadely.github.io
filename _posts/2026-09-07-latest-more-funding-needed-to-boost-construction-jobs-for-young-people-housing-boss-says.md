A long-term plan and additional funding is needed to create more placements for young people within the construction industry, according to one housing association boss.

Iwan Trefor Jones, chief executive at Adra, said it was important that organisations worked together to make sure "that there are opportunities for young people".

Adra had seen a rise in demand in its academy scheme, with one of their recent openings attracting 108 applicants for only six positions.

The Welsh government said work was already under way "to boost construction skills" and new apprenticeships were set to launch later in the year. 

Recent analysis by the Federation of Master Builders showed that the construction sector was facing a shortfall of labour and needed an additional 2,200 workers a year in order to meet demand.

Steven Walker, a construction manager with Williams Homes, said it was "really important" that more young people came into the construction industry.

"We have an ageing workforce across the country" he said. "It's really important that young people are being pushed through to be work ready... to be the next generation of construction workers."

There was always room for more workers, he added.

"We can always create the space for young people to come and work on our construction sites.

"We buddy them up with an experienced worker, we show them the ropes, we give them the training, we look after them, we interview them regularly to see that they're OK and that they're looked after."

Steven Walker wants to see more young people getting into construction

He mentioned schemes like Academi Adra give young people hands-on experience on the job.

"The classroom-based teaching is really important. It prepares them for the work... and then when they come to us, they'll actually put into practice what they've seen in the classroom.

Jones said that it was vital there were enough opportunities in place.

"It's really important that organisations like Adra work in partnership with other organisations to make sure there are opportunities for young people."

Iwan Trefor Jones believes that a long-term plan is needed "to support the funding of placements in work, to support young people"

Speaking at one of Adra's developments of over 58 social and affordable homes in Bangor, Jones said additional funding was needed to give more young people opportunities to get into the sector.

"There's a need for a long-term plan, but also a plan is nothing without the funding behind it," he said.

"We need some kind of assurance that there is a long-term plan to support the funding of placements in work, to support young people into employment and to support employers."

The Academi Adra scheme was established in 2021 with the aim of providing young people with skills and experience in the workplace, offering courses in construction.

Two-hundred-and-thirty participants have been supported since the launch of the scheme, with the most recent course having 108 applicants for only six positions. 

Elin Williams, who oversees the work of the Academi, said funding had been needed to offer more young people the chance to join similar schemes.

She said: "We've been very lucky to be able to have some external grant funding to allow us to run some courses and pay for placement opportunities.

"We're calling for more funding for opportunities and projects like Academi Adra... We want to carry on to develop and expand to meet the rising demand that we're seeing."

Elin Williams is head of community services for Adra

In recent months, Welsh figures, external suggested the proportion of young people in Wales who are not in education, employment, or training (Neet) had risen sharply.

They increased to 17% and returned to levels last seen around 2013, according to Welsh government figures.

Cian Halliday, 26, a carpenter from Llanrug, had been made redundant during the Covid-19 pandemic, something he said had been "difficult, and a struggle".

"Academi Adra were offering courses, and almost 16 weeks of work experience and at the end they decided to keep me on as a labourer. I did that for a year," he said.

"Then they offered me an apprenticeship and I chose to be a carpenter."

He said that being made redundant had been very tough.

"It was difficult, and a struggle to find work, so I decided, the minute this opportunity came up, to start a new job."

While looking for work, Cian said there had not been enough opportunities for young people like him.

"I was happy enough to change careers. I jumped at the opportunity and never looked back," he said.

Daniella Williams says the construction industry is "a great sector to get into"

Daniella Williams, 35 also got into the construction industry after a period of not being in work following an illness.

"I used to work within the NHS. I had problems with my health and I had children, so left that job, and I really wanted to get into an office-based job," she said.

Williams followed a course with Academi Adra to get into an administrative role.

"It's changed mine and my children's lives... I've been able to save, I've bought my own car, and it's given me so much more confidence," she said. 

"I've been able to achieve so much that I wasn't able to before, and it's inspired my children as well to go on and do bigger and better than myself."

Williams said she was also passionate about helping inspire young girls to follow careers in the construction industry.

"'I work with schools... especially to get girls into construction.

"It's not just bricks and building, there are all kinds of different things. We work a lot with local companies, and it's a great sector to get into, I really enjoy it."

With the rise in demand for homes to be built, CHC said that opportunities needed to be made to end the "housing emergency".

Clarissa Corbisiero, deputy chief executive at CHC, said: "Investing in our homes is the best investment we can make in Wales' economic health.

"In building 20,000 new social homes over the next four years, housing associations can deliver over 14,000 jobs per year during construction.

"By aligning our housing goals with a bold construction skills strategy, we can turn a national emergency into a wave of opportunity for every community.

"Ending the housing emergency is more than bricks and mortar - it's about health, jobs and opportunity."
