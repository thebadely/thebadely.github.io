Here’s what you need to know: Catering giant Compass Group is planning to switch its primary stock market listing from the UK to the US in a move which will deal another blow to the London Stock Exchange.

A source close to the company told the BBC an official announcement was expected to be made in its full-year results on Tuesday.

Compass Group is made up of several brands which supply food and drink to various places, ranging from football stadiums and visitor attractions to schools, universities and large corporations.

It is understood the proposed shift to the US, which was first reported by the Financial Times, external, is being made due to the number of investors and the increasing business Compass has across the Atlantic.

North America accounts for two-thirds of its business, which drove its total 2025 revenues to $46.1bn.

However, such a move would deal a further blow to the London Stock Exchange (LSE), which has seen a growing number of firms leave the UK to list shares in the US in recent years.

These include tech firm Arm Holdings, Just Eat, Deliveroo and Paddy Power's parent company Flutter.

Long-running speculation also remains over the future of London stalwarts Shell and pharmaceutical giant AstraZeneca, which began listing shares in New York in February.

The company was created during the Second World War after the Factories Canteen Order meant large manufacturing or munitions firms employing 250 people or more had an obligation to provide hot meals for workers.

Today, Compass operates across more than 25 countries and employs more than 600,000 people.

It is understood the group's proposed move to the US will have no impact on the number of UK employees and a consultation process with investors could take more than a year. It is expected to retain a secondary listing in London.

In a sign its listing could change, Compass changed the currency in which it trades its standard shares on the LSE from sterling to US dollars in April this year.

The boss of the LSE recently called on the government to do more to back homegrown companies and make the UK "more attractive" to invest in the domestic stock market.

Dame Julia Hoggett told the BBC's Big Boss Interview podcast: "If we want Britain to back Britain, which is what I hear the chancellor and the prime minister saying, then let's make sure that we're creating structural incentives to do so.
