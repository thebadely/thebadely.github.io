Let's dive into the details: Stirling earned his fourth knockout of the year

Rising star Navajo Stirling earned the biggest win of his career as he knocked out former champion Jiri Prochazka in the second round at UFC Qatar. 

New Zealand light-heavyweight Stirling stopped Prochazka with a clean right hook to extend the unbeaten start to his career to 12 fights.

Victory marked the 29-year-old's fourth straight win in 2026, all by knockout, as he strengthened his claim to be the UFC's fighter of the year.

Stirling's form, coupled with beating 34-year-old Czech Prochazka in such convincing fashion, would usually open the door to a title shot, but there remains an obstacle to the fight happening.

The light-heavyweight champion is Stirling's team-mate, Carlos Ulberg, who trains at the same gym in New Zealand.

When asked who he'd like to fight next, Sterling said: "I'm not too sure yet, we'll figure something out and see who's above me."

It's rare for fighters to compete against their training partners, with whom they form close friendships in the gym.

Ulberg beat Prochazka for the light-heavyweight title in April, but has not fought since after tearing his anterior cruciate ligament (ACL) during the contest.

Prochazka, who has won 32 of his 40 professional fights, was regarded as the toughest test of Stirling's four-year career, but he looked comfortable against the former champion.

He peppered Prochazka with leg kicks in the early stages, before waiting for his moment to strike in the second round.

After dodging a punch, Stirling countered with a right hook which sent Prochazka to the canvas, before the referee rushed in to stop the contest after a follow-up strike on the ground.

After the fight, Prochazka conceded Stirling was "the better fighter" on the night, as the New Zealander celebrated with his team-mates inside the octagon.

On the undercard, Ireland's John Mitchell made a statement in his first UFC fight as he knocked out fellow debutant Shoaib Yousaf.

The pair took the fight on only four days' notice but it was Mitchell who triumphed after dropping his British opponent with a knee in the first round.

Victory extends 31-year-old Mitchell's record to 12 wins and three defeats.

After the fight, he revealed he nearly lost his foot last year after requiring three surgeries on an injury.

"It's mad, this time in December last year I had three surgeries on my foot and thought I was gonna lose the foot. But we didn't lose it, so happy days," said Mitchell.

Elsewhere, former featherweight title contender Brian Ortega announced his retirement after defeat by Dan Hooker.

Ortega, 35, fought Max Holloway for the featherweight belt in 2019 and Alexander Volkanovski for the title in 2021, losing both fights.

The American leaves the sport having won 16 of his 23 fights since debuting in 2010, with eight of those victories coming via submission and three by knockout.

"I wanted to do this because I came from nothing and I wanted to do something and I was fuelled by hate and by anger," said Ortega.

"And I don't have that any more. I healed, so as of right now, I think this is it."
