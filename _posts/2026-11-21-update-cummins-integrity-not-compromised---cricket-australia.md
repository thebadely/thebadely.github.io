Cricket Australia has "unequivocally rejected" the suggestion that captain Pat Cummins' integrity has been compromised by the sandpaper scandal.

On Saturday CA gave its "unanimous support" to Cummins, and he said he would take part in the independent forum set up for players and staff.

Cummins has faced claims to step down as captain after Cameron Bancroft's detailed testimony supported claims made by Usman Khawaja that the entire Australia team knew of the plan to tamper with the ball during a Test against South Africa in Cape Town in 2018.

CA and Cummins met this week, with the former saying it was "determined to steward Australian cricket through this very difficult time with diligence and patience and to listen and learn".

"We've expressed our support to Pat and our concern for everyone impacted by the events of the 2018 tour of South Africa," CA said.

"We have learned from that experience and are proud of the team and the culture Pat and other leaders in the team have fostered over the past eight years and unequivocally reject any implication that Pat's integrity has been compromised."

CA chief executive Todd Greenberg said Cummins has been an "exemplary leader" in his five years as captain.

"He has consistently spoken and articulated what happened back in 2018 in his version of events. Nothing's changed. Thus, nothing's changing with our captaincy," Greenberg told BBC Sport.

Greenberg acknowledged that people's version of events "directly contradict" each other but said Cummins has "not moved one inch" from his story, giving the same account now as he did when interviewed the day after the Cape Town Test in 2018.

Cummins is due to lead Australia in the opening match of a four-Test series against New Zealand starting on 9 December in Perth.

The pace bowler reiterated his stance that he had no involvement in or knowledge of the sandpaper plot during a 68-minute news conference last week.

Cummins has said he will attend the forum that has been set up by CA for players and staff to share their version of events.

"I support the forum 100%. I will be there," he said.

"All of the players and staff I have spoken with are very keen to go and to have their say.

"There will be a lot of difficult conversations, but that is what's needed.

"Otherwise, from this point, captaining the Australian team in Perth will be my focus and priority."

The row over the sandpaper saga, one of the biggest controversies in Test history, has engulfed Australian sport since Khawaja's claims emerged two weeks ago.

In 2018 Bancroft was banned for nine months and captain Steve Smith and vice-captain David Warner for a year after Bancroft was caught rubbing sandpaper on the ball during the third Test at Newlands.

Khawaja, who retired from international cricket this year, wrote in his autobiography that all the Australia team knew and should have taken responsibility. He has since been supported by Warner and bowlers Chadd Sayers and Jon Holland.

Cummins' said in his news conference that Khawaja's assertions were "100% wrong" and "nonsense".

Fox Sports has taken Khawaja and Warner off commentary duties for the next few weeks.

Fox Sports managing director Steve Crawley said it was "unfair" to put them in a position where it would be "difficult to focus on the game".

In backing Cummins, Greenberg said he was not accusing Bancroft, Khawaja and Warner of lying.

"I'm not putting someone's version of events over somebody else's," he said.

"I don't think they're lying, but I can't tell you what they know and what they don't know. I can only take on face value what they're saying."

Greenberg said it was an "almost impossible" task to put together the truth of what happened given people have different recollections of what happened.

He said CA is "determined" to give everyone the opportunity to have their say "without judgement" in the forum and share as much information as possible to "put a full stop on this once and for good".
