Let's dive into the details: Craig Bellamy's Wales seriously tested reigning Nations League champions Portugal in Cardiff

Craig Bellamy kept coming back to one word. Belief.

His Wales side had just lost a fifth game out of six to finish bottom of their Nations League group. Bellamy would have been forgiven for feeling a little bruised.

But there are ways of losing and ways of finishing bottom.

Wales were fearless against Portugal, exhilarating in attack and proving they can compete at the highest level of this competition, even as they were beaten 3-2 in Cardiff.

And while they were already consigned to the foot of the Group A4 table, Wales' previous performances mean they at least have the back-up of a relegation play-off in March to preserve their top-flight status.

"Belief's the biggest one," Bellamy said when he was asked what he and his team had taken from this campaign.

"Not just this game, but in general. We are beyond close to this level. We can more than compete at this level.

"We demand and want to expect to be. Getting into League A is big for us, but also staying in A is going to allow us to develop.

"You can only develop and improve by playing the best teams, and I felt we've taken another shift."

Wales have certainly fared better than they did when they were last in the top tier of the Nations League, when they finished with one point from six games and were relegated.

Victory against World Cup quarter-finalists Norway last month was their first at this level and, had they been more clinical in Oslo on Saturday, they might have got a second win of the campaign.

Wales had the best record of the four teams who finished bottom of their top-flight groups, meaning they have a second chance to avoid relegation in the form of a two-leg play-off in March.

At Friday's draw in Switzerland, Bellamy's side will discover whether they will face Slovenia, Hungary, Republic of Ireland or Bosnia-Herzegovina, who beat them in a World Cup play-off earlier this year.

Those teams all finished second in their League B groups and, as the League A team, Wales will have home advantage in the second leg.

"Going into the next one, we have to be in this group," said Bellamy. "If we want to develop as a football nation and keep going forward, we have to be playing against the best teams. It's only going to improve you.

"I know we can go back to League B... It's about playing the best teams. That's how we're going to get better. I honestly believe it."

Bellamy, who has a contract running to 2028, said: "I won't be here for the next Nations League. I won't be the manager then. So I'm doing it for the next one, but I'm also doing it for the players.

"We've got to. That's how we're going to be the best version of us and keep improving."
