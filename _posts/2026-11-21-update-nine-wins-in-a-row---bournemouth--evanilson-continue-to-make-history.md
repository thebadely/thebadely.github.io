"We have to stick together and face reality."

That is how Bournemouth manager Marco Rose responded after his side had dropped into the Premier League relegation zone with a 5-1 defeat by Chelsea.

Six weeks on, it is an entirely different story.

The Cherries are up to fourth in the top flight, having clinched a club record ninth successive win in all competitions - with five in the league.

It is a far cry from that thumping defeat at Stamford Bridge, which left Rose without a victory in his first six Premier League matches in charge.

Bournemouth earned just three points from those six games (draws against Everton, Newcastle and Brentford - when the Cherries squandered winning positions) and fans may have been fearing the worst.

From the outside looking in, it seemed as though the appointment of Rose might not deliver the fortunes that had been expected.

The 50-year-old, who accepted the unenviable task of succeeding Andoni Iraola, called for his side to take "responsibility" after the defeat by Chelsea.

"Three points after six games, we need a reaction. Everyone has to take responsibility to do better," said Rose at the time.

"We knew it was going to be a demanding season. We have to stick together and face reality. The reality is we have three points after six games."

A reaction is what he got.

The Cherries have not only responded to Rose's rallying cry, they have exceeded all expectations. 

Those nine straight wins include five in the league - and his side are now only four points behind second-placed Brighton.

After easing to a 2-0 victory against Nottingham Forest on Saturday, Rose said: "We don't think about records, we just think about the next game.

"We took the right approach. We knew it would be tough and we would have to stay patient, and that's what we did. It was a top performance and a deserved win."

Bournemouth's nine-game winning streak is a club record - beating two separate runs of eight in 1998-99 and 2012-13

Since arriving in England in July, Rose has come across as a composed presence, regardless of Bournemouth's results.

However, the former Borussia Dortmund and RB Leipzig manager was unable to hide his delight at his side's record-breaking victory against Forest.

Shortly after the full-time whistle sounded at the Vitality Stadium, Rose unleashed a series of celebratory fist pumps in front of the delighted home fans.

It was arguably the first time Rose had let down his guard since arriving on the South Coast four months ago - and who could blame him, given the significance of the result?

It is the first time in the club's history they have won five consecutive Premier League matches.

The prospect of securing European football for a second consecutive season is growing by the week.

In addition to wins over Sunderland, Manchester United, Leeds, Ipswich and Nottingham Forest in the Premier League, Bournemouth have beaten AC Milan in the Europa League and Aston Villa in the League Cup.

Their remarkable run of nine wins across all competitions surpasses their previous record of eight, achieved in both the 1998-99 and 2012-13 seasons.

Bournemouth are currently also second in the Europa League league phase and are one of only three teams to have won all four of their matches so far.

"Tonight I will smile, but not just because of the table - because we win," Rose told Match of the Day.

Meanwhile, Justin Kluivert - who scored Bournemouth's second goal against Forest - urged his team-mates to remain "humble" and "not settle for less" before upcoming matches against Celta Vigo and Fulham.

One of the main reasons behind Bournemouth's upturn in form has been the hot streak forward Evanilson is currently enjoying.

After failing to find the back of the net in his first five appearances of the season, the Brazilian became the first player in club history to score in six consecutive Premier League matches when he broke the deadlock against Forest.

Since joining the Cherries in a club record deal from Porto in August 2024, the 27-year-old has struggled for consistency.

In his first season in England, Evanilson managed 10 goals and five assists in 31 league appearances.

Last season, though, was less productive for the forward, who managed only six goals and three assists in 36 top-flight appearances.

By scoring against Forest, Evanilson has already matched his return from the previous campaign.

"He is lovely, he works hard and his quality does the rest," said Kluivert. "I am happy he has been scoring, and working hard for the team."

It is not only goals that Evanilson is providing for his side this season. He has also proven that he is able to drop between the lines - and out wide - to allow Bournemouth to be a fluid side in transition.

That was evident for the Cherries' second goal against Forest, when Evanilson pulled out to the left touchline and threaded the ball down the line for Marcus Tavernier to cross for the unmarked Kluivert in the centre.

"The way he works for us out of possession, works as a link player and goes deep," added Rose. "The way he scores is quite impressive."

The season has not even reached the halfway point, so no-one at Bournemouth will be getting carried away just yet.

What is possible for the Cherries this term will largely depend on what happens - or, perhaps more importantly, what does not happen - in January.

Bournemouth's impressive recent form - and that of certain individuals - is likely to attract attention from some of the biggest clubs in the Premier League when the transfer window opens in January.

Players such as Evanilson, Rayan, Alex Scott and Junior Kroupi, in particular, are likely to be the subject of interest from elsewhere.

So what should Bournemouth do if some of the Premier League's 'bigger' clubs come knocking for their crown jewels?

Should they resist the premium prices the mid-season market is likely to bring and keep hold of their star quality to preserve their own aspirations this season?

Former Manchester City and England goalkeeper Joe Hart believes the Cherries should try to keep hold of their stars to bolster their own hopes this term - insisting the "sky is the limit" for Rose's side.

"Rather than setting a specific target, like Champions League qualification, they should just enjoy what they are doing," Hart told BBC Sport.

"It is crucial they keep everyone fit and firing, given they have the extra demands of playing in Europe plus the busy Christmas period to come. 

"Junior Kroupi and Alex Scott came off the bench today, and that kind of squad depth could be massive for them.

"It is all about keeping themselves in the running because a lot of teams are stumbling right now, whereas Bournemouth are really in a rich vein of form."
