The Women's Super League says an investigation is under way after Crystal Palace breached a rule by using four substitution windows in Sunday's 1-0 home win over Everton.

League regulations only allow a maximum of three substitution windows per team per match.

The Eagles had already made changes in the 62nd, 83rd and 90th minutes when boss Jo Potter brought on forward Kirsty Howat in the seventh of eight minutes of stoppage time.

The Athletic reported, external the match officials, including referee Megan Wilson, have been stood down from the next round of fixtures.

Wilson has been officiating in the WSL since 2023 and has overseen more than 45 games across the WSL 2, the WSL and Cup competitions.

The league confirmed the investigation will involve both clubs and referee body Pro Ref, formerly the PGMOL.

There is no automatic punishment for substitution rule breaches in the WSL's rules, but Palace could be referred to an independent tribunal, which has the power to impose sanctions ranging from a warning or fine to a points deduction or other sporting penalties.

Both clubs have been approached for comment.

Palace travel to Arsenal in their next WSL fixture on Sunday, while Everton welcome Charlton.
