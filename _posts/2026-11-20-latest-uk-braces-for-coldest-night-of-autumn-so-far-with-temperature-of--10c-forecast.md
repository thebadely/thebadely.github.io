Experts revealed that The UK will experience the coldest night of autumn so far overnight on Friday, with a widespread frost developing as temperatures plummet below zero. 

It will be particularly cold in northern areas, where temperatures of minus 1 (30F) to minus 5C (23F) are widely expected from the Midlands northward.  

However, in rural areas of Scotland where there is some lying snow, it could get as low as minus 10C (14F).

It will stay cold with more overnight frost over the weekend, with cold health alerts from the UK's Health Security Agency remaining in force across northern England until Monday morning. 

Frost is expected in many areas of the UK from the Midlands northward overnight on Friday into Saturday. This map shows temperatures expected at 06:00 GMT on Saturday.

The coldest night of the autumn so far was on Thursday night when the temperature got down to minus 8.4C (18F) at Tulloch Bridge, Highland.

Temperatures for many of us have been up and down recently due to cold Arctic air temporarily sweeping southward across the UK.

Southern areas of the UK have mostly held onto milder air from the Atlantic.

During Friday though a northerly wind will once again bring some of the colder air from the Arctic, and bring a colder day for most of the UK.

Any early cloud and rain in southern England will clear and for most of the UK it will be a bright day with some sunshine.  A few wintry showers are possible across Scotland.

By night, temperatures will drop with frost expected widely, mostly across the Midlands, northern England, Northern Ireland and Scotland.

Although it is likely to be the coldest night of the autumn so far, with minus 10C predicted in rural Scotland, we are a long way off the coldest November night on record, when a temperature of minus 16.1C was recorded in Cumbria in 1912.  

We came closer to that record low temperature in 2025, when the UK experienced the coldest November night in 12 years with minus 12.6C in Moray.

After a cold start on Saturday morning, there will be a lot of dry and mostly sunny weather across the UK.

But milder air will start to move into the south-west of England again along with areas of cloud and rain by the end of the day.

Saturday night will not be as cold for most but frost is still likely to cover large areas of north-east England and much of Scotland with temperatures widely dropping to between zero and minus 5C.

Rain will continue on Sunday in south-west England, south Wales and even into parts of Northern Ireland.

Elsewhere should be mostly dry and while fairly cloudy, there should be some brighter interludes.

Maximum temperatures over the weekend will be on the colder side of average with 4 to 8C expected.

The polar vortex is the strong wind that circles high above the Arctic.

Very unusually the winds of the polar vortex weakened last week - the earliest time this has happened on record.

In some situations, the weakening of the polar vortex can lead to colder weather and even snow some weeks later.

But it is too soon for this weakening of the polar vortex to be having an influence on our weather. 

Instead this colder snap is just part of the variability of weather in the UK.

The polar vortex has started to strengthen again and should get back to its normal strength in the coming weeks.

Scientists are still unsure how or if the weakening last week will have any influence on our weather in weeks to come.
