Discussion about how Ireland head coach Andy Farrell would approach Friday's Nations Championship fifth-place play-off against Australia was ended emphatically by the team announcement on Wednesday. 

While Farrell would have been excused for fielding a rotated side in the wake of last week's bruising victory over world champions South Africa, he instead opted for continuity and is challenging his first-choice line-up to produce another chest-thumping 80-minute display against the Wallabies. 

Farrell has made two changes from the side that started Saturday's 20-17 success over the Springboks in Dublin, both of which are enforced. 

Jacob Stockdale replaces the ill Mack Hansen on the left wing while in-form back row Nick Timoney is restored at number eight in place of the banned Dan Sheehan. 

While Ireland cannot win the inaugural Nations Championship, Farrell has emphasised the importance of Friday in the context of the team's journey to next year's World Cup. 

Victory over Australia - who they beat 33-31 in Sydney in July - would be four wins in as many weekends. Do that down under next year and Ireland will find themselves in another World Cup quarter-final.

The six-day turnaround between last week's win over South Africa and Friday's contest with Australia serves as useful preparation for next year's tournament, where Ireland will have six days between their pool games against Portugal and Scotland and potentially a quarter and semi-final, should they reach that stage. 

With no six-day turnarounds in the 2027 Six Nations, Farrell is keen to test his side within those parameters before they return to their provinces for a busy festive schedule.

While the World Cup unavoidably edges into view, Ireland's immediate priority is to close out a largely positive 2026 on a winning note. 

Sobering defeats by France and New Zealand cast doubts over the squad's trajectory, but last week's narrow win over South Africa served as a reminder that Farrell's side can still mix it with the world's best. 

And while slaying the Springboks may have restored hope among fans, senior squad figures have carefully tempered expectations as they attempt to round out the year with a 10th win from 12 Tests.

"If you're winning games by the skin of your teeth and not performing well you don't really gain that belief, but when you're playing well and then start winning well the belief comes and that's something that we certainly gained before the 2023 World Cup," said Tadhg Beirne, who will captain Ireland against Australia in Sheehan's absence.

"But we're building that and we're often talking about that in terms of creating that belief, creating that identity that we want to see of ourselves, but again we're nowhere near where we need to be if we want to be competing to win a World Cup next year.

"I think there's so much more growth but we have to keep delivering in these big games for everyone to feel it and I know how it feels back in 2023 going into that World Cup.

"I think everyone in that squad felt it and I think we're certainly on the way there and if we can keep performing the way we've been performing but getting better we'll certainly be there by next year."

Jacob Stockdale (left) starts alongside Ulster team-mate Stuart McCloskey (right), who has been involved in every Ireland Test this year

Friday night is a big opportunity for Stockdale, who was not involved in the wins over Argentina, Fiji and South Africa. 

Nearly nine years removed from his star-making performances for Joe Schmidt's Ireland in the 2018 Grand Slam campaign, the 30-year-old Ulster wing is no longer a nailed-on starter. 

But having been picked ahead of Tommy O'Brien - who scored two tries in the win over Fiji - Stockdale must seize an opportunity to impress on what will be his first run-out at Twickenham since a try-scoring cameo off the bench in a behind-closed-doors Autumn Nations Cup defeat by England in November 2020. 

Stockdale needn't look far for inspiration. Seven of his Ulster team-mates are in the matchday 23. Two of them - Stuart McCloskey and Timoney - have featured in all 12 Tests this year (alongside Beirne), while jet-heeled right winger Robert Baloucoune is enjoying his best run as an international after a couple of tries against South Africa last week.

In January 2016, Stockdale made his Ulster debut under the province's then director of rugby Les Kiss, the man now leading the Wallabies.

The 61-year-old replaced Schmidt following the July leg of the Nations Championship, and while scrutiny on the Queenslander intensified following defeats by England and Scotland, last week's 29-26 victory over Wales has boosted the Wallaby morale as they attempt to snap a six-game losing streak against the Irish. 

Kiss, who was Ireland's defence coach during the historic 2009 Grand Slam triumph, has turned heads with his own selection in the number 11 jersey, with former Northampton Saints winger James Ramm earning his Test debut at the age of 28. 

Kurtley Beale - who started Australia's last victory over Ireland in June 2018 - is an eye-catching addition to the bench as the 37-year-old closes in on a first Test cap in five years. 

With Kalani Thomas earning a first start at scrum-half and a new back-row combination in Tom Hooper, Carlo Tizzano and Rob Valetini, it is an intriguing Wallabies line-up.

But if Ireland can replicate the intensity and edge they showed against South Africa, they may pass Farrell's World Cup rehearsal and ensure they approach the tantalising possibilities of 2027 in buoyant form.
