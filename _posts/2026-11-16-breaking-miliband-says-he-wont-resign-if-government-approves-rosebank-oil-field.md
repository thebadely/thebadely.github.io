It has been recently reported that Ed Miliband said he will "definitely not" resign if the government approves the Rosebank oil field, despite previously calling the scheme "climate vandalism".

The foreign secretary told the BBC he would "obey collective responsibility", which requires ministers to publicly support decisions made by the government even if they disagree with them.

A decision on the future of the oil field off Shetland will be taken by Miatta Fahnbulleh, who succeeded Miliband as energy secretary in July. 

Some Labour MPs oppose the project, while the Conservatives argue there is a need to "get Britain drilling". Miliband publicly raised his concerns about Rosebank while Labour was in opposition.

Appearing on BBC Radio 4's Today programme, Miliband was reminded that in 2023 he said the project would be a "colossal waste of taxpayer money and climate vandalism", external.

Asked if it could be assumed he would resign if his successor decided to develop Rosebank, Miliband replied: "No, definitely not because I obey collective responsibility.

"Miatta Fahnbulleh will make the decision on this. As you know, it's what they call a quasi-judicial decision so she looks at all the evidence and then reaches a conclusion."

Miliband said he would not "second guess her decision" on Rosebank. 

He added he supported the energy secretary recently giving the green light to the Jackdaw gas field after years of uncertainty and a court case.

Pressed further about his previous remarks on Rosebank, Miliband said: "I'm a member of the government and I will abide by collective responsibility on this."

He went on to say there are "direct and very bad impacts at home" for the cost of living linked to Russia's invasion of Ukraine and the Iran conflict.

Miliband added: "What does that teach us? That we can't get economic and energy security from fossil fuels, we've got to keep on with this transition to wind power, solar power, nuclear power; homegrown clean energy we can control."

Catherine Fookes is among a group of Labour MPs, reported to number at least 75, who have signed a letter opposing Rosebank.

The MP for Monmouthshire told Today: "We need to really look at what we're doing and ensure we don't exacerbate our climate challenges and make climate change worse.

"And that's exactly what drilling in the North Sea would do."

Steve Yemm, Labour MP for Mansfield, offered his support to Rosebank and said it would be a "test of whether, as a country, we can support investment and skilled work in the North Sea whilst meeting our climate obligations".

The UK is committed to reaching net zero by 2050 - meaning the UK must cut carbon emissions until it removes as much as it produces - and wants to limit global warming to no more than 1.5C above the pre-industrial period.

Rosebank is Britain's largest untapped oil field and would also produce some gas. It is about 80 miles (128km) north-west of Shetland.

In 2023, the then Conservative UK government granted a licence for Rosebank and a legal challenge followed.

The Court of Session in Edinburgh ruled last year that the government had made the approval unlawfully, leaving the current Labour government to make a new decision on whether to give the go-ahead.

If approved, Rosebank is expected to fill up to 500m barrels of oil over its 25-year lifetime.

But prices are determined by international markets, which means there is no guarantee that increased supply would result in cheaper energy bills in the UK.

Environmental campaigners believe approval of Rosebank would hamper efforts to tackle climate change and would have no meaningful impact on the UK's energy security or bills.

They have also argued that Rosebank would be even more damaging for the environment than Jackdaw, as it would produce far more planet-warming greenhouse gases.

On Monday, it was announced that gas has begun flowing from the Jackdaw gas field.

Conservative shadow energy secretary Andrew Bowie said: "One gas field does not make an energy strategy.

"Labour cannot claim to back the North Sea while continuing to block new licences, undermine investor confidence, and hammer the industry with a punishing windfall tax."

Bowie added "it is clear that Labour cannot be trusted to deliver the energy security we need" with some of the party's MPs "seemingly poised to rebel" over the approval of Rosebank.
