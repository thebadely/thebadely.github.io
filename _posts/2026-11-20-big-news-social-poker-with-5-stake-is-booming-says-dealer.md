A man who organises social poker sessions in pubs and clubs said the game's growing popularity could be down to the cost-of-living crisis.

Phillip Steer, from March, Cambridgeshire, has run poker nights in Fenland for 14 years and said he has seen a 35% rise in the number of players in the area since 2022, with now about "100 players across 15 clubs and pubs".

Steer added that as people can only place a maximum Â£5 stake, the game was often "cheaper" than other pastimes.

Player Violet Cunningham said: "I have been playing poker for about 10 years; it's nice to get out of the house... it's fun and exciting."

Tom Brady, the director of Live Pub Poker League (LPPL), said he had also seen an increase in the number of players joining for social tournaments in pubs and venues post-Covid, with the past 12 months being particularly busy.

Steer said that poker can reduce social isolation and also improve players' numeracy

On a chilly November evening about 15 people congregated in a pub in March, where they are given the use of a warm side room to set up their poker tables, cards and chips.

Steer said all sorts of people get involved in the game, adding: "We have men, women, retired and working people."

He said most people treat the game as a bit of fun and he did not worry that players would get into debt by taking part, due to the low-value stake. 

"You buy 12,000 chips at the start of the night for Â£5 and once those have gone it is game over," he added.

Donald Rofe, 81, is dubbed "Stetson" because of his hat and says he does not mind if he wins or loses but just loves "getting out and meeting people"

Donald Rofe is a widower and lives alone in March, he said his family live in Wisbech and Cambridge. 

"[Poker] gets me out of the house, although I have recently had my cataracts done, so I have some issues with reading the cards properly," he said.

Rofe admitted that he mistook a diamond for a heart, which cost him a victory.

"I put in all my chips because I thought I had a flush but I didn't, but it was still fun", he added.

The cost of play is another bonus for Rofe, who added he normally has one soft drink that will "last me all night". 

Violet Cunningham plays poker under the name of "Ultraviolet"

Violet Cunningham is a full-time carer and said she liked to relax by playing poker.

"I have been playing poker for about 10 years, it's nice to get out of the house - it makes your brain tick, it's fun and exciting, but can be frustrating," she said.

Cunningham said she and her family grew up playing card games on holiday and she has always enjoyed playing them.

"I think women are harder to read than the men, play a bit tighter, unless they have it [a good hand] they probably won't bet on it," she added.
