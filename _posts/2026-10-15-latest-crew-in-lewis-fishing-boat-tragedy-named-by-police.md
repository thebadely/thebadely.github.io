A fisherman whose body was found after a boat went missing off the Western Isles on Tuesday has been named as Douglas Craigie. 

The 49-year-old's crew-mates, Norman Murray, who is 40 and known as Tam, and 23-year-old Murdo Morrison are still missing.

The three men, who are all from Lewis, had been fishing in a boat called Kilda.

The vessel went missing off Port of Ness in north Lewis.

The search for the missing crew and boat was suspended on Wednesday night, before being resumed on Thursday.

The Maritime and Coastguard Agency (MCA) said aircraft and coastguard teams were involved.

Police Scotland said it was believed the fishermen had sailed from Brevaig in Lewis at about 07:30 on Tuesday before travelling to the Port of Ness area to fish.

The Kilda was reported overdue at about 21:20 on Tuesday. 

Extensive searches were conducted on Wednesday despite bad weather, with gale-force winds. 

The body of Douglas Craigie was recovered in the Port of Ness area at about 02:45 on Wednesday.

Ch Insp Ross McCartney, local area commander for the Western Isles, said: "Our thoughts are with everyone affected by this tragic incident.

"The families of all three men involved are being provided with specialist support as our searches continue. We would ask that the privacy of the families of all three men are respected at this difficult time."

He added: "We are working closely with the HM Coastguard and RNLI to search for the vessel and the two missing men

"We know that the local community will be keen to help in any way they can.

"However, conditions are very challenging and I would urge people not to put themselves at any risk. Please do not attempt to carry out searches in areas which put you at risk of harm.

"Instead, if you believe you may have any information which could assist emergency services then please call 101 quoting reference 3575 of 13 October."

McCartney said a police presence would remain in the Port of Ness area.

A spokesperson for the MCA said: "A range of search and rescue assets have been sent in response, including HM Coastguard helicopters and aeroplanes, coastguard rescue teams, lifeboats, and a nearby vessel.

"The incident is currently ongoing."

The search is taking place off Ness, a sparsely populated area on the northern tip of Lewis.

It is an area of rugged coastline, with many of its roughly 1,300 people living in crofting townships - small holdings where families can raise livestock and grow crops while working other jobs.

The parish has one of the highest concentrations of Gaelic speakers in the Western Isles.
