Roy Coyle, the most successful manager in Irish League history, has died at the age of 80.

Coyle won 13 league titles during his 32-year managerial career, achieving periods of sustained success with Linfield and Glentoran. 

He also managed Ards, Derry City and Newry City and had a spell as Glentoran director of football. 

In a statement, Glentoran said the club were "deeply saddened to announce the passing of Roy Coyle, our former player and manager and one of the greatest figures in our history".

"A league champion in Glentoran colours, Roy went on to become the most successful manager in the club's history. His association with the Oval spanned more than half a century," their statement continued.

"His teams gave supporters some of their most treasured days, while his influence on players and colleagues endured long after his years in the dugout.

"To generations of the Glentoran family, he remained simply 'The Gaffer'."

Belfast native Coyle moved into management at the age of 29 when he was appointed player-manager of Linfield in November 1975. 

A midfielder during his playing days, Coyle had spells with the Glens, Sheffield Wednesday, Grimsby Town and Linfield after starting out with Ballymena United, and he also won five caps for Northern Ireland.

During his 15 years as Blues boss, Coyle won 31 trophies, guiding the club to 10 league titles in just 12 seasons, alongside two Irish Cup successes and an All-Ireland crown in 1981.

"Everyone at Linfield Football Club is deeply saddened to learn of the passing of former player and manager, Roy Coyle," the club said.

"His remarkable achievements and contribution to one of the most successful periods in Linfield's history will forever be recognised and remembered.

"The thoughts and deepest condolences of everyone at Linfield Football Club are with Roy's family, friends and all who knew him at this sad time."

After his contract was terminated by Linfield in 1990, Coyle had a spell in the League of Ireland with Derry City between two stints at Ards.

In 1997, he took over at Glentoran and transformed their fortunes, leading the east Belfast club to three league titles and four Irish Cups.

Coyle went on to become the most successful in Glentoran's history, winning 17 trophies before his nine-year tenure at the Oval came to an end in 2006.

He had a brief period in charge of Newry City and also returned for three spells as caretaker boss of Glentoran before retiring in 2017.

Coyle retired from management in 2017 after his third spell as Glentoran interim manager 

Glentoran chairman Colin Jess paid tribute to Coyle, who was inducted into the club's Hall of Fame in 2025.

"Roy was one of the greatest figures in Glentoran's history, and his passing is a profound loss for our club," said Jess. 

"He gave generations of supporters some of the happiest days of their lives. People will always remember where they were and who they were with when his teams won those championships and cups. That is a wonderful thing to have given so many people.

"He set high standards and earned lasting respect. His former players continued to call him The Gaffer long after their playing days had ended, and that says so much about his influence and the place he held in their lives."

Jess added: "We will miss seeing Roy at the ground, enjoying his company and talking football with him. It is difficult to imagine the Oval without him.

"Our deepest sympathy goes to Abbie, the whole family and all those closest to Roy. We are immensely grateful for everything he gave Glentoran, and we will remember him with great affection and pride."

Glentoran owner Ali Pour described Coyle's contribution to Glentoran as "extraordinary". 

"He helped make the club champions as a player, led it through years of success as manager and continued to serve it long afterwards," added Pour. 

"I often asked Roy for his advice on matters at the club. He knew Glentoran so well, and I valued his judgement and hearing how he saw things. I'll miss those conversations.

"The pride with which supporters remember his teams, and the affection his former players have for him, show how deeply his work mattered to people.

"We owe Roy a great debt of gratitude. His achievements are an enduring part of Glentoran, and we will ensure that future generations understand what he gave this club and why he meant so much to it.

"My family and I extend our heartfelt condolences to Roy's family, friends and everyone mourning his loss. He will always have an honoured place at the Oval."

Northern Ireland Football League (NIFL) chief executive Gerard Lawlor said Coyle's contribution to football in Northern Ireland "went far beyond the trophies he won".

"He was a man who dedicated so much of his life to our local game, earning enormous respect across the football community, regardless of club loyalties," their statement continued.

"Roy represented the passion, commitment and pride that make Irish League football so special. His passing marks the loss of one of the great characters and personalities of our game, and his legacy will live on for generations."
