As the nascent Premier League table takes shape, the sight of Leeds United in third place feels like a throwback to the turn of the century, when they secured five top-five finishes on the bounce.

Monday's 4-1 home win over Newcastle United means Daniel Farke's unbeaten side have made their best start to a top-flight season since 2001-02 under David O'Leary.

In truth, Leeds, with eight points from four games, have been playing well for some time, and it can all be traced back to a bold change of formation by Farke almost nine months ago.

The German switched from a back four to a back three at half-time against Manchester City on 29 November, a game in which his team battled back from 2-0 down at the break to level things at 2-2 before conceding in stoppage time at Etihad Stadium.

Starting with the subsequent home win over Chelsea on 3 December, Leeds have lost only five of their past 29 league games, results which would place them fifth in the hypothetical league table for that timeframe.

So how have they done it? And can they push for European football for the first time in 24 years?

In the 2025 summer transfer window Farke and the club's new sporting director Adam Underwood made a point of signing tall players who could "add a special physicality" to the squad they were building for the robust demands of the English top flight.

This summer, after losing centre-back Pascal Struijk to Brighton, Farke moved for two World Cup players - Bosnia-Herzegovina's Tarik Muharemovic and Switzerland's Nico Elvedi - to bolster his defence.

Leeds lost eight of their first 13 league games in 2025-26, leaving them in 18th position and facing the very real prospect of relegation.

Reports at the time suggested Farke, whose side had pipped Burnley to the Championship title the previous season, was in real danger of losing his job.

The most notable beneficiary has been Dominic Calvert-Lewin. Since last December only Erling Haaland, Ollie Watkins and Joao Pedro have scored more top-flight goals than Calvert-Lewin's 14.

Having toiled as the lone centre-forward in a 4-3-3 formation, relying on infrequent support from out wide, Calvert-Lewin has thrived with a proper strike partner, often in the form of Nmecha or Okafor, who boasts seven goals and an assist in his past 11 league appearances.

Although Farke still finds himself alternating between 3-4-3 and 3-5-2, Calvert-Lewin himself has said: "I enjoy having bodies around me."

With Crystal Palace the visitors to Elland Road on Saturday, Calvert-Lewin is aiming to become the first Leeds player to score in six consecutive league home games since Arthur Hydes in 1932.

The former Everton forward missed out on a place in England's World Cup squad but, with three goals in all competitions this season, he is surely in with a chance of an international recall on Friday as Thomas Tuchel prepares for the Nations League quadruple-header.

Elland Road was bouncing on Monday night as the vast majority of the 36,693 fans savoured Leeds' stunning performance.

The famous old ground has been something of a fortress under the lights in recent seasons. Before consecutive losses to City and Sunderland in February, Leeds had gone 25 evening kick-offs in league and play-off games without a home defeat, winning 20 and drawing five.
