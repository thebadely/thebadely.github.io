It has been recently reported that John Paul Feely has five pets and used the pet food bank to help relieve the pressure of growing bills

At Burngreave Foodbank in Sheffield, a queue of worried people are here not to collect groceries for themselves, but food for their pets.

The RSPCA has launched a pop-up food bank to hand out cat litter, veterinary vouchers and pet toys as well as food.

Many of the dog and cat owners using the service say that rising bills are leaving them in fear they will not be able to care for their beloved companions.

John Paul Feely, 59, has four cats and a dog, and currently spends Â£35 a month on them.

"At the moment we're facing rises in fuel costs, food costs and you've got to make a choice at some point, can we look after the animals?" he says.

Feely worries that unexpected vet bills as well as the day-to-day expenses could tip him over the edge when his energy bill goes up in January.

Energy bills will soar by 26% for millions of households in January after a surge in gas prices sparked by the Iran war.

A typical bill will increase by about Â£37 a month in the first three months of the new year.

The government will pay millions of households Â£100 from 4 January to help cover winter costs on top of an existing VAT cut on electricity.

Feely lives with his partner and explains that they are already making tough decisions about "turning the lights out and not putting the heating on".

He is able to take home food supplies and a dog chew toy.

Angela Gillot says the pop-up pet food bank is a big help

As food bank volunteers offer tea and coffee to those waiting in the queue, Angela Gillot accepts a small dog jacket from the RSPCA stall with a smile. 

She explains with a tear in her eye that since becoming unemployed she has felt the pinch of rising bills. 

The RSPCA estimates that the cost of keeping a small dog like a Jack Russell could be up to Â£209 a month. 

Energy costs going up next year "will affect everything," Gillot admits, as she describes how she and her dog huddle up under a blanket to keep warm. 

"We do love these pets. We're a pet-loving nation aren't we? But it's getting harder.

"When I got my dogs I was working, so I try my best, but the price of food, especially what's of quality, I just really can't afford it. 

"It's not often you go to these places and they think about your pets," the 58-year-old says. 

Tricia Watts, the upbeat assistant manger at Burngreave Foodbank, is enthusiastic about the support from the RSPCA. 

She explains that while the food bank works with people in food poverty, it cannot always help people with animal care as it relies on donations. 

She has seen demand increase, and donations fall, in the seven years she has worked at the food bank. 

"I think pets are so important to people. If people are going through hard times, they're still important and they don't want to lose them." 

Growing bills are of particular concern to exotic animal owners as many birds, reptiles and fish must be kept in carefully maintained conditions that are often energy-intensive. 

Carl Chandler runs Sustainable Aquatics in Rotherham. He says rising energy prices have had a "gigantic" impact on his business. 

Chandler caveats that fish are one of the most wallet-friendly pets. 

"To run a tropical fish tank you're looking less than a cup of coffee a week." 

Other species, however, require specialist equipment to replicate their natural habitats.

"Reptiles, frogs and amphibians are incredibly expensive to heat because you need UV bulbs and to keep the right humidity."

Katie Hetherington, left, and Lucy Sollner, right, work with the RSPCA to support pet owners

Back in Burngreave, the pop-up event has managed to give away as much as 50kg of food, the equivalent of more than 100 tins, to pet owners in need. 

Sometimes, of course, owners find the challenge simply too much, and give their pets up for rehoming.

"We've seen heartbreaking examples across the country of people leaving notes with animals saying they just can't afford them any more," Chris O'Brien, a spokesperson for the RSPCA, says.

"The cost of giving crisis has put new levels of pressure on us in terms of animals coming in quicker than we can rehome them.

"As these cost of living bombshells keep coming, unfortunately it's going to be a continued difficult time for animal owners across the region."
