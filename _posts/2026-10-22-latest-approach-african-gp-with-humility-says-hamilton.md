Lewis Hamilton has addressed concerns on human rights grounds about holding an African Grand Prix in Rwanda by saying people should "approach it with humility".

Formula 1 has signed a 10-year deal with the central-east African country starting in the early 2030s.

Briton Hamilton, who has campaigned for a race in Africa for many years, said: "I don't get to choose where we go. Naturally, there are problems all over the world today, so I've seen many of the stories. But I think we really have to approach it with humility.

"There's a lot that we don't know. Growing up in England, they never taught us anything about Africa. It needs to be on the curriculum. A lot of the problems they have there are still results of colonialism and the West intervening."

Rwanda's east-central African neighbour, the Democratic Republic of Congo, has filed a case against the country at the International Court of Justice, accusing it of breaching multiple international treaties covering genocide prevention, racial discrimination, women's rights and torture.

And the DRC's Minister for Foreign Affairs Therese Kayikwamba Wagner said in a statement last week that "giving (Rwanda President) Paul Kagame this platform would help polish the image of an authoritarian leader whose government has helped fuel a devastating war in our country".

She pointed out that the US had sanctioned the Rwandan army and four senior officers for their role in destabilising the region, conducting military operations inside the DRC and supporting, training and fighting alongside the M23 rebel group.

She added that F1 "should require credible, independent guarantees that no money used to host the race comes from conflict-linked minerals or the exploitation of Congolese resources".

Hamilton, who will be 42 in January and has a contract until 2027, has suggested he could prolong his career in order to race in Rwanda. 

"I'm now forced to stay a lot longer because we have a race in Africa, and that's been my dream, to be able to race in Africa," Hamilton said. 

"I have to keep fighting to try and stay fit. If I can still punch out good results over the next year, we'll keep it going.

 "Africa is a place that deserves to have a grand prix. It's about more than just one country. It's about the continent itself, and there are so many beautiful countries within it.

"The people are so lovely. The culture is so rich, and I think it's important that we get to show that to the world. We get to do that with our sport. We get to go to all these different countries, and people see some of these countries they didn't really know too much about.

"All of a sudden, it becomes something that's on the screen, and kids are talking about it. They go to school talking about it. So, I'm really, really hopeful for that grand prix."

However, the Ferrari driver added: "Going there doesn't mean that you neglect the things that are happening there.

"So hopefully many people start to educate themselves a little bit more about what is happening there and what has happened there for hundreds of years. I hope that does spark the conversation.

"And then I guess I'll finish it off with this: I think we need to make sure we're pushing to free Congo, free Sudan, free Syria, free Palestine. I think there's a lot of peace that I'm hoping we get to at some stage."
