Rhys McClenaghan hopes to do just that at the Men's Artistic European Championships in Zagreb this week.

McClenaghan is not overhauling the routine and certainly is not rushing into any panic measures.

He has bounced back before. Four years ago he failed to reach the final of the Europeans before going on to win his first world title just a few months later.

That kick-started a stretch of two world titles, two European successes and the Olympic gold in Paris, a run of majors that ended in Glasgow at the Commonwealth Games.

"I don't like to make too big of a change when there's only a few weeks between Commonwealth and Europeans," he added.

"Changing everything would mean that I didn't trust that routine in the first place. I do trust that routine. Just some tweaks here and there are to be made. 

"It's a good place to be. Just making small adjustments that will give a much bigger result."

The Newtownards gymnast is still making his way back from the shoulder injury that forced him to miss all of 2025.

The Commonwealth Games was his first major competition since the Olympics in Paris two years ago.

Even great champions get nervous, something he admitted to in Glasgow.

"I mean, nerves are going to play a part. I think they'll always play a part. I'm nervous in every competition I do but I guess it's just, becoming familiar with those nerves," he said.

"The more I compete, the more I know how my body responds and know how my mind responds to those nerves.

"So after Commonwealth Games, you can really just take a lot of lessons, not just with the technicalities of the routine, but just how I felt and how I'll be feeling going into the next competition. I feel like I'm going to be a much more measured and relaxed athlete going into these Europeans."

McClenaghan faces new challenges as he prepares over the next two years to defend his Olympic title.

New rules in gymnastics favour the all-around gymnast as opposed to the apparatus specialist like him.

Without being too technical, McClenaghan's lengthy routines, packed with difficulty and skills, would separate him from the rest of the field.
