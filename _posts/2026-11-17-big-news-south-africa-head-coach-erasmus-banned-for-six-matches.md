South Africa head coach Rassie Erasmus has been banned for six matches by World Rugby over comments he made about match officials in his side's Nations Championship defeat by France last week.

Speaking at a news conference conference in Dublin on Monday before the Springboks' match with Ireland, Erasmus questioned what he perceived as double standards in officiating to the point where he asked: "is that not match-fixing?".

The two-time Rugby World Cup-winning coach was referencing the implementation of new rules surrounding hip-drop and knee-drop tackles, and felt the laws were not being applied correctly.

It comes after Ireland fly-half Sam Prendergast became the first player to be cited for a hip-drop tackle in Ireland's win over Fiji on Saturday, which was not penalised in-play by referee Angus Gardner and television match official [TMO] Mike Adamson.

World Rugby said an independent panel determined that Erasmus' comments "suggest that match officials acted with bias/prejudice or deliberately influenced the outcome of a match". 

In response, South Africa Rugby said it has requested a full misconduct hearing and an opportunity for Erasmus to "contextualise his comments and assure the panel that it was not his intention to allege that any match official had acted with ulterior motives". 

It is the third time Erasmus has been sanctioned by World Rugby over his comments about match officials. 

When he was South Africa's director of rugby, he was suspended for two months by World Rugby after he posted an hour-long video criticising the refereeing in the Springboks' opening Test defeat by the British and Irish Lions in 2021.

World Rugby found the video contained "multiple abusive and insulting comments and attacks on the officials".

In 2022, he received a two-match ban for a series of tweets criticising officials in a defeat by France.

Erasmus' latest ban has been imposed under World Rugby's match official abuse sanction process, which was introduced by the governing body in July. 

If the suspension is upheld, Erasmus will miss Saturday's game with Ireland in Dublin (16:40 GMT), next week's finals weekend fixture at Twickenham's Allianz Stadium and the start of his side's Rugby Championship campaign next summer.

Erasmus said that 17 hip-drop or knee-drop tackles had been missed in South Africa's 21-19 defeat in France by match officials. Andrea Piardi was the referee for the Stade de France fixture, while Matteo Liperini was the TMO. 

Erasmus added that if TMOs were not intervening for reviews to limit the number of interruptions during a match then it was "manipulation", before he added that his comments were "not an accusation".

"I'm saying if he didn't see it, it must be a technology problem. If he saw it and decided not to act on it, is that not match-fixing?" Erasmus added.

"If the TMO didn't see it, maybe then technology must be better. If he saw it and he ignored it, what does that boil down to? Manipulation of the game or the result or injured players getting injured.

"So all I'm asking, don't take TMOs out and discourage them to get involved. Then the wrong team will win and players will get injured. That's all I'm saying."

In a statement on Tuesday, World Rugby said an independent panel determined that Erasmus' comments had met  "the threshold for serious misconduct".

World Rugby confirmed the ban will stop Erasmus having any match day involvement for six matches, which includes coaching, direct or indirect contact with the team management or players on match days, entering the stadium, coaches' box, playing enclosure or technical area.

"The panel determined that the comments suggest that match officials acted with bias/prejudice or deliberately influenced the outcome of a match, and when the conduct is considered as a whole a reasonable person would conclude that the conduct constituted an unacceptable attack on the credibility and competence of the match officials," its statement said.

"In respect to the sanction, the panel determined that the conduct clearly amounts to disrespecting the authority of the match officials by impugning their integrity. 

"Therefore, the panel reviewed a number of elements of the commentary including the timing and reach of the statements, combined with the allegations of bias or bad faith, and found that the comments attracted a six-match automatic suspension."

World Rugby added that "respect is fundamental to rugby, and match officials are central to the sport". 

"Protecting their wellbeing and integrity, and their right to perform without abuse or intimidation, is a responsibility World Rugby and unions take seriously," their statement added. 

"Coaches, players, unions and everyone in the game must uphold these standards.

"Robust, respectful debate is natural, but teams have access to well-established and supported channels and protocols for raising officiating matters before and after matches. Challenging a decision is not the same as questioning an official's integrity or making comments that expose them to personal abuse."

In the same news conference, which lasted more than half an hour at the Springboks' team hotel, Erasmus also questioned what he felt were double standards in terms of sanctions.

The outcome of the citing has yet to be released, but he predicted that Prendergast will be available to play against South Africa due to a "technicality", and also questioned the decision not to ban Ireland prop Tadhg Furlong for an alleged groin grab while playing for Leinster ahead of the November Tests.

Last year, South Africa prop Jan-Hendrik Wessels was handed a nine-match ban for a similar offence and missed November's matches for the Springboks.

This is the third time in five years Erasmus has been banned for comments about referees, all of them coming after a Springboks loss.

In 2018, he picked South African rugby up off the floor and has won two World Cups to establish himself as one of the greatest coaches of all time.

To his rugby public - the people who really matter in Erasmus' eyes - he will forever be a hero in his homeland.

But outside South Africa his propensity to harangue officials after a rare defeat will tarnish his reputation in the eyes of many.
