According to new developments, After every round of Premier League matches this season, BBC football pundit Troy Deeney will give you his team and manager of the week.

Here are this week's choices. 

Do you agree? Give us your thoughts using the comments form at the bottom of this page.

James Trafford (Leeds):  It's been a big-pressure move for Trafford going to Leeds. I think at the World Cup with England he thought, 'I should be playing more' and he obviously didn't get the time and needed a move away. He produced a strong performance for Leeds at Nottingham Forest and was one of the few goalkeepers to keep a clean sheet.

Keane Lewis-Potter (Brentford): He was excellent defensively and attacking wise and got his goal. He is one of Brentford's underrated players and sort of sums up their team. Nobody speaks about him or Brentford but they hammered Tottenham. 

Nobel Mendy (Hull City): He was so good and powerful. He offered so much defensively to Hull and scored their second goal. He was a real powerhouse. Hull will definitely need him and he is one of those defenders where you think, 'I don't want to play against him'.

Maxence Lacroix (Chelsea): Lacroix just added a level of experience and calmness to Chelsea and also got an assist. He read the game and understood what was needed and provided the maturity that will add so much to Chelsea going forward. It was a very good debut for the France defender. 

Maxim de Cuyper (Brighton): He scored, had an assist and was excellent for Brighton. Opponents Aston Villa were poor, but if you watched the first 20 minutes you would see he was really aggressive and really set the tone with the pressure.

Christos Tzolis (Arsenal): A lot of people are talking about Arsenal and how they're great. That is absolutely true. Anthony Elanga (Newcastle): Last season I though he was treated poorly by Eddie Howe because he was one that kept being put on the bench, being introduced late or would be the first one to come off. But he showed in a new formation, under a new manager and somebody who believes in him that he can use his pace and power. As a result he looks like a completely different player and he looked happy. He ran past Milos Kerkez like he wasn't there despite the Liverpool left-back having a five-yard start and produced an unbelievable finish for his goal.

Cole Palmer (Chelsea): One we were all talking about, and should've gone to the World Cup with England. Feels like he's got a point to prove, feels like he's been working hard. The injuries from last year skewed everyone's vision. Everything he did at Fulham was positive. Ran forward with pace, looked like he could move. He had those issues with the groin but now he's chopping and turning.
