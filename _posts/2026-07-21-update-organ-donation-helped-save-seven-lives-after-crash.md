Let's dive into the details: Edward Pearce's family said that his organ donation helped save seven lives

The family of a 20-year-old man who died in a crash on the A27 have paid tribute to his generosity after his organ donation helped save seven lives.

Edward Pearce, known as Bunty, from Heathfield in East Sussex, was travelling to work when he suffered fatal injuries in a crash near Pevensey on 13 July.

His family said that Pearce, a registered organ donor, donated six organs, helping seven people in urgent need of transplants.

"Even in passing, Edward's kindness and compassion have given others the precious gift of life, creating a legacy that will endure for generations," his family said.

They described him as a much-loved son, family member and friend whose days were filled with "purpose, laughter and adventure".

Pearce was training as a plumber and hoped to work in Australia, they added.

The family said that he became a bone marrow donor at the age of four to help his brother William, who later died from leukaemia.

"Edward will be deeply missed by all who knew and loved him," they said.

"His memory will live on in the countless lives he touched, the happiness he brought to others and the extraordinary gifts he gave to those he never met."

Sussex Police said that the crash involved a white Mercedes Sprinter van and a white VW Caddy van on the westbound A27 approaching Golden Jubilee Way at about 06:30 BST.

A 31-year-old Bexhill man was arrested on suspicion of causing serious injury by dangerous driving and drug driving and bailed.

Officers have appealed for witnesses and footage of the crash.
