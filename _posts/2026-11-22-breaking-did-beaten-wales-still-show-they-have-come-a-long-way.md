For experienced hooker Ryan Elias, the proof of Wales' progress came when he looked around in the post-match huddle and saw heads down after a narrow defeat by Australia.

Steve Tandy's side failed to back up their historic victory against New Zealand when edged out 29-26 by the Wallabies in the Nations Championship.

Wales could not claim another scalp against the Aussies, who are two places above them in seventh in World Rugby's rankings, but they went close.

"It wasn't that long ago, not even 12 months ago, that we probably would have been chuffed with that result," said 54-cap forward Elias.

"You look around the circle at the end of that game and there were boys with heads down because this is one that got away.

"That just shows the growth of the group and the belief that's coming."

Next Sunday is the one-year anniversary of Wales' 73-0 defeat by South Africa in Cardiff, a hammering that was followed by heavy Six Nations losses against England and France.

Tandy's squad have since enjoyed victories against Italy, Fiji and Japan before the statement success against New Zealand.

"It's hard to recreate what they did against the All Blacks," former Wales centre Tom Shanklin said on Scrum V podcast.

"It's hard to get to that level every week, but it was really positive. We're still in a building phase.

"We said the best thing about the Japan game was that it was a bonus-point win, and that was about it.

"That feels a long time ago. We've come a long, long way.

"The effort was there against Australia, but there just wasn't that clinical edge and maybe that's a psychological thing."

Wales were nervy 28-21 winners at the start of November against a Japan side who have since been thumped by England and Scotland.

They face the Brave Blossoms again on Saturday (13:10 GMT) at Nations Championship finals weekend at Allianz Stadium in Twickenham.

"This is a big game now to see how much we've changed and developed from the first game of the campaign to the last," said Shanklin.

Wales chalked up four-try bonuses in all three games in Cardiff and have added to their threat from driving line-out.

They have profited from consistency at 10-12-13 with Sam Costelow, Max Llewellyn and Eddie James.

"You're seeing a team that seems to have found an attack," said Shanklin.

"Matt Sherratt has worked really hard with them and we've got various options now, we're not too predictable.

"We're playing with a lot more quick ball and we're quite clever in how we're breaking teams down.

"There are multiple options now when taking the ball to the line and we are getting it into the hands of our strike runners."

Wales did, however, waste a number of 22 entries in the first half and the final try by Ryan Elias came after pressure that ate into the clock.

"We were really good up to about five or 10 metres out, then we lost the detail," said former Wales and Lions wing Alex Cuthbert on Scrum V.

"We didn't do a lot to get held up over the line. We were quite predictable around the corner and Australia won every physical battle and were very good defensively."

Louis Rees-Zammit vowed in the summer that he would fire during his second season back in rugby after his crack at the NFL.

The 6ft 2in wing has been true to his word and arguably the 2021 Lions tourist is a better player now than he was before heading to America in 2024.

Rees-Zammit provided more evidence of his pace and added power when creating Blair Murray's try down the right in the second half.

"I was in line with where Zam had the ball and thought that surely Harry Potter was not going to give him the outside," said Cuthbert.

"It was only three or four yards and he did a shimmy in and if he gets half a yard outside then he is so quick.

"But now he is so strong from the hip down, so broke through Potter and then [Joseph-Aukuso] Suaalii tried to come across and it looked like they got an arm on him.

"It's just that power and watch Rees-Zammit carry now and he makes yard after contact."

The Bristol back has returned to wing and Murray, one of Wales' star performers in 2024-25, has profited from starting at full-back.

"He's a class player isn't he?" said the Scarlets man about Rees-Zammit. "He is one of the best wingers in the world, and he shows it week in and week out.

"We're working well together, you know. Whether I'm putting him through holes or he's getting through and passing it back inside.

"That connection is growing and we want to get the ball in our hands as much as we can."

The pair and experienced wing Josh Adams look sure to start the Six Nations, fitness permitting.

Tandy opted to change a winning team, going back to the starters that faced Japan.

His decision to rest flanker James Botham is in the spotlight after his man-of-the-match display against the All Blacks.

Alex Mann was to the fore in a different way despite suffering a painful early poke in the eye from team-mate Ben Carter, coming up with a turnover, claiming five line-outs and riling the Wallabies.

"You're certainly looking at the squad now thinking you're a lot more confident if there are injuries," said Shanklin.

"To have strength and depth with two quality players in every single position then you have got to play them.

"You cannot just have your first team out every game, then when players come in they are not going to be up to that level or have that experience. You've got to mix and match a bit."

Tandy is poised to rotate his squad for Saturday's fixture against Japan, when Wales travel to London after three games on home soil.

It will be the first game of a double-header on the day, and a warm-up for England's rematch with New Zealand.

Wales will play two home games in the 2027 Six Nations

Wales' win against the All Blacks gave a boost to ticket sales for the Wallabies fixture despite the late kick-off time.

The crowd of 66,307 was larger than the 52,425 against Japan and 55,152 against New Zealand.

Crucially, after the lows of last season's heavy defeats by South Africa and France, the connection seems strong between Tandy's team and the supporters.

"The atmosphere was amazing again," said Cuthbert, who scored two tries in the bedlam of the famous 2013 victory against England.

"You've got to give credit to the Principality Stadium staff, they are really making the place have that bit of theatre and entertainment."

Wales will hope it gives them an extra lift for the visits of Ireland and England in the Six Nations.
