It has been recently reported that Itoje has returned to lead England after being rested for the team's three Nations Championship matches in July

Captain Maro Itoje says a clearer sense of purpose and unity has coincided with improved performances from England this autumn.

They have won their last four games, racking up victories over Fiji, Argentina, Australia and Japan in the Nations Championship.

Prior to that run, England had lost five successive Tests - a streak that included their worst ever Six Nations campaign.

They host New Zealand on Saturday, with the All Blacks stung by their first defeat by Wales in 73 years last time out.

"It feels a lot more cohesive," Itoje told BBC Rugby Union Weekly.

"It feels, from a player's point of view, that our understanding of what we're trying to do is a lot clearer.

"The level of understanding has grown from campaign to campaign and we have a very clear sense of what we're doing, what we're trying to do and we're getting into those positions a lot more effectively now."

A Times report earlier this month, external claimed senior players had sought a summit meeting with head coach Steve Borthwick after their July Nations Championship campaign due to differences of opinion over the team's direction.

Itoje was rested for that tour and Jamie George, who had reassumed the captaincy in his absence, said parts of the article were untrue and the squad were "incredibly disappointed" by its publication.

"As senior players part of our job is to challenge Steve," said George at the time. "Steve is so open to us giving feedback and having challenge."

In turn, Borthwick has credited his senior players with leading a drive on discipline that has helped England's results turn a corner.

Across their first eight Tests in 2026, England were shown 14 yellow cards and a red.

It was highlighted as a key area to improve before the autumn and they have responded with no cards and only 15 penalties conceded across their two November Tests so far.

"It's never just one thing, but I will talk about one matter," added Borthwick of the reasons behind the improvement.

"A number of weeks ago, the senior players led the players in groups. It was a big discussion topic and coming out it was a series of commitments that the players made about what they need to do.

"They've backed up those commitments in this first couple weeks. Now we know that's going be tested even more this weekend."

Itoje admitted the arrival of New Zealand brought a different atmosphere to training.

"There are weeks...and then there are weeks," he said.

"The step-up often first starts with the mind, then goes down to behaviours and actions.

"There's a sharpness needed when playing against a team like New Zealand that for other games you may not need to get to.

"Without any disrespect, when you play in New Zealand your mind has to be sharper than when you play a team like Japan."

Before the final round of matches and Nations Championship finals weekend, New Zealand lead the southern hemisphere standings, putting them on course for the title decider at Allianz Stadium on 29 November.

South Africa, who are a point behind in the table and could overtake the All Blacks if they slip up at Twickenham on Saturday, visit Ireland later in the day.

The build-up in Dublin has been dominated by Springboks coach Rassie Erasmus' criticism of match officials and subsequent six-Test ban.

While Borthwick would not be drawn on Erasmus' situation or comments, he stressed the importance of respect for referees and their colleagues.

"When I first picked up a rugby ball aged 11, my schoolteacher told me, 'you call the referee sir'," he added.

"For so many years I still called the referees sir, because it's just so well ingrained. For this brilliant sport we're involved in, respect is integral. It is different to other sports and it's one of the reasons I love it."
