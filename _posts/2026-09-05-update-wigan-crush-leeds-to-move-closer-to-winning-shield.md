Wigan Warriors produced an outstanding display on the big occasion once again as they defeated Leeds Rhinos to take a giant step towards lifting the League Leaders' Shield.

The Warriors leapfrogged the Rhinos to move two points clear at the top and if they match Leeds' result in the final game of the regular season on Friday, they will claim their second piece of silverware of the year.

It was their 14th straight Super League win and it is hard to imagine them not beating Catalans Dragons at home to finish the job, with the Rhinos welcoming Hull FC at the same time.

Two early tries from Adam Keighran put them in charge at a sold-out Headingley before Ash Handley got Leeds on the scoreboard before the break.

Teenage winger Austin Daniel scored only his second Super League try to restore a 10-point advantage before fellow winger Liam Marshall crossed early in the second half to put Wigan firmly in control.

Their defence remained resolute as Leeds tried to get back into the game, and further tries from star backs Bevan French and Jai Field and a hat-trick from Keighran showed why they will be strong favourites to win the Grand Final at Old Trafford on 3 October.

The Rhinos have been top since May in pursuit of their first silverware since 2020 â a long wait for one of rugby league's powerhouses - while they have not finished top of Super League since 2015.

But the Warriors have been in their rearview mirror as they have been imperious through the summer, on the back of a winning streak which started more than three months ago with a dismantling of Hull KR in the Challenge Cup final at Wembley.

After a shaky spring, Wigan have been back to their best as they chase a treble, to go with the quadruple they secured two years ago.

Super League's leading try scorer, Maika Sivo, did not make the Leeds squad after recovering from a broken arm, but there was a setback for the hosts early in the game as full-back Lachie Miller limped off with a hamstring injury.

With the Rhinos reshuffling, Wigan pounced as the great teams do. Sharp handling from French and Keighran got the ball to 19-year-old Daniel who, on just his fourth league appearance, showed a turn of pace down the wing and then awareness to set up Keighran to score.

Oliver Partington had a try disallowed, but the Warriors were not to be denied as French's bullet pass put fellow Australian Keighran over again.

Leeds were stunned but found a response as Connor hoisted a high kick which was brilliantly caught and then touched down by captain Handley.

They were right back in the contest, but French simply dipped into his box of tricks to move Wigan further clear as the stand-off threw a sensational pass that took out three defenders and gave Daniel a walk-in.

Connor kicked a goal on the stroke of half-time to reduce the gap to eight points. But the visitors claimed the first score of the second half through Marshall's trademark finish in the left corner, although the unhappy hosts believed Wigan had knocked on in the build-up.

There was a hint of desperation as Leeds tried to force their way back into the game, but there were no chinks in the Warriors defence and their class was on show at the other end.

Harry Smith's clever kick found space and French, on his 200th career appearance, was first on to it, before Field's dazzling feet grabbed Wigan's sixth try. 

Keighran then completed his hat-trick as the clock was ticking down to cap another glorious night for this storied club.    

Leeds Rhinos head coach Brad Arthur told BBC Radio Leeds:

"We're disappointed, we didn't put our best foot forward tonight. It was a big occasion for the club and not through effort, but we let ourselves down and we let the club down.

"It's certainly not over, but we've got to dust ourselves down. We've got a big game next week and we need to regain some form.

On Lachlan Miller's injury: "It's not great. He's obviously got to go and have scans, but I think he's torn his hamstring. We'll have to wait and see.

"It's hard to replace a player of his quality, but then it's hard to replace a full-back, they're so integral to your defensive line, the organisation of that and how he returns the ball.

"We were a bit disjointed from it, but we've got to be a bit more resilient."

Wigan Warriors head coach Matt Peet told BBC Radio Manchester:

"I was pleased with the performance. I knew we were coming up against a really good team - a great arena, the build-up, the crowd.

"We knew it was going to be a big occasion, so to get the job done, I'm pleased, I'm proud.

"Leeds get the basics of the game right and challenge you in so many ways, so concentration is a big one and then embracing the big occasion. These are the sorts of games you want to be involved in as players.

"We played well, Leeds will learn a lot. They were unfortunate with the injury at the start, they've got some players to come back, they'll go away and come back better I'm sure, so we have to be prepared to keep improving as well."
