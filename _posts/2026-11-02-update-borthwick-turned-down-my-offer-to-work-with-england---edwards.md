According to new developments, Shaun Edwards is now committed to Argentina for the 2027 Rugby World Cup

Decorated defence guru Shaun Edwards says his offer to work for England at next year's Rugby World Cup was turned down by head coach Steve Borthwick.

Edwards, 60, left his post with France earlier this year and, after a short-term role with England's women's side, has signed up to work with Argentina until the end of the tournament.

"I had a good chat with Steve over the phone," Edwards told Rugby Union Weekly.

"I said to Steve he can make me assistant kit man and I'll be there, I'll be ready for work.

"He was very, very honest with me and I thanked him for that.

"He said he was happy with his staff and the way things have been going, and I respected him for that."

Edwards is one of the most successful defence coaches in the game, having won three Six Nations Grand Slams during a 12-year spell with Wales and then helping restore France as a global powerhouse - with three Six Nations titles secured on that side of the Channel.

He also won two Champions Cups and four English titles during his time with Wasps, initially as a defence coach before taking on the club's top job.

Edwards, who was a hugely successful rugby league star with Wigan and Great Britain in his playing days, has long been linked with England's men's side.

Approaches were made by the Rugby Football Union to bring him into their coaching pathway in 2006 and 2008, while chief executive Bill Sweeney had informal talks with Edwards in 2021 but opted against competing with France for his services.

Borthwick's backroom set-up is already set for Australia 2027, with Richard Wigglesworth overseeing defence, supported by Byron McGuigan and Joe El-Abd.

England have endured fluctuating form over the past two years, with a 12-Test winning streak ended by Scotland in February and followed by five successive defeats.

They most recently beat Fiji and Argentina and now face Australia, Japan, New Zealand and a Nations Championship play-off on successive weekends through November.

While Edwards is yet to work with England's men's team he briefly worked with the women's side in a consultancy role this year

Edwards says that his brief experience with the Red Roses was eye-opening. 

He was involved in the build-up to England's win over New Zealand at Allianz Stadium, in which the hosts lagged behind on possession and territory, making only seven 22m entries compared to the Black Ferns' 15.

However, Zoe Harrison's last-play try gave the hosts a memorable 26-19 victory.

"The team made 310 tackles against New Zealand and we only conceded 19 points. That is probably the biggest stat I've ever been involved with in my whole career," said Edwards.

"Normally if you go over 250 tackles, you lose because the other teams obviously had all the ball."

Edwards added that the performance was all the more remarkable to him because of the different pre-match atmosphere around the Red Roses, compared to men's teams he has coached in the past.

"The girls are very joyous on matchday whereas, as men, we've got our heads down almost like we're going to a slaughterhouse," he added.

"I was thinking afterwards that maybe that's the way to go - be a bit more relaxed before the match."

Edwards, who was linked with the vacant Fiji job secured by South African Jake White, added he is surprised that his experience has not yet led to a head coach role with a Test side. 

He believes he was a strong contender to take over the Wales job from Warren Gatland when the New Zealander ended his first stint with the country in 2019.

Wales opted instead to appoint Scarlets coach Wayne Pivac.

"I thought I might have got the Wales job when Warren decided to leave, but then I went on to something which I'd always wanted to do - which is to help France," he said.
