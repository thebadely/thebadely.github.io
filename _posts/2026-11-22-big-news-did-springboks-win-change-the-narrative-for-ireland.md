Here’s what you need to know: Robert Baloucoune (right) scored two tries as Ireland beat South Africa in Dublin

Ten months out from the World Cup, Ireland needed to make a statement.

They had lost their last six matches against South Africa, New Zealand and France, and questions were starting to be asked if this was an Irish team past their best.

A year ago, South Africa had demolished the Irish scrum on their way to a 24-13 win in Dublin.

But on Saturday, it was Ireland who came out on top with a 20-17 victory as Andy Farrell's side blocked out the pre-match noise after Rassie Erasmus' six-game ban.

"We knew what a lot of the players were capable of, but for them to come out and do that just changes the narrative around the Irish rugby team," former Ireland scrum-half Conor Murray said on the Ireland Rugby Social podcast.

"To do that to the world champions, and there were some whispers from the South Africans that they weren't great, but we made them look average with the physicality and the scrum.

"Everyone looked so up for it. It changes the feeling of it."

Former Ireland forward Mike McCarthy said it was a "phenomenal performance" as Robert Baloucoune scored two tries and Ronan Kelleher also crossed in Dublin.

Despite having a mixed game with the boot, Sam Prendergast ultimately kicked the decisive points as Ireland held off a late fightback from the back-to-back world champions.

"Everyone wrote them off pre-game. The boys have had to live with that for a year, when they capitulated at scrum time," he said.

"All the talk was how South Africa were hurting after the France game and they would do it for Rassie, but it was phenomenal from every player."

Speaking to the podcast after the game, Ireland back row Josh van der Flier said it was "one of those special days".

"It's brilliant and the lads dug deep. South Africa are a brilliant side and everyone knows how good they are.

"We worked unbelievably hard, and we're delighted to get the result."

In contrast to the roar of 70,000 supporters in the Aviva, egged on by Ryan Baird at every scrum, Erasmus sat alone in South Africa's team hotel in Dublin.

It came after South Africa's head coach was handed a six-game ban over comments he made about match officials in his side's Nations Championship defeat by France

Instead of shying away from it, Erasmus hosted a watchalong on YouTube. Bar one set of comments at half-time, it turned out to be in near silence as he sat in an empty conference room. 

A one point, a member of staff was clearing chairs away in the background.

But the slamming of the desk as South Africa lost the ball in the final play said more than any words could, before Erasmus hastily packed his laptop away.

After a defeat by France the previous week, losing in Dublin was the first time the Springboks had lost back-to-back matches in four years.

South Africa still topped the southern hemisphere table, but it has proven the best in the world are still beatable.

"We actually didn't talk about it. If anything it was a bit of motivation," said van der Flier on the pre-game noise, in which Prendergast was also cleared of a citing from the previous win over Fiji.

"We talked about playing rugby well and not getting into any of the chat. I've anything rugby-related muted on my phone so I could ignore it all.

"It happens every week, but especially against South Africa where there's more chat than normal."

Despite finishing on 24 points - which would have been enough to finish top of the southern hemisphere table - Ireland had to settle for third behind France and England.

The victory made it three wins from three in November, after big victories over Argentina and Fiji at the start of the month.

That means they will face Australia on Friday in a fifth-place play-off, and McCarthy feels it is a "shame" to follow up the Springboks' game with what he feels is a "dead rubber".

However, Murray pointed to the World Cup next year, and believes it could be a chance to follow up a big win with another strong performance - something that could happen in knock out rugby in Australia.

"This was such a high, but if you fast forward to a World Cup and you end up playing a big team in a quarter-final, you still have to show up the next week.

"Andy will frame it like that. This could happen down the line, to see if you can go and repeat it. 

"That's what tournaments and World Cups are like. This was the peak, but now you have to go and do it again."

For van der Flier, the playing group will not look beyond next week.

"We have probably disappointed ourselves in the past year with a good performance and then a bad one, but it's been good in this period with three wins," he said.

"Hopefully we can keep building that momentum into next week and into next year."
