The International Ice Hockey Federation (IIHF) will appeal against a Court of Arbitration for Sport (Cas) ruling to reinstate Russia in next year's World Championship.

Russia has been banned from competing in IIHF events since the invasion of Ukraine in February 2022, with the sport's global governing body ruling earlier this year that the exclusion would continue.

On 30 October, Cas upheld appeals from the Russian Ice Hockey Federation against the ban, claiming arguments made by the IIHF "citing safety, security and other concerns" were "not supported by sufficient evidence".

The IIHF has cited alleged undisclosed links to Russia from two of the three Cas panel members in its appeal to the Swiss Federal Tribunal.

"The decision is based on concerns around the independence and impartiality of two of the three arbitrators on the CAS Panel," an IIHF statement said.

"Especially following the information that has recently come to light regarding potential links between the two arbitrators and Russia.

"Given the significance of the ruling and its implications for international ice hockey, the IIHF considers it definitively essential to pursue the matter before the Swiss Federal Tribunal."

Ukraine's ice hockey federation expressed "deep outrage" when the ban was overturned, while Austria, Czech Republic, Denmark, Finland, Latvia, Norway and Sweden said they would refuse to face Russia in IIHF competitions.

Russia's reintegration into international sport suffered a setback in November when hosts Canada said they would deny entry to Russian athletes and officials for the 2027 World Junior Championships.
