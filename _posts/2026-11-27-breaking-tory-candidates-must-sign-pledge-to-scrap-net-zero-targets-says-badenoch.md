All Conservative candidates will have to sign a pledge committing themselves to leaving the European Convention on Human Rights and scrapping net-zero emission targets ahead of the next election, Conservative leader Kemi Badenoch has said. 

Unveiling her "candidates covenant" at an event in the East Midlands, Badenoch said both policies were "necessary for a stronger economy and a stronger country".

The move could see some senior Conservatives blocked from standing for the party at the next general election.

Lord Barwell, a former Conservative minister and No 10 chief of staff, said: "After a summer in which the impacts of climate change were clearer than ever, this is bad policy." 

He added: "Given the seats Conservative strategists say they are targeting, it is also bad politics."

Tobias Ellwood, a former Conservative MP and defence minister, told Matt Chorley on BBC Radio 5Live he had applied to stand as a candidate in the next election but had not passed what he described as "the purity test". 

He criticised Badenoch's covenant, saying: "Can you imagine if... prior to the war, you had to sign up to [Neville] Chamberlain's 'peace in our time' speech, you know, [Winston] Churchill wouldn't have been allowed to stand."

"I mean, there must be times where you can have different views and different views within a party are actually helpful," he added. 

Addressing party activists in Newark, Badenoch said: "The government that cleans up Andy Burnham's mess is going to have to make some very difficult decisions... it is not going to be for the faint-hearted.

"I need everyone who stands as a Conservative candidate at the next election to know what they are standing for - I don't want to hear complaining from MPs that they didn't know what we were going to do."

The covenant also states that candidates must support staying out of the EU, reducing the size of the state and changing the welfare system. 

Sir Mark Spencer, a chief whip in the last Conservative government, became the first to sign the new pledge.

He lost his seat in the 2024 election, but is now standing in Newark, where he hopes to unseat the Conservative-turned-Reform UK MP Robert Jenrick. 

Responding to Sir Mark's appearance at the event, Labour posted on social media: "Tories: 'We've changed'. Also Tories: 'Failed Tory Cabinet Minister under Liz Truss and Boris Johnson is the first to sign our new candidate pledge'."

Reform UK leader Nigel Farage said: "Kemi Badenoch tells us the Conservative Party has changed - it's hard to believe when she's surrounded by so many Remainers and her party is selecting lots of failed ex-Tory MPs who got kicked out at the last election."

Both the commitments to leave the ECHR and repeal net-zero targets represent a shift from previous Conservative governments. 

The net-zero target was originally legislated for by the Theresa May-led Conservative government in 2019. The party's 2024 manifesto said it was proud of its record on reducing emissions and remained "committed to delivering net-zero by 2050".

In the run-up to the election, then Prime Minister, and Conservative leader, Rishi Sunak resisted demands from some in his party to commit to leaving the ECHR. 

Since becoming leader, Badenoch has argued that the net-zero target - which commits the UK to reducing its net greenhouse gas emissions to almost zero by 2050 - couldn't be achieved "without a serious drop in our living standards or by bankrupting us". 

Last year, she also announced that, if elected, her party would take the UK out of the European Convention on Human Rights, the treaty that guarantees certain human rights such as the right to privacy, protection against torture and a right to a family life. 

Badenoch said the convention prevented the UK from being able to secure its borders and deport foreign criminals. 

Over the past year, the Labour government, along with other European countries have agreed to negotiate a new approach to the convention with the aim of making it easier to deport people.
