Here’s what you need to know: After every round of Premier League matches this season, BBC football pundit Troy Deeney will give you his team and manager of the week.

Here are this week's choices. Do you agree? Give us your thoughts using the comments form at the bottom of this page.

Gianluigi Donnarumma (Manchester City): Not only is he one of the best goalkeepers in the world, but he is a big-match player. We've seen it year on year and he's just won Manchester City two extra points against Manchester United. He'll get them 10 points over the course of the season, he's that good. After the Community Shield, people were saying he wasn't good enough and they should have kept James Trafford, but he proved again why he's such a big-time goalkeeper.

Daniel Munoz (Nottingham Forest): I thought he was a breath of fresh air in the win at Villa. No-one in that Forest team can do what he does. Pace, power, he can defend and offers you a lot going forward. I thought he was a steal. I can't believe Liverpool and Manchester United didn't go for him. Forest did unbelievable business getting him. 

Ezri Konsa (Arsenal): Had a tough old battle against Brian Brobbey as they beat Sunderland. Last season, he ended up bullying Gabriel for the equaliser and he wanted no part of it. Konsa said 'no problem' and stepped up. That penalty was never a penalty. 

Lewis Dunk (Brighton): Who knew the big 6ft 5in centre-half could put one in to the top bins, straight off the crossbar? Unbelievable. He goes in just for that strike against Coventry alone. 

Tarik Muharemovic (Leeds United): What a beast he is. I don't know where Leeds found him but he heads it, kicks it, runs through people. Didn't get the clean sheet against Newcastle but deserved it. 

Leif Davis (Ipswich Town): Very, very good. Very underrated. He delivered in a six-pointer because I think Palace are going to be down there, with a goal and an assist.

Enzo Fernandez (Manchester City): He had his best game in the last two years against Manchester United. He actually did everything the team needed - ran with the ball, looked after it, bought fouls, tried his best to get others sent off. I thought he was a catalyst for the team. His best performance for City so far. 

Pascal Gross (Brighton): Goal and assists. The old head who they brought back steadies that ship - it's a really young team. He is even more important now with no Welbeck, no Milner. He is in and is a steady hand. 

Kevin Schade (Brentford): Two goals again at Bournemouth. I feel like he's the next one they'll sell. Pace, power, scores goals and works hard for the team. 

Mohamed Belloumi (Hull City): Was decent in the Championship but he's really taken to the Premier League. Looks like he's stepped up and thought he'll have a bit of this. Two goals away at Chelsea is huge. They probably felt like they should have won. 

Dominic Calvert-Lewin (Leeds United): Steady away, isn't he? Goes about his business. Got one goal against Newcastle, should have had two. Excellent as a forward. Leeds have done something to keep him fit and he's changed the mentality for sticking in there. Maybe it's the Elland Road factor. They won't allow for anyone to not give 100%. He stays in the game all the time. 

Enzo Maresca (Manchester City): An honourable mention for Mikel Arteta. Got the job done away again. We could have had Daniel Farke. We'll give it to Maresca. I'm not his biggest fan, but tactically he got it right at Old Trafford. He understood the moment and gave his team the best platform to do what they needed to win. Made a brave call in bringing Cherki off for Ndiaye.
