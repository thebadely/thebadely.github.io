Hibs fans frustration 'justifiable' - Gray

Ten days ago, Hibernian were six minutes away from creating history and reaching the league phase of the Conference League. 

David Gray, already lauded in Leith for his Scottish Cup heroics as a player, would have been glorified to an even greater extent.

Never before has a Hibs side reached league phase - or group stage - football in Europe. They could almost touch it, and the riches that come with it.

But then there was a complete capitulation against Gent as they fell at the final hurdle for the second year running.

One week later - after beating Dundee in between - they were back at Easter Road for the first Edinburgh derby of the season. Only a win would suffice, but it was Hearts who emerged victorious.

Victory against St Johnstone on Sunday would not have atoned for a bruising derby defeat, but it would have boosted morale.

Instead, a lifeless and rudderless Hibs side huffed and puffed to a lacklustre loss in Perth, leaving supporters shaking their heads, pointing their fingers and venting their frustrations at Gray and his players.

The head coach cut a dejected figure at full-time and in his post-match interviews after a performance that, once again, left him with a raft of questions about his team and the direction they are headed.

Deflated and rather despondent, there was a shift in Gray's manner after the action at McDiarmid Park. 

How long ago must the post-match celebrations in Gent following their goalless draw in the first-leg feel for him?

He would be forgiven for admitting being confused by his team, because it's certainly how many other onlookers are feeling.

Last weekend at Dens Park, they played some glorious football. They started the second half of the derby well on top, and looked good value for an equaliser before Jack Iredale's mind-boggling red card.

But at times on Sunday, they were like a bunch of strangers searching for someone to step up, take control and grab... something, anything.

Instead, two of Gray's supposed leaders let him down. 

Although only signed in the summer, Jason Kerr, the boyhood Hibee - who captained St Johnstone to their 2021 cup double - inexplicably allowed Josh Fowler a simple tap-in from close range before skipper Martin Boyle - who was hooked at half-time - committed a clumsy foul in the box for a clear-cut penalty.

Hibs have now failed to win any of their past 18 league games when conceding first.

"The manner in which we lost the two goals today summed up our first-half performance," Gray told BBC Sport Scotland.

"We were nowhere near aggressive enough to stop the ball coming into the box. We were very passive, gambling and guessing in the box rather than staying goal side.

"Then, we give away a needless penalty which you just can't do.

"You can't give two goals away like that and not come under criticism for it and I know that's my responsibility."

While he is taking responsibility, it doesn't look like his players are on the pitch. 

Jamie McGrath could leave McDiarmid Park with considerable pass marks, but he was the exception.

Of course, Gray has to shoulder some of that. It's his plan - or otherwise - they are attempting to carry out, but they looked devoid of direction on the field.

"When I watch Hibs, I don't know how they're trying to score goals, I don't know what they're trying to do," former Scotland defender Charlie Mulgrew said on Premier Sports.

"That's the first time I've watched St Johnstone this season and I can clearly see what they're doing. 

"Hibs are scratching for something. It's not like they're going, 'this is our style, this is what we're doing and we're just going to hammer home with that - even if we don't do it right'.

"They completely changed the midfield on Thursday, they made two changes at half-time against St Johnstone, I don't see what they're trying to do."

Although towering forward Nathan Lowe netted his first Scottish Premiership goal, the striker has flattered to deceive since joining on loan from Stoke City.

One of Hibs' plays is to punt the ball up to the 20-year-old and although it seldom sticks, Gray is persisting with the striker for now. 

Perhaps of greater concern is that his pool of available defenders shrunk further when Grant Hanley gingerly left the field with a hip issue.

Pre-match, the head coach bemoaned his lack of options at the back. Jordan Obita limped off against Hearts with a calf issue and Marko Ivezic was not ready after signing on deadline day as Warren O'Hora's replacement.

O'Hora, 27, was a commanding figure in the Hibs backline and regularly performed admirably with the armband in Joe Newell's absence.

His departure for Leicester, one year after losing star striker Kieron Bowie, was a big blow for Gray and Hibs. Another one.

Gray assured he "still has the group" and believes he will be able "to show the players why I think this can turn very quickly".

Hibs go to Ross County in the League Cup quarter-finals on Saturday before back-to-back home matches against Kilmarnock and Aberdeen.

It feels like nothing short of three wins will be accepted after their latest aimless performance.
