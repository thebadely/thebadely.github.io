World number two Jannik Sinner says he has had "minor surgery" on his knee, just seven weeks before January's Australian Open begins.

The five-time Grand Slam champion has not played competitively since winning Wimbledon on 12 July.

In a post on Instagram, Sinner said he had undergone surgery to "improve the condition of my iliotibial band" - a thick band of tissue that runs along the outside of the knee.

"Over the past few months, together with my team we've tried everything to treat my injury without surgery," the 25-year-old wrote.

"After some very encouraging progress early on, my knee unfortunately didn't respond as we'd all hoped.

"I really miss being on court and will now begin my recovery process together with my team."

Earlier in November, Sinner said his goal was to go to Australia and potentially "play a few tournaments" before the first Grand Slam of the year begins on 17 January.

However, the Italian has also stressed the need to think of his long-term career in protecting his body.

The Instagram post is the first time Sinner has specified what the knee issue is.

In a video posted on his Youtube channel in October, Sinner said he had also lost "a lot of weight" after having his tonsils removed.

He also described having "days where I felt nothing and could play without any problems, and then days where I couldn't move at all".

He suffered a nasty fall in the first match of his Wimbledon title defence and also had blood seeping through his shoes because of a problematic toenail.

Alexander Zverev has become world number one in Sinner's absence, while rival Carlos Alcaraz swept four titles after returning from his injury lay-off in September.

Sinner comes from behind to retain Wimbledon title against Zverev

Sinner was the dominant player for the first half of the season, winning 44 and losing just three of his singles matches.

He won five Masters 1000 titles in a row between March and May, becoming the youngest man to complete the 'career Golden Masters', and reclaimed the world's top ranking from Alcaraz along the way.

However, he suffered a shock second-round exit at the French Open, with Zverev ultimately stepping up to win his first Grand Slam title.

Sinner bounced back on the grass courts, demolishing Novak Djokovic in the semi-finals before coming back from a set down against Zverev in the Wimbledon showpiece.

He ended his season in early October, meaning he was unable to defend the points he claimed with title triumphs in China, Vienna, Paris and the ATP Finals at the end of 2025.

Zverev went on to win the US Open and secure the year-end number one ranking.

Having met in three of the four Grand Slam finals last year, Sinner and Alcaraz played each other only once in 2026.

Sinner won their final at the Monte Carlo Masters shortly before Alcaraz spent four months out with a wrist injury.

Alcaraz's victory at the season-ending ATP Finals means he is only 350 ranking points behind Sinner - although the Spaniard will be defending 2,000 of those at the Australian Open.

"It's a shame we don't have Jannik," Alcaraz told SuperTennis TV, external after winning the ATP Finals on Sunday.

"We really miss him. I have not seen him for many months right now since I got injured in April. 

"I can't wait to see him fit, healthy. I am pretty sure that he is going to come back even better and stronger."

Alcaraz will be the defending champion in Melbourne, having beaten Djokovic to complete a historic career Grand Slam in January.
