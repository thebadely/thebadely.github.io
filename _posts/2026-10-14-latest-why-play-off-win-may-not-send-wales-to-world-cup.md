After Tuesday's semi-final success against Albania, Wales will play the home leg of the final at the Cardiff City Stadium on Tuesday, 1 December before the return in Norway four days later.

The frustration for Wilkinson's side is that if they can upset Norway, they may well be the team who miss out on an automatic route to South America.

There is a ray of hope, however, after the play-off semi-final results.

However, Finland's semi-final victory against Serbia has changed the picture.

As a League B runner-up, Finland were below Wales - who topped their League B pool - in the group stage rankings.

The Finns, ranked 22nd in the world, now face Italy â who are 14th in the Fifa rankings â in their play-off final.

Should Finland win, a Welsh triumph against Norway would send Wilkinson's team directly to the World Cup.

If Finland are eliminated, however, Wales will know success against Norway would mean the inter-confederation play-offs await.

Results in the six other European play-off finals are irrelevant because no other side is ranked below Wales.

The European nation who ends up in the inter-confederation play-offs will enter at the second stage, meaning they would need to win a single knockout game.

There will be six teams involved in all, including two Concacaf nations, one from Conmebol and two sides who come through a preliminary phase - featuring Chinese Taipei, Uzbekistan, South Africa, Papua New Guinea, Ghana and Ecuador - which takes place in November and December.

While plans must be made for a potential inter-confederation play-off, the immediate concern for Wales is Norway.

Speaking after the Albania triumph, former Wales captain Kath Morgan suggested Norway was "the best draw" Wilkinson's players could have hoped for.

"They are a very good team but they will have a little bit of doubt in their minds," Morgan said.

Nevertheless, Wales will start as underdogs against a Norway side who are up to 13th in the Fifa rankings - 18 places above Wales - after an 8-0 aggregate win over Romania in their play-off semi-final.

While Wales' women have never previously reached a World Cup, Norway have never missed one - and were champions in 1995.

Wales will have to break new ground if they are to progress, having never beaten a side ranked in the world's top 20 in a competitive game, but Wilkinson is adamant they can upset Gemma Grainger's team.

Grainger was Wales boss when they lost to Switzerland at the play-off final stage in qualifying for the 2023 World Cup.

The fact she will now be in the opposite dugout, having quit Wales to take the Norway job in 2024, will be an interesting subplot this time around.

Having been forced to include a number of youngsters in the last couple of weeks because of a lengthy injury list, Wilkinson was quick to point out that many members of her squad never worked with Grainger.

"Gemma was here for a while and I think that's for storylines," Wilkinson said.

"I don't know Gemma. I'm sure that the players will be happy to see her."

While there are new faces coming through under Wilkinson, the fact is that many of the group facing Grainger will have previously played under her.

"We have had a really good relationship with Gemma and we respect her a lot for what she did here," said Wales captain Angharad James.

"But of course it adds a little bit of spice to everything.
