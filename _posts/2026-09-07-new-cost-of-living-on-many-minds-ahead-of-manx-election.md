Residents, businesses, and charities say they are feeling the pinch of rising costs, with many describing the economy as a key priority ahead of the Isle of Man general election.

Several independent shops in Ramsey said custom was down and running costs had become harder to manage, while the island's foodbank said demand for support had doubled in two years.

The Isle of Man Chamber of Commerce warned the Island faced "a very hard five years" ahead, citing a shrinking tax base and the recent loss of many highâearning jobs.

Candidates have been urged to explain how they will fund their plans, support businesses, and reduce the pressures on working families.

Residents in Douglas said rising prices were affecting everyday life, with several describing the cost of living as their biggest concern ahead of the election.

Sharon Cox, from Douglas, said life was "not affordable.. especially for older people and vulnerable people". 

Prices were rising "not just by pennies", but by "25 or 30 pence" each time she visited the shops, she added. 

Sharon Cox said daily life on the Isle of Man had become unaffordable

Mum-of-one Caitlin Gibson said she was living with her mum and struggled to save anything while caring for her young daughter. 

She said: "I can't really afford to move out. I can't afford to save anything, so everything I earn, which is very little in the first place, is going on my baby."

Another Douglas resident, Andrew Cairns, said rising fuel and energy costs were having "a massive impact". 

Andrew Cairns said he could notice prices rising month on month

"Petrol is going up, electric, gas. You've only got to go in a shop a month apart and you can see the prices rising," he added.

He said it was something on his mind ahead of election, adding there was "a lot we can do to cut down our costs".

A number of businesses described the last few years as some of the hardest they had faced.

Tania Dolores has run a shoe and accessory store in Ramsey for more than 10 years

Tania Dolores, from Tania Dolores Shoes and Accessories, said: "Every day is a struggle. We are not making what we used to make." 

She said her takings were "exactly the same amount of money as ten years ago", but that her bills were "much higher".

Amy Salter, from the Brunch cafe, said the cost of supplies and energy had changed how the family business operated.

When the business opened three years ago, it was "a lot easier than it is now", she said, adding that even small price rises in stock were having a big impact. 

Linda Murphy wanted candidates to be looking out for small businesses

Linda Murphy, from Oma Sustainable Living, said footfall dropped sharply after government services were removed from the Post Office earlier this year.

She said she wanted to see in charge "somebody who understands and is looking out for small businesses, somebody who understands the value of the community, the high street and what it benefits".

Chairman of the Isle of Man Foodbank David Gawne said the third sector was also seeing the impact, and said the charity had become "something of a barometer" for the island's economic pressures.

"Two years ago, we were giving out 200 parcels a month. We're now giving out 400 parcels a month and we're pushing that boundary now," he said.

He explained that 80% of people it supported were working full time, but "not earning enough now to be able to support themselves or support their family". 

Gawne said many families were living on a financial knife-edge.

He called for the next administration to prioritise the economy.
