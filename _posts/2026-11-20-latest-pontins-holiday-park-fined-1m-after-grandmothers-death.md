Pontins holiday park resort has been fined Â£1m after a grandmother died in a "catastrophic incident" that was "an accident waiting to happen", a judge ruled.

Wendy Jones, 68, of Hereford, died six months after a 40m (131ft) section of air conditioning duct and ceiling collapsed onto guests at Pontins' Brean Sands resort, Somerset, in February 2019.

On Friday, Pontins' parent firm, Britannia Jinky Jersey, was sentenced at Bristol Crown Court to pay a Â£1m fine.

Jones' son James Havard-Jones said: "There is a temptation to describe what happened as an accident. I cannot do that. An accident is something unforeseeable. This was not unforeseeable. This was preventable."

Contractor Stephen Bennison also received a 10-month prison sentence, suspended for 18 months.

They both admitted failure to discharge a duty under health and safety laws.

Bennison received a suspended prison sentence

During sentencing, Judge Neil Moody said Bennison was contracted to work on the ceiling because he was cheaper than other bids.

Bennison then subcontracted to untrained individuals and failed to carry out a risk assessment, which Judge Moody described as a serious failing.

"It was an accident waiting to happen. Cutting costs at expense of safety was a factor," he said.

The court heard on Thursday that Bennison carried out work on the ceiling despite not being a qualified structural engineer.

He ordered more slabs than were needed and it was agreed Bennison would put the leftover slabs on top of the ventilation ducting, the court was told.

The prosecution added that "the whole job was done without proper planning on either side". They said it showed the parent company "put profits over safety".

Hayley Kendall, Jones' daughter, said life was not the same without her mum

Jones was one of the 18 people injured during the incident which unfolded ahead of a bingo game.

Havard-Jones said in a statement after the trial that the fine is "substantial and should serve as a lesson to other companies that they have to behave properly".

"My Mum's life was cut short because of the incompetence and negligence of others.

"It's been seven long years of anxiety, stress and worry."

Jones' daughter Hayley Kendall added: "Nothing will ever replace the loss of our mum. 

"No-one should ever have to go through what we went through as a family."

Det Supt Roger Doxsey, senior investigating officer at Avon and Somerset Police, said the "catastrophic incident lasted a mere matter of seconds", but its consequences are still "painfully felt" seven years on.

"Wendy's family are absolutely right when they say this incident was entirely preventable.

"It is a minor miracle, given the weight of the ducting, the nature of its collapse, and the volume of other people present in the Fun Factory at the time of the incident, that more people were not seriously injured and killed," Doxsey added.

About 100 people were in the entertainment hall about to play bingo when part of the ceiling and the ducting collapsed

Pontins: Rundown & Ruined?

The ruling follows an investigation from BBC News, which exposed how a former site manager at Brean Sands raised concerns at the time that the insulation had been unsafely installed and told Pontins' head office he feared the ceiling would collapse.

Brean Sands and Pakefield in Suffolk are currently under contract to house construction workers building Hinkley Point C and Sizewell nuclear plants.

Britannia, which bought Pontins out of receivership in 2011, also runs more than 60 hotels across England, Wales and Scotland.
