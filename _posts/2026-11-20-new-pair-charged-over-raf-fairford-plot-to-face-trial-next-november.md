Two British nationals charged in connection with an alleged plot linked to Iran at RAF Fairford last month will go on trial next year, a court has been told.

London's Old Bailey heard from lawyers representing Mohmed Abdulkarim, 24, and 25-year-old Nouman Essghir that the pair would be denying wrongdoing, so a date for a four-week trial was set for Woolwich Crown Court from 22 November 2027. 

The two men, both from London, appeared via videolink charged under the National Security Act after vans packed with petrol were driven to the airbase in Gloucestershire in September. 

Julia Faure Walker, prosecuting, said both defendants were in contact with a person named Ali who was acting "in the interests" of Iran. 

A major incident was declared after three suspicious vans were seen near the US-operated airbase on 27 September.

Dozens of residents were evacuated and a large cordon was put up in the area around the base. 

No explosive materials were found in the vans by police but a quantity of petrol was seized.

During the hearing on Friday, both defendants confirmed their identities and their lawyers indicated both would deny the charges against them.  

Abdulkarim, from Westminster, is accused of assisting a foreign intelligence service. It is alleged he acted as an "intermediary" for Ali, who was not in the UK and allegedly directed the operation.

Prosecutors allege he aided Ali, and bought equipment for the mission and received a number of payments from him totalling around Â£70,000.  

Essghir, from Notting Hill,  is charged with entering a prohibited place for a purpose prejudicial to the UK, in relation to an alleged "reconnaissance" mission on 22 August. 

He is accused of filming the perimeter of RAF Fairford at Ali's request. 

Essghir was among the five men arrested on 27 September, and was then re-arrested on 20 October. 

After the hearing, Essghir was remanded in custody while Abdulkarim was granted continued bail.  

Iran has previously denied any involvement in the incident at RAF Fairford.

The base has been used as a forward operating location by the US Air Force for decades.

In recent months, the US has flown B-1 and B-52 bombers from the base to the Middle East, after the UK approved a request for it to be used for defensive strikes against Iran.

After the incident in September the US pulled its bombers out of the base and returned them to America.

US President Donald Trump said the aircraft were removed due to "some threats" to the base, without giving specific details.
