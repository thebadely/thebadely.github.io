The start of the Nations Championship finals weekend will play out in front of thousands of empty seats on Friday with Twickenham's Allianz Stadium set to be filled to about half its 82,000-seater capacity.

The uppermost tier of the stadium is entirely closed, while just over half of the middle tier is open.

Tickets are on sale closer to the action, with a large proportion of seats in some lower-tier sections, notably in the north-west corner of the stadium, remaining.

The finals weekend is the climax of a new format, which repackages July and November Test fixtures and culminates in six play-off fixtures between northern and southern hemisphere sides over three days.

Friday's double-header fixture begins with Italy and Fiji, the two lowest-ranked nations, playing at 16:40 GMT, before Ireland and Australia meet in the fifth/sixth place play-off at 20:10 GMT.

Sales for Saturday and Sunday's action, which are headlined by England v New Zealand and a South Africa v France grand final respectively, are stronger.

Around 65,000 tickets have so far been bought for each, with momentum fuelled by England's five-game winning streak and France's dramatic win over the Springboks in Paris a fortnight ago.

With the identity of the teams playing in each play-off game only confirmed last weekend however, organisers have been up against time to market fixtures to the public.

Diary and budget constraints in the run-up to Christmas and the fact Allianz Stadium has already hosted capacity crowds for England's victories over Australia, Japan and New Zealand over the previous three weekends have added to the challenge.

A limited amount of 'follow my team' tickets, which allowed fans to buy further in advance and guarantee they would see a specific team over the finals weekend, were made available.

The sheer size of Allianz Stadium, which is behind only Wembley in terms of capacity in the United Kingdom, is also a factor.

A crowd of 22,734 came close to filling the Bluenergy Stadium to watch Fiji beat Italy in Udine last weekend. Had Ireland's match against Australia been staged at Dublin's Aviva Stadium, a 51,700 attendance would be the maximum possible.

Most of the seats on sale in the middle tier are opposite the gantry from which television cameras capture the main match action, in an apparent attempt to improve the spectacle for viewers at home.
