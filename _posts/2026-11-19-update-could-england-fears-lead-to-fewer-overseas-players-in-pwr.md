World rugby has endured a few shocks and upsets in 2026 - and England's women losing back-to-back Test matches to Canada was among the biggest.

But with 25 Canadians plying their trade on English soil in Premiership Women's Rugby (PWR) last season, their success on the international stage comes at a time when PWR has been in discussions with the Rugby Football Union (RFU) to review the numbers of overseas players in the league.

"We are keen to attract star talent into PWR to continue to make it the best league in the world," said RFU chief executive Bill Sweeney.

"But we've also got to protect England Red Roses' interests as well."

Changes have been planned for the 2027-28 PWR season around injury dispensation for foreign players, with the removal of club injury credit which helps to cover the cost of replacing an injured player.

Only players who sustain an injury while on international duty for one of England's representative teams will be eligible.

However, discussions have gone further around the numbers of English Qualified Players (EQP) required in matchday squads.

Speaking to BBC Sport, Sweeney said: "We're talking about it. Currently, there's 13 EQP in PWR. It's 15 in the [men's] Prem. 

"We're always concerned about the amount of game time that's occurring for under-23 players coming through the system. Are they getting enough game time? 

"We're having conversations with PWR in terms of how we can maximise that, so it is a live topic."

England had been on a 41-match unbeaten streak - dating back almost four years - before twice losing to Canada in the WXV Global Series last month. 

It prompted a warning from head coach John Mitchell that too many overseas players in the PWR could be detrimental for future Red Roses sides.

The league has increasingly attracted the best global talent. In recent years, a majority of Scotland and Wales players have played in PWR and since the 2025 World Cup, more players have joined from New Zealand. 

The highest-profile arrival last season was Black Ferns captain Ruahei Demant linking up with Bristol, while Ruby Tui has joined Trailfinders Women for the new campaign.

And while many neutrals believe Canada's growing success is good for the women's game, Sweeney says England must keep "raising the bar".

"It doesn't feel it's good for the game for us, we never want to lose - but it had to come at some stage," he said. 

"Seeing the growth in the Canadian team, largely due to where they play in PWR - it's encouraging that the standards are rising.

"We're conscious that unions like Ireland are investing much more heavily now into the women's game as well, so it is good for the game. 

"From our perspective, we just want to keep raising the bar and challenging people to catch us and challenge us."

Canada and Saracens scrum-half Olivia Apps was named PWR player of the season when her club lifted the 2025-26 trophy.

She says exposure to the PWR has massively benefited Canadian rugby.

"It's the best women's league right now to play in and the best quality competition," she told BBC Sport.

"We don't have a league back home, so to be able to come from the universities and play with and against the best players in the world is going to make us better for sure."

Asked if she was worried Canadian players would not get as much game time in the future, Apps said: "I would hope not. I think that would diminish the quality of the PWR.

"Internationals make this league what it is, and credit to the English players, but the internationals really do contribute in a great way. 

"Any coach or team that has a Canadian on their side loves to have that Canadian because they work hard, they bring the physicality, the athleticism and a great style of rugby."

Reducing foreign imports would allow young English talent more PWR game time, but the move does not come without concerns.

"The league itself is to create Red Roses and to create English players for the future. If we are going to bring non-EQP players into the league, then they have to add to the league, so they have to be of a world-class standard," said Loughborough Lightning head coach Nathan Smith.

But Smith does envisage a problem if the league changes before investing in the pathways.

"There's a pinch point at the start of next season where we'll have Lions, England camp and a Premiership Cup. My simple question is, are there enough players to fulfil all three?" he said.

"England Under-20s and Under-18s aren't performing on the world stage, therefore what can we do to support that? We need a level of competition that sits below the PWR that fulfils their needs for development."

A PWR spokesperson said: "There is no difference of opinion between us and the RFU on this and developing the next generation of Red Roses is a specific, stated objective of the PWR. 

"We will continue to work collaboratively with the RFU on EQP and any other matter which is important to the future of women's rugby in this country."

The new PWR season starts on Friday as Loughborough Lightning host Trailfinders.

For now, overseas stars will remain a key part of the league's success, but how many are allowed to feature in the future may define its next chapter.
