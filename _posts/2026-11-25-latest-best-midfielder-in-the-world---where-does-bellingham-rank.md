Is there a better central midfielder on the planet than Jude Bellingham right now?

Not according to Jose Mourinho. The Real Madrid manager might be trying to manage the Englishman's minutes, but he did not hold back in his praise after the 23-year-old came off the bench and scored a superb volley, helping Real overturn a half-time deficit to beat PSV Eindhoven 5-1.

"He has physicality, he has technique, he has tactical understanding of the game, he has ambition, he has absolutely everything," Mourinho said.

"He's an absolutely extraordinary player and for me, without any doubt, the best midfielder in the world."

Bellingham's impressive 45-minute cameo came just nine days after an outstanding, all-action display for England in a 3-2 Nations League win in Spain.

While the former Birmingham and Borussia Dortmund player also earned plenty of plaudits for his displays at the 2026 World Cup, where he scored seven times, Mourinho believes Bellingham's unique blend of craft and graft isn't yet fully appreciated.

"He can play as a '10', as an '8', with two pivots, with one," Mourinho said after the win against PSV. "That's why I said, when the Ballon d'Or was discussed, that it was outrageous he wasn't among the top 10."

While wingers like Lamine Yamal or Michael Olise are also likely to be contenders for the 2027 award, is there anyone you would rather have in central midfield than Bellingham? 

BBC Sport looks at what makes him so good, and who his closest challengers are.

Is Bellingham currently 'the best player in the world'?

Although only 23, Bellingham is already in his fourth year at Real Madrid and is on course for his most productive campaign since his debut season in 2023-24, when he scored 23 goals and provided 13 assists, including setting up Vinicius Jr in the Champions League final.

He has seven goals and four assists in 18 appearances this season. Only three central midfielders have more goal involvements across Europe's top five leagues, but Bellingham's all-round game is what sets him apart.

He leads his Real team-mates for possession won, duels won and fouls won. And while only Kylian Mbappe has had more shots on target, Bellingham offers far more than just late runs into the box.

He has played more passes into the final third than any other Real player, leads the club for ball carries and is second only to Arda Guler for chances created.

When Bellingham's coaches at Birmingham gave him the number 22 shirt, it reflected their belief that he could play any role, as a number 10, 8 or 4.

He has fulfilled that potential to become the complete midfielder.

While Bellingham made his senior debut with Birmingham, Vitinha also spent part of his formative years in the Midlands, scoring once in 22 appearances during a loan spell at Wolves - a spectacular long-range strike against Chorley in 2021.

But goalscoring isn't what the Portuguese playmaker is best known for - it is his metronomic passing.

The 26-year-old has played a pivotal role in Paris St-Germain's back-to-back Champions League triumphs, with manager Luis Enrique saying he "embodies the perfect midfielder".

He was named man of the match in the victory over Arsenal last season, having completed 141 of his 150 passes, matching the Champions League final record set by Barcelona's Xavi in 2011.

Vitinha finished third in the Ballon d'Or voting in 2025 but slipped to 14th this year, a ranking Luis Enrique disagreed with.

"It's a real pleasure to have a player with such physical abilities and that mentality," the PSG boss said last month. "He's at an incredible level."

Rice finished one place below Vitinha, and four below England team-mate Bellingham, in the 2026 Ballon d'Or rankings after helping Arsenal win their first Premier League title since 2004.

He briefly played at centre-back as a youngster at West Ham before establishing himself as a top-class defensive midfielder for the Hammers. But since joining the Gunners in 2023 he has taken his game to another level, adding significantly more attacking output.

Rice created 17 big chances in the Premier League last season, as defined by Opta, a total bettered only by Bruno Fernandes and Rayan Cherki.

The 27-year-old has also registered 14 assists in all club competitions since August 2025. While set-pieces have undoubtedly contributed to that tally, he offers much more.

He ranks among the top 4% of central midfielders across Europe's top five leagues for progressive carries, while posting similarly impressive figures for chances created, touches and expected assists.

The 2024 Ballon d'Or winner missed Spain's Nations League defeat by England this month through injury, and his absence was keenly felt as the hosts uncharacteristically struggled for control, at least against 11 men.

It was a far cry from Spain's triumphant Euro 2024 and 2026 World Cup campaigns, both of which ended with him being named player of the tournament.

The 30-year-old completed 756 passes as Spain won their second global title, the highest total ever recorded at a World Cup.

His performances this summer were all the more impressive given that he had taken time to return to his best after tearing his anterior cruciate ligament, an injury that sidelined him for most of the 2024-25 campaign.

The former Manchester City player has slotted in seamlessly since joining Barcelona in August, and averages 106 successful passes per 90 minutes in La Liga - the second-highest figure, behind Vitinha, of any player in the top five European leagues.

Pedri may be six years younger than Rodri, his midfield partner at the Camp Nou, but he boasts extraordinary experience for a 24-year-old, having made more than 250 appearances for Barcelona, not to mention playing at two European Championships and two World Cups.

The Tenerife-born midfielder combines tigerish work-rate with quality in possession.

He has created 26 chances from open play in La Liga this season, the sixth-highest total across Europe's top five leagues, and wins possession an average of 7.6 times per 90 minutes, the 10th-highest figure.

However, such is Spain's wealth of midfield options that he is not even guaranteed a starting place for his country, with Fabian Ruiz preferred for their final three matches at the 2026 World Cup.

Paris St-Germain midfielder Ruiz, who finished 10th in this year's Ballon d'Or rankings, also deserves to be in any debate about the world's best midfielders.

He might not yet have the profile of Barcelona team-mates Rodri or Pedri, but on current form alone, versatile youngster Lopez is one of Europe's best box-to-box goalscorers.

The 23-year-old already has nine goals and seven assists this season. Counting only goals scored in Europe's top five leagues and the Champions League, no central midfielder has more.

While Lopez can also operate on the wing, the presence of Lamine Yamal and Anthony Gordon means he has mainly featured as a number eight or a number 10 this term.

The Barcelona academy graduate played only a bit-part role as Spain won Euro 2024 and was unfortunate to miss the World Cup after breaking a bone in his foot in May.

However, a summer off enabled him to hit the ground running this season, and he has not looked back.
