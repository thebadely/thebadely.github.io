It has been recently reported that Hallgrimsson says Sunday's second game with Israel will be "tricky and challenging" for his players

Republic of Ireland head coach Heimir Hallgrimsson admits Sunday's second game against Israel is "not going to be a friendly" following comments from the latter's camp after last week's match in Hungary. 

Having faced calls to boycott the Nations League fixtures because of the ongoing Israel-Gaza war, the Republic of Ireland did not engage in pre-match formalities and bowed their heads during the Israeli national anthem in Debrecen. 

After a 3-0 win for the Republic of Ireland, Israel captain Manor Solomon said "we have a country 10 times better than theirs". 

"Obviously they hate us. We don't like them," he added.

Following Thursday's 2-2 draw with Austria in Dublin, Hallgrimsson was asked if those comments will make for a tense encounter with Israel in Sunday's behind-closed-doors game in the Serbian town of Backla Topola. 

"I guess so it's not going to be a friendly," said Hallgrimsson. 

Hallgrimsson has spoken about the toll the controversy surrounding the Israel fixtures has had on his squad and said fatigue because of "everything going on" was a factor on Thursday as Austria fought back following Troy Parrott's first-half double. 

"It is going to be tricky and challenging for the players to be ready [for Israel], but we will do everything and the medical team will work day and night to get them fit and fresh for that game," he added.

"[It's] just another football match, but [there] is more at stake for all of us."

Hallgrimsson thanked the Irish fans for their support 

The Israel fixtures have dominated this international window with the Republic of Ireland urged to withdraw from the games by the 'Stop The Game' campaign - organised by the pressure group Irish Sport for Palestine - who staged protests outside the Aviva Stadium before the Austria game.

The first half of Thursday's game was also briefly interrupted by a tennis ball protest and a pitch invader.

Hallgrimsson said he had worried prior to the match about the atmosphere inside Aviva Stadium but said the squad were buoyed by the support they received.

"Yeah, I'm not going to lie. I didn't know what to expect," said the 59-year-old.

"Obviously, the 'Stop the Game' protest is loud. I don't know how many but it's been loud, but it was just good to see the support from the fans and I just want to repeat the appreciation for people showing up and showing support tonight, it helps us a lot. 

"It gives us even more pride to play for the country."

Before last week's game in Debrecen, Republic of Ireland goalkeeper Gavin Bazunu made himself unavailable for both Israel matches, saying he felt "it would be wrong to participate". 

On the eve of the Israel game, the Republic of Ireland players' vote on whether to play the game passed by what Football Association of Ireland (FAI) chief executive David Courell called a "significant majority".  

When asked if he feels the support during the Austria game will help the players heading into the second Israel fixture, Hallgrimsson said: "I don't know, you'll have to ask them. 

"I hope so. I hope we all feel more appreciated than probably before this match. If that answers the question, I think we feel more appreciated."

Sunday's game is the Republic of Ireland's 'home' fixture, which was moved to TSC Arena in Backa Topola amid ongoing fan protests from fans over the FAI's decision to fulfil the fixtures.

Hallgrimsson added he knows "absolutely nothing" about the town, which is around 100 miles north of Belgrade, Serbia's capital.

"Everything about this [is strange]," he said.

"First of all, the four-game window has been strange and challenging and testing. It will be different playing in front of 45,000 or 50,000 supporters and then playing in an empty stadium. 

"But that's just another angle of the story and we need to be ready for that one."
