It has been recently reported that Former Business Secretary Peter Kyle has said the government must act urgently to "level the playing field" for Royal Mail or risk the business becoming unviable.

He said he was concerned that the firm was being "undercut" by rival parcel delivery firms who did not employ staff under the same conditions.

Royal Mail, which is owned by International Distribution Services, has struggled in recent years with increased competition, fewer letters being sent and fines from the regulator for missing delivery targets.

A government spokesperson said it would consider whether further reforms are required to the parcel market after the watchdog, Ofcom, publishes its annual monitoring report  in December.

Kyle had started to prepare officials at the Department for Business and Trade (DBT) to look at the delivery market for parcels shortly before he was replaced in his job by Jonathan Reynolds as business secretary in July.

He said a "crunch point" had been reached where the government had to make a decision about whether it wants Royal Mail "to be a viable proposition in the future".

He said his review would have looked at where the costs in that market lay and where the profit was being made, to see successful companies upheld "the values of business in Britain today".

"If we leave it another few years then Royal Mail will just become unviable and it will just collapse under the weight of having full-time employees," he said.

According to Royal Mail, 98% of its 130,000 workers are permanent employees, with rights to benefits including pensions, sick pay and holiday pay.

Some other parcel delivery companies use self-employed delivery drivers, with some offering an opt-in service that gives couriers limited additional benefits.

Royal Mail is also legally obliged to offer customers a "one price goes anywhere" service across the UK and to deliver first class letters six days a week to every address.

Kyle's proposed review would not have considered further changes to Royal Mail's universal service obligations or to terms and conditions for its staff.

Royal Mail's performance has improved in recent months but it is still missing its targets for delivering first and second class post.

Ofcom agreed last year to scrap second class deliveries on Saturdays and to reduce the target for delivering first class post within a day of collection to 90%.

A government spokesperson said the current business secretary had met Royal Mail and the Communication Workers Union, which represents postal workers, last week "to seek timely progress on improving their quality of service and to reiterate our commitment to the company's success."

DBT has also launched a consultation on tackling, external what it calls "bogus" self-employment, which it describes as "a practice whereby some businesses mislabel staff as self-employed to avoid statutory obligations".

Royal Mail has welcomed the consultation, and a spokesperson for the company claimed some parts of the delivery industry were "driving a race to the bottom" on employment standards and said it would "continue to push for a level playing field, with strong and enforceable standards across the industry."

Many MPs across the UK have reported receiving high volumes of complaints from constituents about missed and delayed deliveries.

Shadow business minister Bradley Thomas said that Royal Mail's management needed to address the business's failings.

"Customers are being asked time and time again to pay increased prices, yet the service they're receiving is not improving," he said. 

"Be in no doubt, this is not the fault of the hardworking postmen and women, it's the fault of Royal Mail's management.

"Nationalisation isn't the solution to this problem. Instead, we need ministers and Ofcom to bear down on Royal Mail's management to ensure they deliver on the improvements they've promised their customers."

The Communication Workers Union, which represents Royal Mail workers, is also putting pressure on ministers, with General Secretary Dave Ward calling for a "new model of ownership".
