England netball head coach Anna Stembridge says she will "reassess" whether she can continue in the role after the Roses missed out on a medal at the Commonwealth Games.

Her team were outclassed 68-50 by world number one side Australia in their quest to win bronze in Glasgow.

Stembridge - who also led England to fourth place at the 2014 Games - was appointed in March after her predecessor Jess Thirlby stepped down, with discussions about the longer-term role always expected to take place after the tournament.

"I think it's a real tough one to swallow in terms of not coming away with a medal," Stembridge told BBC Sport.

"I've also been really honest from the start. I was fiercely focused on this tournament and I will just take some time to reassess and decide what's right for me and my family, but then also what's right for England Netball. 

"I think it was important that we were transparent, that we were open around the interviews that will take place in the near future, but I just need to sit in this hurt for a minute and get over this loss," she added.

New Zealand, who beat England in the semi-finals, won the gold medal with a 56-48 victory against a resilient Jamaica side to claim their first title since 2010.

England tried to rally themselves following their semi-final defeat by New Zealand, but came up against a Diamonds team recovering from one of the biggest shocks in the tournament's history.

Australia - who had previously been in every Commonwealth final and won the tournament four times - were stunned by Jamaica on Saturday afternoon, giving them less time to recover for the bronze-medal match than England.

The Roses worked hard to shut down the Diamonds' forward-passing options from the start, but the team in green and gold found their rhythm with devastating consequences, overturning an early 9-7 deficit to lead 19-11 in a bruising first-quarter spell for the Roses.

Stembridge's side, who lost 66-47 to the same opponents in the pool stage, then lacked impetus in the second period, showing signs of tired legs against one of the world's most established shooting partnerships in Kiera Austin and Sophie Garbin.

A loose pass from England captain Fran Williams allowed Austin to calmly extend the gap to 28-16 and her side kept the margin steady, resulting in a half-time score of 35-24.

The presence of substitute shooter Eleanor Cardwell in the third quarter helped the Roses score five without reply to trail 45-34, but they were still 49-36 behind with 15 minutes left to play.

Bronze is not a colour of medal that Australia ever imagined they would be playing for, but what seemed just as important to them was reminding the world what they are capable of and why they have been such feared opponents.

In that sense, Stacey Marinkovich's Diamonds shone as ultimate professionals, and England again came up short against a top side, losing by one less point than the pool stage match between the teams.

"It's so tough after we lost yesterday and the disappointment of not making a final, to pull yourself up. I'm just so proud of the girls, the fight that we had through this 10 days to keep showing up and responding," Williams told BBC Sport.

"Probably a few of our errors came through that fatigue and the mental drain that being in tournament netball is, and that is no excuse.

"For so many of us, 10 of us, that was our first Commonwealth Games experiencing that and maybe that showed in a final-day game where you've got to turn it on when medals are on that line.

"I'm not going away from that World Cup next year without a medal and standing on that podium, hopefully with a gold, so we'll be doing as much as we can in the next 12 months to make sure that we are in a better position to fight in these final games."

New Zealand have three victories in six Commonwealth Games gold-medal matches

New Zealand maintained their perfect winning record in the tournament by holding firm in a battle of nerve against Jamaica in the gold-medal match.

The Sunshine Girls could not reach the brilliant level they produced to stun defending champions Australia in their semi-final, but fought hard to remain just three points behind after one quarter, with the Silver Ferns leading 28-25 at half-time.

Jamaica came out roaring after the break. Their talismanic shooter Romelda Aiken-George helped them to draw level at 35-35, but their spells of pressure were not sustained enough, and New Zealand kept finding Grace Nweke in the circle to lead by nine with one quarter to go.

In the final moments, captain Karin Burger took firm hold of the ball to stop a Jamaican attack, epitomising the team's spirit as they closed out the game to win their third Commonwealth title.

In all eight editions of netball at the Games, just New Zealand, Australia and England have won gold, but after back-to-back final defeats, Jamaica have proven that they can challenge the very best and they certainly played their part in an exhilarating tournament.
