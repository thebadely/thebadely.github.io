Her comments were criticised by Paralympian Richard Whitehead, who accused her of "weaponising" his sporting success, while Prime Minister Andy Burnham called the Tory leader's words "crass and insensitive".

But in an interview with the BBC Radio 4's Today programme, Badenoch stood by the remarks, insisting that "if people even with significant problems can work, then those who have ADHD and mild anxiety should go to work".

Asked if she accepted the comments were insensitive, Badenoch said: "No, no, no. I do not accept it. I will speak the truth, and I will say what I believe is common sense. I will speak the truth, and I will say what I believe is common sense.

"What I know is that if people can work, then we should encourage them to work."

Badenoch acknowledged she had used "extreme examples to illustrate things", but added: "If someone can do that, then yes, your condition of ADHD or anxiety is mild."

"I will say things as they are. People may disagree with things that I say, and that's fine, this is a free country, people are free to disagree, but they know where I'm coming from," she said.

"I start from first principles: If you can work, you should work, and the Conservative Party is going to help you get a job."
