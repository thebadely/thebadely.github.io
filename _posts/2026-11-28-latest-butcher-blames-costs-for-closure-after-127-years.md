Here’s what you need to know: Andrew Parsons is the fourth generation of his family to run the shop 

A family butcher's shop has blamed rising costs as it prepares to close later after 127 years.

Bennett Butchers, in Spalding, Lincolnshire, was opened by Henry Bennett in 1899 and is now owned by Andrew Parsons, his great-grandson.

Parsons said the shop in Winsover Road was still popular and profitable, but a number of issues meant its future was "unviable".

"We're a labour-intensive business. We're also high energy users. The hike in National Insurance from the government last year and all these [things], they all make it very, very difficult," he added.

"We're still a profitable business, but we're not as profitable as we were. And we've reached a point where we need to invest a lot of money in the business to drive it forward." 

A spokesperson for HM Treasury said the government was providing "business rates support and action to cut costs for firms".

It was "doubling" the Employment Allowance, which allows employers to reduce their annual National Insurance liability, to Â£10,500, and ensuring electricity bills were "being cut by up to 25% for over 10,000 eligible businesses".

Parsons says the shop has been incredibly busy over the past few weeks

Parsons thanked the town's residents for the "outpouring of love" shown to the business since it announced the closure on 18 October.

He said the shop had "sold more meat over the last six weeks than we would over two Christmases".

"I just can't thank everybody enough for how they've rallied round and supported us over the last six weeks and filled their freezers," he said.

He also thanked the shop's staff, who he had "been amazing", despite facing redundancy, and said he was relieved many of them now had new jobs.

As well as customers visiting the shop and commenting on social media, an anonymous individual has paid homage to the business with a plaque.

Parsons said it "arrived unannounced on Wednesday evening" and described the wording, which celebrates the shop's long-standing customer service, as "fantastic" and "amazing".

The butcher said he was planning to "relax" after the shop's closure as it was "the first Christmas that I haven't worked for 44 years", but he was not planning to retire just yet and hoped to stay in the meat industry.
