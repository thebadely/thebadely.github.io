It has been recently reported that Carlo Tizzano is a rare starter for Australia while Jac Morgan is a go-to pick for Wales

Australia flanker Carlo Tizzano is looking forward to tackling Jac Morgan again when Wales host the Wallabies in the Nations Championship in Cardiff on Saturday.

The pair were involved in a controversial clearout incident during the British and Irish Lions' series-clinching second Test win in Melbourne in July 2025 and will face each other for the first time since.

Tizzano will test his skills against Morgan after being given the Australia number seven jersey ahead of Fraser McReight, who has been named on the replacements bench for the game at Principality Stadium.

"[Morgan] would be up there with the best open-side flankers in the world," said Tizzano.

"He is playing well for Gloucester and you want to test yourself against world-class players, and he is one of those."

Jac Morgan and Carlo Tizzano were both replacements in the second Test in Melbourne

The Morgan and Tizzano collision proved the most controversial incident of the Lions' 2-1 series win in Australia in the summer of 2025.

With Australia leading 26-24 in the final minute of the match, Tizzano attempted to snatch a turnover as Lions lock James Ryan went to ground, but Morgan arrived at the ruck a split-second later and cleared him out.

In the following phase, Lions full-back Hugo Keenan went over for the winning score.

Morgan's clearout was reviewed by the television match official (TMO) and the try allowed to stand.

Australia coach at the time, Joe Schmidt, believed Morgan should have been penalised but former Lions flankers like James Haskell and Sam Warburton criticised Tizzano's reaction to the challenge, accusing him of "diving" and "simulation".

Tizzano addressed the issue last year and now declares it ancient history.

"It's in the past," he said.

"Whatever take you have on it, it doesn't matter, the result is done, although it would have been cool to have that third game as a decider."

Schmidt rested Tizzano for the third Test in Sydney - which the Wallabies won - citing the amount of online abuse his player had received over the incident.

Tizzano praised Morgan for seeking him out after the series finale.

"Not only is he a hell of a player but he is also a good bloke," said Tizzano.

"After all that stuff happened with the Lions in game two he made an effort to say g'day and have a yarn after game three.

"He didn't have to do that and say he hoped I was all right."

Tizzano said he appreciated the way the Welshman went about his business.

"It takes a good bloke to do that. He is a proper gentleman and hopefully I will get to swap jerseys with him on the weekend, regardless of the result.

"I am keen to get that and a photo, and maybe aggravate a couple of the Lions fans!"

Australia head coach Les Kiss has since replaced Schmidt and the Wallabies are having a mixed year, suffering defeats by England and Scotland in this Nations Championship campaign.

Kiss, a former specialist defence coach, says he was impressed by Wales' 35-28 victory against New Zealand as Steve Tandy's side made a record 346 tackles.

"It became something the All Blacks couldn't work out in their own headspace," said Kiss.

"How do we beat this wonderful emotional energy, this intent, this way that was compellingly beautiful, wasn't it? There was something about it that was wonderful.

"I texted Steve [Tandy] after the game and just said that was awesome and it was pretty cool to see them do that."

Kiss says he has been impressed by Wales' development under the former Scotland and Lions assistant.

"They've had that time together and at some stage they were going to push through that threshold and click," said Kiss.

"I've coached against Wales probably eight or 10 times, they are a proud rugby nation. 

"They've had tough times but at some time a game like that was going to come alive for them."
