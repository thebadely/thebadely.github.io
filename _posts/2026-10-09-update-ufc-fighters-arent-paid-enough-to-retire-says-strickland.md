Sean Strickland says UFC fighters are not paid enough money to retire from the sport on their own terms.

The 35-year-old middleweight champion criticised the promotion earlier this week, saying his "heart isn't in MMA anymore" after injuries to his groin and shoulder.

Strickland added he would prefer a move to boxing, citing the sport as "significantly easier" with "more money" to be made, before calling for a fight with Jake Paul.

Following up his comments in a post on Instagram, the American says fighters are ruining their legacies by having to fight on for money instead of walking away from the UFC.

"I'm going to say the quiet part out loud that nobody says. You watch these champions, they run the division and they take [a loss], and then they come back and they fight three or four times and get flatlined," said Strickland.

"You're like, 'dude, what happened to you?' And you realise the UFC happened. They just don't care anymore. 

"They don't pay you enough money to walk away. The UFC builds a legacy and they don't pay you enough. So you have to come back and ruin your legacy. I don't want to do that."

Zuffa Boxing is owned by UFC president Dana White, so critics have asked why the 57-year-old isn't paying similar amounts to his MMA fighters under contract.

When asked about criticisms amid the Benn deal, White said "it's a good thing" when people make more money and pointed to fighter pay continuously rising since 2001.

The UFC gives about 20% of revenue to fighter pay, compared with boxers who get about 60% of revenue from their events.

Fighters can't compete under other major promotions while in contract with the UFC - a clause which Strickland has criticised.

"I want to box. I want to go win a boxing title. So we're trying to work that out," added Strickland.

"But if the UFC says, 'hey, we're not going to do that. We're going to make you fight.' Well, I'm going to stick and move, and you guys will boo and I'll boo while doing it.

"Just stick and move, collect my check, and then move on. But I mean, hell, here we are."

Strickland is a polarising figure in MMA and has made homophobic and racist remarks as well as derogatory comments about women.

He became a two-time UFC middleweight champion by beating Khamzat Chimaev in May.
