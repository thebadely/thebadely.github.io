Here’s what you need to know: Gregor Townsend has been in charge of Scotland since 2017

Scotland should be grateful for the work Gregor Townsend has done in his nine and a half years as head coach, according to his Japan counterpart Eddie Jones.

The Australian brings his side to Murrayfield in the Nations Championship on Saturday.

Townsend and Jones locked horns regularly when the latter coached England, including a memorable 38-38 Calcutta Cup draw at Twickenham in 2019.

Opinion is split among Scotland fans as to whether Townsend has overachieved or underachieved with a talented group of players since taking over in 2017.

However, Jones, who only got the better of Townsend once in five Six Nations meetings, is adamant credit is deserved.

"To do 10 years in charge at a top-eight team in the world is a great accomplishment," said the 66-year-old.

"Gregor's done a great job and I think Scotland should be grateful for the job that he's done. In that period Scotland have established themselves probably closer to the top tier of tier one than they have done before.

"There's been some generational players come through like Finn Russell that have helped that progress. But he's certainly done a great job.

"He's lost all his hair as most coaches do, although he's probably lost it a bit earlier than a few of us! So, it can be stressful, but we all make decisions to coach. We don't have to coach. We know what we're getting into.

"There are two ways to look at that. Scotland has been knocking on the door of the top four for a while now and, for a small rugby country, that's a great achievement. 

"If you look at the number of players you've got to pick from with two professional teams, I would say that if data looked into how Scotland is performing in relation to the talent pool that they have they would be doing pretty well. So, that's a big tick.

"At the end of the day, everyone likes to have trophies. But to be knocking on the door of the top four for Scotland is a fantastic achievement."

Townsend has made sweeping changes for the visit of Japan, with Ollie Leatherbarrow earning a first start at number eight and Liam McConnell chosen at blind-side flanker.

There are 10 alterations to the XV that started last weekend's 15-12 win over Australia but no place in the matchday 23 for New Zealand-born back Josh McKay, who now qualifies on residency grounds.

Jonny Gray and Max Williamson form the second row, with Gregor Hiddleston selected at hooker and Nathan McBeth at loosehead.

Rory Hutchinson and Stafford McDowall are the centres, Kyle Rowe lines up on the left wing and Ollie Smith will operate at full-back.

There are positional switches for Jamie Dobie and Tom Jordan, who feature at scrum-half and fly-half respectively, having been on the wing and at centre against Australia.

Ben White, Jack Dempsey and Scott Cummings all picked up injuries in a bruising contest with the Wallabies, while captain Sione Tuipulotu was a late withdrawal and continues his recovery from a knee problem.

Finn Russell, Huw Jones, Kyle Steyn and Ewan Ashman are rested altogether while Blair Kinghorn, Matt Fagerson, Gregor Brown and Rory Sutherland move to the bench, where they are joined by Seb Stephen and Magnus Bradbury.

Rory Darge will again lead the side in the absence of Tuipulotu.

Scotland sit fourth in the northern hemisphere table going into the last round of fixtures before the inaugural tournament's finals weekend.

Ireland and England are both five points in front as they prepare to take on South Africa and New Zealand respectively.

Japan, who lost 78-15 to England after a seven-point defeat in Wales, are fifth in the southern hemisphere table with one win from their five outings.

Scotland last hosted Japan in autumn 2021, prevailing 29-20.

Scotland: Ollie Smith; Darcy Graham, Stafford McDowall, Rory Hutchinson, Kyle Rowe; Tom Jordan, Jamie Dobie; Nathan McBeth, Gregor Hiddleston, Zander Fagerson, Jonny Gray, Max Williamson, Liam McConnell, Rory Darge (capt), Ollie Leatherbarrow

Replacements: Seb Stephen, Rory Sutherland, Will Hurd, Gregor Brown, Magnus Bradbury, Matt Fagerson, George Horne, Blair Kinghorn.
