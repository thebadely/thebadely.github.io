Let's dive into the details: Liverpool won two games and lost one during their pre-season tour of the USA

Andoni Iraola's verdict after Liverpool's 4-2 defeat to Leeds in Chicago on Sunday was telling.

"Obviously it's not the result you want but I think [it was] probably the most useful friendly we played," he said. 

"In a positive way in the first half and in a negative way in the second half. We've learned a lot and I think we can take good conclusions and solve some things."

If something needs solving, then it is a problem and Liverpool require reinforcements at the back. In three matches in the USA, they conceded six goals but four of those came against Leeds in the space of 25 second-half minutes.

Liverpool started that game with a backline of Jeremie Frimpong, Luke Chambers, Ifeanye Ndukwe and Milos Kerkez. They ended it with Calvin Ramsay, Mor Talla Ndiaye, Ndukwe and Kostas Tsimikas.

Against Wrexham four days earlier in New York, a similarly youthful backline deserved credit for keeping a clean sheet. 

But against fellow Premier League opposition in Leeds and a solid top-flight striker in Dominic Calvert-Lewin, Liverpool were brutally cut open. 

In fairness, Kerkez is the only one of those who you can class as an expected starter for the season ahead. Captain Virgil van Dijk is back in training but Liverpool do not have the depth to challenge seriously. 

In fewer than three weeks, Van Dijk will need a partner at centre-half when Liverpool start their Premier League campaign at Newcastle. 

The worry for Iraola is that neither Joe Gomez or Giovanni Leoni will be available while new signing Jeremy Jacquet failed to play a minute on this tour as Liverpool continue to manage his return.

Iraola has said repeatedly that his side are "thin" at the back, not helped by the departures of experienced defenders Andy Robertson and Ibrahima Konate.

Jacquet and Leoni are huge talents but not proven and given his injury record, Gomez will not be able to paper over the cracks.

Sporting director Richard Hughes was at the friendlies in Nashville and New York but not at the game on Sunday. He will know as much too.

At the very least, Liverpool need to bring in at least one name that can play at centre-back and potentially at right-back, given Conor Bradley is still "way off" a return. 

Arne Slot used Curtis Jones and Dominik Szoboszlai as emergency right-backs last season and that can't happen again. Ideally, they need someone with experience.

"You have to ask the manager, not me," Szoboszlai said after the Leeds defeat on what his role in the team looks like going forward. 

"Last season I played a lot of positions. Let's see. Hopefully he puts me in midfield but if he needs me at right-back, happy to do that."

Former Liverpool captain Phil Thompson told BBC Sport in Chicago that Iraola will rely on more experienced players, both old and new.

"You look back when Jurgen [Klopp] won the league and you had [Jordan] Henderson and [James] Milner. And a fantastic leadership group with Virgil, Alisson, Mo [Salah] and even Trent [Alexander-Arnold] went on to become a leader.

"That has dwindled. So you need new ones to come in. We've lost some good players so people need to step up. 

"That dressing room needs to be tight and they need to lay down some player rules. Because when you have a difficult time, they need strong voices. 

"Right now, there's only Virgil and Ali [Alisson]. Someone like Szoboszlai has to step up too and I think he will."

When BBC Sport spoke to Liverpool CEO Billy Hogan in New York, the message was very much that FSG will continue to invest when it comes to doing what is best to put the team in a place to win.

With Szoboszlai, Ryan Gravenberch and Alexis Mac Allister, Liverpool have solid options in midfield. 

Szoboszlai and Gravenberch signed new contracts this year while Iraola will be hoping to get the best out of Mac Allister, who was nowhere near his best last season.

Liverpool enquired about Adam Wharton earlier in the year but they are not the only club interested in the Crystal Palace midfielder.

Given the departure of Salah, expect to see more of 17-year-old Rio Ngumoha down the right flank.

Ngumoha looked a lot more comfortable down his preferred left against Leeds but he has the attributes to be a force down both sides for a manager who wants his wingers to be able to play on either flank.
