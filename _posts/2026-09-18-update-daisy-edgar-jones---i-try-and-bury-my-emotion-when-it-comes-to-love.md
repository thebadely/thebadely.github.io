Here’s what you need to know: Daisy Edgar-Jones has been well cast in her new film.

She plays Elinor, the rational elder sister in Sense and Sensibility, opposite the younger, more romantic sister Marianne.

Speaking to the BBC, Edgar-Jones says that in her personal life, she really is like her character.

"When it comes to love, I think I am very much more Elinor in every sort of way," she says.

"I definitely lead a lot with my head and try and push aside, bury my emotion."

But she notes that one of the joys of the film is seeing Elinor learn to enjoy a "wilder, passionate side" of herself. "And I feel like I learnt to do that too," she says.

Best known for starring in hit TV series Normal People with Paul Mescal, Edgar-Jones, 28, is now taking on a lead role in the latest adaptation of Jane Austen's book.

Jane Austen seems to be everywhere right now.

Last year marked the 250th anniversary of her birth and, in recent years, we've enjoyed two BBC TV series, Miss Austen and The Other Bennet Sister, the West End's Clueless musical (based on the cult 90s teen film adaptation of Emma), and Ava Pickett's modern stage adaptation of Emma.

There's also a new adaptation of Pride and Prejudice written by Dolly Alderton and starring Emma Corrin coming to Netflix.

For Edgar-Jones, the themes in Austen's books are just as revelant today.

"She has an incredible way of articulating the female experience," she says.

"Her ruminations on love, on romance, on sisterly love, on family dynamics, on interpersonal social dynamics - she's such a talent."

Austen also wrote about the sort of dating behaviour that people complain about on TikTok (like situationships and ghosting), before terms like that had even been invented. Does that help Gen Z audiences relate to her, I ask?

"Well, massively," replies Edgar-Jones.

"We still have the same human heart as we did 200 years ago. Our capacity to love and feel heartbreak, and that early first love feeling, is something that we all experience in various ways.

"And so yes, I think definitely the trials and tribulations of the two women [Elinor and Marianne] as they navigate life, love and all that becomes as you grow up, definitely is so relatable to all generations."

"These women are very relatable," agrees Creed-Miles.

"I don't know if seeing a movie is going to fix everything, but it might make you feel a little bit less alone in some of the things you're feeling."
