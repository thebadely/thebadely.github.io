According to new developments, Mollie Mulheron is campaigning for the NHS to protect fertility after chemotherapy

A woman who recovered from blood cancer has said she is fighting for better protection of young survivors' fertility after being told her recovery meant she could no longer access funded treatment.

National guidelines advise offering "cryopreservation" to those facing infertility due to a medical condition or treatment, external. 

Mollie Mulheron, 28, was unable to freeze her eggs before chemotherapy because she needed urgent treatment. After recovering, she was told by NHS Lancashire and South Cumbria Integrated Care Board she no longer met eligibility criteria.

She said it "doesn't make sense" funding was no longer available and believed being seen as no longer eligible was "clearly unfair".

Mulheron, who lives in Padiham and runs a language academy, was travelling in the Galapagos Islands after university when she said she started to feel ill.

After returning home, doctors found a melon-sized tumour, which she said was "crushing my heart and left lung".

After being diagnosed with stage four blood cancer, she underwent about 800 hours of chemotherapy. 

"It was hell, but I did manage to beat it," she said.

Cancer treatments often have side effects and for young women one of the most significant can be reduced fertility.

The main treatments - chemotherapy, radiotherapy, surgery, hormonal therapy and targeted therapy - can affect fertility in different ways.

In many situations, the NHS may provide egg freezing and storage for free, but it can typically take up to three weeks.

If cancer treatment needs to begin immediately there may not be enough time to complete this process.

National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) guidance advises offering "oocyte (egg cell) or embryo cryopreservation" to women who are "preparing for medical treatment, or who have a medical condition, that is likely to make them infertile".

Mulheron said she did not have time to freeze her eggs before treatment due to the cancer being very aggressive, but added that doctors at St James's Hospital in Leeds told her she could access fertility preservation after chemotherapy.

However, after moving to Padiham, she said she was told funding was not available, which had made it feel like "a postcode lottery" for treatment.

Mulheron said she had taken her fight to change fertility guidelines to Parliament

She said her ovarian reserve, a woman's total remaining supply of egg cells, had more than halved in a year and had been told she was likely to go through an early menopause.

She said she feared that without fertility preservation treatment, she may not be able to have children in the future, adding that she was struggling with the prospect of losing that option, particularly as she did not want to feel pressured into starting a family immediately after recovering from cancer.

"I'd like to have some time... just to rebuild my life," she said.

She said she believed the NICE guidance was not mandatory and could be interpreted differently by local NHS bodies, creating inconsistencies in access to treatment.

She added that the process had left her feeling frustrated and trapped in a cycle of uncertainty.

"It's... going around in circles, and it's just so unfair," she said.

She said she had started a petition calling for better protection of young cancer survivors' fertility.

A spokesperson for NHS Lancashire and South Cumbria Integrated Care Board said they were aware of the 28-year-old's case and "understand the patient's concerns".

"However, decisions such as this one are in line with the current eligibility criteria of the Assisted Conception policy for Lancashire and South Cumbria," they said.

"Due to patient confidentiality, we are unable to comment on specific details of individual cases."

They added that the board was "currently working collaboratively with Greater Manchester and Cheshire and Merseyside ICBs to review a number of clinical commissioning policies, and assisted conception services have been identified as a priority, particularly in light of updated national guidance".

"The ICB would welcome any review of national policy that gives due consideration to the needs of all patients."
