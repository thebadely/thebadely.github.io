A charity set up by the family of renowned pandemic fundraiser Capt Sir Tom Moore has applied to be struck off.

The charity is now known as the 1189808 Foundation, having dropped Sir Tom's name at the request of his family last year.

An application to have it struck off appeared on the Companies House register on Friday, meaning it has applied to dissolve itself as a company.

However, the charity founded in his name became mired in controversy after the charities watchdog found misconduct and mismanagement on the part of his daughter and her husband in their running of it.

The Charity Commission's report uncovered a "repeated pattern of behaviour" through which Hannah Ingram-Moore and her husband, Colin, made private gains and which the regulator said would have left people feeling "misled".

The millions raised by the late Sir Tom and donated to NHS Charities Together before the foundation was formed were not part of the commission's inquiry.

The father-of-two died aged 100 in February 2021 with coronavirus.

A charity can only apply to remove itself, external from Companies House if in the last three months it has not taken any actions other than those necessary to closing it and winding up its affairs.

If no-one objects to the request, it would be struck off Companies House after a two-month period following the publication of a notice in The Gazette.

After this it would mean it ceased to legally exist, external.
