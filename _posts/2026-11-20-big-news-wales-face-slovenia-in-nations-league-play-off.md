Here’s what you need to know: Wales have never faced Slovenia away in a men's senior international game

Wales will face Slovenia in a two-leg play-off next March to maintain their top-flight status in the Nations League.

Craig Bellamy's side face the relegation play-off after finishing bottom of Group A4, while Slovenia were runners-up in B1.

The first leg will be played in Slovenia on Saturday, 27 March, with the return fixture in Wales on Tuesday, 30 March.

The winner will play in League A for the next edition of the Nations League, while the loser will be in League B.

Wales have only played Slovenia once before, a goalless friendly in Swansea in 2005.

Slovenia lost only once in their Nations League campaign, 2-1 away to group winners Switzerland, and took four points off Scotland, who finished third.

They drew 0-0 at home against the Swiss and Scotland, who they beat 2-1 in Glasgow last month.  

Bostjan Cesar's side also beat North Macedonia 2-0 at home and 3-0 away in their final game earlier this week.

Wales' only victory was 2-1 at home to Norway in October. They were beaten in Oslo and home and away by both Portugal and Denmark.

There are eight places between Wales (41st) and Slovenia (49th) in the Fifa world rankings.

Wales game against Slovenia in 2005 was the first international in Swansea for 17 years, among the first matches at what was then know as Liberty Stadium

Although Wales still have a chance to retain their League A status for the 2028-29 Nations League, finishing bottom in the current campaign means they will not be among the top seeds in qualifying for Euro 2028 - which includes matches in Wales as co-hosts with England, Scotland and the Republic of Ireland.

Bellamy's side could therefore be drawn alongside one of Europe's heavyweights in their qualifying group.

Spain, France, Italy, Netherlands and Portugal are among the sides already guaranteed pot one seeding for the draw on 6 December in Belfast.

England will also be a top seed, although they will be kept apart from Wales and the other Euro 2028 co-hosts in qualifying regardless.

When it comes to reaching the Euros, the 12 group winners and eight best-ranked runners-up qualify directly.

For any of the co-hosts who do not make it via that route, there are two automatic spots available. Those will be awarded to the best-ranked between the four nations who have not qualified directly.

If Wales still have not made it, there is also the back-up option of the play-offs.

And given play-off places are based on Nations League rankings, Wales are highly likely to already have that safety net.
