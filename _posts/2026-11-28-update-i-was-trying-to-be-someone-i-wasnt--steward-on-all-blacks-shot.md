England's Freddie Steward says he is determined to play his own game as he starts against New Zealand on Saturday ahead of full-back rival George Furbank.

Furbank put in impressive play-making performances in wins over the All Blacks and Australia this autumn, stepping into first receiver and linking play out wide.

Steward has different strengths. The Leicester man is a huge aerial presence under the high ball and a powerful rangy runner.

While the 25-year-old has also developed other areas of his game, noticeably laying on a try for Will Joseph with a superbly weighted grubber kick against Japan, Steward has learned to be confident in his own abilities, rather than be concerned about comparisons.

"I've probably been guilty in the last couple of opportunities of trying to prove something or be someone that I'm not," Steward told 5 Live Breakfast.

"Against Japan I said to myself I just want to go play with a smile on my face and actually just go and enjoy it and be who I am.

"I'll be trying to do the same this weekend for sure."

With Marcus Smith's ability to play full-back, along with his more accustomed fly-half, making him a more versatile bench option, Steward's only route into the England matchday squad would appear to be as the starting full-back.

It is a role he made his own after making his debut against the United States in July 2021. He started every game in the following two Six Nations campaigns and was first choice through England's run to the semi-finals at the 2023 Rugby World Cup.

However, he was dropped from the starting line-up in favour of Furbank in the middle of the 2024 Six Nations. His emotional phonecall telling his girlfriend that he was out of the team was captured in the Full Contact Netflix documentary that covered the tournament.

With Smith and Elliot Daly also given opportunities at full-back, Steward has had to battle for his position and overcome set-backs. He was substituted in the first half of England's 42-21 defeat by Ireland in February.

But he says working with a psychologist and avoiding social media reviews of his performances have helped power an excellent start to the current campaign with table-topping Tigers.

"When I first started working with psychologists, I was probably quite young and I really didn't appreciate the mental side of the game," Steward added.

"But I think the more you experience things like being dropped, you actually need to work on the mental side of it; how you react to those situations, how you deal with pressure, how you come back from those hurdles.

"It's been a long and winding road, but I just feel like I've learned so much. It's been a massive education for me.

"I go nowhere near that [social media comments] now. That was a lesson I learned pretty early on.

"You jump online, you're going to see some stuff that isn't nice reading. That's just the way it is in the world we live in now.

"What matters is the fans who turn up every week to watch us, the coach, my family, my team-mates. They're the people I do it for."

Steward says his mother finds it too hard to watch him leap for high balls. It is an area which is more fiercely contested than ever, with a law tweak having made it easier for chasing players to get into the same airspace and battle for possession.

Steward is treated on the pitch during his debut against the United States in 2021

"It is scary. I won't be afraid to admit it," said Steward. "No, it's terrifying. The ball goes up and it is pretty much now a complete free-for-all. You've just got to launch yourself up in the air, try and bring it down and, unfortunately, you don't have time to look at everyone else.

"More often than not you go for the ball and you end up with a knee in your ribs or your chest.

"It's scary, but it's good fun.

"My dad loves it. Mum, not so much. She's got her hands over her eyes. Every time I go down [on the floor] I honestly try and get up as quickly as possible and show it's not serious, because I know Mum is probably in the stand panicking if I'm down for any longer than about five or 10 seconds.

"I'll try and do that for her, bless her."

England and New Zealand have been paired for the final weekend of autumn internationals after finishing second in the Nations Championship's northern and southern hemisphere standings respectively.

After wins for Italy and Ireland on the Friday, the northern hemisphere are 2-0 in the Ryder Cup-style race for the inaugural hemisphere shield. France and South Africa will play on Sunday afternoon for the individual team title.
