It has been recently reported that Aaron Wainwright and Josh Adams both missed the historic win over the All Blacks

Wales have made four changes to the side that made history against New Zealand for the Nations Championship fixture against Australia.

Head coach Steve Tandy has tinkered with the starting line-up that beat the All Blacks for the first time in 73 years at Principality Stadium.

Wales go with the same XV that opened the Nations Championship with a 28-21 victory against Japan.

Alex Mann comes in for James Botham at blind-side flanker, Aaron Wainwright is back from a leg injury at number eight, lock Ben Carter returns for the injured Adam Beard and Josh Adams gets the nod on the wing.

Botham was man of the match against New Zealand but is not included after a huge defensive effort.

His partner is expecting their first child this week, but Wales say that is not the reason for the flanker sitting out the Wallabies game.

Exeter flanker Kane James is set to win his second cap from the bench after making his debut against Argentina in July.

Scarlets centre or fly-half Joe Hawkins also comes in for Ospreys playmaker Dan Edwards, who suffered an ankle injury in training that has led to Cardiff's Jarrod Evans being called into the Nations Championship squad.

Taine Plumtree will cover the second row in the absence of Beard, who suffered a shoulder injury against New Zealand.

Number eight Taine Basham and wing Gabriel Hamer-Webb drop out of the matchday squad after starting against the All Blacks.

Tandy says he had always intended to make changes.

"We made nearly 350 tackles, the ball in play time was one of the highest in Test rugby in the last three years and we have got competition," said the Wales head coach.

"The plan was always to freshen up and we are likely to do similar for the week after, because we need to find out about players.

"We have fresh and hungry players coming in, which will add the energy that we need at the weekend.

"The training environment is getting more competitive in certain positions we have the ability to put people in and feel confident about performance."

Eleven players will have started all three Tests - eight from English clubs and the Scarlets trio of full-back Blair Murray, centre Eddie James and fly-half Sam Costelow.

Wing Adams will win his 75th cap and returns after being left out against New Zealand despite scoring twice against Japan.

The 31-year-old Cardiff back has shown no signs of disappointment according to Tandy, despite missing out on history.

"He is a class rugby player but also a class man," he said. 

"He is a big leader and is over the moon for the group. You saw him driving standards on the Tuesday and he knows where we need to go and how we need to keep building.

"He is an incredible person and did really well against Japan, but we need to have competition, to grow and keep pushing."

After beating Japan and the All Blacks, Wales are looking to complete a Cardiff hat-trick against the Wallabies.

They then travel to Twickenham's Allianz Stadium for finals weekend when they will play a southern hemisphere opponent, which could well be another fixture against the Japanese.

Tandy said: "It's our last home game of this competition. The support that fans give to the players and staff has been incredible and we can't wait to get back out at Principality Stadium."

The Welsh Rugby Union (WRU) has stated it has already sold more than 60,000 tickets for the fixture at Principality Stadium, which holds just under 74,000.

That figure exceeds the 55,152 that witnessed history against the All Blacks, despite the late kick-off time against the Wallabies.

Exeter's Tom Hooper has been named in the second row for Australia while flanker Carlo Tizzano starts in five forward changes from the team that lost to Scotland.

The front rows rotate roles for this Test with Isaac Kailea, Brandon Paenga-Amosa and Taniela Tupou selected as the starting combination, with Angus Bell, Billy Pollard and Allan Alaalatoa the replacements.

Lock Jeremy Williams and Fraser McReight are also on the bench as Australia contend with a six-day turnaround.

Wallabies head coach Les Kiss said: "We know we need to step up against a Welsh team full of confidence and momentum after their historic win against the All Blacks last weekend.

"Pulling on the gold jersey against Wales at Principality Stadium in Cardiff creates one of the great atmospheres in world rugby and we are focused on being up for the occasion."
