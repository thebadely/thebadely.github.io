London City Lionesses forward Izzy Goodwin has been named in the England squad for the first time for the World Cup qualifying play-off against Ukraine.

The 23-year-old has been in great form this month and scored in front of England manager Sarina Wiegman at Emirates Stadium on Sunday in a 1-1 draw with Arsenal.

"We have always seen her as a huge talent with potential," Wiegman told BBC Radio 5 Live. 

"I think London City are developing really well in the games they have showed. She is tall and can head a ball attacking-wise, but also defending. 

"She is really quick with her feet and can score goals. I really want to see her in our environment, what she can bring and how close she is to the level that we expect."

There is also a first call-up for goalkeeper Katie Cox, on loan at Nottingham Forest from Chelsea, while Manchester United midfielder Ella Toone returns.

Manchester City duo Niamh Charles and Alex Greenwood, who is injured, are among those to miss out.

The Lionesses play Ukraine in Spain on 1 December before hosting them at Wembley on 5 December. The winner qualifies for next year's Women's World Cup.

"We know what's at stake in these games and our focus is on producing two strong performances to secure our place at the World Cup," said Wiegman.

"We have worked hard to put ourselves in this position and now we have to finish the job. 

"Ukraine will provide a challenge and we will prepare with the same focus and determination that we always do."

Chelsea defender Lucy Bronze could make her 150th appearance at Wembley in the second leg.

England travel to Pinatar, Spain a few days before the first leg and opted not to play a friendly match during this extended international break.

"The first part of the block we are [at St George's Park]. We have training sessions and a period of time where we can do some tactical stuff," added Wiegman. 

"We also selected more players so that helps us create situations where we can train things and everyone is important. I'm really excited to get ready for these games."

Sarina Wiegman led England to victory at Euro 2022 and Euro 2025

Wiegman was speaking to media at St George's Park for the first time since signing a new contract that will run until the end of Euro 2029.

It means she could oversee England's fortunes at next year's World Cup if they beat Ukraine, as well as heading up a Team GB squad at the 2028 Olympic Games.

Asked why she wanted to extend her stay, Wiegman told BBC Radio 5 Live: "Well, first of all the FA is happy with me and I'm really happy with them.

"I'm really grateful for that environment. That is one [reason]. Secondly, I still feel a great connection with staff and players, and of course the country!

"Then we have the challenges ahead. The major tournaments always motivate me. You don't get many moments with the team because it's not like it is at a club.

"In a tournament, you have those moments where you are really together, you can get somewhere with the group and that really excites me. 

"Yes, the Olympics would be really great. Next year it's the World Cup, then it's the Olympics, then it's the Euros â that journey ahead really excites me."

Wiegman's contract renewal was kept under wraps and it came as a surprise following their two-legged victory over Greece in October. 

The Dutchwoman, who has guided England to successive European titles, said conversations had been ongoing for a while but she had not planned ahead.

"When I signed that first contract in 2020 to join in 2021, it was a four-year contract and I thought, 'ok, this is a long contract, it would be really great if things go well'.

"We all got so excited so I signed another contract until 2027 and now I have until 2029. That is eight years. I never expected that. I also never planned so far ahead. 

"I want to feel that I can contribute to the environment and the development of things. I also want that from the other party. You want it to fit and for it to work.

"That decision came over time. At one point you think: 'Yeah, I'm getting really excited again.' That is what you want. To feel the energy, to feel the excitement.

"I still think we have things we can improve. We know the game is improving so quickly so we have to adapt all the time. When you stand still, you are lost."

Wiegman is also determined to improve as a coach and has enjoyed overseeing the development of younger talent as the Lionesses transition.

"It starts with me and about how I am going to develop. I keep learning things about myself too. You need to know yourself really well to do this job," she added.

"The staff and the players will always challenge me in the right ways. It goes back and forth. That is what I want. I really want to improve myself all the time."
