Experts revealed that The Briton scouted while watching a friend, now on the brink of the NFL

When medical student Emmanuel Oyebadejo went to watch his friend's American football game, he had no idea he would end up being scouted himself for a sport he had never even played.

He wasn't even interested in it. In fact, while still at school in Manchester he'd delivered a speech titled 'I have a dream' in which he outlined plans to do "very well in basketball, return from the NBA and be the prime minister of the United Kingdom".

But now, four years later, he is being tipped to become the first Briton to be a first-round pick in the NFL Draft.

It is a rapid rise for the 22-year-old, who had to learn the game from scratch and win over his parents who "begged" him not to play and instead focus on his medical studies.

His biggest challenge at the start, though, was finding a pair of boots big enough for his size 16 feet.

Emmanuel Oyebadejo was dreaming of a basketball career before swapping sports

It was 6ft 6in Oyebadejo's physique that had caught the eye of the coaching staff at Exeter Demons when he was a spectator for his friend's university game that was taking place at the club's home ground, The Crumb.

"I was talking to one of the other coaches, Jack Mullins, and we sort of turned around [and thought] 'that guy's freaking huge! We should go talk to him'," Lydon Ward-Best, one of the Demons' coaches, tells BBC Sport.

Oyebadejo had been a talented basketball player, playing for Manchester Magic - one of England's top junior clubs - from the age of 11 to 18. But by the time he was at the University of Exeter he was no longer playing competitively.

Lydon Ward-Best persuaded him to give a new sport a go.

"He started coming to training and just had the most ridiculous intro to American football I think I've ever seen," the coach adds.

A straight-A pupil, who had also learned the piano, guitar and drums while studying medicine, Oyebadejo had always been a quick learner.

"I always say that American football is kind of like violent chess," says Mullins, the Demons' current head coach.

"[Emmanuel] got the violent bit pretty quickly. He was fine at that - 'I am much bigger than you, watch me go'.

"The chess bit took a little longer because there's all these things that you have to think about."

But as Ward-Best puts it, "he's not just a big sort of jock athlete, he's a smart guy. He is so dedicated and disciplined with everything."

Emmanuel Oyebadejo had to win over dad Oladini (left), who said of his American football interest: "I don't like this game, I will not support it. I will not back you because it's dangerous. It's risky. Why would anybody want to do it?"

Oyebadejo played for the Demons at the end of the 2022-23 season, before the team went unbeaten in his first full season, 2023-24. When back home he also played for the Manchester Titans and helped them win the Britbowl in September 2023.

By December 2023, he decided he wanted to take things further. Could he make it in the United States? Would college teams be interested if he sent them videos of his highlights?

Coach Ward-Best told Oyebadejo it would be "tough", adding: "You know the percentages are not on your side here, but you are 6ft 6in and 280 pounds (20 stone), so you've got a better chance than most.

"I wanted to be realistic with him," he adds. "But I said I'd do whatever I could to help get him out there."

Emmanuel's father Oladini says he thought it was a "joke" and that he did not support him playing this "dangerous" game, explaining: "He had never played this game before. He'd only done it for a few months, so why would anybody from America want him?"

But they did.

There was interest from several colleges and Oyebadejo ended up at Jacksonville State, where he would study biology and play under legendary college coach Rich Rodriguez, having managed to win his parents round thanks to the influence of an uncle who lived in Dallas and understood the sport.

Oyebadejo joined Florida at the start of this year

His first season was hit by injury but, despite coming up against players who have played American football since they were young children, his second was a big success.

As a defensive end, Oyebadejo's job is to get past the offensive linemen trying to block him and tackle the ball carrier or claim a sack - when a defensive player tackles the quarterback behind the line of scrimmage.

He recorded 4.5 sacks, forced two fumbles and had 41 total tackles during the 2025 season.

Now the big college teams were paying attention.

College football is America's most passionate sporting pastime. While the NFL plays on Sundays, the college game dominates Saturdays during the autumn months.

And Oyebadejo transferred to the University of Florida to play for one of college football's biggest teams in the most prestigious conference, the Southeastern (SEC).

The Florida Gators are three-time national champions, have about 50 former players currently in the NFL and get home crowds of 90,000 at 'The Swamp'.

Oyebadejo was undaunted as he burst on to the scene. He had 2.5 sacks in a win at rivals Auburn in September and followed that up by impressing in big wins over Mississippi and Texas, the latter in front of 103,989 people.

After barely being on the radar of NFL scouts at the start of the season, Oyebadejo's form has propelled him into contention to be an early-round draft pick in 2027.

NFL teams do not have youth systems or academies. Almost all players have come through the US college system and they are recruited via the NFL Draft when they graduate.

One of the biggest events in the American sporting calendar, the NFL Draft takes place over three days in April, with the 32 teams taking turns to select players across seven rounds.

First-round picks in April will be guaranteed a rookie contract of at least $19.1m (Â£14.4m) and they are often fast-tracked into starting roles. The contract values decrease in a sliding scale so, for example, a third-round pick (top 96) will be guaranteed $7.3m (Â£5.5m).

Among the latest prospects, ESPN's veteran draft analyst Mel Kiper has ranked Oyebadejo as the fifth-best edge rusher (defensive end). That is regarded as the sport's second-most important position behind quarterback - and therefore the second-best paid in the NFL.

The reason is that Oyebadejo has everything needed to be a starting defensive end in the NFL. Firstly, his height and weight are ideally suited for the grappling battle required in what is known as 'the trenches'.

He has very long arms (7ft wingspan), perfect for winning a wrestling match against the kind of enormous opponent he faces, and unusually for a man as big as him, he's extremely quick. That's the money-making trait.

Although he now weighs about 300lbs (more than 21 stone), it is believed he has a remarkable body-fat percentage of about 12%. All his measurables are seen as ideal for a defensive end, if not better.

There are simply very few people with Oyebadejo's physical talent, and given the fast-tracked nature of his rise in American football, he does not look as raw as you might expect.

"He's British and has only played the sport for three [full] seasons," says his mentor and former coach Ward-Best. "You'd therefore think he must be very green, but he doesn't look that way.

"The physical qualities are clearly there but he's worked a lot on mastering aspects of the position as well."

Menelik Watson is the highest-drafted British player, having been picked in the second round in 2013

Menelik Watson, also from Manchester, holds the record for being the highest-drafted British player. He was the 42nd pick in 2013 and spent six years in the NFL, earning a total of $16.4m (Â£13.7m).

London-born Osi Umenyiora, the 56th pick in 2003, enjoyed an illustrious 12-year career - winning two Super Bowls with the New York Giants.

"I have spoken to Osi, he's a really cool guy," says Oyebadejo, who is also of Nigerian heritage.

"To be a first-round pick out of the UK, it would be amazing. It would do so much for the American football community back home."

Oyebadejo impressed again in an upset win at Kentucky on Saturday and has 4.5 sacks with two regular-season games left. Florida could then play in a 'bowl' game, an end-of-season exhibition.

After that, Oyebadejo is expected to participate in a physical-testing event called the 'scouting combine', where he will be assessed by representatives of all 32 teams in March.

The draft then begins in Washington DC on 27 April, and if Oyebadejo hears his name called, he will complete an unlikely journey and make his now fully supportive parents very proud.

"It's mad," says Ward-Best. "I've been doing this for 20 years and I've never seen anything like this. We almost have to pinch ourselves."

Oyebadejo has stayed in touch with his former coaches in Exeter. The Demons hold watch parties for Florida games and there is a real sense of pride over where his journey began.

"It was just complete chance that he wanted to wander up to The Crumb and watch a game, just being there for his mate," says coach Mullins.

"If those things didn't happen, we wouldn't be having this conversation. He would be four years into his medical degree."
