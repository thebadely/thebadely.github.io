Let's dive into the details: Luke Littler is aiming to become just the third back-to-back winner of the World Matchplay

Defending champion Luke Littler strolled into the World Matchplay final with a 17-5 demolition of unseeded Dirk van Duijvenbode in Blackpool.

The world number one raced in an 8-2 advantage and was averaging 117.10 and on course to beat Phil Taylor's tournament match average record of 114.99

Littler admitted afterwards that distracted him and his level did drop slightly as he finished with an average just below 111. 

However, that leaves him well placed to beat Taylor's tournament average of 106.31, with Littler's currently at 110.88.

Littler has averaged more than 109 in all four matches in this year's tournament as he looks to join Taylor and Michael van Gerwen as the only back-to-back winners of the Matchplay.

The run is also continuing his sublime form in longer-format matches - this win extending the streak to 28 successive wins in games with 20 or more legs. 

His last defeat over a match of that duration was when he burst on to the scene in the 2024 World Championship and lost to Luke Humphries in the final.

That gives an indication of the challenge that Price will face on Sunday in his second Matchplay final. 

The pair have a bit of rivalry with Price's on-stage roars and antics often provoking Littler into doing similar, but the teenager denied that Price can get "under his skin".

Earlier in the week, Price said that some players can "make it too easy" for Littler. 

Despite their battles, Littler has won the past nine encounters between the pair, with Price's last success in their opening meeting of 2026. 

The Welshman has never beat Littler in a game that has contained more than 15 legs so will have to reach new heights in the best-of-35 final. 

Speaking to Sky Sports, Littler said: "Me and Gez always have great games. Gez will want to try and get me into a roaring battle. As long as I can get into the lead that will be the thing."

Price broke in the opening leg of the game and charged into a 4-1 lead, before a scrappy run of legs saw both players break throw regularly to leave Price leading 11-9 at the final mini-break. 

From there, the seventh seed powered on and stretched away from Van Veen in scoring and finishing to seal a comfortable victory. 

Both players missed 23 darts at a double across the match, while Van Veen was broken nine times in 14 legs throwing first on what was a difficult night for the world number three.

The victory sees Price climb to fourth in the order of merit, above Jonny Clayton, and in turn becomes Welsh number one. 

Price told Sky Sports he was "happy" to reach the final, but "getting one up" on good friend Clayton was his main focus going into the tie.
