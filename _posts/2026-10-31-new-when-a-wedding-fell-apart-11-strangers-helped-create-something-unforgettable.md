It has been recently reported that Eleven strangers from across the world travelled to Northern Ireland to celebrate Bridget's 'non-wedding'

When her fiance suffered a medical emergency and their planned elopement and relationship fell apart, she could have quietly drawn a line under the chapter.

Instead, Bridget Devaney turned to the internet with a proposal that sounded equal parts hopeful and improbable - come to Northern Ireland and celebrate the wedding that never happened.

What followed was a story that has resonated worldwide as 11 strangers answered the call and helped transform loss and heartbreak into an unforgettable week of connection, companionship and adventure.

For Bridget and her bevy of 'non-bridesmaids', the experience was a refreshing reminder that community is all around, "if you want to find it", she said.

Once associated with secretive romances and hurried ceremonies, elopements have evolved into destination weddings set against spectacular backdrops. 

An increasing number of couples are choosing intimate celebrations as the way they begin married life, with many crossing the Atlantic to exchange their vows against the rugged Irish coastline.

Now based in Killarney, County Kerry, Bridget says she chose for the photoshoot to be a 'reframing day'

Following her separation, Bridget - who is based between the US and Killarney in the Republic of Ireland - said she was "left reeling".

It wasn't until April of this year, when a photographer emailed to check in on her postponed wedding plans, that she realised she had to make a decision.

Spurred on by a friend, who suggested that she should advertise a female-only group trip on her anonymous social media profile, Bridget soon found herself overwhelmed by hundreds of inquiries. 

"When these women signed up for this trip, they didn't know my name. They didn't know what I looked like. They didn't know anything about me," she told BBC News NI.

Janae Pettit, a design consultant from Utah, came across the invitation on 1 April.

"It was April Fool's Day, but Bridget was like, 'this is not a joke!'," she recalled.

"All the stranger danger we had learned growing up was just out the window."

Janae (bottom left wearing a hat) says she initially thought the post was an April Fool's joke

Over the course of a few months, Bridget responded to questionnaires and set about organising the trip with women from the US, Canada and Wales offering to join her in Northern Ireland.

Each had their own reasons for wanting to join.

Janae said she discovered Bridget's post while approaching her 40th birthday and struggling with feelings she had not expected.

"I thought, this is a birthday present to myself. I'm going to go."

What began as a chance to travel soon evolved into something more meaningful.

"I wanted to go and meet these women and support this non-bride. I wanted to do something outside of myself," she said.

"I was sad because I was like, nobody cares about me. I'm unimportant. I'm finally entering middle age and I should do something for me."

The group are now planning a reunion and say they are 'lifelong friends'

For Shana Johnson, from Minnesota, the trip answered a different need.

Many of her friends were now focused on raising families and travelling with partners and children. When she saw Bridget's invitation online, she recognised an opportunity.

"I love to travel, I thought, 'what the heck? Let's see what this is all about'."

"I feel like we got something very special out of this trip," says Bridget

Earlier this month, the group of women spent a week exploring the north coast, visiting the Giant's Causeway, the Dark Hedges and Londonderry.

Although many of the women had met online beforehand, seeing one another in person was different.

"I was so nervous. I [usually] can't remember anybody's face and name," Janae said.

Epic Love Photography founder Rob Dight, who has spent more than a decade photographing couples escaping the pressures of traditional weddings, said the day was unlike anything he had encountered before. 

With the deposit already paid for a photoshoot in 2024, Dight offered Bridget a two-year extension after learning that her fiance had been injured.

"Just me and a bunch of friends are coming over now", was how the project was later pitched, he said.

As the photoshoot progressed, he noted a deep sense of connection between the women and was "blown away" to learn, mid-shoot, that they had met just three days earlier.

"It was kind of a 'jaw-on-the-floor scenario'... It was just so wholesome," he said.

"Something clicked into place in my mind, like: 'Wow, this is really special'. 

"I just did what I could on my end to encourage it, to let them have that time to shine and to celebrate being with each other."

"There was such a level of uplift, like I could turn anywhere and get a hug if I needed it," Bridget said, recalling the photoshoot.

"Everybody was there for everybody that day."

Later in the week, the women took part in a wellness session focused on new beginnings, an experience Bridget described as particularly meaningful for the group.

"There was not a dry eye in the room," she said. 

Weeks after returning home, the group's messages continue to flow, with future trips in the pipeline.
