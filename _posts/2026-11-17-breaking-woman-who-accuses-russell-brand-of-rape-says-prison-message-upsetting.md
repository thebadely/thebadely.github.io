A woman who has made allegations against Russell Brand has said she finds it "upsetting" and "really shocking" that he was able to claim he was innocent from prison, following his conviction for rape and sexual assault.

Speaking after the comedian's wife Laura read out a message from him on their Sunday Service show on YouTube, Mia Latter told BBC Radio 4's Today programme she found it "appalling" to hear "this Christian message alongside absolute lies". 

"I just felt for those women that stood trial and everything they went through," she said.

Latter, 37, who has alleged she was raped by Brand when she was 16, was not a complainant in his recent criminal trial and he denies her claims.

Brand is currently serving a 10-and-half-year prison sentence after being found guilty at Southwark Crown Court of one count of rape and three counts of sexual assault, involving four women.

In recent years he has positioned himself as a Christian influencer, and has a large following, including in the US. In the message read out by his wife on their show, Brand said: "I am not guilty of the offences I have been jailed for."

Latter said the broadcast "was really shocking to me... it was appalling, frankly, it was upsetting to watch".

She said the woman who gave evidence in the trial "got their verdict, and for him to say, 'I'm innocent, I'm not guilty' - well, you are, you're a convicted rapist now. There's no arguing with that".

Downing Street said on Monday it expects action to be taken if Brand is found to have broken prison rules.

Justice Secretary Alex Norris said that "prisoners are rightly banned from making statements that cause distress to victims".

Latter said she was 16 when she met Brand, who was 30 at the time. 

She was one of six women who made allegations against him as part of a Times, Sunday Times and Channel 4's Dispatches investigation in 2023. None of the women were involved in his recent trial.

In an interview with the Sunday Times this week, Latter said Brand told her to keep their relationship secret from her school friends and claimed that he referred to her as "the child" in front of his entourage.

She has waived her right to anonymity in order to campaign for a staggered age of consent to be introduced in the UK that would make sexual relationships between 16 and 17 year-olds and adults illegal. 

Latter told the Sunday Times the Crown Prosecution Service decided not to include her as part of Brand's criminal trial but police wanted her to be part of "subsequent" proceedings. She told the paper that she would not continue with the criminal process so she could focus on her campaign.

Latter told Today a staggered age of consent could have stopped her relationship with Brand "dead in its tracks".

She said: "It would have 100% made a difference because my parents tried to stop the relationship.

"They tried to prevent me from seeing him...  But if it had been a criminal offence for him to be having a relationship with me, none of the subsequent abuse would have ever happened because they would have been able to stop the relationship dead in its tracks."

Latter also told Today that she and the complainants in Brand's trial had been accused of being "money hungry".

She said she had initially been reluctant to speak out because "I knew that the audience that he'd cultivated over the past few years would come for me".

"It's a criminal trial, there's no money.

"Me talking to the Times, me talking to you, me doing any interviews, there's no money, there's not a penny. We're just doing this in the pursuit of justice." 

But Latter said Brand had been "monetising all these platforms" during his trial by continuing to make videos that appeared with advertisements on social media.
