Prem reluctant to commit on Anglo-Welsh league

The Prem has consisted of 10 clubs since the 2022-23 season, when Wasps, Worcester and London Irish all folded within the space of a few months.

Promotion and relegation to and from the league was also scrapped as of this season.

Previous conditions said teams would need to compete in the second-tier Champ for a season before being eligible to play in the Prem, and that any bids would be assessed on a range of criteria including finances, investment potential, infrastructure and how their geographical base could help spread the Prem's appeal.

Earlier this month RFU chief executive Bill Sweeney confirmed the WRU had asked to join the Prem and form an Anglo-Welsh league.

Wales currently has four professional sides - Scarlets, Ospreys, Cardiff and Dragons - who play in the United Rugby Championship (URC), although the WRU plans to cut one of the teams by 2028 because of financial issues.

A statement from Prem Rugby and the RFU in October said that the focus was on expanding the league with English clubs although Sweeney confirmed just over a week later that they had been approached by the WRU and that discussions would continue.

The URC has so far not commented on one of its shareholders courting another competition.

The WRU has also not publicly responded to Sweeney's comments about its approach to the RFU but will face questions about the issue at the AGM.
