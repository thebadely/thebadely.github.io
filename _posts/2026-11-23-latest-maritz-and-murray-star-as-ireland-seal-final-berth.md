Let's dive into the details: Maritz scored her first international T20 half-century in Ireland's win

ICC Women's Emerging Nations Trophy, Ajman

Ireland 162-3 (20 overs): L Maritz 57*, A Hunter 46; S Siegers 2-24

Netherlands 118 (19.3 overs): P Molkenboer 46; C Murray 5-17

Ireland Women won by 44 runs

Scorecard

Ireland are through to the final of the Emerging Nations Trophy in Ajman with a 44-run victory over the Netherlands.

Leg-spinner Cara Murray claimed her first T20 five-wicket haul, while player of the match Lara Maritz hit a maiden international half-century as Ireland booked a meeting with Sri Lanka in Wednesday's final.

While the Irish had a mixed start to the tournament, losing two of their four games including an opening loss to their final opponents, they recovered well in the Super Sixes to advance.

"It was a brilliant win," said team captain Gaby Lewis.

"It was always touch and go coming in here as T20 can be a crazy game, especially this tournament.

"I'm really proud of the performance today. Everyone stood up. We're on a bit of a streak now, so here's to one more."

Netherlands won the toss and opted to put Ireland into bat.

The opening partnership of Lewis and Amy Hunter got off to a fine start with 61 runs from 42 balls before Lewis was caught by Robine Rijke from Caroline de Lange's delivery at the end of the seventh over.

It failed to slow the Irish attack with Maritz stepping in to deliver 57 not out with five boundaries including one six.

While Hunter exited on 46 in the 14th over after her partnership with Maritz had amassed 52, Ireland were building a healthy tally and, while Louise Little was out for a duck, Abbi Harrison chipped in with 16 not out as the Irish completed their 20 overs on 162-3.

The Dutch response got off to a shaky start with Aimee Maguire removing Sanya Khuana for one, while Kia McCartney took the first of her two wickets with Sterre Kalis gone for eight.

Netherlands appeared to have steadied the ship as opener Phebe Molkenboer struck six boundaries on her way to a tally of 46, but Murray would announce herself with the first of her wickets.

The Belfast-born leg-spinner ripped through the Dutch middle order with five wickets for just 17 runs from 24 balls, helping the Irish end the Dutch challenge on just 118 to book their place in the final via a second place finish in the Super Sixes standings.
