Let's dive into the details: Katie Taylor won Olympic gold in 2012

Katie Taylor's career was soaked in blood, tears and triumph.

The Irishwoman has stepped away from the sport aged 40 after 27 fights and 26 wins after a memorable night at Dublin's Croke Park.

Making her amateur debut in 2001, Taylor won every medal and title she could have  and more importantly had a lasting effect on women's boxing and women as athletes.

BBC Sport takes a look back on some of the best pictures from her iconic career.

Taylor helped legalise women boxing in Ireland, competing in the first sanctioned female fight in 2001 aged 15

Taylor won five world championships as an amateur including in 2012 when she also won Olympic gold in London

Her father Peter, who had been her long-time coach, was there to celebrate with her

Taylor is one of 15 Irish athletes to win gold at an Olympics in any sport and was the only Irish gold medallist in 2012

She returned in 2016 but suffered a shock exit in the quarter-finals

Taylor quickly moved on, convincing Matchroom promoter Eddie Hearn to sign her as a professional. Taylor was the first female fighter Hearn signed

She made a winning debut at Wembley Arena in November 2016, which was packed with Irish fans

She took just 11 months to claim her first world title - the WBA lightweight belt 

In her bid to become Ireland's first four-belt world champion at Madison Square Garden in 2019, she was almost beaten by Delfine Persoon

The wounds of war were obvious on Taylor's face after the battle with Persoon

Taylor returned to MSG in 2022, this time to face Amanda Serrano. Years of talks finally bore fruit as the rivals finally stepped in the ring together at a sold-out MSG. It was the first time two women had headlined a boxing fight at the iconic New York venue

A 10-round classic could have been very different for Taylor, had she not stayed on her feet and edged a split decision points win

Taylor and her mother Bridget, a constant figure by her side, shared a moment backstage at MSG

After nine years without a fight in Ireland, Taylor finally got her chance in front of her home fans in November 2024

A hugely emotional night turned into disaster as England's Chantelle Cameron produced a masterclass to strip Taylor of her undefeated record

But from devastation...

...to joy six months later

Taylor outpointed Cameron in rematch in Dublin to become a two-weight undisputed champion

Taylor has had countless famous visitors to her dressing room down the years

Taylor went back on the road to fight Serrano again in 2024, this time in front of 60,000 people in Texas and live on Netflix

The rivals produced another classic decided on the smallest of margins as Taylor prevailed 

Despite beating Serrano twice, Taylor agreed to a trilogy at MSG...

...and closed out the trilogy with a clean sweep

Taylor was on top of the world as undisputed light-welterweight champion and looked ready to sail off into retirement...

...but there was one itch left to scratch - Dublin's iconic Croke Park

Taylor soaked up every moment of her final fight week and made Clodagh Keating's dreams come true when she invited the 10-year-old to join her for some boxing drills at the public workout

Taylor made her final ringwalk to over 80,000 at Croke Park

Bridget Taylor's smile beamed from ear-to-ear as she added another WBC belt to the collection following Katie Taylor's unanimous decision win over Flora Pili 

That's all she wrote. Taylor laid her gloves down in the centre of the Croke Park ring to bring the curtain down on her illustrious career
