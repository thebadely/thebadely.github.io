She is already one of the most decorated fighters in British boxing history, and now Wales' Lauren Price is set to headline a summer stadium show at Cardiff City Stadium or Cardiff Arms Park.

Price dominated Michaela Kotaskova in Cardiff on Friday night, winning every minute of every round as she became Wales' first unified boxing champion by locking up the welterweight division.

With her stack of titles added to an Olympic gold medal, Price is now set to get to the next level, with negotiations already progressing for a stadium contest in the summer, with Chantelle Cameron or Mikaela Mayer â who fight on Christmas Eve â among the list of possible opponents.

The 32-year old Welsh fighter was so dominant in unifying the division on Friday it prompted her usually reserved trainer, Rob McCracken, to name her as the world's best female fighter, and now Price is set for a bumper 2027.

"If we can bring the big names and the big fights to a stadium, it's going to make a massive difference to Wales and Cardiff and everyone involved, and Lauren is very passionate about that," Price's promoter Ben Shalom told BBC Sport Wales.

"She is unique in the attention that she can generate and therefore I think she can demand to make sure that the fights happen in Wales.

"A lot of eyes will be on [Chantelle] Cameron and [Mikaela] Mayer's fight, and that is a fight [the winner versus Price] that we'd love to make.

"That fight in May or June at Cardiff Rugby Club or Cardiff City Football Club, you know, you're doing 15-20,000 ticket sales with Lauren Price there, so fingers crossed."

BBC boxing expert Steve Bunce says a deal for a stadium fight in the summer has been agreed, with a mandatory defence likely to come first in February. France's Emilie Sonvico has been touted as a possible opponent.

"She has a sneaky fight in February and then an outdoor fight in May or June, at Cardiff City Stadium, that's done and dusted, that's going to happen," he told BBC Sport.

"It won't be Claressa Shields in the opposite corner, but it will be a big opponent in May or June outdoors in the Welsh sunshine."

Price feels she needs to move to bigger capacity venues to secure her legacy, despite already being one of the most decorated fighters in British boxing history.

"It's about now moving on to what's next, and there is talks that it could potentially be a stadium fight in the summer, and that's what it's about now, the legacy and the fan base growing," she explained.

"My team know I will fight anyone."
