A 52-year-old man has been sentenced to life in prison for the attempted murder of three children outside a primary school in Dublin's Parnell Square in November 2023.

Riad Bouchaker, of no fixed address, was also found guilty of assault causing serious harm to creche worker Leanne Flynn, and of assaulting two other children and a teenager.

On Monday, Judge Tony Hunt imposed the overall sentence of life imprisonment in Dublin's Central Criminal Court.

Rioting across many parts of Dublin following the attack led to the deployment of the most riot police in Irish history.

Bouchaker was originally from Algeria but had lived in Ireland for 25 years prior to the attack, and he had Irish citizenship.

He was assisted by an Arabic interpreter and a speech-and-language expert during the trial, who were present in court for the sentencing.

A group of 13 children, aged five and six, were lined up on Parnell Square waiting to go to their afterschool provision. Childcare worker Leanne Flynn was helping a little boy zip up his jacket when Bouchaker pulled his knife on the children.

A five-year-old girl was left with a lifelong brain injury after being stabbed in the heart.

Now eight, she is non-verbal and can only communicate by blinking, uses a wheelchair and has to be fed through a tube in her stomach as she cannot swallow safely by herself.

The judge said the child will never live independently and that her life, and the lives of her family, had been permanently altered.

He also noted that Flynn suffered life-threatening injuries, including a collapsed lung and the loss of a spleen, as well as profound psychological consequences and her services not being available to children.

The judge said she had acted fearlessly to protect the children and hoped she could return to work in the future, but said the possibility of such remains an unanswered question.

Judge Hunt said this was an unprecedented, highly unusual case involving a sudden, ferocious, sustained attack on young children and their childcare worker.

He said he had to mark the exceptional gravity of the offending against very young children and their carer, with a lethal weapon leading to consequences of the utmost gravity.

He said the circumstances were uniquely horrific and marked a profound assault on the most fundamental values protected by the criminal law.

The judge said the children happened to be selected because they were there and he was not yet sure what inference could be drawn from how Bouchaker found himself at their collection point.

At an earlier hearing, the court heard he received brain surgery in 2021 and a portion of his skull was removed, leaving a part of his brain vulnerable.

He suffered a head injury when members of the public intervened during the Parnell Square attack, after which he received hospital treatment for a month.

His defence team, which the judge ordered cannot be named due to safety concerns, said Bouchaker's cognitive function, memory, concentration, communication and decision-making were affected as a result of the impairment.

The most riot police in Irish state history were deployed to deal with the rioting following the attack

The attack was followed by riots across much of Dublin.

The most riot police in Irish state history were deployed to deal with the unrest.

Within hours of the attack, rioters destroyed 11 police vehicles, while 13 shops were badly damaged and more were looted during clashes with riot police.

Three buses and a tram were also destroyed and several police officers were injured during three hours of sustained violence.

Ireland's police chief at the time, Drew Harris blamed the rioting on a "lunatic, hooligan faction driven by a far-right ideology".
