Why are ICE agents getting controversial shock gloves?

US Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) officers may soon be equipped with gloves that can deliver  electric shocks as they carry out their law enforcement duties.

ICE plans to spend up to $20m (Â£14.8m) to buy the Generated Low Output Voltage Emitter (GLOVE), according to a note published on Monday by the Department of Homeland Security (DHS).

The gloves can deliver up to 380 volts of electricity, roughly three times the charge of a standard US power outlet.

Civil rights groups, including the American Civil Liberties Union (ACLU), have expressed alarm over its use. ICE is under scrutiny for its tactics during enforcement activity, which have resulted in several fatal shootings by officers.

Officers and agents in Homeland Security Investigations (HSI) and Enforcement Removal Operations (ERO) would use the gloves, according to a DHS note

The DHS said in a statement to the BBC: "ICE is constantly assessing the needs of our officers in the field to ensure they have the tools and equipment necessary to safely arrest and remove criminal illegal aliens from our country."

Compliant Technologies LLC, which makes the gloves, told the BBC it was unable to comment on the sale. A no-bid purchase contract could be published as early as Friday, DHS said.

The GLOVE devices are activated by pushing a button to deliver shocks and can deliver up to 30 pulses per second. They also feature built-in microprocessors that keep a record of activation. Compliant Technologies states on its website that they do not cause injury and are used by many agencies.

A user manual on the company's website recommends avoiding use on the elderly, small children, pregnant women and the "severely handicapped", as well as that an additional officer be present to restrain the subject while the gloves are being used. 

The GLOVE features a button that is pushed to activate the shock feature

Unlike a taser, which uses sharp barbs in the skin to deliver a shock of up to 50,000 volts, the gloves do not complete an electrical circuit inside the body.

The gloves instead deliver a sharp shock to convince a subject to comply using "pain deterrence", says Lt Butch Gallion, an administrator at Hawkins County jail in Tennessee.

"We may or may not deploy them. It just depends on how they act. When you push the button to turn them on there's a light that displays. The inmates understand that light, and often just seeing the light brings compliance," he says.

According to the DHS note, GLOVE would be used by officers and agents in Homeland Security Investigations (HSI), which targets transnational criminal organisations, and Enforcement Removal Operations (ERO), which is responsible for arresting, detaining and deporting people who violate immigration laws in the US. 

Some law enforcement agencies are already using the gloves but it's believed that the planned ICE purchase would be the largest from Compliant Technologies to date.
