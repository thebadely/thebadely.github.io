Let's dive into the details: Scotland's Melanie Woods has won the Commonwealth Games women's 1500m T54 for the second time in under 40 hours after streaking to victory in the restaged final - however England's Ellis Kottas could not replicate her silver medal.

Woods set a new Games record to triumph in Glasgow on Wednesday evening, but it emerged on Thursday morning that Para-athletics authorities had decided the race should be held again following appeals surrounding a first-lap crash.

However, the Scot kept her composure in her home city to replicate her initial success, break the record again, and reclaim the gold that was already hers - in mind if not around her neck.

"I'm happy, I'm excited, but it feels like relief, to be honest," Woods told BBC Sport Scotland, after backing up the triumph with a win in her 400m heat 90 minutes later.

"It's created a bit of a storyline and I feel like people have been following for the plot, so I'm just happy to be able to do it again."

Kottas, though, finished fourth, having said she was "low-key traumatised" when it became known that the race result was overturned. However, she refused to cast blame after her joy was stolen away.

"If I had that original medal, with half the competitors wiped out, it wouldn't have been on my own merit. They just outraced me," she said.

"It's very mixed emotions. Obviously it's a slight disappointment but there's still a lot of excitement. There are no hard feelings in that sense."

Woods led from the start in her home city, although Noemi Alphonse of Mauritius and Australia's Eliza Ault-Connell kept her honest over the first couple of laps. 

The latter had fallen away by the final 200m, though, and the Scot held off the Mauritian as she was roared by down the home straight by the crowd to finish in a time almost a second quicker than she did on Wednesday.

"It was in my mind 'what have I got left?' and you do question what you've got in your arms," she said. 

"But I just needed to be confident I could stay out in front and it was nice coming down the home straight and not seeing her wheel moving in front of me."

'I wasn't really able to celebrate fully' - Woods

Woods - who has used a wheelchair since being hit by a car while riding her bike in 2018 - had celebrated her success at Scotstoun swathed in a Saltire on Wednesday.

It was not until she left the track, heard the chatter and saw people scurrying into the officials' office that she realised there might be an issue and went "into self-protection mode".

Uncertainly cloaked the rest of the evening until it was decreed around midnight that she would have to go and win gold all over again.

"There was obviously a bit of disappointment and I wasn't able to celebrate fully," she added. "It definitely dampened the excitement but I just had to park that and come out and do it again today. 

"I'm pretty good at compartmentalising and trying not to ride the wave of the ups and the downs. I'm no stranger to that and it's not something I've dealt with before."

Woods had conducted similar interviews on Wednesday, talking about being the gold medallist. So was she sure this time that the prize was definitely hers?

"If they call me back for a rerun, I'm not doing it..."
