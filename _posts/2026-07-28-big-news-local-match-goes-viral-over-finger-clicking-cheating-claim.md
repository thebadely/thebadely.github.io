An amateur cricket match has made global headlines after a fielder was accused of cheating by clicking his fingers to get a wicket.

The North Yorkshire and South Durham Cricket League has started an investigation into a second XI Division 2 game between Saltburn and Norton.

A viral video on social media appears to show a Saltburn fielder clicking his fingers as the ball passes Noman Shabir's bat before being caught by wicketkeeper Josh Bowes. Saltburn appeal and the umpire gives Shabir out.

It has been suggested that the finger-clicking was designed to trick the umpire into thinking the batter edged the ball.

Saltburn won by 159 runs and are top of Division 2 by 18 points.

"The league have received a formal complaint regarding alleged incidents in a Division Two game on Saturday," the league said in a statement on X.

"A full investigation has been initiated and until such time as the formal processes are complete no further comment will be made."

Last week Bowes took eight catches in Saltburn's 22-run win over Shildon Railway. The men's Test record for catches by a keeper in an innings is seven.

Bowes leads the league standings with 31 catches, 10 more than any other player.

Rory Cotterill, who captained Middlesbrough second XI during a 29-run defeat by Saltburn last month, responded to a social media post containing the video and alleged two further incidents of cheating by Saltburn in that match.

The original video of the incident has had more than five million views on X, with publications in Australia and New Zealand among those to report it.

BBC Sport understands a meeting between Saltburn, the league and legal representatives is due to take place on Tuesday night. 

Norton declined to comment.
