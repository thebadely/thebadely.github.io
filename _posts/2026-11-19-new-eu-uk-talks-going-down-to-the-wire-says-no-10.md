Let's dive into the details: Negotiations between the EU and UK are "going down to the wire" ahead of Friday's summit in Brussels, Downing Street has said. 

The summit is aimed at reaching new agreements between the two sides on food trade rules, visas for young people and carbon emissions pricing. 

The prime minister's spokesman said that if a deal is secured, the government will introduce legislation to Parliament next week to implement changes. 

Conservative leader Kemi Badenoch has accused Andy Burnham of "giving up the freedoms Britain voted for" in the 2016 Brexit referendum. 

Writing in the Daily Mail,, external she said: "The three central purposes of Brexit were control over our money, over our laws and over our borders. On each of those, Burnham is moving us backwards."

The No 10 spokesman said Brexit had done "more harm than good".

"The choice over the coming days is clear: stand with Boris Johnson and Kemi Badenoch defending a broken status quo, or with Andy Burnham delivering change, cutting costs for business, helping with the costs of the weekly shop and creating opportunities for young people," he added. 

One of the deals expected to be reached on Friday includes a new youth mobility scheme, which would make it easier for young people to work and travel in the EU and UK. 

The UK already has schemes in place with 13 non-EU countries including Australia, Canada and India. 

In 2024, the EU proposed allowing 18 to 30 year-olds to work or study in the EU and UK for up to four years.

Labour, which was then in opposition, rejected the deal arguing it amounted to reinstating freedom of movement. 

Since coming to government, the party has suggested it would consider a more restricted scheme with a time limit, a cap on the numbers coming to the UK and a ban on participants from claiming benefits.

The UK's request to limit numbers has proved to be a sticking point in the talks, along with the EU's request that EU students should pay the same tuition fees at UK universities as domestic students. 

An agreement on trade could also remove checks and paperwork faced by food companies exporting to the EU.
