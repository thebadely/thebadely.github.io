The Prince of Wales has expressed his sadness after the Diana Award, set up in memory of his mother, was placed into administration.

Prince William thanked "everyone whose dedication and hard work helped" encourage thousands of young people "over so many years".

A statement on the Diana Award website said it was announcing the news with "heavy hearts", but independent advice had "made clear" it could "no longer continue to meet its obligations".

"We are proud to have shone a spotlight on a generation of young people," said the charity, which was founded 27 years ago to support their development.

Diana, Princess of Wales, died in a car crash in Paris in 1997.

More than 50,000 young people have received the award - which recognises outstanding contributions to social action and humanitarian efforts.

The charity said it had supported thousands more "through programmes to power the impact created by these inspirational young leaders on the lives of those around them".

It was created "to honour Diana, Princess of Wales and her belief that young people had the power to change their community, their country and the world", the charity added.

The Duke of Sussex has also supported the charity.

A statement on the Diana Award website said of the news: "This is not the outcome any of us wanted.

"We thank everyone who has supported this charity over the years and, above all, the young people whose achievements have always been at the heart of it.

"Their achievements matter, and nothing about today's decision changes that."
