Spurs revival continues with victory over 10-man Crystal Palace

The long wait is over. After almost 21 months, Tottenham Hotspur have won three Premier League games in succession again.

Spurs cruised to victory on Saturday against Crystal Palace, who were reduced to 10 men after 13 minutes when striker Jean-Philippe Mateta was sent off for an ill-judged lunge at Micky van de Ven.

It means Tottenham have moved into the dizzy heights of the top half of the table.

Archie Gray and Jan Paul van Hecke scored as Spurs built on a platform established by their first league win of the season at home to Coventry City, then fortified by the outstanding victory at Chelsea - only Spurs' second league triumph at Stamford Bridge in 36 years.

But the prevailing mood inside the Tottenham Hotspur Stadium before kick-off against Palace, undoubtedly shaped by the past, was that the visit of a struggling side represented a banana skin rather than an opportunity.

Tottenham's players weren't having that, though. Not a bit of it. Instead, Spurs grasped gratefully at the helping hand offered by Mateta's recklessness to end up convincing winners.

Three straight wins represent riches indeed. The last time this happened for Tottenham was in February 2025, more than 600 days ago.

Three wins is as many as Spurs had in their previous 26 league games combined, in which they drew 10 and lost 13, going back to midway through last season.

And, if a further indicator was needed as to just how poor Spurs have been in recent times, this is the first occasion when they have recorded back-to-back home wins in the league for two years.

This latest sequence represents consistency of the sort that has eluded Spurs in recent years, the previous incarnation being the consistency of regular defeats and two successive 17th-place finishes in the Premier League.

Tottenham boss Roberto de Zerbi gives his post-match verdict

We are witnessing a significant mood shift at Spurs, reflected inside this magnificent arena since the psychological barrier of the vital first league win was crossed against Coventry.

Head coach Roberto de Zerbi told BBC Sport's Match Of The Day: "The passion we showed - we didn't lose the focus, the concentration. We made an incredible reaction when we lost the ball.

"We could have scored more goals, but we created many chances. We have to be more nasty, especially in the last third of the match, especially in the finishing. I'm happy because I knew before this would be a tough game."

It all left De Zerbi delighted as he talked with renewed optimism and ambition, saying: "Listen, we are Tottenham. We can't forget to be ambitious, to be hungry, to win games in a row, and to make our fans happy.

"At the same time, we can't forget two weeks ago where we have been in the table, to change the last two to three seasons of this club. Three games in a row is not enough. We have to keep this behaviour, this spirit. This condition to prepare every game in the same way.
