Here’s what you need to know: World Rugby could decide to introduce the smaller ball in the wider women's game in February 

World Rugby has extended the women's 4.5 ball trial for a second season after releasing data showing improvements in several areas of the game and receiving "overwhelmingly positive" feedback from players and coaches.

Data collected from the Sevens trials showed ruck speeds were on average 10% faster, while a four-year trend of declining passing rates was reversed. Goal kicking accuracy was unaffected.

The 4.5 size ball will be used in the 2026-27 HSBC SVNS Series and Championship, starting on 28 November in Dubai.

World Rugby confirmed that a decision on the 4.5 ball for the whole of the women's game would be taken in February, with data from the XVs game still being gathered.

The smaller ball debate has split opinion, with many England players expressing their dislike of the trial, including fly-half Zoe Harrison describing it as the "worst decision ever" earlier this year.

However, Wales wing Lisa Neumann is a fan, saying "it's changed the game" for her passing because of improved grip on the ball.

The data gathered in Sevens compares four seasons of size 5 ball data with one season using the 4.5 ball.

The home nations played with the 4.5 ball in the recent WXV tournament but those who have returned to Premiership Women's Rugby (PWR) are back playing with a size 5. 

World Rugby confirmed that PWR declined the opportunity to use the smaller ball this season, while New Zealand's Super Rugby Aupiki and Australia's Super W opted to use the 4.5.

World Rugby's Chief Player Welfare and Rugby Sevens Officer Mark Harrington confirmed that the timing of the trial is specific.

"This was the window of the 12 to 18 month period where it didn't necessarily conflict with Olympic qualification or Rugby World Cup qualification," he said.

"Wherever we land [in February], it's the same ball for both codes."

Should World Rugby decide to adopt the 4.5 ball, Head of Women's Game Sally Horrox said: "There'll need to be some proper planning and a sensible transition.

"You don't want to put that burden on the game or that opportunity on the game without having the chance to plan."

She also confirmed that support for the community game would be taken into consideration if changes were implemented. 

"We are considering the economics of the situation and any phase change or rollout," she added.

"If a change is recommended, then we, the unions and the high-performance committee would weigh up the costs, the supply and the impact on the community game before anything happened. That would be part of the conversation in February."
