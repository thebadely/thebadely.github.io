An aspiring Welsh sailor hopes to follow in double Olympic champion Hannah Mills' wake with competitive success on the water while highlighting environmental issues off it.

Like Mills, Merle Nieuwland's interest in the sport began as a child in Cardiff, leading her to the 2025 Youth Sailing World Championship 420 female class title alongside crew Sabine Potter at Vilamoura, Portugal.

Mills, 38, will become the only female driver on the start line of the global 12-event SailGP in 2027 having sailed under fellow British Olympic star Ben Ainslee for five seasons.

Nieuwland said of Mills' junior exploits: "Obviously Hannah Mills is one [example] with her being from Cardiff as well, and doing the same steps as me is quite inspirational and quite cool."

Merle Nieuwland and Sabine Potter have been known to sing together to boost morale during difficult races

Now a member of Great Britain Sailing's academy for boats with two crew members, Nieuwland's sights are on the 2032 Olympics.

"I'm getting my first glimpse of Olympic sailing now and I really enjoy it," she said.

"It's definitely a marathon rather than a sprint."

Her former coach Jonny McGovern believes Nieuwland is a natural team leader with a "really great attitude".

"You could see the potential in her very quickly," he added.

"She's got great morals, great attitude, someone that's just desperate to be better.

"She was just constantly picking people up around her."

McGovern says Nieuwland's success so far is "monumental", but also counsels that the transition to senior competition will be "really testing".

He added: "You need to feel failure along the journey to success."

For her part, Nieuwland hopes the challenges she has already faced - including balancing education, sacrificing social life and missing friends and family - will have prepared her for what lies ahead.

"It was pretty full on, I might only have one weekend off in a month," said the former pupil of Ysgol Gyfun Gymraeg Plasmawr in Cardiff.

"I think some people tried to sugar-coat it, but it was really tough.

"I would not change it because I now have two really good sets of friends, from sailing and school.

"I think I just count myself really lucky with that."

However, Nieuwland admits to having questioned if this was truly a path she wanted to take, having missed school trips and other elements of her social life during her school days.

"If I hadn't made those choices, I probably wouldn't be where I am," said Nieuwland, who has taken a gap year to focus on her sailing ambitions while preparing to study biological sciences at university.

Winning the world youth title last year boosted Nieuwland and Potter's belief in their potential at senior level.

"This was a bit of a realisation moment for us that we could actually do quite well here," said Nieuwland. 

"It was really special and felt it was all worth it."
