The UK will provide Ukraine with 1,000 long-range strike drones to target Russia beyond the front line, Prime Minister Andy Burnham is expected to announce later.

The drone support will be announced at a meeting in Berlin of Ukraine's allies,  the Coalition of the Willing, and is part of a new Â£260m package to boost its war defences this winter.

The government said the drones are able to fly up to 625 miles (1,000km) and carry heavier warheads, enabling Ukraine to target military production facilities.

The move is the latest sign of a growing British involvement in drone technology for Ukraine. The government said 80% of the package will be spent in the UK.

The Berlin talks come as US President Donald Trump is meeting with Russian President Vladimir Putin and Chinese Premier Xi Jinping.

Ahead of that meeting on the sidelines of the Asia-Pacific Economic Co-operation (Apec) summit in China, Trump said both he and Xi want to see an end to the Ukraine war.

In a statement released ahead of the latest meeting of the group of 36 countries committed to supporting Ukraine in the event of a ceasefire or peace agreement - the government said the UK is also building maritime, logistical and surveillance drones to help Ukraine's war effort.

Burnham, who is co-chairing the meeting, said the UK must do everything it can to make sure Ukraine can defend itself from Russian attacks throughout the winter.

The prime minister said: "Plunging millions of Ukrainians into the freezing cold and starving them of heat and warmth won't see Putin win this war, it will only cement our resolve to support Ukraine.

"That is why I am determined the UK will do everything we can to ensure Ukraine is able to defend itself from Russia's barrage of attacks and systematic striking of critical national infrastructure."

In recent days, Ukraine has struck oil refineries in southern Russia, and has repeatedly hit the Crimean Bridge - a significant logistical and symbolic asset to Moscow's war effort.

Meanwhile, Russian forces have been focused on knocking out power in Ukraine before winter with attacks on critical infrastructure.

The UK is among a number of countries also supplying Ukraine with lightweight missiles which are being used to intercept Russian jet-powered drones. 

Ukraine's strike rate is now close to 70%, more than double what it was just a couple of months ago, Downing Street said.

The UK also pledged further air defence support this week and sanctioned Russia's 10 major oil producers.

Defence Secretary Wes Streeting said the government was delivering on a promise for more support for Ukraine at the Budget.

"Ukraine's frontline is the frontline for freedom and democracy, and we will stand with them for as long as it takes," he said.

Meanwhile, on Friday Burnham will head to Brussels for a UK-EU summit, where both sides hope to unveil new agreements on food, youth visas and re-linking British industry to the EU's carbon trading regime.
