Let's dive into the details: Lee Reeves had been in a coma since his bout with Gary Cully on 1 August

Irish boxer Lee Reeves has died aged 31, three months after he suffered serious injuries in a bout in Dublin, his promoter Queensberry has announced.

Reeves was fighting Gary Cully for the vacant BUI Celtic light-welterweight title on 1 August but was knocked out in the 10th round at the 3Arena.

The Limerick southpaw was carried out of the ring on a stretcher after the fight and admitted to hospital in a "criticial but stable" condition.

Jonathan O'Brien, Reeves' friend and coach, led tributes, saying he "fought so hard till the end".

"Goodbye son, sleep tight, you fought so hard till the end. We love you so much, rest in peace with your mam and brother waiting at the heavenly gates. Look over your sisters and your father, give them the strength to get through this. We love you Lee," O'Brien posted on Instagram.

Reeves, who was nicknamed 'El Champo' turned professional in 2018 and he won 16 of his 18 bouts in the ranks.

He spent large parts of his early professional career fighting in Canada and Colombia, and impressive performances across the United Kingdom and Ireland led him to face Cully in Dublin in August.

Reeves was knocked down twice in the 10th round and was carried out of the ring.

Outside of boxing, Reeves - who lost his mum, Cathy, to suicide - was an ambassador for the Limerick Treaty Suicide Prevention group.

He also appeared on the BBC reality show Survivor in 2023, which saw contestants stranded on a desert island.

Cully, Reeves' opponent in his last bout, said on Instagram they had known each other for years.

"I still remember the stadium days when I used to watch Lee in the nationals, the weight above me, and [I would] always be impressed by his fights and his flashy gear," he said.

"What a character and a gent. It's never been nothing but love between us champ, can't really believe I'm writing this but RIP brother."

Family friend and Limerick City councillor Catherine Slattery also paid tribute to Reeves: "Limerick is a very dark place tonight with the loss of Lee Reeves. Lee was not just a boxer that excelled in his career - he was a mental health advocate who highlighted mental health and how it can affect young people," she said.

"Lee was a very proud Limerick man, he loved Limerick and its people and any chance he got to promote Limerick, he took it.

"Lee loved his family. He was very proud of his dad, Tony, and he adored his sisters and his nieces and nephews and they, in return, adored him.

"Lee had great faith in God and the only [consolation] is that Lee's mam and brother are embracing him in their arms in heaven.

"The loss of Lee will be felt in Limerick for a long time to come and I am heartbroken for Lee that he lost his fight.

"Rest in peace El Champ - you will be missed by us all."
