Here’s what you need to know: Dafydd Jenkins will win his 35th Wales cap on Saturday against Japan

Dafydd Jenkins says he feels more ready now to be Wales captain, having been given the honour of leading his country again in the Nations Championship finale against Japan on Saturday.

Regular captain Dewi Lake and deputy Jac Morgan have both dropped down to the bench for the match at Allianz Stadium in Twickenham, as head coach Steve Tandy rotates his side for the-ninth place play-off.

It leaves Exeter lock Jenkins, who turns 24 next month, as the natural choice to reclaim the armband - more than two years after he was given a baptism of fire as Warren Gatland's skipper for the 2024 Six Nations.

With Lake and Morgan injured for that tournament - and a number of senior players having retired after the 2023 World Cup - Jenkins endured a tough test, as Wales suffered a clean sweep of Six Nations defeats for the first time since 2003.

"I am definitely a different person," said Jenkins, who at 21 was the second-youngest Wales captain behind Sir Gareth Edwards.

"I was looking back at when I got it the first time, I feel a lot more settled now within the squad.

"To have that experience before puts me in good stead now."

Jenkins will - for now - retake the role for just one match, with Lake and Morgan having led Wales for the past two years.

However, the 6ft 7in (2.01m) Exeter lock has always been a natural leader, claiming the record as the youngest captain in the English Prem at just 19 years and 342 days old in November 2022.

He has followed that up by becoming the Chiefs' permanent skipper for the past few seasons.

"I'm always trying to develop my game and leadership skills," said Jenkins.

"I'm probably not the loudest leader, but I try to lead through my actions and hope boys follow me.

"I love doing it at Exeter, I love putting the jersey on here with Wales, so whatever's best for the team. 

"It will be another special occasion for me and my family."

Tandy says that as part of his squad's leadership group, Jenkins was the natural choice to captain the side on Saturday.

"It's massive credit for him, so well-deserved and a great opportunity for him to lead the team," said the Wales head coach.

Jenkins, Morgan and Lake make up that Wales leadership group, along with others including hooker Ryan Elias, wing Josh Adams and scrum-half Tomos Williams.

"I can't take away from the players I have around me and it is a privileged situation as a captain," added Jenkins.

"I'm very lucky to be in a situation where you have people like Josh on the wing, Ryan in the pack and Adam Beard calling line-outs."

Jenkins on his 38 tackles against New Zealand

Jenkins, who is set to be one of seven Wales players to start all four games this November, has produced a colossal defensive shift across the campaign.

His most effective contribution was the 38 tackles he made in the victory against New Zealand, during a game in which Wales made a team record of 346.

Jenkins' personal tally equalled the men's individual Test record set by France flanker Thierry Dusautoir against New Zealand in the 2007 World Cup quarter-final in Cardiff.

After missing the first half of World Rugby's new competition during the summer through injury, Jenkins has made 77 Nations Championship tackles, the most of any player, without missing one.

When asked about the tackling records, he says it is just part of his job.

"That's exactly it," said Jenkins. "If someone runs into you, you're going to make the tackle.

"If you look at [prop] Gareth Thomas, he's come off the bench [against New Zealand] and made a stupid amount of tackles, 19 in 26 minutes. 

"That is ridiculous and if he's on the whole game, he'd probably double mine!

"It was a huge defensive shift from all the boys after we had been clinical in that first half.

"That's something we probably lacked [in the following week's defeat] against Australia, so we are looking to get that back now against Japan."

Tandy is full of praise for Jenkins.

"I just love Daf's energy," said the former British and Irish Lions defence coach.

"The work he does in the week is phenomenal and he is the ultimate professional. 

"There's a lot of skill to his game also, while the power of his mindset is one of the best I've seen.

"You look at the performance he produced against New Zealand, but he found it to go again against Australia. He made a couple of cover tackles against the Wallabies that probably saved us."

Jenkins is hoping Wales can finish off a successful November by making it three victories in four matches.

"It has been one of the most enjoyable campaigns I've been a part of," he said.

"Obviously because we beat the All Blacks, but the group is as tight as it's ever been since I've been here.

"The competition's there, the training standards are a lot higher than previously, so that's a huge positive to grow for the future."
