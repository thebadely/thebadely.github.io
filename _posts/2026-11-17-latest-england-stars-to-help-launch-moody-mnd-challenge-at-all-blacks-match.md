Lewis Moody says he has been buoyed by the England team's support as he launches his latest challenge in aid of Motor Neurone Disease (MND) research at Saturday's match against New Zealand at Allianz Stadium.

England will wear training tops and specially-painted boots publicising Moody's 'Front Up' campaign, which is calling on supporters to converge on the British coastline in June for a day of fundraising walks.

"I just couldn't be more grateful to the team, the lads that have been in touch and the energy and excitement they have wanted to bring to this," said Moody.

"To have their buy-in is wonderful for me. It speaks to the connection the rugby family has, whatever the team, the era, the nationality, and the strength of that bond."

Moody, who won 71 England caps, was part of the 2003 Rugby World Cup-winning side and captained the team at the 2011 tournament. He was diagnosed with MND last year.

The muscle-wasting condition impacted the lives of Doddie Weir, the former Scotland and British and Irish Lions second row, and rugby league star Rob Burrow in recent years. Both have since died.

"He's been there from the start for me, on the phone as someone to lean on," said Moody.

"I can't speak highly enough about him as an individual."

Moody, who took part in a 500-mile bike ride in June, is working with Weir's My Name'5 Doddie Foundation again on his latest challenge.

There are 175 routes, each 26 miles long, in England, Scotland and Wales. Participants can walk on 12 June, Moody's birthday. Moody himself is planning to take part either in Cornwall or on a leg in Scotland. The aim is to raise more than Â£1m for research into MND.

"I'm doing OK, progression remains slow for me," said Moody of his own condition.

"Everyone's journey is slightly different. The harsh stats are 50% are gone within two to three years [of diagnosis] but that means 50% aren't. I remain positive with that, as do my family. I am in a good spot at the minute."
