Highlights: Slovenia 0-0 Scotland

New head coach Sebastien Pocognoli said his Scotland bow against Slovenia was "two games in one game" as his side claimed a Nations League point after a much-improved second half showing.

The Scots struggled in a disjointed first half, but took control of the game in the second period and created enough chances to win, but had to settle for a 0-0 draw.

"First half, was not what we expected in terms of structure, " Pocognoli told BBC Scotland.

"We were in between for the press, actually we were a little bit surprised about the formation of Slovenia, but at half-time we fixed this and you can see that the second half was much better.

"We produced what we wanted to show every minute, but first half we learned to stick together, to defend the box, to be resilient and to be patient to change the game."

There were two debuts in Ljubljana, a return for Oli McBurnie, and a change of shape after seven years of Steve Clarke ended after the World Cup in the summer.

It is only the first of four games in this international window, but here are the key takeaways from Pocognoli's debut.

'Eternally grateful' McBurnie ready to show what Scotland have missed

Napoli midfielder Billy Gilmour missed the World Cup campaign after a heartbreaking injury against Curacao in Scotland's final home warm-up game.

He returned to the heart of midfield alongside Lewis Ferguson and, according to former Scotland midfielder John Collins was "the catalyst for everything" in the second half.

"Straight from the start of the second half he was getting the ball, playing forward passes and following his pass to the strikers," Collins said.

Gilmour played further up the pitch than Ferguson, "close to a creative 10" according to former Scotland winger Pat Nevin, and played 32 passes into the final third, more than any player on the pitch.

The former Chelsea man combined well with McBurnie on the edge of the box to set up a shot for himself; he slipped in Robbie Ure who opted to cross rather than shoot; and picked out Kieron Bowie with a wonderful through ball which should have resulted in a goal.

Those were just a handful of his key moments. Pocognoli spoke afterwards about the half-time tweak to bring Gilmour deeper to make it harder for Slovenia to play through them and have greater control of the ball.

"We fixed the position of Billy to be a little bit lower and to have Oli McBurnie a little bit more in his box, with two sixes and two 10s, and we were more compact to regain the ball when we lost it.

"Also, that creates some imbalance in the defence from Slovenia, where we can go through the lines with a good chance of Billy or through the depth with the chance of Kieron Bowie or with some crosses.

"He's a fantastic player, I really like him. He's a creative player who always looks forward, always ready to find a solution with a clear game plan, also a good presence around the box and also very good defensively."

Swansea City defender Stephen Welsh was one of two debutants in Slovenia, playing on the left of a back three despite being right footed.

Scotland struggled defensively at times in the first half, with Slovenia playing over the top of them, but Welsh's positioning after the break also helped change the game.

In the first half, his average position was short of the halfway line, but by the end of the game it was in Slovenian territory. 

His most common pass went from being to his fellow centre-back Scott McKenna, to Andy Robertson at wing-back.

Scotland defender Stephen Welsh (purple number five) played on the front foot for Scotland

"It was the starting position of the back three for which changed it - they were in a really good starting position," former Scotland defender Callum Davidson told BBC Scotland.

"In the first half they were quite deep and worried about the ball over the top. But we managed to nullify that and then keep the ball."

The former Motherwell and Celtic defender created a clear goalscoring chance with a wonderful through ball to Bowie, and he finished the game with 16 passes into the final third and the highest accuracy rating.

More aggressive play from Welsh helped set the tone after the break, as he looked assured on his international debut.

Welsh reflects on Scotland's goalless draw in Slovenia

With a new manager, injuries to Che Adams and Lawrence Shankland, and on the back of his strong form with Hull City, Oli McBurnie made his first Scotland appearance since 2021 in attack.

Ure, fresh from his move from Sirius in Sweden to Sevilla, made his debut while Sassuolo's Kieron Bowie came off the bench in the second half.

Those three have one club goal between them this season - Bowie's against Juventus - and none for Scotland in their limited caps.

All three were presented chances to score in the second half, though. McBurnie nicked possession and went through, but failed to round Jan Oblak in the Slovenia goal.

His header from a corner was also beaten away by the Atletico Madrid's goalkeeper. Ure drove a low shot off the post in the first half from outside the box, a difficult chance, but then later opted to go for a cut back when slipped in by Gilmour.

Bowie ended with the highest expected goals rating in the Scotland team (0.94) despite playing only 25 minutes - after twice staring at the whites of Oblak's eyes and failing to find the net.

It is only one game, and Scotland will not face a goalkeeper of Oblak's quality in every match, but there is a lingering concern about the national side's goalscoring ability - they have managed one in their last four competitive games.

For all the positives the attackers brought in the second half in general play, do Scotland have someone with a killer instinct?

"International level is all about taking your chances, you don't get too many," Davidson said. 

"It's about being really clinical, so for these strikers it's important when you get games like this to take your chances. That's the disappointment from today."
