Experts revealed that Lilly Jones body was found face down in a woodland in the town of Blaina

Warning: This article contains details readers may find distressing 

A teenage girl was stabbed "around 55 times" by a 14-year-old boy, a murder trial has been told.

Lilly Jones, from Blaina, in Blaenau Gwent, was stabbed in the heart and both lungs as well as an artery in her neck. There were 17 stab wounds to her neck, and knife wounds were found on her face, jaw, chest and bottom.

She had left her home at about 18:30 on 20 June, telling her father she was "popping out for a bit".

Her body was discovered face down in woodland close to Dyffryn Park two days later.

A 14-year-old boy, who cannot be named because he is under 18, denies her murder.

Dressed in a white short-sleeved shirt and tie, he sat in the dock at Newport Crown Court on Monday, flanked by security staff and an intermediary.

Anna Pope KC, prosecuting, told the jury a camping knife containing a complete DNA profile had been found in the boy's bedroom drawer.

"It was calculated to be a billion times more likely if this DNA had come from Lilly Jones than if it had come from someone else," she said.

Pope said the boy had not given an explanation as to why Lilly's DNA was on the knife.

She added scratch marks were discovered on the inside of his arm and forensic swabs taken from Lilly's fingernails identified his DNA.

Lilly's phone has never been found.

Newport Crown Court was told Lilly and the boy became friends in March this year after a chance meeting in the street and were "in some kind of relationship", sharing an interest in PokÃ©mon.

They were captured on CCTV cameras in Blaina and were last seen together at 18:50 as they walked towards a footbridge leading to the park, near to where her body was later found.

At 22:19 that evening, Lilly's family contacted the police to report her missing. It was the day after her 14th birthday.

Police searching for Lilly spoke to the teenager the following day. He told officers he was supposed to meet up with Lilly at the Red Lion pub in Blaina, but she never arrived.

He later told police they had been together for part of the previous night, before they separated. He said he came home after she told him she was going to meet up with someone else.

In a further conversation with police on the day Lilly's body was found, he said she had told him she wanted to show him a disused park, before leaving.

When he was arrested he told police he did not murder Lilly and did not know where her phone was.

The trial continues.
