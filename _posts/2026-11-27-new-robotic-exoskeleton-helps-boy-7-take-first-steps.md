A robotic exoskeleton has enabled a boy to walk for the first time, at the age of seven - a "just amazing" moment, his family says.

Ellison Fishwick has a rare form of epilepsy which has affected his strength and his development, and now fundraising work is going on to get the Â£65,000 he needs to buy the device.

His mum Olivia, from Shrewsbury, said the demonstration walking frame, developed in Canada,  became available in the UK this year for the first time, and a trial at a special clinic in Wales saw Ellison take his very first steps.

It was "just amazing" his mum said. "When you watch videos of other families sharing their child's first walk, that's exactly how we felt."

The walking frame, which has robotic legs attached, has been designed to help people with a range of mobility issues caused by conditions including cerebral palsy, muscular dystrophy and brain injury.

While the frame supports their body, the robotic legs help them move their legs in a walking motion.

The machine can be adjusted and controlled by parents and carers, helping the user to build strength, endurance and the confidence to use their legs.

Ellison walked for the very first time while trying out the exoskeleton

Fishwick said her son was diagnosed with the drug-resistant epilepsy when he was seven weeks old, just two weeks before the first coronavirus lockdown.

While it can be controlled, it means he has developmental delay, low muscular tone, communication issues, and seizures.

Both she and her husband are able to care for him at home and she said: "It is just get your head down, get through the few days and hope that it passes.

"You do get used to it."

Not being able to walk is a big issue for Ellison.

At the moment he relies on a buggy to get around and his mum said now he is seven years old, he "wants to be included".

"If you're down in a buggy, people do tend to overlook you. Not on purpose, but it just happens," she explained.

Being unable to stand or walk affected all sorts of interaction, she said, even things like going out to see the animals at the Park Hall Farm attraction.

"He can't get up close because he's in a buggy," Fishwick said.

Ellison also often finds other children standing in front of him, blocking his view.

She believes that if he could stand he could get closer and that would boost his confidence.

Ellison is cared for at home by his mother and father

When Fishwick learned about the Trexo Robotics, external exoskeleton, which was developed in Canada in 2019, she knew she had to try it.

She said: "I pestered the company to death when I saw it on Instagram and they contacted me and said, we're holding a clinic in South Wales."

They said the device aided his mobility, but also takes cues from him and reports how much effort he puts into walking.

The results were impressive, and and his mum said: "Ellison walked for the very first time in his whole life."

The device reported Ellison had put in 50% of the effort himself.

"[From that] we know the want to walk is there," Fishwick added.

In the long term, it is hoped that being able to walk will help Ellison's hips, his back and his posture, and Fishwick said "health wise, the health benefits are massive".

She said it would also help his digestion, which he struggles with.

After just 20 minutes trying out the demonstration model, she said "his confidence just grew and grew and grew".
