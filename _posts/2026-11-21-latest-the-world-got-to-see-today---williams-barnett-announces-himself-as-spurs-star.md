Teenager Williams-Barnett shines as Spurs beat Ipswich

Luca Williams-Barnett's performance in his first Premier League start was that good it had two of his Tottenham team-mates labelling him "incredible".

The 18-year-old winger delivered the opener with his first senior goal and then played a pivotal role in Dominic Solanke's second as Spurs comfortably beat Ipswich 2-0 at home.

As full debuts go, it does not get much better than that.

Yet what might be a sign of the excitement about his potential was manager Roberto de Zerbi calmly explaining that "his talent is bigger than this performance".

The Tottenham boss obviously believes this is just the beginning for the talented teenager.

Williams-Barnett became the youngest Spurs player to score in the Premier League since Gareth Bale against Fulham in September 2007.

They would be pretty impressive footsteps to follow in if he was to get close to the player Bale once was for the north London club.

But for the slightest of touches on his fizzed ball across the area from Ipswich goalkeeper Christian Walton before Solanke turned home, Williams-Barnett would have become the second-youngest player ever to both score and assist in a Premier League game, aged just 18 years and 51 days old.

Only Michael Owen, who did it at 17 years and 364 days for Liverpool in 1997, would have done it at a younger age.

"He's incredible, honestly," Solanke told Final Score. "We get to see him every day in training so we know what a player he is - the confidence he has, the ability he has. 

"The world got to see it today, which is great for him. His confidence and ability is amazing. He'll help us a lot."

'Unbelievable' - How Spurs youngster Williams-Barnett impressed on first start

Williams-Barnett was born in Luton and played for his hometown club before moving to Spurs at the age of 10.

The teenager has been generating excitement with his performances for the club's underage teams for some time.

Back in the 2023-24 season, he played for Tottenham's Under-18s aged just 15 and in the following campaign he scored the Under-18 Premier League goal of the season against Leicester having become renowned for fine long-range strikes.

Williams-Barnett has been around Tottenham's first-team since the 2024-25 campaign as he was on the bench for three Europa League games and a Premier League match but his debut came last season as a substitute in a Carabao Cup game against Doncaster Rovers under previous boss Thomas Frank.

Last December, he scored five goals in a Under-19 UEFA Youth League game against Slavia Prague.

But this season looks to be his real breakthrough.

Williams-Barnett  featured in the EFL Cup as a substitute in a 5-1 victory over Charlton in August before coming off the bench against Crystal Palace and Leeds in the Premier League.

Yet it was still a bold choice from De Zerbi to make Williams-Barnett the club's sixth youngest starter in Premier League history given Spurs were coming into this game on the back of a defeat by Leeds United which checked a recent upturn in form.

However, the Spurs boss was rewarded with a man-of-the-match worthy display.

His goal was one of endeavour as he brushed in front of Ipswich defender Abdoul Ouattara to reach Pedro Porro's cross and become the club's youngest scorer on their Premier League full debut.

Throughout the game, he was looking for the ball constantly and not afraid to run with it and try to make things happen.

"Obviously I am so happy for him, every day in the training session he shows his quality," Tottenham defender Destiny Udogie told BBC Match of the Day. 

"Honestly he's just an incredible football player. I hope he keeps doing what he's doing."

The winger was unfortunate not to get the assist but the goal does not happen without his powerful cross.

The England youth international was not afraid to take on a shot and his four attempts were all on target while he had a passing accuracy of 77.4% among his 44 touches. 

Only Solanke took on more shots in the game with four of his five on target.

"To see him come in and do that on his first Premier League start is crazy," added Solanke. 

"He's a great person and is obviously still young, you know what young boys are like. 

"But he's very dedicated and so brave on the ball which is great, in his position I think you need that."

Roberto De Zerbi post-match interview - Tottenham Hotspur v Ipswich Town

Williams-Barnett was replaced by Savio in the 64th minute which brought a standing ovation from the crowd but, with the Brazilian then being forced off later on with an injury, there may be more opportunities in the coming weeks.

Obviously there will be more excitement about the talented teenager now which De Zerbi will have to manage and he has picked midfielder James Maddison as a good mentor for the attacker.

"He's played very well, but his talent is bigger than this performance," the Spurs boss told BBC Match of the Day. 

"It was the first game and I am very happy, I am very delighted for his performance, but he is a big, big, big talent.

"The management of the younger talented players is different because you can't take risks to lose their enthusiasm and their positive mentality. But I think Tottenham's dressing room now is a good environment for him.

"I put [James] Maddison to play very close to him to speak the same language but also to help him and put him in the best conditions to play. To play with freedom, to play with his qualities because his qualities are very big.

"He has to work hard but also he has to keep the passion to keep the love of football."
