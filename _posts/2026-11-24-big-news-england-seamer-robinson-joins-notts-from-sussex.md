England seamer Ollie Robinson has left Sussex after 11 years to join Nottinghamshire for an undisclosed "compensation package" on a three-year deal.

The 32-year-old had been linked with a move to Trent Bridge for a number of weeks, with confirmation finally coming just three weeks after Robinson was named England's cricketer of the year for 2026 following his remarkable return to the Test team.

After two years in the wilderness, Robinson claimed 28 wickets in four Tests at an average of just 9.30. Before that, he had taken 76 wickets across 20 Test matches between 2021 and 2024.

He captained Sussex in the County Championship last season but because of international commitments he featured in just six red-ball games, taking 18 wickets.

Robinson has said he moves to Notts, a side that took their defence of the County Championship title to the final round of matches last season, with a "real desire to win silverware".

"I have a huge desire to become the very best and hopefully getting some fresh eyes and a new environment, I can maximise my chances of achieving that," he told Nottinghamshire's website., external

Sussex, a club that has grappled with financial issues, finished just above Nottinghamshire in the Division One table last season despite having been hit with a 12-point deduction at the start of the campaign because of "sustained operating losses".

The club had a financial framework imposed on them by the English and Wales Cricket Board earlier this year, which came with a spending cap on player salaries., external

Robinson has taken 520 first-class wickets since he made his County Championship debut for Sussex in 2015.

Before that his professional career comprised of just 10 appearances in limited over matches for Yorkshire, who sacked him, external for a "number of unprofessional actions".

Robinson said it is "incredibly difficult to put into words" what Sussex means to him, having given him a way back into the game and platform to become one of world cricket's most fierce wicket-takers.

"I leave having achieved things I could only have dreamt of when I first walked through the doors, from representing England to having the honour of captaining the club," he added.

"I hope Sussex is proud of everything I have done for them. 

"It's not been an easy decision to leave and it's going to feel very strange not walking into the Sussex dressing room next season."

Nottinghamshire head coach Peter Moores says he is "incredibly excited" to have Robinson part of his side at Trent Bridge across all three formats.

"His red-ball record stands up against the very best bowlers in the world," Moores said.

"It feels like he's joining us at a crucial time when we are looking to deepen our red-ball squad to help us be competitive across the whole season."

By Adrian Harms, BBC Radio Sussex commentator

Rumours have been circulating for some time on the south coast that Robinson would be joining Notts but, even so, this news will come as yet another hammer blow to beleaguered Sussex supporters.

The four-man seam attack who started the season at Leicester in April has been decimated with only Tom Price remaining, as Robinson, Fynn Hudson-Prentice and Henry Crocombe have all now left the club.

Add to that list the experienced John Simpson and Daniel Hughes, as the guts continues to get ripped out of the Sussex squad, and the full implications of the club's past financial misdemeanours become startling apparent.

But as captain and talisman, the loss of Robinson is the biggest blow, not least as his contract ran for a further year, and raises the question as to whether other players currently under contract, could be heading through the revolving exit door at Hove.

Robinson's record speaks for itself. From scoring a century on his debut against Durham in 2015, to a magnificent haul of 408 first-class wickets at an average of 21.27.

He has bowled the club to victory on numerous occasions but perhaps his performance in taking 9-78 on a flat Cardiff pitch against Glamorgan in 2021will live longest in the memory. 

That performance belatedly convinced the England selectors what everyone at Sussex already knew, that Robinson was, and had been for some time, an international class bowler.

His loss cannot be overstated - a student of the game, he showed his leadership qualities in the County Championship last season as Sussex confounded a 12-point deduction by finishing third.

Hove will not be the same without him, bowling down the hill from the Cromwell Road End, beating but more often that not catching the outside edge - this truly is the end of an era.
