Let's dive into the details: Ash Barty retired from professional tennis in 2022

Australia's Ash Barty says she felt a sense of "impostor syndrome" after being elected to the International Tennis Hall of Fame.

The former world number one and three-time Grand Slam singles champion will be inducted at a ceremony in Rhode Island in August next year. 

The 30-year-old joins an illustrious group of former players in the hall of fame, including Roger Federer, Billie Jean King and Margaret Court. 

"I feel very, very humbled and grateful. And to be honest, I've got a little bit of impostor syndrome where I feel I don't quite belong," said Barty.

"The Hall of Fame is for the absolute legends of our game, and I'm not sure I'm quite there."

Barty announced her surprise retirement aged just 25 in 2022, less than two months after winning the Australian Open title. 

That triumph made her the first home player to win the Australian Open men's or women's singles title in 44 years, having previously won the French Open in 2019 and Wimbledon in 2021. 

Barty had also played cricket professionally in Australia's Women's Big Bash League when she took a break from tennis in 2014.

She will be the second Indigenous Australian in the hall of fame, following Evonne Goolagong Cawley, who won seven Grand Slam singles titles.

Now a mum of two, Barty said that she does not regret retiring at a young age and ruled out a comeback, saying she is happy focusing on her family.

"I'm probably most excited to tell my team because it feels like it's as much for me as it is for them," Barty added.

"My kids don't even know that I played tennis, and I'm sure that at the induction next year they'll wonder what's going on.

"I'm deep in the trenches with my two little ones at the moment, and I absolutely love it."
