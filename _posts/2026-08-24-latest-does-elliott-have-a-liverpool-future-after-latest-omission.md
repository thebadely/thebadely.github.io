Harvey Elliot on playing for Liverpool in pre-season

It was a little under a month ago when in the bowels of the Yankee Stadium in New York, Harvey Elliott spoke candidly about his emotions after a "tough" loan spell at Aston Villa and why this summer felt like a second chance for Liverpool.

There was a smile on his face when he spoke about his love for the club - the team he has supported since he was a boy - and wanting to be free and enjoying himself again with his football, because he felt like he had not done that enough.

You only had to see Elliott's joy when he came off the bench in Liverpool's friendly against Monaco earlier this month as all four sides of Anfield stood up and applauded to see what it meant.

With a number of senior first-team players absent, Elliott started Liverpool's first friendly under Andoni Iraola in Nashville and again when Liverpool took on Wrexham in New York four days later but as the World Cup contingent returned, the midfielder was benched and where he stood in the squad became clearer.

On the first weekend of the Premier League season, Elliott was left out of the Liverpool squad entirely for their trip to Newcastle and it now looks increasingly likely that the 23-year-old could be on his way out before the transfer window ends.

Liverpool have received no offers for Elliott - who spent last season on loan at Aston Villa - and his exclusion from the squad on Sunday was not transfer related, nor is the midfielder carrying an injury. 

Elliott's contract expires next summer and if first-team football is not possible at Liverpool he needs to go somewhere where it is, to avoid another lost season.

In America, Elliott spoke about how we would speak to Iraola at the "right time" and emphasised the need to be "selfish". 

"Ultimately, I'm still wanting to be selfish, doing what's right for me," said Elliott. "Whether that's staying here and fighting for my place or potentially going elsewhere, I'm still unsure at the moment until I get some more intel."

Another year on the fringes would be no good for the man who was named player of the tournament when England Under-21s won the European Championship in 2025.

Harvey Elliott signed for Liverpool from Fulham in 2019

By now, much has been said about his situation at Villa last season which Unai Emery described as "embarrassing" for all involved. 

Elliott played only nine games and a total of 277 minutes after Villa decided early on in the campaign that they did not want to trigger an obligation to sign him from Liverpool for Â£35m if he made 10 appearances.

Those at Villa stressed Elliott's professionalism and his positivity throughout and to his credit, the attacking midfielder returned early to Liverpool for the start of pre-season after a personally-challenging year. 

Iraola himself was impressed by his attitude, saying as much in his introductory press conference.

"I've seen him with this eagerness to show himself. He will have a chance during the pre-season. He came one week earlier and I hope we can see him in a good place," said Iraola. 

"Last season had to have been very difficult for him as it was a strange situation that meant they [Aston Villa] couldn't let him play."

In effect, the move to Villa wasted a season of Elliott's career even if he was adamant that it was a "learning curve" in a year where Villa won the Europa League.

"I don't wish it upon any other player, because it's not nice at all," Elliott said. 

When he left Liverpool last summer, the primary factor was more game-time after making just one Premier League start during Liverpool's title-winning campaign under Arne Slot in 2024-25. 

So with a week to go of the transfer window and with further attacking signings expected at Anfield, perhaps the writing is on the wall. 

A move lower down the Premier League or somewhere in Europe may be an option. 

After all, this is a player who scored the winner for Liverpool at PSG in a year where the Ligue 1 club won the Champions League. 

He is neat on the ball and unafraid to take risks - a return of 15 goals and 21 assists for Liverpool, across all competitions, showcases what he can do.

Either way, it is imperative for all involved that Elliott finds somewhere to play football regularly this season because it seems unlikely at Liverpool and at 23, he cannot afford to have a season like last.
