Nobody was more aware of how divisive the selection of Mikey Lewis for England's squad at the 2026 Rugby League World Cup was than the man himself.

The 25-year-old was picked by coach Brian McDermott as one of three half-back options alongside Harry Smith and captain George Williams, despite a Super League season pockmarked by inconsistency and disciplinary issues.

Lewis' selection ahead of 2025 Man Of Steel Jake Connor added fuel to the fire; the fact the Hull KR man was then left out of the squad for England's opening defeat to Tonga only added to the feeling of Lewis being more trouble than he was worth.

But Lewis, speaking to the media on Wednesday from the England team hotel in Wollongong, said that despite his up and down season he never doubted that he had earned his place.

"The way my year went wouldn't be the best, I don't need anyone else to tell me," he said. "I just had to put myself in the shop window. I have earned my spot."

And after all the talk, Lewis has the chance to be the man who helps save England's floundering World Cup hopes.

The 30-12 loss to Tonga has England firmly on the back foot, but they gave themselves a chance last weekend with a crushing 82-0 win over France.

"I will always represent my country to the best of my ability," Lewis said of his two-try cameo in Perth. "I was told to run with the ball, and I wanted to play with a smile on my face.

"We just have to go out and do what we have planned. It will be a tough game, but we have the strike and desire.

"We have a fighting chance, we believe in that. So, we just have to put the hard work in. It is a unique position, so just enjoy that."
