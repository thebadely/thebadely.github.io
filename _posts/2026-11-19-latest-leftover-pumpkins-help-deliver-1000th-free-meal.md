Let's dive into the details: Alex Vardill from Cambridge Sustainable Food says local growers have donated leftover pumpkins and squashes after Halloween

Leftover pumpkins have helped a sustainable food organisation provide its 1,000th free meal to low-income households.

Cambridge Sustainable Food started producing recipe kits earlier this year using fruit and vegetables that would have otherwise gone to waste.

The group received masses of pumpkins from local farmers after Halloween, including about 300kg (661lb) of them from one supplier alone.

Alex Vardill, from the organisation, said many of the meal kits had been received by families who sometimes struggled to access fresh produce.

"This has been a real help with improving that access at a time when food is really expensive," the 25-year-old said.

"They provide everything you need to make a meal in one bag, already measured out, with a recipe there in front of you."

The kits are funded by Cambridge City Council and South Cambridgeshire District Council.

Cambridge Sustainable Food launched its recipe kits in the summer

The Halloween leftovers were used in 50 pumpkin and lentil soup recipe kits, which were assembled by a team of volunteers this week.

The organisation is based in a warehouse in the Barnwell area of Cambridge, which is stocked with food donated by local supermarkets and residents.

Vardill, who also volunteers at a community kitchen, said they had noticed an increased demand for both services.

"It's a really tough time for a lot of people," they added.

The recipe kit initiative launched in July, making use of excess blackberries.

Since then, other recipes have been themed around leftover tomatoes, courgettes, and apples.
