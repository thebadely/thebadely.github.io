Here’s what you need to know: Deputy Lucy Stephenson said she had constructive talks with Jersey's treasury minister

A bid to reduce fuel duty for three months to help motorists deal with the cost of living crisis has been withdrawn.

Deputy Lucy Stephenson put forward her proposition to Jersey's States Assembly in June which called for the tax - currently around the 65p per litre mark - added to petrol and diesel prices to be cut by 10p a litre from 1 October to 31 December.

However, Stephenson said she had withdrawn the proposal following "constructive discussions" with Treasury Minister Alan Maclean.

She said the talks had produced a "pragmatic way forward" to help islanders deal with financial issues.

When Stephenson lodged her proposition in June, external, the St Mary, St Ouen and St Peter deputy said the ongoing conflict in the Middle East, which led to limited traffic moving through the Strait of Hormuz, had exacerbated "spiralling costs" for Jersey residents.

She said her discussions with Maclean and other colleagues at the States had left her satisfied the issue was being taken seriously.

"I think there were very clear messages at the recent election from voters about the cost of living," Stephenson said.

"It's obvious people are struggling and we have heard that loud and clear."

The budget is due to be lodged by the government later in September before being debated by the States in December.
