It has been recently reported that Diego Simeone beat the record of Luis Aragones, who recorded his final La Liga victory in 2004

Atletico Madrid's 3-0 victory over Getafe may have been routine, but the win was another landmark in manager Diego Simeone's lengthy tenure at the club.

Sunday's triumph at Estadio Coliseum was the Argentine's 345th La Liga win in his 567th match, surpassing the record for the most victories in the Spanish top flight. 

Simeone beat the mark of legendary former Atletico and Spain manager Luis Aragones, who won 344 matches but needed 189 more games to reach that total.

"It makes me happy," Simeone said of setting the new mark, with his side now third in the table after 10 wins from 13 games.

"I don't usually find many things to be happy about, but there is an incredible amount of work behind this."

Questioned about the perception that his team are defensive, Simeone said that was a "constant criticism we face". 

He added: "I don't place much value on it, but I do take note of it.

"This narrative that we're a defensive team, as if we're always playing solely to defend. You don't win 345 matches just by defending. Is that even possible?"

The 56-year-old has been in charge of Atletico since December 2011, joining the club in an era when Jose Mourinho's Real Madrid and Pep Guardiola's Barcelona were the dominant teams in Spain.

He outlasted the pair of managerial superstars, leading Atletico into a period of consistent success and staying with his side long enough to witness Mourinho's return to Real this summer, 13 years after his initial departure.

Having won the Europa League in May 2012, after his appointment on 23 December 2011, Simeone led Atletico to the European Super Cup title three months later.

He then won the Copa del Rey in his first full season and secured the club's first La Liga title since 1996 - when Simeone was an Atletico player - in the following campaign.

Atletico have established themselves as the third force in Spanish football under his leadership, qualifying for the Champions League in every full season under his charge.

However he has been unable to win European football's premier competition, losing in the final to city rivals Real twice in heart-breaking fashion - in extra-time in 2014 after Real scored a stoppage-time equaliser, and on penalties in 2016.

Simeone's last silverware arrived in 2021 when he won a second Spanish league championship, while he also guided the team to a Europa League and Super Cup double in 2018.

His contract expires at the end of this season with the Atletico fanbase now waiting to see if their manager intends to extend his remarkable victory record into another campaign.

There has been an evolution since Simeone arrived, but his peak came when his players were happy to defend without the ball, deep in their own box. That is how the titles arrived. 

He has not found the same effort or quality with a squad built to attack. In the peak years he found the perfect balance between deep defence and counterattack, yet picking it is harder now that his team must take the initiative.

None of that diminishes his legacy. He turned the club's mentality into a winning one and sustained it for over a decade, the two hardest things in football.

After losing a second Champions League final to Real Madrid in 2016 he could have left, but he renewed his energy.

Atletico have flirted with Luis Enrique and Unai Emery, yet everyone agrees he goes when he decides. For now, he finds plenty of reasons to stay.
