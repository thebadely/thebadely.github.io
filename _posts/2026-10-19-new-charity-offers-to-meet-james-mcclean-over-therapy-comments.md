It has been recently reported that Men's Action Network has offered to meet James McClean after he was reported as saying he would view seeking therapy as a sign of "weakness"

A men's mental health charity has offered to meet footballer James McClean after he told a newspaper he views speaking to a psychologist as "weak".

In an interview with the Sunday Independent, external, the Derry City player said he had never tried therapy because "as a man I would see that as a weakness".

Michael Lynch, from Men's Action Network (MAN), said the charity would welcome the opportunity to meet with him to discuss the importance of encouraging all men to feel comfortable seeking support when they need it.

On Monday night, his wife Erin responded on social media, saying she had been "strongly encouraging him to seek therapy" and that he was "struggling at the moment".

McClean has previously spoken out over his lack of playing time at Derry City this season

McClean has been contacted for comment but the club has distanced itself from his views and agreed to meet MAN.

McClean, who is currently not playing for his hometown club amid an ongoing contract dispute, gave the comments during a wide-ranging interview on topics including his new book, which details the social media abuse he has faced for refusing to wear a poppy, as well as death threats he has received.

The former Sunderland, West Bromwich Albion and Stoke City player spoke about his wife's view that he had "built-up anger and frustration" that he had blocked out, to which the interviewer asked him if he had ever tried therapy.

McClean responded that he had heard his brother, fellow Derry City player Patrick McClean, "on a call to a club psychologist, having a lovely chat".

"And a lot of the players speak to him and for me that's just weak.

"As a man, I see that as weakness. I don't need to open up.

"I see it as a weakness that I'd have to speak to someone about my feelings, my emotions."

Writing on social media, external, McClean's wife Erin said she had been encouraging him to seek therapy "to deal with issues from his early life as well as to face the end of his career".

"I do not agree with James' opinion on the subject of therapy and mental health," she said, adding the footballer was "struggling at the moment and has been for some time".

"He may not agree with me sharing that, but I've told him that I need to explain this. I'm working on getting him to a place where he recognises that speaking about this is not a sign of weakness," she said.

Erin McClean says she has been encouraging her husband to seek therapy

When McClean is struggling with stress or anxiety, she added, his instinct is "to lash out".

"It has been a daily battle in a year where I feel he hasn't been given the support that he requires when he is clearly struggling," she said.

Erin McClean added: "I know James to be very supportive to personal friends of ours when they have gone through difficult times. 

"While he continues to push back at the idea of therapy for himself, that does not mean he does not care about other people's struggles or the importance of seeking help."

On Monday night, spectators unfurled a banner reading "It is not weak to speak" during Derry City's league of Ireland game against St Patrick's Athletic at the Ryan McBride Brandywell Stadium.

Michael Lynch says James McClean is an influential figure, particularly for young men

Lynch said he would welcome a meeting with McClean to discuss his views, as first reported by Derry Now, external.

He told BBC News NI that McClean was entitled to his views and "neither I nor anyone else is entitled to say otherwise, because men's mental health is such a complex issue".

"In some ways, I agree with him, in the sense that he mentioned that the only person who can decide whether seeking support is right for them is the person themselves, because nobody can do it for you.

"But what I and others involved with MAN would say is that there is certainly still a stigma around men feeling that they cannot reach out at times and say, 'I am not OK and need to seek support', when that is so far from the truth."

Lynch added that "asking for help isn't a sign of weakness". 

"For men to recognise that they need support and act on it is incredibly brave."

Lynch said James McClean was an influential figure, particularly for young men from Derry, given his sporting and personal achievements.

"We would obviously welcome a meeting with someone of that stature, for him to come and meet with the people we speak with every day, and we would love for him to have a conversation with us.

"I think it would be really beneficial for people to hear from someone who has achieved so much in his career and has been a role model for so many people, to show what can be achieved with hard work."

In a statement, Derry City Football Club said McClean's views "do not reflect our core values or beliefs".

"The club places the highest priority on the mental health and wellbeing of our players, staff and the wider Derry City community," the statement said.

"Supported by our in-house psychologist, the club actively encourages anyone facing mental health challenges to speak openly, seek support and know they never have to face such challenges alone."

James McClean has had an acclaimed club and international career, having earned 103 caps for the Republic of Ireland. 

The 37-year-old began his career at Derry City before moving to Sunderland and appearing for a number of clubs in the Premier League and Championship, including Sunderland, West Bromwich Albion, Stoke and Wigan Athletic. 

He later made more than 100 appearances for Wrexham in all competitions, scoring eight goals, before returning to Derry City in January last year.

His return to his hometown club, however, has been derailed by a contract dispute which he has described as "incomprehensible".

McClean has described the past few months as "one of the most difficult periods of his life" and also apologised over social media posts in which he aired frustrations over the ongoing issues at Derry.
