It was a night to remember for West Ham and their 4,500-travelling support.

Skipper and club talisman Jarrod Bowen opened the scoring early, then secured victory late in a five-goal thriller at Bolton.

The result took West Ham to the top of the Championship. It was their third successive league win - the first time they have done that since December 2023.

That was the run which prompted a contract offer to David Moyes that went unsigned and was eventually withdrawn prior to the Scot's departure six months later. The decline continued and ended in relegation last May.

With elation in short supply recently, West Ham's fans made sure they enjoyed this night, staying to acknowledge their team after the final whistle, delaying departure on a journey that, for many, won't end until the middle of the night.

Amid homage to Bowen, singing about bubbles and West Ham being 'massive', the visiting supporters also chanted repeatedly about former chairman David Sullivan. None of the songs about him were complimentary.

Sullivan was not at Bolton for the Championship encounter. He has been at all of the Hammers' home games so far this season, though. He was also at Watford for a 1-1 draw last month.

For some, this involvement is a surprise.

When the 77-year-old resigned as chairman in June, saying his intention was to fight allegations made against him in a joint BBC Panorama and Times investigation, there was an expectation it would eventually trigger his exit from the club.

That and Karren Brady standing down as managing director in April, had been cited as a significant factor in West Ham selling a remarkable 50,000 season tickets for their first campaign outside the top flight since 2011. 

Their average home crowd in the league so far tops 60,000. The 53,369 who watched their EFL Cup first round victory over Portsmouth was a record for that stage of the competition.

But Sullivan remains. Providing the complicated agreement announced on 3 September goes through as anticipated within the next few weeks, he will end up with 40% of the club. Sullivan has clearly not disappeared from the West Ham picture.

He is nowhere near as influential, though.

Talk to people inside the club and they will tell you how much has changed since the summer.

Czech billionaire and Royal Mail owner Daniel Kretinsky is now chairman. It is intended he will become the biggest shareholder with 46%.

Karim Virani is making a positive impression as chief executive. He has earned praise for his willingness to engage with supporters and confront issues important to them, not least the club's badge, which was changed in 2016 to coincide with the move away from the Boleyn Ground.

Director of football Nils Koppen has influence in recruitment discussions. Recruitment as a whole is now a transparent affair involving the right people within the club rather than deals being executed without adequate discussion.

In a summer of huge change, 12 players were sold - including Crysencio Summerville and Mateus Fernandes - and, eventually, nine came in.

Yet, this being West Ham, the situation is not quite so straightforward.

Uncertainty remains over the intentions of Amanda Staveley. It was the former Newcastle director's deal with former co-chair Vanessa Gold in July that triggered the pre-emption rights process at West Ham which eventually led to the deal that not only sees Kretinsky and Sullivan increase their stakes, but also fellow shareholders Tripp Smith and Terry Brown.

In theory, if Staveley agreed a deal with Sullivan and Smith, who will have 11%, she would own over half the club.

Yet that would also be subject to pre-emption rights.

Sullivan talks positively about Kretinsky privately and is unhappy the recent process diverted attention away from the transfer window. His words do not seem to be those of someone about to create even more turmoil at the London Stadium.

But some fans are wary.

"It seemed Sullivan was going when he stepped down, but that hasn't happened," said Simon Lane, a Hammers season ticket holder from north Wales, who stopped for a chat 90 minutes before kick-off.

"No-one really knows what to make of it all. Does he want more shares? Does he want to get out? Why would he do a deal with Kretinsky and then sell the whole thing?

"It's difficult to know what is for the best really. Some fans do like what Amanda Staveley did at Newcastle but Kretinsky puts the right people in place and Karim is talking with fans, which is what we have wanted.

"I am optimistic for the season though."

In Bowen, Engels and others, West Ham have class few Championship rivals can match.

Nuno Espirito Santo knows a successful club needs more than that.

Stability and unity are not words often used about West Ham in recent years. But Nuno can see the path to a bright future.

"I hope it's better," he said. "I think it's the belief of everybody, starting from the ownership to the staff, to the players, and fans, that we are trying to improve and have better days.

"What I see, and feel is unity in all the departments of the club. Five thousand fans on a Tuesday night. (What happened in the past) is behind us. It's very important to be unified. There is no other way."
