Already circled as one of the must-see fixtures in a packed November schedule, interest in the meeting between Ireland and South Africa has intensified following their contrasting disciplinary fortunes in recent days.

Not for the first time, Springboks head coach Rassie Erasmus landed himself in hot water with the powers-that-be for criticising match officials. 

After receiving a six-match ban for questioning the integrity of referee Andrea Piardi and television match official Matteo Liperini in South Africa's defeat by France last week, Erasmus has been defended and condemned.

There is no doubt, however, over where the Springbok camp stand, with assistant coach Mzwandile Stick throwing his support behind Erasmus. 

"He's been hands-on with our team [in the build-up], and we're in a good space," Stick said on Friday. 

"Irrespective of what has happened, we love him, we appreciate him, and no-one is going to break us. I can tell you that for sure."

Erasmus also said South Africa let their country down against France, with captain Siya Kolisi bristling at a suggestion that it was a "dramatic" assessment.

"It might sound dramatic to you, but to us it means so much more," Kolisi said during his pre-match news conference. 

"Rugby has done a lot for our country, and it's done a lot for many of us who come from South Africa.

"We know how happy people are and what the mood is like when we win, and when we lose, it gets tough. People care a lot about it.

"When we play, we think about the people back home. We don't take representing the country lightly because we represent every single person in South Africa when we put on that jersey."

While Erasmus has dominated headlines this week, he will not be in attendance at Aviva Stadium on Saturday.

Instead, in a bizarre twist, the two-time World Cup winner will host a watchalong of the game from the team's Dublin hotel, with the conditions of his suspension prohibiting him from matchday contact with players and management.

Erasmus was not the only South African to receive a suspension this week with back row Jasper Wiese banned for four games for a dangerous ruck clearout against France. 

While Wiese's fate did not attract the same level of scrutiny, South Africa's sense of injustice will perhaps be fuelled by the sight of Ireland fly-half Sam Prendergast on Saturday. 

As predicted by Erasmus, Prendergast was cleared to face the world champions after being cited for an alleged hip-drop tackle on Fiji back row Peceli Yato. 

"I will tell you now for free, if that was a Fijian player tackling an Irish player with that hip-drop, that player would be in Fiji now," said Stick.

"If that was a Fijian player, he would be sitting in Fiji now doing some extra lessons of how to tackle."

Dan Sheehan says he is not fazed by switching to the back of the scrum

Focused on erasing the pain of last year's 24-13 loss to the Springboks in Dublin, Ireland have sidestepped questions about Erasmus this week, with head coach Andy Farrell opting not to comment on his opposite number's suspension.

Irish captain Dan Sheehan said the commotion has not "affected the squad at all". 

"Obviously it's a big media story for fans to get behind, but in terms of us playing the match it has zero impact," he said. 

While there may be added scrutiny on referee Christophe Ridley, Sheehan said he plans to leave the official alone unless compelled to bring something "major" to his attention. 

The Irish skipper has enough on his plate anyway. In addition to leading his side against the back-to-back world champions, Sheehan has been thrust into the number eight role.

The 28-year-old has won all 42 of his previous caps at hooker but moves to the back row with Caelan Doris, Jack Conan, Sean Jansen and James McNabney all ruled out. 

Ireland hope Sheehan's inclusion in the back row will facilitate his powerful ball-carrying capabilities, but he insists he is not going to "overthink" it. 

"I just get on with stuff. Like, eight on my back, just pick the ball up and go," said Sheehan, whose only previous start in the position was in Leinster's win over Munster last month.

"It can't be that complicated. That's my approach to it, to be honest."

Erasmus will watch Saturday's game from South Africa's team hotel 

Had it not been for the furore surrounding Erasmus, chances are the pre-match talk would have been dominated by scrums. 

Last year, Ireland were shown five cards and conceded six penalties and a penalty try during a chastening night against the Springbok scrum.  

That set-piece shellacking may have given the Irish pack nightmares over the past 12 months, and while South Africa won nine scrum penalties against France, Kolisi expects a closer contest in Dublin this time. 

"Their scrum is much better than it was a year ago," he said. 

"You can see they're getting penalties. But our focus is not on what they're going to do. It's about making sure we stick to our plans and do what we need to do in the scrum."

The Springboks have been boosted by the return of record cap holder Eben Etzebeth, but his second-row partner Ruan Nortje was ruled out through injury on Friday. He has been replaced by Franco Mostert, who was not originally in the matchday squad.

Ireland - who have lost their past six contests against New Zealand, France and South Africa, the three teams above them in the world rankings, by double-digit margins - have also been forced into a late change, with Thomas Clarkson replacing Sam Illo as the replacement tight-head prop. 

The myriad permutations mean Ireland and South Africa could meet again in the finals weekend at Twickenham. 

Either way, Saturday's sold-out contest in Dublin promises to deliver enough drama to tide fans over until next year's World Cup in Australia, where they could contest the final.
