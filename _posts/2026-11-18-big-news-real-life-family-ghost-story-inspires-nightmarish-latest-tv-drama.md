Let's dive into the details: Black Doves actress Gabrielle Creevy stars as Liz Rich, bringing "absolute authenticity to the role"

The "real-life ghost story" of a family who experienced "frightening, inexplicable" things after moving to the countryside has been turned into a new TV drama.

The Witch Farm tells the "truly nightmarish" tale of Liz and Bill Rich who left London with their son Jonathan for a remote farmhouse in the foothills of Bannau Brycheiniog, also known as the Brecon Beacons, determined to make a fresh start.

At first the isolation felt like freedom, but the family claimed things began to happen around the house leading them to feel increasingly scared.

Writer and creator, Danny Robins, met Liz who told him "her incredible story", and said the four-part series would "really surprise people".

Danny Robins hopes that new TV drama will raise the question, do ghosts exist?

Robins, best-known for his podcast Uncanny, said the idea for the new drama came after he made The Battersea Poltergeist podcast for the BBC, which he described as being based on "another real-life ghost story".

"It was a hit around the world and afterwards there was a lot of pressure to find a sequel," he said, which led him to discover the story of Liz and Bill Rich.

In 1989, the young couple moved their family into a remote farmhouse at the foot of a mountain. They claimed to have experienced poltergeist activity, apparitions, alleged possessions and even physical injury.

They say this lasted for seven years and changed them forever, as well as affecting the community around them.

Robins said: "The more I dug into it, the more it felt like something that merited a long-form approach and so we made the podcast series called The Witch Farm. 

"Through that process, I met Liz and she told me her incredible story. We also found other witnesses and pieced everything together through a mix of audio drama and documentary.

After finishing the podcast, Robins asked himself "what's next?", admitting that telling the story as a TV documentary "didn't feel right", leading to the decision to dramatise it instead.

The Witch Farm is based on the "truly nightmarish" tale of Liz and Bill Rich

Asked what he hopes viewers will feel when they watch the series, Robins said: "I absolutely want people to feel scared, but I also want them to be moved by it.

"It's an incredibly powerful and emotional story, that was one of the things that grabbed me when I first came across it for the podcast."

He also hopes that viewers will "laugh a little", adding that "humour has always been important" to his work.

Robins also hopes to raise the question - "do ghosts exist?", sparking the debate between belief and scepticism.

"What's crucial for me is that I want viewers to think about it and reach their own conclusions," he said.

The upcoming drama, which was filmed in Wales, stars Port Talbot-born actress Gabrielle Creevy, who recently starred in the new season of spy thriller Black Doves where she also uses her Welsh accent.

She said: "I'm from Wales and I love working here. It's always a pleasure and crews are always so great. I love working in the middle of nowhere in the fresh air - I think I'll always feel the same."

Robins said she brought "absolute authenticity to the role" through her Welsh accent."

Robins said The Witch Farm was "such a Welsh story", which was considered throughout making the production.

He said an "almost entirely Welsh" crew was used in its creation, adding: "It's been important that this feels like a story made and told in Wales."

The Witch Farm will be available on BBC iPlayer, BBC One and BBC One Wales from 22 November
