Some people say they are finding it hard to heat their homes after increases in heating oil costs fuelled by the US-Israeli war with Iran.

Residents in the South West told the BBC they have held off buying fuel for the winter in the hope prices could come down and feared some people might go without.

It comes as the government announced new measures aimed at regulating the market and reviewing the minimum amounts some people have to buy.

Heating fuel costs have risen by up to 82% since the start of the conflict, according to the Minster for Energy Consumers Polly Billington.

Billington told the BBC: "We need to make sure the market is working as fairly as possible."

She said it was "striking" how rural communities with "so many people off grid" were "some of the most exposed" to the price increases.

"I'm looking at ways to spread the cost when I have to bite the bullet and get it," she said.

Earlier in October, the government promised better regulation of the sector.

Ministers said they would tighten up rules, promote competition and give customers clearer compensation routes if things went wrong. Minimum order levels, set for some customers, would also be reviewed.

Sharpe said she was not hopeful the government measures would help.

 "I can't see how regulating it is going to bring costs down."

Pauline Trubody, from Porkellis, Cornwall, said she had already spent more on two deliveries of 500 litres this year compared to previous years.

The 76-year-old said: "I probably won't put it [the heating] on as much as I would have.

"There is going to be a lot of people who can't afford it and folk who are going to go cold."
