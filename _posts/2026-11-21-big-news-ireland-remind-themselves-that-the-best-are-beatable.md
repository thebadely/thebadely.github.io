Let's dive into the details: Ireland had lost 24-13 in their previous Test match against South Africa

After six deflating losses in a row to South Africa, New Zealand and France, Andy Farrell was asked this week what made him think Ireland could get back to the levels they had reached when considered squarely among that group.

Because they had got there before came the typically uncomplicated response from the side's head coach.

The longer the 2023 World Cup receded into memory, however, fewer and fewer believed that his side still had within them the type of performance they produced to beat the Springboks 20-17 on Saturday. 

Undoubtedly Ireland's biggest win of recent years, having not beaten one of the three sides who started this weekend above them in the rankings since the summer of 2024, this was one of their most needed too.

This Test had long been viewed as one of the crucial measuring sticks for Farrell's side building towards next autumn's World Cup - the last time they will face the back-to-back champions before boarding the plane to Australia. 

Having settled into a pattern where they could not beat the best, but almost always found a way past the rest, Ireland could not have headed down under with genuine belief of making history without first showing they were still capable of this level. 

"It's something that we've been looking forward to, to challenge ourselves and delivering when it matters against top-line sides," said Farrell.

"We knew that this fixture was coming, playing against the world's best by a stretch at this moment in time

"It was a game that we we wanted to challenge ourselves and see how we went and, obviously fronting up and showing the character that we did, and coming out on the right side of the win is a big night for everyone who is Irish."

For all that had gone on in the build-up, from explosive news conferences to coaching suspensions, from dismissed citing complaints to eye-catching positional switches, by the time of kick-off, it almost felt as if a reminder was needed that there was still a game to be played.

Some two hours later, with the banned Rassie Erasmsus having watched on from a nearby hotel with only a host of virtual followers on a near-silent YouTube stream for company, his Springboks side had lost consecutive games for the first time in four years.

If his comments this week were designed to illicit a response from his side after last week's loss to France, he did not get the desired reaction. Instead, it was Ireland who it seemed had appropriately fuelled the fire.

Virtually scrummaged off the park in this fixture last year, prop Andrew Porter was among those who said that day had stuck with them in the months since.

While the tables were not turned at scrum-time, rather than allow the set-piece to again become the dominant thread of the game, this time around Ireland weathered the storm and came out the other side.

Their aerial work in particular was key, with Farrell typically highlighting Robert Baloucoune's efforts to regather possession before mentioning the winger's second double in as many weeks.

"We were very good at just getting on with it," added the head coach.

"It's not always going to go your way against the world's best, certainly at scrum-time, they're world-leading in that regard. We took everything in isolation and just kept going on to the next moment.

"That was the story of the game, really."

Even if South Africa had wrestled the game back in those frantic closing moments, this would not have been a loss to catalogue alongside the others against South Africa, France and New Zealand in recent years.

Having started 2026 with their intent questioned after defeat in Paris, Ireland played their last home game of the year showing an appreciation that in these sorts of contests, it is the small moments that make for big results. 

A first-half scrum penalty of their own was greeted as raucously as a score while, whether it be a Joe McCarthy maul turnover, Mack Hansen's try-saving tackle on Canan Moodie or Hugo Keenan eating up the ground to thwart Kurt-Lee Arendse, Ireland continued to show that this time they were to stay in the fight. 

There will still be talk of whether this was truly Ireland absorbing South Africa's best punch - with both the Boks and All Blacks having lost twice in succession, questions will be asked of how much the four-Test Rugby's Greatest Rivalry series has taken out of both sides. 

"We're not playing to our best potential," said captain Siya Kolisi.

"We know as a group we have better. I don't question the effort or the mindset, but it's executing better."

Still, Ireland must believe that this was a game that said more about themselves than their opponents.
