Welsh cricketer Aneurin Donald is set to experience one of the most special moments of his life when he opens the batting for England in Cardiff on Thursday.

Donald, 29, made his international debut in the crushing 119-run opening T20 victory against Sri Lanka in Southampton on Tuesday, 15 September.

He scored 12 from five balls with captain Harry Brook hitting a brutal unbeaten 114 not out from 49 balls and Jos Buttler scoring 80.

Two days on - set to be the first man who has represented Glamorgan to play for England since Simon Jones in 2005 - Donald is looking forward to his Welsh homecoming.

"Getting an international cap on Welsh soil would one of the most special moments in my career," said Donald.

"I've got the opportunity to fly the flag for everyone that's put some time and energy into me, but not just that, the wider Welsh game.

"It's an added responsibility but I'm determined to try and fly the flag."

Aneurin Donald was presented with his first cap ahead of the first Vitality T20 International Series match between England and Sri Lanka at Utilita Bowl with his family present

Donald will be representing Gorseinon cricket club, located in a Swansea suburb, with his parents Andrew and Jocelyn, brother Gafyn among his biggest supporters, alongside Aneurin's partner Heather.

"It would make all the cold, wet winter days in our little one indoor lane shed down there worth it," said Donald.

"It [Gorseinon] is such a special club. Everyone has made sacrifices, from my family through to all my coaches and everywhere I've been before. 

"I am sure there'll be a few people coming up and my family have probably bought a stand! There would be a loud corner of people cheering for me."

Donald, who came through the Glamorgan ranks, has been rewarded for his fine form for Derbyshire in the T20 Blast and Trent Rockets in the Hundred.

Following an injury to fellow Welshman Phil Salt, Donald is opening the England batting, with Buttler his partner. 

"It's surreal and I don't think I've quite come to terms with it," said Donald.

"If you spoke to me a month ago, I'm not sure I would have had this on my radar. It has been been a whirlwind few weeks but a cool place to be.

"Everything you do as a youngster and through your teenage and professional years is building up to this moment."

Aneurin Donald hit a six in his first over of international cricket

Donald became the oldest England batting T20 debutant in nine years, with the achievement coming a decade after he captained the under-19 side.

He has had to overcome serious knee injuries suffered in 2019 and 2020 to fulfill his potential.

So did he fear this day would never come? 

"Without a doubt," he replied. "I thought this had maybe passed me by. 

"I came onto the scene with a lot of opportunity and this (England) was always the aim.

"It is everything you work for but I've had my fair share of ups and downs throughout my career.

"Certainly with my couple of ACL injuries and a few tough years afterwards, an international cap would have been the furthest thing on my list, I was just trying to get back to playing professional sport and hoping my career was not over.

"You think it's probably got away but you never give up. Suddenly here I am."

Aneurin Donald left Glamorgan in 2018 to join Hampshire before moving to Derbyshire in 2023

Donald has established himself as a top-order hitter and excelled in the past couple of seasons.

Captaining Derbyshire this season, he scored 489 runs in 12 matches in the Vitality Blast and hit the most sixes in this year's competition with 37.

That was rewarded with a place in the Trent Rockets squad and he helped them reach the Hundred final, with his strike rate above 200 in both competitions.

Donald also scored 31 from just eight balls for England Lions in a victory against Sri Lanka A at Chelmsford on Friday.

"I've always had an attacking mindset and ability to hit the ball," said Donald.

"In the ever-changing cricket landscape, that's a good place to be.

"The last few years I have doubled down on what am I good at and worked out what my point of difference is.

"I feel as if I've whole-heartedly bought into that and I have to thank Derbyshire for the way they have helped me."  

Donald's final achievement was to impress England white-ball coach Brendon McCullum in the Hundred final at Lord's with 38 runs in 19 balls.

"I guess the final tick in the box is being able to get a few runs under pressure on a big stage, while having the England head coach there watching was the ideal setup," said the explosive batter.

"International coaches want to see it with their own eyes and confirm the numbers I've been putting up. 

"Hopefully I can play a brand he enjoys and wants to play with England."

Aneurin Donald played in the second half of the Trent Rockets Hundred campaign after coming in for the injured Tom Banton

Donald is expecting not to be asked to change his approach this week.

"I have been given an opportunity to play a specific role and hopefully bring some wins for England," said Donald.

"That is what's got me to this point, it's a unique way of playing but feel like that's the best for me. 

"It's my job to break open games, get ahead of the rate and put on as much pressure as I can earlier, stretching the powerplay.

"I feel I'm right up there with anyone in the country doing that and when you look at all the players around me, it leans even more for me to take on that role.

"When you have got some world-class players sat behind you there's no point in messing around and wasting balls."

Donald certainly does not want to just make up the numbers.

"I want to show I can compete at this level, not just play a small role, but hopefully win games for England," he added.

"On a more holistic level, it is trying to soak it all in.

"I hope I'm going to get more opportunities after this but if this is all I get, it's about enjoying every moment. 

"It's about trying not to get caught up in the whirlwind.

"You never know where things end up. It's already been a life-changing few weeks and hopefully that's going to carry on to a few more."
