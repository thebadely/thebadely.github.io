Perhaps unusually, it is a headline that encompasses three separate stories from Thursday -  the fires that raged across the West Midlands, the rail accident near Lewes that left two people seriously injured, and the ongoing heatwave that saw the UK record its hottest day of 2026. 

By contrast, the Daily Mail opts for a much simpler single-word headline "Inferno" across a picture of  burning houses in the West Midlands. It opens its coverage of the hottest day of the year saying: "Wildfires caused apocalyptic scenes last night as they engulfed homes."

A similar photo of the houses on fire is also splashed across the front of today's Daily Mirror beside the headline "Britain on fire". It also runs an inset on the train derailment near Lewes as part of its coverage of what it calls "Heatwave devastation."

The Daily Star knows when it is a day for serious news - and chooses the train crash to focus upon, with the stark headline "Horror on the 2.24pm" . A large photo shows emergency crews near the overturned train lying in shrubs next to the railway. It also runs a small image of the burning homes with the headline "'Apocalyptic' wildfire".

The Daily Telegraph front page also carries a large photo of the derailed train with people standing atop it, but the larger headline of its front page is on claims about "China 'funding Palestine marches'." The paper says a "US committee claims Beijing is supporting anti-Israel protests on Britain's streets" after examining whether China has been backing the activities of Code Pink, "a far-left feminist protest group established in America".

Metro is alone in choosing the high temperature as its main focus for the front page.
