According to new developments, All four babies have names that start with N, in tribute to their parents

A specialist "quad squad" of 30 clinicians helped deliver a shocked couple's four babies.

When doctors told Glenna Alphonse and John Poul Josh they were expecting quadruplets, the gobsmacked couple did not believe them and insisted the scan was wrong.

But it was not, and in what was likened to "a military operation" they became one of only a handful of couples on average to have four babies in the UK each year.

Following the one in 700,000 pregnancy, the two boys and two girls arrived earlier this month and were continuing to receive specialist neonatal care to help them feed and grow.

"At first, I argued with them, saying 'it's not quadruplets', said Glenna.

"But they took the scan a couple of times and called in experienced colleagues, and it was true.

"It gave us a big shock."

Following what was described as a complex pregnancy, the babies were born at Cardiff's University Hospital of Wales (UHW) at 27 weeks.

They weighed between 745g (1lb 10.3oz) and 1.18kg (2lb 9.6oz) and have since been moved to Singleton Hospital in Swansea, to be nearer the family home.

As the common letter in Glenna and John is "N", all the new arrivals have a name starting with that.

Some staff members worked over shifts or returned from home to help with the births

The couple first discovered they were expecting quadruplets during a scan at the Princess of Wales Hospital in Bridgend.

Because of the complexity of the pregnancy, Glenna was referred to Dr Bryan Beattie - the lead consultant for fetal medicine in Wales, based in Cardiff.

"At first we were scared," said John Poul.

"But we met Dr Beattie and he gave us very good support. He explained that the team would give us good care and told us not to worry about anything.

"He arranged counselling for us and that was very helpful."

But following a routine scan at 22 weeks, the pregnancy took an unexpected turn.

"We were planning a baby shower and gender reveal party on the Sunday," said Glenna.

"But on the Thursday, at 22 weeks, we went for a regular scan.

"It was found that the cervix was close to opening. We had to cancel everything."

Most new parents are kept busy, but Glenna, John Poul and his mum Mary-Helen are set to have a bit more on their plates when the babies arrive home

Glenna was admitted to UHW and the specialist team worked to give her babies as much time as possible before they were born.

So they did not arrive too early, a cervical stitch, known as a cerclage, was inserted.

Glenna then spent five weeks in hospital with regular reviews.

"Mum was a brilliant part of the team. She was incredibly patient," said Dr Beattie.

"She was very, very uncomfortable and, from her point of view, it would have been nice to have delivered the babies long before.

"But she just put up with everything that we suggested in the best interest of the babies."

A specialist team calling themselves the "quad squad" was formed and they put together a plan to deliver the babies.

They prepared for two scenarios - an urgent delivery due to complications, or elective delivery by caesarean section, external. 

A theatre was identified, with space for each baby, while a specialist backup rota of medical and nursing staff was created in case of any issues.

The four babies are continuing to receive treatment in hospital 

When Glenna went into labour spontaneously at 27 weeks, the team's preparations were put into action.

"It was like a military operation," said Consultant Neonatologist Dr Rachel Hayward.

"A team of five neonatal staff was allocated to each baby, in addition to the theatre team plus NICU (intensive care) staff looking after the rest of the neonatal unit and attending other deliveries."

Staff stayed beyond shifts, while some returned from home to offer support for an extra few hours.

"I had been struggling mentally, but after seeing my babies' faces the struggle was gone," admitted Glenna.

"It was totally, fully worth it."

John Poul agreed, paying tribute to the team who "gave 200% for us".

He added: "Everything was gone. There was only happiness."
