It has been recently reported that "New prisons outrage" declares the Sun, writing that "jet bomb plotters" could be "free in weeks" after launching parole bids. The paper quotes the parole board's response: "Reviews are undertaken thoroughly and with extreme care. Protecting the public is our number one priority."

"Days of danger" is the Daily Mirror's headline, leading with swimming warnings as "temperatures set to soar again". It writes that campaigners for water safety, made up of bereaved families, are sounding the alarm of "open water swimming" amid "40 water-related tragedies this summer". 

The i Paper says "sixteen million people could see their areas move into drought status this week", meaning that "car washes and swimming pools could face water bans" amid heatwave conditions. It could be the first time in two decades that such water restrictions are "authorised", according to the paper.

Several papers lead with the possible consequences of the government's early prisoner release scheme. "Sister of student mown down by 'drunkest driver' demands he is not released early" is the Daily Express' lead story. Fenella Shepherd's younger sibling Rosie issues the plea: "You must keep our Fenella's killer in jail", the paper declares.

"Labour jail plan to spark 89,000 more crimes," warns the Daily Mail in its headline, citing analysis commissioned by the Conservative Party that suggests "sex offenders, robbers, arsonists and violent criminals" could commit the extra offences once they are released.

The Guardian says: "Labour wants 'deeper' ties to EU under Burnham, says minister", quoting Prime Minister Andy Burnham's "new chief post-Brexit negotiator" and cabinet minister "in charge of the EU reset talks" Hamish Falconer.
