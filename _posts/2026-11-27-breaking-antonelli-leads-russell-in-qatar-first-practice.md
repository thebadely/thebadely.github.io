World champion Kimi Antonelli headed Mercedes team-mate George Russell in first practice at the penultimate race of the season in Qatar. 

The 20-year-old Italian posted his fastest time of 1:23.217 on the medium tyre, while Russell's marker, 0.507 seconds adrift, was set on the soft compound. 

Red Bull's Isack Hadjar finished third, more than six tenths back on Antonelli, with McLaren's Lando Norris and Oscar Piastri fourth and fifth, 0.850 and 0.860 seconds back. 

Four-time world champion Max Verstappen ended his session three places lower than team-mate Hadjar in sixth, almost a second off the pace, and complained of shaking on the rear axle of his car.  

Drivers will be limited to a maximum of 27 laps on a set of tyres at the Lusail International Circuit, meaning teams will have to make at least two pit stops during the 57-lap grand prix on Sunday, which starts at 16:00 GMT.

The decision is caused by concern over the integrity of the Pirelli tyres on the high-speed, 16-corner track. Laps to the grid, formation laps and any laps completed after the end of the race will not be included. 

Lewis Hamilton asked his Ferrari pit wall if anyone else experienced tyre degradation "because mine is massive", to which his engineer replied: "Yes, much higher than expected on all compounds." 

Hamilton smashed into a marker board during the opening hour in Qatar, while Aston Martin's Lance Stroll, the Williams of Carlos Sainz and Alex Albon plus Gasly also had moments off the dusty track. 

Charles Leclerc was seventh quickest, one place above Scuderia team-mate Hamilton, with Racing Bulls rookie Arvid Lindblad and the Alpine of Pierre Gasly completing the top 10 in ninth and 10th. 

Red Bull were running different specifications of front wing in first practice as they continue their investigation into what caused Hadjar's failure during last weekend's race in Las Vegas.  

Russell is looking to salvage something from a difficult season which has seen him slip to fourth in the drivers' championship after his early push for the title. 

He finished ninth in Las Vegas a week ago but the headlines were dominated by his latest fallout with race winner Max Verstappen. The pair exchanged strong words towards each other but on Thursday, Russell said the spat was not something he was looking to continue. 

Ferrari are also dealing with the aftermath of another on-track incident between seven-time world champion Hamilton and Monegasque Leclerc. 

The two made contact in Vegas following their collisions in Italy and Mexico and Hamilton conceded himself, Leclerc and team principal Fred Vasseur were facing "very, very challenging times". 

Qatar and next week's finale in Abu Dhabi are going ahead as planned despite the ongoing conflict between the United States/Israel and Iran.
