French tennis player Arthur Rinderknech says he expects to be exonerated over allegations he refused a doping test.

The International Tennis Integrity Agency (ITIA) confirmed the world number 57 is under investigation, after a report by L'Equipe, external earlier on Wednesday.

It relates to a "surprise" test in June, with French newspaper L'Equipe claiming there was a "possible misunderstanding with the sample collector".

Former Wimbledon champion Marketa Vondrousova is currently serving a four-year ban for refusing to take an out-of-competition doping test in December 2025, and has appealed against the decision.

Rinderknech issued a statement on Instagram in response to L'Equipe's report, saying: "I have already advised the ITIA that I deny the allegation and I have already requested that the matter be heard and determined by an independent tribunal, as is my right."

The 31-year-old, who beat current world number one Alexander Zverev in the first round at Wimbledon last year, believes he will then be "exonerated of the test refusal allegation".

"I have never taken any banned substance and I am proud of my accomplishments in professional tennis, all of which have been achieved through hard work and with integrity always," he added.

The ITIA said the case would be heard by a tribunal early next year and expressed regret that the investigation was first reported by a media outlet.

"We go to great lengths to keep cases confidential, and we are disappointed that the details have become public in this way," an ITIA statement added.
