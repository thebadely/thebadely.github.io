A man who mistakenly travelled to Spain using his wife's passport has said he is still baffled about how he was able to make the trip without being stopped in the UK.

Ben Moore, from Talke, in Staffordshire, noticed there was a problem soon after getting off his Ryanair flight while he was queuing to get his passport checked at Alicante airport.

"I opened up the passport there and, to my horror, it was my wife's photo on the passport," he said. "The lads who I was with basically said the colour drained out of me immediately."

Third-party provider Swissport, which carries out Ryanair's checks at Manchester Airport where Moore flew from, said it was now reinforcing mandatory boarding procedures.

Moore, who was travelling to Alicante on a stag party with friends, told BBC Radio Stoke he initially thought he must have picked up his wife's passport in addition to his own.

He said he approached Spanish police who - along with the language barrier - appeared to be as confused as he was about how he had turned up there without his own passport.

"I searched through all my luggage about eight times thinking there's no way I could have got to Alicante without my own passport," he said.

"They could not believe that I'd got to Spain on my wife's passport."

Ben Moore's celebrations were cut short after he accidentally picked up the wrong passport

Moore added that he was "treated a bit like a criminal," and had found their handling of the situation "a little bit intimidating".

He owns a property in Spain and said he did the journey a number of times each year.

On this occasion, however, his trip ended with him being returned to the UK while his friends continued their holiday without him.

Manchester Airport said the responsibility for checking passports was with the airlines and that airports only needed boarding cards from passengers when they are passing through security.

The Department for Transport confirmed it was the responsibility of airlines to check passports, as part of the check-in and boarding process.

It added that passengers were screened for security purposes before boarding an aircraft.

A spokesperson for Swissport UK & Ireland said: "We take our document verification and security responsibilities extremely seriously.

"We are carrying out a full internal review into this incident and are actively reinforcing mandatory boarding check procedures with our operational teams."
