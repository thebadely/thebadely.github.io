Here’s what you need to know: Ipswich and Everton fans stood to applaud in Noah's memory as a picture of him was shown on the big screen

A tribute to three-year-old Noah Woods was shown on the big screen ahead of the Everton vs Ipswich Town Premier League match, while players wore black armbands in his memory.

The toddler became separated from a relative after visiting a playground in Merriam Close, Brantham, on Tuesday afternoon, Suffolk Police said.

About 1,300 members of the public joined a search to find Noah, who police said was non-verbal, partially deaf and had been undergoing an assessment for autism.

Met Police divers recovered his body from a lake, known locally as Decoy Pond, on Wednesday afternoon and Suffolk Police said his death was not being treated as suspicious.

Three minutes into the game, won 1-0 by the home side, both sets of supporters stood to applaud in memory of Noah, Everton said in a post on X, external.

Captain Dara O'Shea and goalkeeper Christian Walton were among the club delegation who laid flowers at the playground where he went missing.

He spoke to the BBC after the Everton game, describing how he joined search efforts after Ipswich played Arsenal on Tuesday and stayed until 03:00 BST the following morning.

Davis explained his recent experience of becoming a father himself made the situation feel close to home.

He said: "Even if I wasn't a new father, I would have been out there... It's something that I would want someone else someone to do if it was my little girl or boy.

"It was a sad thing, it really hit us, and I hope his family and everyone close to him are strong and stay safe as well."

On Thursday, Noah's father Rhys Woods paid tribute to his son in a message shared on a community Facebook group.

He wrote: "There are no words to describe the pain of this loss, we are all absolutely heartbroken, Noah was the sweetest and most innocent little boy, with nothing but love to give, and we are going to miss him so very much."

Suffolk Police said on Thursday that the body of the child recovered by police divers had been formally identified as Noah, subject to confirmation at formal inquest proceedings.

The force said police still have a number of inquiries to complete before submitting a file for the coroner.
