Let's dive into the details: Downing Street has not ruled out inviting the creator of a video which was branded "not acceptable" by Andy Burnham to future events at Number 10.

Conservative leader Kemi Badenoch called on the PM to apologise for "debasing his office" by allowing Max Balegde to record a video on the steps of Number 10, which included "vile" and "obscene" comments about women.

Burnham said the video had been taken down and Balegde had "rightly apologised" for it - but he said he would not apologise for engaging with influencers who could reach young people.

Asked repeatedly whether Balegde would be invited back, the prime minister's official spokesman said: "I'm not going to get ahead of future events."

Max Balegde has 3.6m followers on TikTok and 640,000 on Instagram

The spokesman was responding to a comment the influencer filmed after he had left the event on 9 November and posted in a YouTube video before the public criticism.

In it, Max Balegde said of the event the PM hosted, "well guys that was a huge success, Andy's invited me round for tea".

The PM's spokesman was unable to confirm whether that invite was legitimate or whether a further meeting would go ahead.

Downing Street also could not say whether Belegde had ever been paid taxpayer money to collaborate with the government on social media posts.

No influencers were paid to attend the Downing Street reception, the PM's spokesman added, but influencers have in the past been paid when working with other government departments.

About 60 content creators were invited to the Downing Street reception on 9 November, with videos from the event, including of Burnham DJing, subsequently emerging online.

In one video, Max Balegde, whose real name is Max Trobe, mouths the words "bomboclaat pussywagon chicken nuggets", using audio from a meme that is trending online.

At Prime Minister's Questions, Badenoch said the words used were "so offensive that I will not repeat them in this House". 

"Disgusting and pejorative comments about women's private parts, obscene language that referred to white women as cheap meat.

"This is grotesque. Labour MPs constantly tell us about their commitment to fighting violence against women and girls. Those MPs were silent yesterday, I won't be."

Burnham said the Tory leader was "right to call out that, because it was not acceptable" but he would "not apologise" for engaging with young people.

"She sneers at TikTokers, she sneers at influencers," he added, accusing Badenoch of "having nothing positive to say to the country" and of "constantly talking it down".

The two leaders continued the argument throughout their weekly Commons clash, with Burnham saying "it's true I have spoken to influencers, and then I have to come to this House, with the opposite of influencers".

Badenoch said the PM preferred to "take questions to people dancing around Downing Street", a reference to a video of another content creator dancing through Number 10 and down the stairs in an attempt to recreate a scene from the film Love, Actually.

This prompted Burnham to make a jibe about "lockdown parties" in Downing Street during the Covid pandemic.

Responding to the row about his now-deleted TikTok video in an Instagram post on Tuesday, Balegde said: "My content is always meant to be silly, fun, and light-hearted.

"If anybody was offended by that video, I sincerely apologise."

But he said "nobody was offended for weeks until revolting right-wing people tried to put this disgusting narrative on it".

"And if anybody then looks at the video and is offended, I get that. So hence why I took it down because I think that is disgusting."

Sophie Tea, from the West Midlands, now lives and works in London

Videos which were filmed inside the Downing Street premises were recorded on government phones.

Attendees at the event have confirmed they had to hand personal devices in and were given a phone and a camera to film with, with the footage they recorded sent to them after the event.

Artist Sophie Tea, who has more than 1.3 million followers on TikTok, told BBC Radio 4's Today programme she had been "excited" to be invited to the Downing Street reception, describing it as a "privilege". 

"I believe in the democratization of information and I think this opportunity was perfect for me to be able to understand more what's going on. 

"Number 10 is meant to represent the whole country and the more people feel like they belong there, the better."

She said she was looking to use her social media platform to "communicate to the UK some of the brilliant things that are happening in government".

Asked if she was being used by the government, she said "absolutely" but it was "mutually exploitative" because, as an independent artist, she had used it to "prop up my own value".

This isn't the first time influencers have been invited into Downing Street. Former Prime Minister Rishi Sunak was interviewed by content creators during his time in office.

Nor is it the first time they have descended onto Number 10 en masse, with the first content creator reception taking place under Sir Keir Starmer's leadership in 2025.

Content creators are also regularly invited to press conferences and government briefings, including an event at Number 11 Downing Street on the day of the Budget last month.

Writing on X, Luke Tryl, the UK director of pollsters More In Common, said it was "sensible" for Burnham to use influencers to reach a wider audience and it was "helping the PM seem in touch". 

But, he added, the PM's "biggest weakness" was being seen as a "people pleaser" and he should also do "non-softball interviews with traditional scrutiny media" to help tackle that.
