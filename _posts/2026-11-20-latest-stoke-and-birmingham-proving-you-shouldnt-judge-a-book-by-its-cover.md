Stoke City v Birmingham City: Third v first in the Championship.

That is a "tale of two cities" that did not look too likely to get written when Mark Robins' Potters started this season with three straight defeats and Blues won only two of their first eight league games under Chris Davies.

But, just like you should never judge a book by its cover, the footballing equivalent is to never judge any league table until after 10 games.

The season is now three months old - after Saturday's meeting at the Bet365 Stadium it will be more than a third of the way in.

And a clearer picture is starting to emerge as to whether what was once the "worst of times", especially in Stoke's case, might yet become the "best of times" before this 46-game campaign ends.

With 15 games gone, there are three clubs right at the top all on 29 points in what is already looking a ridiculously tight Championship table.

And two of them meet head on in front of an expected full house in the Potteries, having both been bang in form going into the latest international break.

Stoke are on a run of nine wins and only one defeat in 12 league games since the start of September, while Blues have won six out of seven since football returned after the first autumn international break.

So, how will things start to shape up after the second such break?

Davies: 'We have to keep adapting'

Stoke had the edge last season, winning 1-0 at home in September before drawing 1-1 in the return at St Andrew's in January, prior to Blues going into their late-season decline - and Davies can see the difference in the Potters.

"I watched the game back from when we played them a year ago and it looks very different," the Blues boss told BBC Radio WM.

"He [Mark Robins] has made a big impact from where they were last season. A real stamp of his identity.

"They've gone to a back five and, since then, they've conceded the fewest goals in the league - nine in 12 games.

"They've looked strong, particularly in the central areas. They've got bigger. They're now one of the most physical starting XIs in the league."

While extolling the Potters' defensive qualities, Davies is also well aware they have an attacking threat and he is an admirer of Stoke's American winger Jack McGlynn, who is their top scorer with five goals.

"I already knew a little bit about the boy McGlynn," said Davies. "He's been exceptional with the goals he's got.

"They're a team much like ourselves. I want us to go there as a measure to see how far we've come, if we can display what we've been showing recently."

Mark Robins' Stoke and Birmingham City, under Chris Davies, have both recovered from dodgy starts to sit tied on 29 points

Stoke boss Mark Robins' main concern is determining the fitness of his international contingent, having had 10 out of his 20-man matchday squad from their 2-1 win at Portsmouth away for almost a fortnight.

Of the 14 players involved that day, Swedish keeper Viktor Johansson, Bosun Lawal (Ireland), Robert Bozenik (Slovenia), Luke Graham (Scotland) and euphoric Northern Ireland pair Ethan Galbraith and Justin Devenny all played, while McGlynn was part of new Manchester United boss Mauricio Pochettino's USA send-off.

But now focus turns to Birmingham.

"They've changed a little bit," Robins told BBC Midlands Today. "They've clearly taken a lot from the Championship last season.

"They've got some players in form and some coming back from international duty, although less than we have.

"A lot [of their players] have been together a few seasons now - and they've got some real talent. But it gives us a chance to pit ourselves against the team at the top. The last time we did that was against Charlton."

Having already seen off one table-topping team in the Potteries this season, Robins is not getting carried away though.

"It's nice to be where we are," he added. "We've never been in the conversation before and we want to be in that conversation."

Stoke will hold a minute's applause before the game to mark the recent passings of Potters legend Peter Dobing and former manager Gudjon Thordarson.

Robins: 'We want more all the time'

One local celebrity fan looking forward to Saturday's game more than most is a man with a foot in both camps - ex-Birmingham and Stoke full-back Ian Clarkson.  

Boyhood Blues fan Clarkson made 174 appearances for the club between 1988 and 1993, when he moved on to Stoke, where he made a further 95 appearances.

After seeing out his career with Northampton Town, Kidderminster Harriers and Nuneaton Borough, Clarkson, now 55, then took his talents into the press box, reporting on Blues first for the Express & Star then the Birmingham Post - and he now coaches football in his teaching role at Repton College.

He has seen plenty of both clubs this season - and both have taken him slightly by surprise.

"Blues are a lot more inclined to attack than they were last season, they look more likely to take a few risks," Clarkson told BBC Sport.

"The signing of August Priske in January is obviously starting to reap rewards and it's bringing the best out of Jay Stansfield. Plus you've got good contributions coming from their wingers and two more on the bench to come on.

"Blues are playing some lovely football right now. Chris Davies is obviously a manager who works very hard at it. And they just seem to have got it right. They've clicked."

Ian Clarkson still sees a lot of Blues, working both as a BBC Radio WM summariser and some weeks as a St Andrew's matchday corporate host, a job he also does for the Potters

"Stoke are starting to look more like a Stoke City team, in the nicest possible way.

"Mark Robins is just one of those managers who, wherever he is, just seems to get better. The longer he stays at a club the better he gets. And they really do look far more resilient. 

"There's something about the place that just feels different this season. They've got the fans back on side and that's more important than anything. Everyone knows how tough a place Stoke can be when it's bouncing."

As to who will win on Saturday? And where they might be come season's end?

"It's way too early to be talking in those terms," added Clarkson. "Let's be honest, I'm a Blues fan, so in any season I'd want Blues to finish top, then Stoke second. And they've both made great starts. But it's too soon to say. There's a long, long way to go."
