Highlights: Hull shock Man United on opening weekend

"We are trying to prove people wrong."

Hull City certainly managed to support their manager Sergej Jakirovic's desire as they shocked Manchester United - and many neutral observers - with a 2-0 win on their return to the Premier League.

The Tigers have been predicted by many to finish bottom, having only squeezed into the Premier League via last season's Championship play-offs.

After finishing third in the previous campaign, United were again being tipped to finish at least in the Champions League places.

But Jakirovic's side claimed a memorable first league win over United since a Second Division success in 1974 thanks to first-half goals from Semi Ajayi and fellow defender Nobel Mendy - one of 11 summer signings and with more to come.

After their first Premier League game in nine years - the last being a 7-1 defeat at home by Tottenham Hotspur in May 2017 - boss Jakirovic was asked about the club's doubters by BBC Sport.

"This is football. We are professionals," said the first person from Bosnia-Herzegovina to manage in the Premier League.

"We are trying to prove people wrong. I know there are 37 games to go but this is a good base for the future."

Nigeria centre-back Ajayi, released by West Brom just over a year ago, added: "It's just one game, I don't think we've shut everyone up yet. 

"We are here to prove [something] to ourselves. We aren't worried about the pressure from outside. We have to keep putting in performances like that and maybe people will stop sleeping on us."

Mike White, BBC Radio Humberside's sports editor and Hull City commentator, said: "Everyone wrote them off before a ball was kicked, with questions or even predictions that they'd 'do a Derby' [for the lowest points tally in Premier League history]. 

"It was a somewhat lazy, but in so many ways, understandable prediction - because why wouldn't you tip them to go down as the last team to come up and who've had questions about some of their recruitment?"

The manner of the Tigers' promotion made their status as relegation favourites even more pronounced.

Hull only squeezed into the play-offs, overtaking Wrexham into sixth spot on the final day.

They then became the first team to finish sixth and win the play-offs since Blackpool in 2010.

Having beaten Millwall, they won the final 1-0 against Middlesbrough - whose place had only been confirmed three days before after semi-final opponents Southampton were expelled after being punished for spying.

They also gained promotion despite having the fifth-fewest shots and facing the third most shots in the division.

Hull's achievement was made even more remarkable by the fact they had only avoided relegation on the final day - and on goal difference - in 2025.

Five of the starting line-up against United played for the Tigers in that near-relegation season.

Boss Jakirovic spoke out this week after former Premier League players Joe Cole, Carlton Cole and Wayne Bridge mocked Hull's chances of survival on a podcast.

BBC reporter White said: "The comments went beyond understandable observations and made it more personal - resorting to ridicule and mockery and it's rubbed a few people up the wrong way. 

"What it did do was act as extra motivation for a Tigers side who were already fired up to prove people wrong, or more importantly prove to themselves that they're more than a match for football's elite."

Jakirovic had told the BBC: "It's easy to say we will be 20th. If you are so convinced you can bet on it. Earn a lot of money or lose.

"Every prediction is against us. Even for me it is normal. I don't like to see this disrespect of worth against us."

By contrast, the player who crossed the free-kick for Mendy to nod in was Regan Slater, who played for the club on loan in League One, and then cost just Â£50,000 to sign from Sheffield United.

The collective effort impressed BBC pundits and former England captains Wayne Rooney and Alan Shearer.

"Hull's shape was really good," said ex-United striker Rooney. "They stuck to the job and worked hard which is what they're going to have to do to try and stay in this league.

"And obviously, it looked like they identified a weakness in United in putting balls into the box and utilised that really well. 

"All they can do is try to take as many points at home as they can. It's a great start."

Shearer added: "Hull have been absolutely outstanding. They've been miles the better team."

Already the talk of whether Hull could even break Derby's record low of 11 Premier League points in 2007-08 has died down.

And Jakirovic said this result might also make them more appealing to potential signings before the end of the transfer window.

"Even now, players we wanted, it might make their decision easier. We have to continue like this," he added.

Commentator White said: "Today might just be one Premier League win from what will obviously be an incredibly challenging 38-game season, but it's what it represents that means as much as anything.

"Sergej Jakirovic has once again engineered the masterplan to overcome the odds. However, is unquestionably the biggest achievement to date, although the play-off run and the final itself still takes some beating."

The 32-year-old Ajayi, who netted the opener after Oli McBurnie's shot had been tipped on to the post, said: "We've tried to block out the outside noise and focused on what's inside the building and what the gaffer is telling us. 

"We came out the blocks firing, had a really good start. All in all a really good performance and something to build on for the rest of the season.

"We know what we have in the squad, it was about proving that to ourselves and everyone else."
