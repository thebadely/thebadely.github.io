As the dust settles on Wales' historic victory over New Zealand, work is under way to secure another major scalp.

Steve Tandy's side ensured their place in history by ending the 73-year wait for a win against the All Blacks.

Now they must back up what many are describing as the one of the greatest results in Welsh rugby history by beating Australia in Cardiff on Saturday.

Wales have won just one of their past five meetings with the Wallabies and are without a victory over them in Cardiff since 2021.

But victory would complete only their second ever clean sweep of autumn internationals following the statement win on Saturday.

"[Beating the All Blacks] wasn't even by fluke, that's the nicest thing," former Wales centre Tom Shanklin told the Scrum V podcast.

"It wasn't like there were two or three cards for New Zealand. Wales did it the hard way and deserved to win."

So what must Wales get right this week on the final weekend of the Nations Championship group stage?

Not often can rugby results be genuinely described as historic, but the euphoria surrounding a first win over New Zealand since 1953 can easily be excused.

Players and fans broke down in tears at the final whistle on Saturday, as much in surprise as relief. The wait to beat the All Blacks again has been agonising.

But Tandy now has the immensely tough challenge of lifting his players once again to somewhere near that emotional and physical peak.

"Wales need to have that same mindset and belief, that's the biggest task after that emotional high," said Shanklin.

"I think they will be alright. Games like this can change your career [and] Australia are there for the taking."

Former Wales fly-half James Hook added: "We were in a world of pain after going out of the [2007] World Cup, then we go to Twickenham and win when nobody believed we could. The week after against Scotland, we just didn't think we could lose - and we didn't.

"These boys will go into that game against Australia and will feel that they can't lose and hopefully, the crowd gives them the ovation they deserve."

Victory over New Zealand was inspired by Wales' epic defensive stand.

Beauden Barrett said it felt like New Zealand were running into a red brick wall as Wales racked up a remarkable 346 tackles.

Tandy brought in ex-Glasgow defence coach Peter Murchie during the summer to work alongside contact specialist Dan Lydiate and the move is paying off.

Shanklin believes the mind-set and physical comitment was reminiscent of the demands of Grand Slam-winning defence coach Shaun Edwards.

"There was the drive and mindset to keep them out and make big hits," he said.

"There was one player on the floor and the rest in the defensive line.

"They got back up every single time, nobody was slow. Every time the All Blacks looked up there was a red wall."

Australia may have lost their past four Tests and have just one victory in six - albeit against world champions South Africa - but they have regularly been free-scoring against Wales, including eight tries on their most recent trip to Cardiff in 2024.

Wales' attack finally sparked as they racked up a record try tally against New Zealand in Cardiff.

Tomos Williams, Dewi Lake, Archie Griffin, Eddie James and Louis Rees-Zammit crossed as Wales scored five tries against the All Blacks for the first time.

After labouring against Japan, Anscombe said New Zealand looked "shell-shocked" by the accuracy and physicality of the Welsh attack.

Shanklin added: "Wales nailed it. We saw them play with attacking options, width, tempo and quick ball.

"The attack looked slick with options everywhere. Nobody was running on their own and there was a tip-on either inside or outside. Wales made the right decisions and were clever in how they attacked."

Sam Costelow won his 24th cap against New Zealand

At the heart of that impressive attacking was fly-half Sam Costelow.

His place in the Wales team was in serious doubt following the uninspiring result against Japan a week earlier.

But Tandy ignored calls for Dan Edwards to take back the number ten 10 jersey and kept faith in the Scarlets fly-half.

Costelow responded with his best international performance, sparking Wales and crucially kicking all five conversions.

"I am rapt for Costy because he has been through the wars with Wales," said fellow Scarlet and former Wales fly-half Gareth Anscombe.

"He has taken a battering at times. He has been in the team for a while and came in at a young age when things were starting to turn the other way.

"It's tough as a 10 when you are sitting behind a team that is getting dominated up front. It's tough to pull rabbits out of the hat."

Shane Williams was among those calling for consistency in selection.

But Edwards, who started last season's wins against Italy and Fiji, made a telling contribution off the bench, not least when he denied Leicester Fainga'anuku a try.

Anscombe added: "Dan brings energy so potentially suits coming off the bench, particularly if Wales are trying to chase the game with his ability to use his legs to cause trouble."

Botham has 'always wanted to play for Wales'

As well as fly-half, Tandy faces a selection dilemma in his back-row having rung changes between the Tests against Japan and New Zealand.

Taine Basham hugely impressed against the All Blacks, as did Taine Plumtree off the bench, but Aaron Wainwright could be available following an ankle injury.

And while Alex Mann has been a regular first-choice at number six, his Cardiff team-mate James Botham took his place with a man of the match display on Saturday.

"Sometimes you have guys that play well at regional level [but] when they play at the Test arena they go up a couple of notches. Botham is like that," said Anscombe.

"He was physical, made tackles and got through so much work. He has to be a regular in the back-row, even though they are blessed with options."
