Let's dive into the details: Farrell says refereeing a rugby match is more complex than football 

Ireland head coach Andy Farrell says refereeing in rugby is "never going to be perfect" because it is such a complex sport and believes coaches have a duty to "protect the game".

Earlier this week, South Africa head coach Rassie Erasmus was banned for six matches for criticising match officials, the third time he has been sanctioned for such an offence. 

While Farrell did not comment specifically on Erasmus, the Ireland boss was asked why he often remains tight-lipped on officials when so many coaches across different sports air their views on refereeing standards.

"Because it [refereeing]'s tough. You're talking about football, that's easy to referee. Rugby's not," said Farrell.

"It's complex, you know. There's so many moving parts. I think it's one of the hardest sports in the world to referee so it's never going to be perfect. 

"Not just that, we need to make sure that we protect the game in general as far as we want people to referee our sport coming through as well."

During an extraordinary 35-minute news conference in Dublin before Saturday's Nations Championship match with Ireland, Erasmus said that match officials had missed 17 hip-drop or knee-drop tackles in his side's loss to France last week. 

Erasmus added that if television match officials (TMOs) were not intervening for reviews to limit the number of interruptions during a match then it was "manipulation".

"I'm saying if he didn't see it, it must be a technology problem. If he saw it and decided not to act on it, is that not match-fixing?" added the two-time World Cup-winning coach. 

During the news conference, Erasmus also predicted that Ireland fly-half Sam Prendergast would be available to face the Springboks despite being cited for a hip-drop tackle against Fiji. 

Prendergast was cleared after a panel ruled his tackle was not worthy of a red card.

Hip-drop and knee-drop tackles were outlawed before the start of the November internationals, and given the current scrutiny on officiating, Farrell was asked if extra pressure has been placed on Christophe Ridley, who will take charge of Ireland v South Africa.

"I hope not. Christophe is a top-class referee and if you know him professionally then you know he's a level-headed guy who's a very experienced referee and refereed both teams plenty of times," added Farrell. 

"I think he's the perfect guy to take this on."

Farrell is hoping his side can avenge last year's 24-13 loss to the Springboks in a chaotic game in which Ireland ended the first half with 12 players on the field. 

While Ireland have built towards Saturday's Test with the back-to-back world champions with victories over Argentina and Fiji, South Africa were edged out by France in an enthralling encounter at Stade de France last week.

Asked if Les Bleus showed Ireland anything different about how to beat the Boks, Farrell said: "Convert when you get the opportunity. 

"If you get a few chances, that's when you need to put the points on the board because they don't give you many, but France were excellent in that regard. I think that's the bigger picture part of the game. 

"We felt in the game last year, when we went down all those men and it was chaos all over the field that we still had a few opportunities that we didn't take so we have to better than that."
