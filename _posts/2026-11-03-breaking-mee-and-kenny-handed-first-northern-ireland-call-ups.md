Let's dive into the details: Dermot Mee and Eoin Kenny are both underage internationals with Northern Ireland

Manchester United goalkeeper Dermot Mee and Portsmouth forward Eoin Kenny have been handed their first Northern Ireland call-ups for November's final Nations League matches with Ukraine and Hungary.

Mee, 23, is a former Northern Ireland underage international and, although he has not played competitively for United this season, will add competition to NI's first choice goalkeeper Pierce Charles.

"He's been on [Under-]21s in the past so he knows a lot of the players," said manager Michael O'Neill. 

"He's currently third-choice at Manchester United, he's involved obviously in their Champions League squads and I had a good chat with Johnny Evans about him. He spoke very highly of him."

Attacker Kenny, who is the son of former Republic of Ireland manager Stephen, joined Portsmouth from Dundalk in July.

The 20-year-old has made one league appearance for the Championship club, but has been handed a first call after impressing for the Northern Ireland Under-21s under Tommy Wright. 

"I think this is really a reward for a strong campaign for Tommy in the 21s," added O'Neill.

"He made that move from from Dundalk to Portsmouth in the summer and it just gives us a chance to see him."

Striker Dion Charles and goalkeeper Bailey Peacock-Farrell drop out of the squad.

Sunderland defender Dan Ballard is missing after he injured his hamstring in the September win over Georgia in Tbilisi, while defender Eoin Toal, midfielders Ali McCann and Jamie McDonnell are also absent through injury.

Liverpool's Conor Bradley, who has not played since January, remains a long-term absentee. 

Family affair at Croke Park caps Mee's special night

Michael O'Neill's side are unbeaten at the top of League B2 and can earn promotion to the top tier for the first time.

Northern Ireland face Ukraine at Windsor Park on Saturday, 14 November at 19:45 GMT before they travel to Hungary in their final match on 17 November.

Both games are live on BBC iPlayer and BBC Sounds.

The average of Northern Ireland's squad is just 23.5, and 17 of the 25 players selected are aged 23 or under.

O'Neill admitted he would rather have the likes of the injured Ballard, Bradley, McCann and Toal available, but he knows the window will be important in building depth in the squad.

"I think that the squad has to continue to develop players, they have to step in and you have to give players the opportunity to do that," he said.

"It's a very young squad and it will be stretched again. We'll be tested in these two games, but we given ourselves a good chance with obviously what we've done to date in the games."

Northern Ireland are the only side left in the entire Nations League who have yet to concede a goal in this year's competition.

Shea Charles scored a last-gasp winner in Georgia in September before a goalless draw at home to Hungary.

A 3-0 victory over Ukraine was one of the most impressive performances from O'Neill's young side, and they ended the quadruple-header with another goalless draw, this time at home to Georgia.

If Northern Ireland can top the group they would not only win promotion to League A, but they would also be all but guaranteed of a play-off for Euro 2028.
