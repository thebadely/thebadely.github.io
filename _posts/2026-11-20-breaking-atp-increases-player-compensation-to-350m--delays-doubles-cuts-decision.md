The men's ATP Tour is promising its players rewards of at least $350m in 2028 - but the board has delayed a decision on hotly disputed changes to doubles until next month.

The bonus pool will increase from the current $21.5m to $50m in 2028, with payments offered to players ranked in the world's top 30 at the start of each Masters 1000 event.

The world number one will receive the highest bonus of $2m for each of the Masters events they play.

A total of $25m will also be shared out between the world's top 250 players at the end of each season, with the goal for everyone to receive $100,000.

That would make a significant difference to the finances of lower-ranked players.

Players will also no longer be penalised so directly for missing tournaments through injury.

World number two Jannik Sinner, for example, won five Masters events in a row between March and May, but may only qualify for 25% of his bonus as he has not been able to play since Wimbledon.

Proposed changes to the doubles - which includes halving draw sizes and reducing the percentage of prize money from 20% to 10% - have received fierce opposition from the players.

In October, more than 150 men's doubles players wrote an open letter to the ATP Tour urging them to reconsider "drastic" changes to the format.

Chairman Andrea Gaudenzi told BBC Sport the governing body of men's tennis needed "a bit more time to reach consensus".

"It is a sensitive issue," he said.

"I hope we find the right balance between the players' and the tournaments' demands, and I truly hope we reach a compromise."

The proposals will now be voted on next month.

Doubles players have said the changes would make it "effectively impossible" for anyone outside the top 30 to make a living from the sport.

There may be a hint of compromise in the air, but Gaudenzi still believes at least some of the doubles money needs to be redirected towards singles players.

"It's impossible to make everyone happy. It's something we do not take lightly, but we also cannot ignore the fans and the broadcasters - [just] like any entertainment business," he said.

"Whether you are a musician or an actor, it needs to be rewarded as a function of the entertainment you provide to the fans."

Gaudenzi told Britain's Henry Patten, who was unhappy he and CEO Eno Polo were not present when Patten and Harri Heliovaara received the year-end number one trophy, that he had no intention of undermining their achievement.

"I understand his disappointment and he's obviously also driven by what we are discussing, and I do understand it's quite emotional their side," Gaudenzi added.

"But I was here in the hotel together with Eno - we were actually fighting and negotiating twelve hours a day to get this deal done - so we were really in the trenches all day long."

The ATP Board has also approved the formation of ATP Ventures, which will bring all the Tour's media, data and central sponsorship rights under the same roof. This could improve the chances of the ATP and the WTA agreeing to pool their resources and create a 'Tennis Ventures'.

Probably the trickiest issue to resolve is the length of the season - there are just 33 days between this year's Davis Cup Finals and the start of the 2027 season in Australia.

The ATP schedule for 2028 is likely to be released in late January, but Gaudenzi warned not to expect "big changes".

"That's going to be difficult," Gaudenzi added.

"The off-season, I think, will improve, but ultimately it will take time.

"But we have a plan, we have shared that plan conceptually with the players and I think it will take a few years to execute, but we are going in the right direction.

"Don't expect big changes regarding the off-season in 2028. They are a work in progress."
