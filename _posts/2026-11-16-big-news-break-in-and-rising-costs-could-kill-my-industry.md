The owner of a cycle shop says he is considering closing the business after a break-in and soaring costs.

Gordon Kidd, who runs Shifnal Cycles, estimates that a burglary last month cost him about Â£20,000 after three customer bikes and a frame were stolen and the subsequent repairs to his shop.

Along with rising bills and a "lack of government support", the former armed forces veteran said the business in Shifnal may not be viable if financial pressures continue.

A government spokesperson said that, from next year, businesses such as cycle shops would see their business rates either frozen, reduced or cut to zero, with half of the high-street retailers paying no rates.

West Mercia Police said it had received a report of a burglary on 31 October, and an investigation was ongoing.

"They stole customers' bikes, which, for me, as a shop owner, is my biggest fear," said Kidd, who was concerned the burglary could affect his reputation.

"That might put them [customers] off from using my shop, and that kills a shop."

He added: "I've got to do what's financially viable for the business.

"I've got to look at another avenue now, and I've got to look at a more cost-effective way of doing it."

Burglars stole three cycles and a frame and damaged the shop in the raid

Alongside coping with the impact of the burglary, Kidd called on the government to do more to help small businesses.

"I don't know how the government expects small businesses to operate and make a living," he said.

"We just break even with the repairs side of things, and it's tough."

He said the break-in "had not helped whatsoever".

"A lot of people probably think that bike shops make a lot of money when we really don't," he said.

Kidd, who not only sells cycles but also repairs and services them, told the BBC that the break-in had made him feel angry that his safe space had been invaded.

He suffers post-traumatic stress disorder and said the business was "like therapy".

"Working on bikes calms me down; it helps me keep in control of my PTSD and my mental health in general."
