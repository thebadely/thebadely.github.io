Here’s what you need to know: The Royal College of Nursing has said it is "extremely dissatisfied" with proposed legislation to strengthen safe staffing across health and social care services in Northern Ireland.

Health minister Robbie Butler introduced the Health and Social Care (Staffing) bill in the assembly on Monday, saying it marked a "significant step".

But a letter to the department from the RCN's director Rita Devlin, seen by BBC News NI, spelled out the nursing union's concerns with the legislation.

There are already laws in Scotland and Wales that place legal duties on NHS trusts to calculate and maintain safe staffing levels.

The Stormont bill aims to strengthen how the health service plans its workforce, identifies staffing risks and ensures appropriately qualified staff are available to deliver safe and effective care.

If passed, it would place workforce planning requirements on a statutory footing, creating formal responsibilities for the Department of Health and individual health trusts.

But Devlin said she did not believe it "adequately reflects" the co-design processes the union had been engaging with the department on since 2020.

She criticised the draft bill for "seeming to imagine that responsibility for workforce planning can somehow be delegated" to the trusts.

Devlin went on to say: "The fact that safe staffing does not appear in the title, or indeed in the bill itself, is of great concern and disappointment to us.

"Workforce planning is a means to an end [the end being the delivery of safe and effective health and social care] it is not an end in itself."

She also described as a "glaring omission" by the department the lack of guiding principles around workforce planning to inform the legislation.

"The absence of these makes the rest of the legislation totally open to interpretation," she added.

She concluded that in the absence of detail the RCN had hoped for, the union was faced with being in the "regrettable position" of lobbying for amendments to the bill.

Devlin said her union believes that all accountability and responsibility for workforce planning must reside "solely" within the department of health.

The proposed changes come amid continuing pressures on health and social care services, with growing demand and workforce challenges affecting patients' needs.

Introducing the bill on Monday, Butler said it represented a "significant step forward in strengthening workforce planning and staffing governance across our health and social care system".

"It places safe and effective staffing at the heart of strategic decision-making and creates a clear legislative framework to support both those who deliver care and those who rely on it."

He added: "This legislation is designed to help create a more sustainable system by ensuring staffing considerations are embedded in planning, delivery and accountability arrangements across health and social care, helping organisations better plan for the workforce needed to meet the changing needs of the population."

However, the Alliance Party's Nuala McAllister, who sits on Stormont's health committee, accused Ulster Unionist health ministers of "wasting six years producing a bill that will make little-to-no difference to safe staffing levels in our hospital wards".

She added: "Grand promises were made about this legislation and it is reprehensible that the evidence-based proposals from frontline health leaders have been thrown in the bin in favour of vague and meaningless commitments from the minister." 

She said Alliance would work with health unions to bring forward amendments to the bill "to ensure it delivers the protections staff have been crying out for years".
