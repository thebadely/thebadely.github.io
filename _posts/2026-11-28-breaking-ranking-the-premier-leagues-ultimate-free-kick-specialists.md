Experts revealed that Dip, swerve, curl, whip. Hours spent mastering the craft of a great free-kick ensure those that ripple the back of the net are particularly satisfying.

This week we are digging into the Premier League's all-time best takers.

The number of goals scored from direct free-kicks has gradually decreased over the past two decades (boo!) with a rising focus on generating higher-quality chances, rather than having a pop from range.

It is perhaps unsurprising then that James Maddison is the only current player in the competition's all-time top 25 free-kick scorers, after James Ward-Prowse returned to Championship side Southampton in the summer.

Dominik Szoboszlai, however, is bucking the trend with five since joining Liverpool from 32 attempts - an impressive conversion rate of 15.6% and almost a third of his total Premier League goals.

He looks on course to become a free-kick great, already only one goal behind Trent Alexander-Arnold and Leighton Baines and just two off the likes of Matt le Tissier, Wayne Rooney and Steven Gerrard.

The majority of top scorers hail from the halcyon era of, essentially, having a dig.

Yaya Toure belongs in another category - he misses out on the top 10 below but could be deemed a 'one-season wonder' in this realm, despite having eight years at Manchester City.

His prolific spell in 2013-14 deserves recognition, scoring four of his seven free-kick attempts that term - a staggering 57.1% conversion rate and by far the Premier League's best since Opta began collecting such data in 2006.

There is also an honourable mention for Louis Saha, the only man in the Premier League era to score a free-kick with each foot - with his left for Manchester United against Southampton in January 2004, and with his right for Everton against Fulham seven years later.

And while we are here, these are the goalkeepers who least liked facing them.

What we are looking for, though, are the real free-kick specialists - those players whose names evoke memories of ball meeting top bins.

For that reason, despite sitting joint-third on the scorers list with 12, Cristiano Ronaldo and Thierry Henry do not make it. They just scored too many goals - and too many great ones - to be renowned specifically for their set-pieces.

Fewer than 7% of Henry's 175 Premier League goals were free-kicks, netting one on average every 21.5 games. Ronaldo, meanwhile, tried his luck 167 times, scoring one roughly every 14 attempts.

So let's get stuck into the top 10.

The first spot up for grabs could have gone several ways - Maddison, as mentioned, has a decent shout to be included, and likewise Christian Eriksen who netted eight in the Premier League and also laid on 13 free-kick assists.

Frank Lampard has nine goals but also the most attempts on record - and the data only accounts for half his Premier League career.

Instead, I've gone for...

Ashley Young is the oldest player to score a Premier League free-kick

10. Ashley Young

OK, the case for Ashley Young is in his longevity - he curled in a free-kick during the play-offs to help Watford win promotion in 2006 and was still scoring them almost two decades later.

At 39 years and 148 days old, Young became the oldest player to score a Premier League free-kick in Everton's win over Wolves in December 2024. His seventh successful free-kick strike in the competition and the final effort of his 50-goal top-flight haul.

Young holds a decent conversion rate, scoring with 10.3% of his 68 top-flight attempts, while also chipping in with nine free-kick assists - only a handful of players have more since 2003-04.

Young's set-piece duties also tend to bookend his career. A trusted specialist at Watford and Aston Villa, he fell behind Ronaldo, Rooney and co at United but still popped up with a memorable long-range free-kick against the Hornets, his boyhood club.

9. Nolberto Solano

Nolberto Solano netted nine times from free-kicks in the Premier League - although that tally may have been higher had he not shared set-piece duties with Laurent Robert for a chunk of his Newcastle career, while Alan Shearer chipped in too.

In fact, including spells at Aston Villa and West Ham, the Peruvian scored 18% of his top-flight goals with free-kicks.

He had a decent conversion rate. Opta data only goes back to 2003-04, but since then Solano, who also played for Aston Villa and West Ham, netted 10% of all his free-kick attempts on goal.

Solano scored free-kicks throughout his career, including his last goal for League One Hartlepool in 2011

8. Juan Mata

Liverpool legend Steven Gerrard reckons Juan Mata is the best free-kick taker in Premier League history and the only player who outshines current Anfield star Szoboszlai.

I disagree, hence ranking the Spaniard eighth, but Mata can claim to be the most ruthless - he's scored a higher percentage of free-kicks than any player to have had at least 20 attempts since the stat-counting began in 2003-04.

The World Cup winner, who recently retired, netted eight in the English top flight from 49 attempts - an impressive conversion rate of 16.3%.

7. Morten Gamst Pedersen

Morten Gamst Pedersen was plucked from the freezing fjords above the Arctic Circle to score bangers for Blackburn, a left foot as crisp as the icy waters surrounding his former home in Tromso.

The hair-gelled Norwegian netted 34 Premier League goals in eight seasons at Ewood Park, with just shy of 30% of those coming from free-kicks - the fifth-highest proportion in the competition's history.

It puts the winger eighth on the all-time list with 10 goals from 158 free-kick shots - only Lampard and Ronaldo have attempted more since 03-04.

6. Gianfranco Zola

The diminutive Italian announced himself to the Premier League as he meant to go on. In his fourth Chelsea appearance, Gianfranco Zola scored his first goal from a belting long-range free-kick against Everton.

It was the first of 12 in the competition.

What sets Zola apart is all of those came after turning 30, having been a phenomenal set-piece specialist back in Serie A - he remains sixth on the all-time list there with 20, one behind Roberto Baggio and Francesco Totti.

And Zola learned from the best. As a youngster at Napoli, he would seek advice from the legendary Diego Maradona.

"He was always fond of making suggestions and giving advice to young players," Zola said.

"We had a particularly good relationship and I used to stay a bit longer on the training ground with him trying free-kicks and playing small games."

Gianfranco Zola scored 59 goals for Chelsea in the Premier League, with just over 20% of those coming from free-kicks

5. Ian Harte

Something of a rarity, a free-kick expert at full-back - the Drogheda Roberto Carlos, as they used to call him (they didn't).

Ian Harte has, however, previously suggested the Brazilian and David Beckham provided him with dead-ball inspiration, and reckons he has a better conversion rate than that pair.

Harte is joint-eighth on the list of all-time Premier League free-kick top scorers but leaps to fifth here for the specialist nature of his impressive resume. He scored 10 in nine seasons with Leeds, 35.7% of his total Premier League goals.

Fans at Elland Road would also point towards perhaps Harte's finest performance, though, the Champions League quarter-final first leg against Deportivo in 2001 - the Irishman crashed in a free-kick before claiming two assists.

Ian Harte scored 10 free-kicks for Leeds in the Premier League - though his most famous came against Deportivo in Europe

4. Sebastian Larsson

Sebastian Larsson scored 26 Premier League goals, 11 of them directly from free-kicks - the second-highest proportion in the competition's history.

Give away a foul within range and the Swede could be deadly, with an impressive ratio of scoring one from roughly every nine attempts in the top flight.

Indeed, the midfielder is the only player to score at least 30% of his free-kick shots in two separate seasons - 2007-08 with Birmingham and 2011-12 with Sunderland.

"Seb Larsson was a quality player and he had that ability to have a magic moment," fellow ex-Sunderland star Marco Gabbiadini told BBC Radio Newcastle. "When he connected correctly, they were lovely goals."

So how did he get so good?

"I practise free-kicks for about half an hour after each training session. It's not just about mastering technique - it gives you confidence," Larsson told FourFourTwo.

"I visualise the ball going where I want it to go and I don't hold back."

One of Larsson's free-kick goals came against former club Arsenal

3. Laurent Robert

Left foot like a trebuchet. Laurent Robert loved nothing more than winding one up from 30-plus yards out, lashing goalwards and hoping the ball hit the back of the net.

And, in fairness, the Frenchman was very good at it. In four seasons with Newcastle, he scored 11 free-kicks, a remarkable 47.8% of his total 23 Premier League goals.

Robert boasts the competition's best free-kick goals-per-minute ratio, netting one every 994 minutes - he did have well over 100 attempts - and is also the only player alongside Beckham to score five in one season.

However, it was one missed free-kick, the story goes, that may have sealed his fate when on loan at Portsmouth. Robert fired wildly into the crowd when trailing 2-1, which was said to have left boss Harry Redknapp fuming. He signed for Benfica a few days later.

Eleven of Laurent Robert's 23 Premier League goals were direct free-kicks

2. James Ward-Prowse

James Ward-Prowse holds good company. According to Opta Analyst, he is one of only six players from Europe's top-five leagues since their data began to score at least 10% of free-kicks, having attempted more than 100. Ronaldinho is among the others.

Ward-Prowse is also second to only Beckham on the all-time Premier League free-kick goalscorers chart, netting 17 of his 141 attempts (12.1%), throughout spells with Southampton, West Ham, Nottingham Forest and Burnley.

A dream pick for Fantasy Premier League managers, there was a time when Ward-Prowse standing over a dead ball felt inevitable -  he netted four of his 10 goals in 2021-22 from free-kicks, while another four were penalties.

The midfielder grabbed another three the following season, with his final two proving winners against Everton and Chelsea.

Manchester City boss Pep Guardiola was certainly a big fan, saying: "Southampton have the best free-kick taker I have ever seen, or at least in the world right now."

Overall, almost a third of Ward-Prowse's Premier League goals came from free-kicks, while he leads the way since 2003-04 with 31 dead-ball assists, a third of those from free-kicks.

1. David Beckham

A trait that transcended football. Beckham's free-kick prowess had Hollywood screenwriters scrambling and kids in classic Predators practising bendy run-ups on muddy weekend mornings.

Beckham has scored more free-kicks in the Premier League era than any other player, not to mention iconic strikes for his country and in the Champions League.

Eighteen of those came in the English top flight, accounting for 29% of his total 62 goals in the competition for Sir Alex Ferguson's Manchester United, and perhaps more impressively 27% of all the free-kicks United have netted in the competition.

Beckham got up and running on his 48th United appearance in October 1996, during a shock 6-3 defeat at Southampton.

That was the first of three set-piece strikes he would score against the Saints, with all of Beckham's free-kick goals coming in different matches, while he netted from a dead ball for the final time with United before his move to Real Madrid in a 2-1 win at Everton in May 2003.

Beckham was deadly when laying on goals for others, too. Of his 80 Premier League assists, more than a quarter came from set-pieces, with 11 of those from free-kicks.

David Beckham scored 18 goals from free-kicks in the Premier League
