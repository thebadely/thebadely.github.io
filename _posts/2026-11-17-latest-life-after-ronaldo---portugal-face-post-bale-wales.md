Portugal are just one match into life after Cristiano Ronaldo, a footballing titan whose 23-year international career transformed the game in his homeland.

On Tuesday, they visit Wales, who are nearly four years into a similar adjustment after the retirement of their own icon, Gareth Bale.

History might suggest these two teams belong to different echelons of the sport. But in terms of individual players defining entire nations, Wales and Portugal share a profound connection.

Their records only tell half the story. Ronaldo and Bale scored more goals and won more caps than any other man to represent Portugal or Wales. In Ronaldo's case, more than any other man in the world.

What the two represented for their respective countries went beyond their remarkable statistics, though.

Ronaldo led Portugal to their first title at Euro 2016, beating Bale's Wales in a semi-final and ending a momentous Welsh journey in what was their first appearance at a major tournament for 58 years.

Both players gave their countries countless memorable moments, and not only on the international stage.

Former Real Madrid team-mates, Ronaldo and Bale won four and five Champions Leagues respectively, a combined five La Liga titles and a plethora of other team and individual accolades.

Whenever they held trophies aloft, you could bet they would be carrying, or often wearing, the national flags of Portugal and Wales â global ambassadors.

"You're losing an icon of the national team," says Wales captain Ben Davies, Bale's former team-mate and part of the Welsh defence which faced Ronaldo in what proved to be his final international appearance two months ago.

"But it's an opportunity for other players to stand up and try to take that limelight. We've had boys who've really stepped up in that time after we lost probably Wales' greatest player.

"Ronaldo's a big loss but football moves on."

As secure as their legendary status might be, there are telling differences between the two.

When Bale announced his retirement aged 33 in January 2023, he did so because he acknowledged his once otherworldly powers were fading and he felt Wales needed to plan for the future.

Reserved by nature, Bale could not avoid an outpouring of adulation, but he tried to depart as quietly as he could.

By contrast, Ronaldo's exit earlier this month was messy, acrimonious and, according to some, long overdue for the 41-year-old.

He walked out of a Portugal camp in September after being told he would not start a Nations League match against Denmark, resulting in a Â£215 fine and 30-day suspension.

Without knowing beforehand that the Wales game earlier that month would be his last, Ronaldo did not get the send-off he would have wanted.

"I'm grateful that we got to see the longevity of a true great who's had one of the biggest impacts in football," says Wales head coach Craig Bellamy.

"He's left a mark on football that will live forever. I'm grateful I got to see him and I'm grateful I got to coach against him."

No-one can replace Bale - but Wilson's giving it a go

Bellamy makes an important point, highlighting arguably the biggest distinction between Portugal's prospects after Ronaldo's retirement and the task Wales have faced since Bale's departure.

Portugal's squad is among the best in the world, littered with world-class talent in every position, a true embarrassment of riches. As Bellamy says, they will be fine.

The current Wales squad, meanwhile, features four League One players and one on loan in the National League.

The two teams are in the same Nations League group but worlds apart.

While Bale was part of a wider golden generation, the fact that Wales have always had to rely on thinner resources than their rivals only added to the sense of wonder that they could produce this phenomenon.

The likes of Harry Wilson have taken their game to new levels since Bale's retirement but, as the Leeds United playmaker himself said earlier this year, nobody can replace Wales' greatest.

Nobody will claim to be able to replace Ronaldo either. In Portugal, he has an almost religious following.

In pure footballing terms, though, they have more than enough to fill the void. Four of this year's Ballon d'Or nominees were Portuguese.

The reigning Nations League champions travel to Cardiff on Tuesday shorn of an all-time great but still blessed with a galaxy of stars.

"Does it [Ronaldo's retirement] change how we approach the game? It doesn't, because it's just the level of quality you're up against," Bellamy says.

"They still have about 15 Champions League winners in the squad.

"They were fine before him, then they had a brilliant moment with him, and they'll be brilliant after it as well."
