Having lost at home to Argentina in November and then beating Argentina away from home in July, Scotland's next joust with the Pumas is set for Twickenham next Sunday, 29 November - fourth from the northern hemisphere versus fourth from the south on the final day of the Nations Championship.

The warm-up act before the grand final between France and South Africa later on Sunday is not where Scotland wanted to be, but four wins from six games is a decent return. 

A fifth win in seven would be encouraging, if not life-changing for this team. They've all been at this rodeo before.

On Saturday, Scotland's much-changed team tore an understrength, over-powered and severely out-matched Japan to shreds. Eleven tries was bordering on an exhibition. 

It didn't tell us anything we didn't already know about head coach Gregor Townsend's set-up, but it was a special day for a number of people, chief among them the fantastically promising Liam McConnell and the revitalised Darcy Graham - hat-tricks for both and a ton of other wonderful moments besides.

That Scotland had far too much speed, skill, power and ruthlessness for Japan was not surprising. They scored some blistering tries. 

These are fine rugby players, albeit ones who are, just like the players they took over from, prone to giving up soft moments; avoidable penalties, easy entries into their own 22, self-inflicted stress on their own defence, which was strong on Saturday.

Scotland gave up seven penalties in the first half against Japan. The visitors weren't good enough to break their hosts down from all that field position, but the teams that Scotland want to start beating certainly would have been. 

New Zealand were more than good enough only a few weeks ago. It was a large part of Scotland's fall that day.

In public, Townsend accentuates the positives, as he must  - and there was plenty of positivity to go around. His team delivered a thunderous and classy win.  

Privately, he has to be looking at the indiscipline of the first half - it was near- perfect in the second - and cursing it. 

Japan were never going to punish them, but in defining games in the Six Nations against heavy hitters, and in the knockouts of the World Cup against bigger beasts, then he knows what's going to happen if that stuff gets repeated.

This is unforgiving terrain. Scotland are desperate to move closer to a higher level and so they will be brutal on each other - or should be. 

We saw what that level was all about on Saturday - a fast-improving England brutalising the All Blacks, who have now lost back-to-back, and a ferociously physical Ireland taking down the Springboks, who have also now lost two-in-a-row.

The standard is savagely high. Critics of Townsend say that Scotland haven't improved on his watch because they haven't won any trophies. That pays no regard to the improvement that other nations have made in the Townsend years. 

It's not like the biggest countries are standing still. South Africa aren't, France aren't.

Ireland looked for a while like they were falling away a touch but now they appear to be back. England are flying. 

New Zealand are a work-in-progress, but they were too much for Scotland.  Scotland are moving forward, but for all the tries and all the thrills they can produce, they have a long way to go, if they get there at all. They know that better than anybody.

Murrayfield was heaving again on Saturday, a capacity crowd for the visit of Japan, 12,000 more fans for this game against the world number 12 than turned up at the Principality Stadium last weekend for Wales playing the All Blacks.

We should pause for a second there because the strength of the Scotland support in difficult times is quite something. It's easy to sell-out the marquee games, but doing it against Japan on Saturday and against Fiji last season is impressive. 

It follows on from more than 56,000 against the USA and more than 60,000 against Tonga last autumn and another 60,000-plus against Portugal in the autumn of 2024. 

More than 55,000 were at the Georgia game in the summer of 2023. Huge numbers for a nation that hasn't won a tournament - or even come close to winning a tournament - in 27 years.

They go again at Twickenham. Another weekend and another Test and a challenging one at that. 

Scotland are losing players at an alarming rate; Matt Fagerson and Nathan McBeth got injured against Japan and almost certainly won't make for the fourth-placed play-off.

For the Japan game, Townsend was without the injured Pierre Schoeman, Elliot Millar Mills, Scott Cummings, Alex Craig, Jamie Ritchie, Jack Dempsey, Rory Darge, Sione Tuipulotu, Fergus Burke and Ben White. 

Scotland's depth in most of those positions is sound, but with Schoeman out and McBeth probably on his way out, things are looking fragile. Rory Sutherland and Alec Hepburn look set for the looseheads spots at Twickenham.

One of the big, and happy, conundrums facing Townsend and his coaches in the coming days is what to do with the tearaway McConnell. Unleash against the Pumas from the start? Put him on the bench for impact? Or cut him from the 23, a move that wouldn't go down well.

He has verve and pace and a work ethic that makes him a huge prospect. This game at Twickenham is an odd thing - one that Scotland will really want to win for feelgood sake but also the last opportunity for a while to give the McConnells another chance to show their stuff.

Once Scotland hit the Six Nations, it'll be full metal jacket stuff. Their World Cup warm-ups are all serious business - home and away against France and away to the All Blacks. 

There's a little space to experiment there, but not much. Townsend shouldn't be in a rush to change things for Twickenham. McConnell warrants another start. 

He's exciting and intriguing. Let the boy fly.
