According to new developments, Wouter Wrancken under pressure; Mario Frick's beginning; John McGlynn the saviour

The Scottish Premiership returns with an all-action Saturday after what was a bleak international break for the national side.

Most of the intrigue is on men who will not make a pass or a tackle. There is one new manager, one who is being touted for the Scotland job, another who has turned around a failing side and one who is increasingly under pressure.

And none of those are in charge of Old Firm teams. The managerial status at Celtic and Rangers is relatively settled, but of course that can change in the space of 90 minutes.

This is your essential guide to Saturday's action.

Aberdeen have a new head coach for their basement battle with Kilmarnock, with Mario Frick replacing Stephen Robinson

The Liechtensteiner was a fine striker in his day and his main mission is to get the Dons firing in front of goal again.

Frick favoured two up top while in charge of Luzern, so he needs to find a way to supply Kevin Nisbet and Ayoub Mouloua.

Connor Ronan could hold the key to improving a sorry set of attacking statistics, but the Irish midfielder will need to show more than he has since his summer arrival.

Aberdeen have 12 goals from 12 league games. Kilmarnock have 13 from 13. Both sides have amassed just four points from their past six outings, so this may well be something of a scrap.

Killie were slightly more menacing in a 0-0 draw at Pittodrie in September and the same could be said of a League Cup quarter-final at Rugby Park a week later, although the Dons did well to prevail on penalties after a red card early in the second half.

The Ayrshire side have the league's worst defensive record by a distance, conceding three or more goals on six occasions. However, manager Neil McCann will have been heartened by a tenacious showing in the 1-0 loss at Celtic Park last time out.

With four successive matches coming against sides currently in the bottom six before taking on the Dundee duo, McCann will view this pre-Christmas sequence as vital.

Of the leading sides, Celtic have the easier-looking task at home to St Mirren, who have enjoyed just one victory in their past 40 visits to the east end of Glasgow in all competitions.

However, the league leaders have not kicked on since thrashing Heart of Midlothian last month, drawing with Dundee United and losing to Falkirk either side of narrow and unconvincing wins over Hibernian and Kilmarnock.

That 1-0 win at home to Killie was just a second clean sheet in seven Premiership matches for the champions.

Celtic came from behind to win 2-1 in Paisley in September, with Yang Hyun-Jun grabbing the second. The winger's return should add dynamism to their attack.

Since pushing Celtic all the way at home, St Mirren have picked up a mere four points from nine matches.

Rangers go to Motherwell off the back of a 2-0 win away to the Buddies, extending their unbeaten league run to 10 games and maintaining the best defensive numbers in the table.

The goals came from a contentious penalty and a deflected shot and manager Derek McInnes spoke of the need to sharpen up in the final third after several promising situations broke down.

After a six-game losing slump that began in Govan, Motherwell have steadied the ship and played out an entertaining 1-1 draw with Dundee United before the break that included a combined 40 efforts on goal.

Well shared the points with Rangers at Fir Park last season, scoring late on both occasions, and, while not the force they were after so many departures, still retain a core of players who celebrated victory at Ibrox in April.

Rangers have the momentum, although recent draws away to Dundee and Hearts underline the fact they remain a work in progress under McInnes.

Five wins on the spin have propelled Falkirk up to third place and they will be big favourites to extend that run at home to St Johnstone.

Celtic beaten, Hearts hammered... fans are talking about European qualification, having ended a five-year stint in League 1 in May 2024.

John McGlynn continues to work his magic on a meagre budget, with new arrivals Jacob Murrell, Owen Eames, Finley Barbrook and Deniss Melniks hitting the ground running.

Ethan Ross is lapping up a new lease of life, while older heads Scott Bain and Liam Henderson have never been better, although the loss of influential skipper Brad Spencer is a huge blow.

The Perth Saints will be no pushovers. They too are punching above their weight with just four defeats in 13 on their return to the top flight.

Rangers are the only side to beat Simo Valakari's men since a 1-1 draw with Falkirk at McDiarmid Park in mid-September.

That was the first league meeting of the clubs since 2010, but, with such astute managers in place, it looks like this can remain a top-flight rivalry for a good while yet.

Have Hibs turned a corner and have Hearts fallen down a hole?

Marink Reedijk lost his first three games after replacing David Gray, but now the Hibs head coach is looking for a third successive victory at home to Dundee United.

The Easter Road side clambered out of the bottom spot in style, thumping Aberdeen 4-0 at Pittodrie after coming from behind to stun their Edinburgh rivals in the League Cup semi-final.

Hearts went into that game at Hampden as heavy favourites and across the following seven days were outplayed by the team sitting eighth in the Swiss Super League and walloped by Falkirk, ending their 19-month long unbeaten home record in the league.

A growing injury list has hampered Hearts, but Wouter Vrancken's methods are being questioned, with only Kilmarnock conceding more goals in the league.

A visit to Dundee is next and Steven Pressley's much-improved team have only been defeated once at Dens Park so far.

United have also been a breath of fresh air, adopting a new swashbuckling style that has yielded four wins and four draws in their past nine games.

Hibs have taken significant strides, particularly in the midfield area, but this will be a real test of their progress. There were 11 goals in the two games against United at Easter Road last season and another big score would not be a surprise.
