Shoppers and donors at a new charity shop will be able to help decide which organisations benefit from the money it raises.

Twisted Sisters Thrift Store, in Tunstall opened last week, and is inviting customers to nominate causes they would like to support, with a different charity set to receive donations each month.

Owner Julie Rindam said the idea came after the closure of a charity shop; she had run for eight years.

"The pastor is retiring and it was either another empty shop or I took it on."

Rindam said many of the shop's regular donors wanted to continue supporting charitable causes, leading to the idea of allowing the community to decide where the money goes.

"All my donators still wanted to drop off to us, so we thought, well, how about a different charity every month?" she said.

"We ask all the donators who they'd like us to donate to, so every month it will be a different charity benefiting."

Rather than supporting a single organisation, the shop plans to keep a record of suggestions, being completely transparent with customers and donors before selecting a charity at the end of each month.

"Everyone who drops off, we ask and we keep a book and every month we'll pick a different charity to benefit from, whether that's City Dogs Home, Cancer Research, Diabetes or other charities," Rindam said.

The owner hopes that the store will also be a centre for the community.

Shop manager Jeanette Worthington said the approach was designed to ensure local people remained involved in how the money was distributed.

As well as raising money for charity, the store also aims to act as a community hub, offering free food, sanitary products, contraception and school uniforms when available.

Worthington said supporting local people was just as important as supporting charities.

"People know they can come here, it's a safe space," she said.

"We will help anybody if we have the time and if we can't do it, we'll say come back at such and such a time."

Janet Smart had been a regular donater to the shop before it was taken over by the new team. 

Regular customer and donor Janet Smart said the shop had become a valuable part of the community.

"When I'm decluttering everything, it comes to here because I know they don't charge a lot. People haven't got much money today," she said.

"You meet such nice people in here as well."

She added that the social side of the shop was equally important.

"I think for people on their own, it's nice to be able to come in somewhere and people chat with them."

Jack Spencer is managing to find clothes and items that he wouldn't be able to find in more mainstream stores.

Shopper Jack Spencer said the store offered affordable options for people who might otherwise struggle to buy certain items.

"It feels a lot more personal, a lot more out there. You go in, you don't know what you're going to walk into, it changes constantly," he said.

"You might find something that you could have been looking for, but you just couldn't afford it."

For Rindam and Worthington, the hope is that both the charities and the local community will benefit.
