It has been recently reported that Liverpool kept back-to-back clean sheets under Andoni Iraola for the first time this week

A year has passed, and there has been a world of change at Liverpool.

Thursday marked 12 months since the Reds lost 4-1 to PSV Eindhoven at Anfield in the Champions League, the culmination of their worst run of results in more than 70 years.

By the time the final whistle blew that night there were some boos, but Anfield was half empty and belief from the fanbase had evaporated as Liverpool lost for the ninth time in 12 games.

Arne Slot's side had won the Premier League title the season before, but that game against the Dutch side was arguably the nadir of his second campaign in charge. Steven Gerrard said that evening that Liverpool "just keep bleeding".

The bleeding did stop somewhat, and Liverpool qualified for the Champions League, but it was a campaign that left Slot wounded and, come the end of the campaign, he was sacked.

Everyone has moved on in one way or another. The PSV game is in the distant past now.

And after a dizzying few days where Liverpool have scored seven without reply across the Premier League and Champions League, beating Manchester United and Club Brugge, Andoni Iraola's side are giving fans reasons to start believing again.

That is just as well, because Sunday is derby day against Everton.

"You can really see this manager stamping his philosophy and his way of playing on the team," former Liverpool captain Gerrard said on BBC Radio 5 Live this week.

"They are playing a lot faster, a lot more intense, playing a lot more vertical and getting it forward a lot quicker.

"It's a lot more exciting to watch than it was at the back end of last season."

When Liverpool lost to Arsenal at the start of November, they were threatening in phases against the reigning champions but finished the game without a shot on target - despite starting with three attacking players who cost more than Â£100m each.

But they have now scored 10 goals in their past three games, with that trio of Alexander Isak, Florian Wirtz and Bradley Barcola delivering 10 goal involvements between them.

Both Isak and Wirtz are in their best form since making the move to Liverpool in 2025 and both spoke in Bruges this week about how good they are feeling. Isak has four goals in his past four matches while Wirtz has scored in each of his past three.

"It's a dream situation for Wirtz now," said Gerrard.

"Under Iraola, he has given him more freedom, more licence to take risks and to be the difference maker."

Combined, Wirtz and Isak have surpassed their total goal involvements from last season and, alongside the likes of Barcola, Cody Gakpo, Victor Munoz, Rio Ngumoha and Hugo Ekitike - once he returns from injury in January - Liverpool have a youthful and vibrant attack that can rattle fear into defences across Europe.

"It's not just about those two [Isak and Wirtz]. If we play well, someone will score," said Iraola. "If we have more chances then the opposition, someone will score."

Iraola will also be pleased that his midfielders are getting high up the pitch and scoring too, with Alexis Mac Allister and Dominik Szoboszlai getting nine goals between them this season.

The challenge now is for the frontline to deliver consistently and crucially, in those games against the best opposition. In the Champions League, Liverpool have scored 17 times in five games, but it hasn't gone unnoticed that they failed to score once across 180 minutes against Manchester City and Arsenal.

Like he did against LASK, Iraola opted to rest Virgil van Dijk against Club Brugge and the centre-back duo of Jeremy Jacquet and Ronald Araujo helped to keep a  clean sheet. From the eye test alone, Liverpool are looking more assured defensively and Araujo's ability to fill in across the backline is a big part of that.

Admittedly, they have failed to replace the attacking quality that Trent Alexander-Arnold provided, but Araujo is a trusted option and a fan favourite while left-back Milos Kerkez is finding his feet under Iraola after a difficult first campaign. Iraola said Wednesday's game against Brugge was Kerkez's best display of the season.

Van Dijk and Jacquet in particular have excelled, while Conor Bradley and Giovanni Leoni are on their way back from injuries.

Liverpool conceded 53 goals in the Premier League last season, their most in a 38-game campaign in the competition, and it looked like nothing had changed when they conceded twice against both Newcastle and Nottingham Forest in their first two games this season.

But since then they have kept clean sheets against Ipswich, Fulham, Bournemouth, high-scoring Brighton and Manchester United.

And it is worth noting that under the guidance of Tommy Elphick, one of Iraola's assistants responsible for defensive set-pieces, and Lewis Mahoney, the set-piece analyst, Liverpool are no longer conceding for fun.

It is still an area opposition teams are targeting, and they have conceded four non-penalty set-piece goals in the Champions League and EFL Cup, but their rate of 2.3 xG (expected goals) conceded per 100 opposition corners is the best in the Premier League.

It was quite the sight to see Iraola deliver Jurgen Klopp-style fist pumps with a smile in front of the Kop after Sunday's 4-0 win over Manchester United.

"It's not something that I normally do," he said. "But in that moment I felt like everyone was expecting, and I think we were all celebrating."

His side were rampant - albeit against a poor United side who were down to 10 men early on - and could easily have scored more if they were ruthless.

With the Merseyside derby next on Sunday and a chance to close the gap to four points on Arsenal, before the Gunners take on Manchester City later in the afternoon, Iraola will be keen to temper expectations.

Any talk of a title challenge seems premature, and Iraola is of the belief that you must wait until halfway through the campaign before "thinking and understanding" the table - but Liverpool are a side on the up.

From all of the managerial changes in the summer, Iraola has arguably had the strongest start, with his replacement at Bournemouth, Marco Rose, impressing too.

Fixtures come thick and fast now, with three games in the space of seven days coming up, starting with the short trip to Hill Dickinson Stadium. Liverpool host Sunderland on Wednesday before going to Chelsea next Saturday.

But first comes the derby.

"To enjoy it, there is only one way: to win the game," Iraola said on Friday.

It is early doors, but the Anfield hierarchy wanted a fresh beginning when he was appointed and, after a stuttering start, confidence is rising under the Spaniard.

Win against their arch rivals on Sunday and that optimism will only grow.
