Former three-weight world champion Jorge Linares says he has been detained by US immigration authorities in Texas.

The 41-year-old Venezuelan posted a message on social media on Sunday about being held by America's Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) agency.

Linares is based in Miami and has fought more than 10 times in America.

"ICE has me at the Texas border crossing. This is crazy," wrote Linares., external

He retired after being beaten by Britain's Jack Catterall in a light-welterweight bout in October 2023 in the last of his 56 fights.

A renowned fighter, he won 47 of his bouts and claimed world titles at featherweight, super-featherweight and lightweight in a distinguished career which started in 2002.

"I can confirm that my friend Jorge Linares, former WBA world champion, is in ICE custody," said WBA president Gilberto Mendoza, external on social media.

"Immigration attorneys are handling his case, and I am in constant contact with his family.

"As WBA president, I will always support our champions, in and out of the ring. Stay strong, Jorge."

The AFP news agency reported a statement from Wilner & O'Reilly, an immigration law firm representing Linares, which said he was "legally present in the United States and has co-operated fully with the authorities".

They added: "We are working closely with ICE and the Department of Homeland Security (DHS) to resolve this matter as quickly as possible.

"We are confident in Mr. Linares' legal position and expect this matter to be resolved once the process has concluded. We ask his supporters, the press and the public for patience in the meantime."
