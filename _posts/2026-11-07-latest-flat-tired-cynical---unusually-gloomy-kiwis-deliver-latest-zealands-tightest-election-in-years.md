But polling shows this is a pervasive sentiment across the country: the national mood is low and faith in politics is even lower.

More than half the country no longer sees it as a land of opportunity, a recent study found, while a similar portion fears that the future looks even worse.

Many Kiwis feel the economy is in ruins, there are no jobs, the cost of living has become sky high, the sense of community that used to be a source of national pride is breaking down and there's no sense of genuine progress on policy solutions. 

A record number of them are abandoning ship in search of brighter futures elsewhere.

There are still large parts of the country that feel differently, though.

Many of these issues affecting New Zealand are global trends - and it's undeniable that Kiwis' standard of living is better than many others.

But these issues are having a corrosive impact on its national identity.

"It leads to a crisis of confidence about what New Zealand is and where it's going," Helen Clark, who was prime minister from 1999 to 2008, tells the BBC.

Faith in institutions and leaders has taken an alarming turn: only two out of five New Zealanders trust the government to do the right thing, while less than half of the country trusts the courts.
