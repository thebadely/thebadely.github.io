Here’s what you need to know: Kathleen Simms said she relies on food banks because her energy bills are so high

A hike in energy costs in the new year has prompted concerns many families will see a hard winter.

Energy bills are expected to increase by 26% from January after a surge in gas prices sparked by the Iran war, with the government saying it will pay millions of households Â£100 from 4 January to help cover winter costs on top of an existing VAT cut on electricity.

However, residents in Stockton-on-Tees and Darlington said that was not enough to mitigate the rise of about Â£440 annually.

Kathleen Simms, from Stockton, said: "If I can't have the heating on, I'm going to end up in hospital."

Simms, who is in remission from cancer and has a lung condition, said: "It's hard to live now, if they put the bills any higher, we don't know what we can live on.

"We rely on food banks and stuff which we don't really like doing, but if it comes to that crunch, we have to."

Alice Smith Burns said energy price rises might be harder for young parents

In Darlington town centre, Alice Smith Burns said she was fortunate she could "suck up" the extra charges as she lived with her parents, but knew that option was not available to everyone.

"I have my daughter with me, and I'd rather she's not just sat there with blanket on her," Smith Burns said. "I'd rather she just had that heat source.

"I think especially as a young parent, if there are other young parents out there, it might be harder for those kinds of people.

"For low income households, it's a massive toll on everyone."

Alexandra Blowers said solar panels had brought her electricity bills down

Alexandra Blowers said she was fortunate to have solar panels on her home as they had brought her electricity bills down.

However, she still predicts she will limit her heating use.

"The gas price is obviously not the cheapest right now," she said.

"We do tend to not put the heading on as much as we might like to, I layer up because I do tend to get quite cold."

Tim Robinson is worried the increase in energy costs will affect vulnerable people

Tim Robinson said he also felt fortunate as he would not be hit by the 26% rise immediately due to being on a fixed rate.

However, he said he did not know how others would cope.

"I think the vulnerable always have to be protected no matter what circumstance they're in, whether it's in relation to heating, energy or food," he said.

"If you don't look after the vulnerable in society, then you're a very poor society."
