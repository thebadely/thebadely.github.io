Morgan Gibbs-White sent out a message last season. This term he is making sure it gets delivered.

In May he was snubbed by Thomas Tuchel for England's World Cup squad and responded by scoring against Bournemouth on the final day.

He celebrated by pointing to his chest and holding up both hands, in a nod to the number 10 shirt he wears - a clear statement to Tuchel.

This season he has continued his form, scoring a penalty in Forest's 2-2 draw at Liverpool on Saturday after assisting Dan Ndoye's opener.

The captain is Forest's talisman, especially after Elliot Anderson's record sale to Manchester City and, with England facing upcoming Nations League games against Spain, Croatia and the Czech Republic, the noise could get too loud for the England manager to ignore this time. 

With new boss Oliver Glasner looking to him for inspiration, will this be a career-defining season for Gibbs-White?

On Saturday Forest host Tottenham, a team Gibbs-White came close to joining last year.

The fallout led to Forest threatening to take legal action against Spurs as owner Evangelos Marinakis personally took charge of the situation, meeting Gibbs-White at the club's training camp in Portugal and convincing him to stay - reaching a general agreement over a new contract. 

He signed a new three-year deal days later and went on to score a career-best 18 goals in all competitions last season.

His goal at Tottenham in March was crucial in Forest's 3-0 win, giving them the platform to survive and keeping Spurs sweating on their top-flight status until the final day.

Now, without Anderson following his Â£116m departure to the Etihad, the spotlight will continue to land on Gibbs-White. 

Glasner said in August his star was never leaving the club once it was clear Anderson was. 

The number 10 role is his, with Glasner handing James McAtee a deeper role following Anderson's exit - the former Manchester City youngster able to control Forest's tempo, allowing Gibbs-White to roam and threaten further forward.

"There was never a tiny question mark about it. It was complete commitment and alignment that Morgan stays here, also from his side," said the former Crystal Palace head coach.

"I have always seen him as a top player, a player who can make the difference, a player who can create chances and score himself. It's something he's showed since day one. He is very professional, very hard-working. He looks like an Adonis - just muscles. 

"He has all the freedom in the attack and I'm not surprised, I could see what I expected to see. A fantastic player and a great person."

Gibbs-White had enough license to score 10 goals in his final 15 games of last season, although it was not good enough to earn a World Cup call.

Glasner, though, has urged the 26-year-old to put his international future to the back of his mind.

"At the end it makes no sense thinking about it so much. The better he performs the better he will do in his career, for clubs or for England," he added.

"If you perform well and do things right you will be called up. Every manager in the world will call the best players and if you are among them you will be called up. 

"It's the same for Morgan. He started to score, 10 goals he scored from March onward, late in the season. He had seven to eight months before where he didn't score so many goals. For him it's getting consistency into the game."

There is no doubt the former Wolves youngster is one of the Premier League's form players this calendar year.

Gibbs-White has been directly involved in 20 club goals in 2026 (15 goals, five assists), which is the joint-most of any Premier League player in all competitions - along with Erling Haaland.

Yet Gibbs-White has outscored the Manchester City striker, who has 10, with the Forest's star's 13 Premier League goals in 2026 the most of any player.

He averages a league goal every 129 minutes in 2026 - which includes his first senior hat-trick against Burnley in April.

It was a vastly improved performance from Gibbs-White and Forest at Anfield on Saturday, after their opening two defeats by Leeds in the Premier League and Carabao Cup - with the skipper missing the 2-0 Cup defeat through injury.

On his return at Anfield he covered 11.4km, the third highest of the team, and was tied first with 16 sprints - while his expected goals of 0.79 also topped Forest's stats.

"He is not just defined by the number of goals he scored or assists," said Glasner.

"I know how he is working for the team out of possession, how he creates the space with some runs and movements.

"It is as important as scoring a goal. When you need a special moment in the game you usually need a special player, and Morgan is definitely a special player."
