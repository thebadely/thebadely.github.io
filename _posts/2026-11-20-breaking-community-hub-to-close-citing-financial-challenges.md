A cook at a community hub cafe has said she feels "punched in the stomach" after learning services will close following a decision to place the organisation into administration.

The trustees of Romney Marsh Community Hub, which supports the over 50s, said significant financial challenges meant services were being brought to an end, with administrators to be appointed within days, external.

Lisa Green, a cook at the charity's Hub on the Beach cafe, said the hub helped people dealing with bereavement, arthritis, cancer and other needs.

Kent County Council (KCC) said decisions were a matter for the charity's trustees, but it was in contact to understand the impact and help people access support.  

Lisa said one customer brought her mother, who has dementia, to the cafe every Saturday and saw it as a "lifeline", but she'd had to explain to her parent, who was now "really quite confused", that it was closing.

"There are so many people that rely on us," she said. "Some people haven't got anyone."

People could browse stalls and have a coffee in the cafe at a sale held last month

Lisa baked for the cafe, making old-fashioned puddings and cakes such as Victoria sponges that she said appealed to older customers.

She said staff, who had rent and mortgages to pay, had been told they were not going to get paid, but added: "It's more than thatâ¦ We're like a big family down there."

On Facebook, the cafe said it could not open from Friday without "a miracle, external"

Rye's Sunflower Charity Shop, which supported the hub, also said: "Our beloved shop will be closing with immediate effect, external."

Kent councillor David Wimble has said work is ongoing to explore whether a rescue proposal, external can be developed.

Businesses in Romney Marsh High Street are collecting signatures for a petition.

The cafe offered a relaxing coffee and a warm welcome, the hub's Facebook page said

Tony Vaughan, Folkestone and Hythe MP, said he was "deeply saddened, external" by the closure and claimed council cuts to a wellbeing contract had contributed to the charity's difficulties.

He said that some services had already found new homes, external.

A KCC spokeswoman said the authority recognised the important role the hub played in supporting older and vulnerable residents, through community, wellbeing and transport services.

She said changes to community wellbeing services in April led to reduced spending and a lower-value contract for Social Enterprise Kent, which delivers wellbeing services on behalf of KCC.

"We recognise the particular challenges faced by rural communities such as Romney Marsh, including transport barriers and access to local services," she said.

"We will continue to work with Romney Marsh Community Hub and partners to assess the impact of these developments and help residents to alternative sources of support where possible."
