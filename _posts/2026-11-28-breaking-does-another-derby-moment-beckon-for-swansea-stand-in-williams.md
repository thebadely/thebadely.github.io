It is one of the enduring images in the recent history of the south Wales derby.

Ashley Williams celebrating with Garry Monk amid delirium across three sides of what was then the Liberty Stadium following a Wayne Routledge goal.

It was February 2014, and Swansea City and Cardiff City were both under pressure thanks to the threat of Premier League relegation.

Swansea had sacked manager Michael Laudrup only four days before the game, and placed club skipper Monk in charge for the biggest fixture of that or any season in which south Wales' two premier clubs were in the same division.

It proved a wise move, as Swansea triumphed in the derby and - unlike Cardiff - won their battle to stay in the top flight. 

Routledge's strike put Swansea on the way to a comprehensive 3-0 win â and sent Williams hurtling towards the technical area to embrace his long-time centre-back partner.

It was more rugby tackle than hug when he got there, with Williams almost knocking Monk off his feet after Swansea had taken the first step towards victory over their rivals.

"They're the good moments in football," Williams says now, as the interim Swansea boss prepares for Sunday's latest derby edition.

"Some go for you and some go against you, and when they go for you, you want to enjoy it. 

"I think he (Monk) probably thought I was coming to ask him something tactical, or maybe he'd just been annoying me all week and I just wanted to rugby tackle him... you can just see the relief and the enjoyment, the passion in that moment."

Twelve years later, Williams is the man holding the fort following a managerial change.

The circumstances this time are different. Monk swapped the dressing room for the manager's office after a run of six defeats in eight games had left Swansea only two points above the Premier League relegation zone.

Williams, meanwhile, was parachuted back into his old club last week after Vitor Matos walked out on a Swans squad who had made a promising start to the season.

The dugout is almost uncharted territory for Williams, even if he has been part of Wales' backroom team since September.

The 42-year-old has spent most of his time doing punditry work since ending his playing career in 2021.

This weekend it will be match of the season - in south Wales at least - rather than Match of the Day as Swansea do battle with their fiercest rivals.

Would it not be simpler to watch on this Sunday with microphone in hand than to be barking orders on the touchline?

"It is so much easier to do the job you're doing [in the media] than what I'm doing right now," Williams says with a smile.

"But while there's more to do, it does get you excited a little bit more. You wake up in the morning thinking about it, go to bed thinking about it, trying to think have you covered all bases with players, is there something else you've missed or you can add? 

"There's just so many facets to this job that it keeps you going. Even when you feel tired, you're still buzzing around it."

After a run of six seasons together in the second tier, Swansea and Cardiff have had a brief break from rowing with the neighbours after the Bluebirds spent the 2025-26 campaign in League One.

Hostilities resume on Sunday (12:00 GMT) with Swansea eighth in the Championship table and Cardiff nine points worse off in 17th.

Following a difficult start to life back in the second tier, Cardiff have made progress over the last couple of months thanks to their fine home form, despite the unexpected midweek mauling by Preston North End aside.

Brian Barry-Murphy's team have had troubles on their travels, though they will make the short trip along the M4 believing there would be no better time to register a first away victory since April.

For Swansea, meanwhile, the derby does not come at an ideal time given the trauma caused by Matos' exit and a change in form which has left their sparkling start to the campaign fading a little in the memory.

After 10 league games this season, the Swans were top of the Championship with 23 points.

Seven matches and only one win later, they have slipped to the fringes of the play-off picture as they await the appointment of a new permanent manager.

Then again, as Williams might point out when on punditry duty, form often matters little on derby day.

"I just know that the atmosphere is always spicy and it's a proper derby," he says.

"I've played in the Merseyside one, I've played in England-Wales, but this one is the one that stands out to me in terms of the atmosphere. 

"You probably have to manage your emotions a little bit because it's an emotional day and sometimes it can get fiery."
