Let's dive into the details: The UK is in the grip of the coldest spell of autumn so far.

Yellow cold health alerts from the UK Health Security Agency remain in force until 09:00 GMT on Tuesday, warning of possible impacts to health services and vulnerable people.

The cold Arctic air is clashing with mild Atlantic air that arrived in the south-west of the UK over the weekend - a meteorological setup that provides the ingredients for possible snow.

However, there remains uncertainty in where the boundary between the cold and mild air masses will sit.

Even small shifts in temperature could make a significant difference to what falls from the sky and, crucially, the chances of snow.

Weather battleground for the week ahead

A clash between the air masses is already underway, as weather systems arrived from the Atlantic overnight into Sunday, bringing milder air with them.

The coming days will see the UK caught between lingering cold air, held in place by a dominant area of high pressure over Norway, and milder, wetter conditions associated with Sunday's weather fronts.

For many, the main hazards will be freezing fog and icy stretches, particularly where skies clear overnight. 

Further west, rain could still bring a risk of disruption, while uncertainty remains over the potential for snow.

The key question is whether the cold air can hold its ground or whether milder Atlantic conditions will steadily push eastwards.

A cold and frosty start on Monday

Monday will begin on a cold note, with temperatures widely below freezing, bringing frost along with patches of ice and freezing fog.

Blue skies and just a few fair-weather clouds will follow the frost. 

Any patchy fog will clear, but with winds a little fresher than on Sunday, temperatures will reach 8 to 10C.

Meanwhile, Sunday's bands of rain will gradually weaken over southwest England and Wales, where conditions will remain grey, damp and blustery. 

At times, cloud and light rain will also spread into Northern Ireland.

Tuesday afternoon pressure chart

By midweek, the battleground will shift further eastwards.

Early on Tuesday, the weather front - which by then will mainly consist of a band of cloud - will have edged further east. 

As a result, it will be a little less chilly to start the day, but cloudier for most.

Another, more active weather front will quickly follow, moving eastwards across Northern Ireland on Tuesday morning. 

It is then forecast to spread into northern and western England, Wales and much of Scotland through the day, bringing the first chance of snow to the Highlands and Grampians.

Tuesday night and Wednesday will be dominated by cloudy skies, with weather fronts bringing rain at times. 

The situation could be complicated by another area of low pressure developing in the English Channel. 

This would not only bring more rain to the south but also strengthen easterly winds, which would begin to push the cold air back westwards.
