Could the end of LIV be trouble for the European Tour?

Lee Westwood is worried about the future of the Europe-based DP World Tour following the collapse of LIV Golf.

The 53-year-old Englishman was among the high-profile players lured to LIV when the controversial Saudi-backed breakaway tour began in 2022.

LIV this week confirmed its intention to wind down operations after Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund (PIF) withdrew its financial support and potential new investors pulled out of a deal to back LIV continuing in a new form.

LIV's collapse is a boost for the USA's PGA Tour and Westwood's former Ryder Cup team-mate Rory McIlroy agrees with DP World Tour chief executive Guy Kinnings' assertion that LIV aimed to "destroy" their European rivals.

However, Westwood questions how strong a position the DP World Tour now finds itself in.

The former world number one expects the PGA Tour boss to "play hardball" with its European counterparts and suggested that Europe's top players may be better off playing on the Korn Ferry Tour, the US feeder circuit to the PGA Tour.

"LIV going out of business, some people will be disappointed and it might not be people that you immediately think of," Westwood told BBC Sport.

"The European tour, I'm not sure it's such a big win for them."

Although LIV players were banned from the PGA Tour, they could still play in some DP World Tour events, which Westwood felt gave them some "leverage" but "now LIV's out [of] the way, they've lost that".

The PGA Tour also gives tour cards to the top 10 finishers on the DP World Tour who are not already members, but Westwood believes "there are a lot of questions still to be answered" regarding the new terms of their alliance.

"If [the DP World Tour] want to continue having the PGA Tour bankrolling them, there are going to be some big asks from the PGA Tour," Westwood added.

"If I was [chief executive] Brian Rolapp running the PGA Tour, I would be playing hardball and making a lot of demands of the European tour if they expected me to finance it going forward."

Westwood did not commit to LIV 2.0, as it was known, and is currently "unemployed" as he considers his future but may have to serve a suspension before returning to either the PGA or DP World Tours.

The PGA Tour describes the Korn Ferry Tour as its "premier pathway", with 20 cards on offer, and announced a new competitive structure in June with a Championship Series and a lower-tier Challenger Series.
