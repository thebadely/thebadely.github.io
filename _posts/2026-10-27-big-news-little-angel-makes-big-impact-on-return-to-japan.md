According to new developments, Sisilia Tuipulotu made her Wales debut against Ireland in the 2022 Six Nations

It is doubtful that Japan's front row would have considered Sisilia Tuipulotu to be a Little Angel when the Wales tight-head prop was dominating scrums on her return to Tokyo.

The Welsh set-piece helped secure a 17-0 win in tough conditions in the first of two Tests against the Sakura Fifteen in the WXV Global Series.

Tuipulotu was to the fore at the Prince Chichibu Memorial Stadium in the city that she called home for three years.

The prop's father, Tonga back Sione, played for Yokogawa Musashino Atlastars between 2006 and 2010 after moving from Dragons.

Sisilia was enrolled at Little Angels international school and is back in Japan for the first time.

Tuipulotu said: "I remember bits and pieces, but I barely know what I did yesterday!"

Recollections of Tokyo as a child may be hazy but some of the scrums when the first Test was in the balance will live long in the memory.

Sean Lynn's side laid a platform up front to earn a scrappy win, with Tuipulotu playing 78 minutes.

"It was a long time to be on but the rest of the girls helped to keep me going," said the 5ft 9ins, 17st 6lbs prop, who has swapped Gloucester-Hartpury for Harlequins.

"There were lots of words of encouragement from the girls in the back-row because there were loads of scrums in the second half, telling me and Kelsey [Jones, hooker] to keep going.

"That comes down to the work of the strength and conditioning coaches, because before pre-season we had a pre, pre-season and that was savage. They have made us fitter as a team."

Tuipulotu, one of Wales' contenders for the British and Irish Lions tour to New Zealand next year, is showing signs of being back to her best going into Saturday's second meeting with Japan in Kumagaya.

"Lynny just tells me to be myself," added Tuipulotu.

"I play my best rugby with a smile on my face, then the game takes care of itself."

Tuipulotu, 23, was the fifth most experienced member of the starting line-up when she won her 39th cap in Tokyo.

Lynn has been quick to promote promising players when trying to turn around Wales' fortunes in the build-up to the 2029 World Cup.

Last weekend he fielded a second-row partnership of Jorja Aiono and Branwen Metcalfe, who had a combined tally of 15 caps.

"It's credit to the pathway that is bringing through players that are ready for this level," said Metcalfe, who started a capped international alongside her sister Nel for the first time.

"Players are coming through that are ready to perform and the coaches have been good at nurturing us and building further."
