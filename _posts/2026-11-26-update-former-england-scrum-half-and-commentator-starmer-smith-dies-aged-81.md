According to new developments, Nigel Starmer-Smith voiced BBC's rugby union highlights show, Rugby Special

Former England scrum-half and rugby union commentator Nigel Starmer-Smith has died aged 81. 

He had been diagnosed with dementia in 2015. 

Starmer-Smith represented Harlequins and the Barbarians, and made seven appearances for England between 1969-71.

After his retirement in 1976, Starmer-Smith moved into broadcasting and journalism. 

He voiced the BBC's highlights programme Rugby Special for 15 years and commentated for ITV at the 2003 World Cup in Australia, won by England. 

Starmer-Smith's son Charlie spoke about his father's condition in 2021, saying dementia was a "big issue" in sport and describing how the family were only able to see him through the window during the Covid-19 pandemic. 

He said that getting knocked out in a game of rugby was "like a badge of honour" during his father's playing days but also said dementia ran in their family, with his uncle also dying from the disease. 

"Dad passed away peacefully this morning after a long battle with dementia," Charlie posted on social media on Thursday.

"He was (as they say in rugby circles) one of life's great tourists. A rugby player, commentator, editor, writer, raconteur, charity fundraiser, volunteer, speaker, prankster, family man, life and soul of the party, but, above all, the greatest father anyone could wish to have.

"He did everything for us and, despite having to bury two of his children at cruelly young ages, both he and my Mum refused to let the cavernous holes that Charlotte and Julian left behind define them. 

"Just as I choose not to let the cruel condition that robbed him of his ability to communicate (and ultimately of his dignity), define my memories of him.

"He was a modest man and he'd hate for people to make a fuss. He loved to laugh but not as much as he loved to see others laughing. So for those that knew him think of a happy memory and smile. For that's how I'll remember him."
