It has been recently reported that Ofgem has announced the price cap will rise from January

A 26% increase in energy bills in January will make life "almost impossible" for millions of people, a charity boss has warned. 

Matt Cole, the CEO of the Fuel Bank Foundation which helps people living in fuel crisis, said the rise had been expected but would be difficult for people to absorb.

With fuel prices already high, he said: "It's going to make what's a tough life for so many people almost impossible."

Cole, from Bridgnorth, Shropshire, said the government - which is due to hand millions of households Â£100 to tackle rising costs - needed a long term strategy to decrease energy prices and to improve energy efficiency. 

Ofgem's announcement that it is raising the energy price cap means a typical bill will increase by about Â£37 a month in the first three months of the new year.

The price will depend on how much energy a household uses however, so it could be closer to Â£60 extra a month in winter.

The increase is the biggest since Russia's invasion of Ukraine in 2022 drove up prices.

The Fuel Bank Foundation supports about 400,000 people every year who are in fuel poverty and Cole said its own forecast for the price cap rise had been correct "almost to the penny".

He said the rise "really worries me because so many people today are already struggling to keep warm".

Cole explained that a lot of people had been lowering consumption for years. In some cases that meant severe cut backs.

"They're skipping meals, they're not cooking food, they're rationing hot water," he said.

"At the moment we are seeing so many people in fuel poverty and that number is only going to get worse as we go through the next four or five months."

The cap sets a maximum price for each unit of energy, not the total bill. 

Those on fixed deals are unaffected until the end of their term, but for those on variable tariffs it means:

For electricity, average unit prices will go up on 1 January from 26.32p per kilowatt-hour to 32.69p.

Gas will increase from 7.97p per kilowatt-hour to 10.96p, higher than in the wake of the invasion of Ukraine.

Daily standing charges will be 54.77p for electricity and 29.65p for gas.

The cap is set by Ofgem every three months, based on the costs faced by suppliers as well as the cost of running the energy system.

It is based on a three-month period that ends about six weeks before the new cap comes into force. A fall in international wholesale prices in recent days will not be reflected in January's cap, but will be in April's.

Cole said the government needed to do more for struggling households

The government is due to pay millions of households Â£100 from 4 January to help cover winter costs on top of an existing VAT cut on electricity.

Cole said it would provide "a little bit of breathing space," but did not go far enough and was "not much money at all now compared to what people will be paying from the start of next year".

He said the government needed to take "drastic action" and should be "decreasing the cost of energy through careful policy".

His advice to people struggling with the increased cost was to talk to local charities, their local authority and their energy providers, who are obliged to offer support.
