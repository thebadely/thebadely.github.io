According to new developments, Phil Salt and Aneurin Donald first opened the batting together for England during the first T20 international in Melbourne on Sunday

Aneurin Donald and Phil Salt were made in Wales but have made headlines around the world as England's explosive openers.

The batting pair launched a brutal stand of 151 to guide England to an eight-wicket T20 victory against Australia on the Gold Coast with nearly seven overs left.

Donald blazed 85 in just 32 balls, including striking nine sixes into the stands at the Carrara Oval, with Salt adding another four maximums as he lashed 58 in 30 deliveries.

The pair were born four months apart at opposite ends of Wales in 1996. They have now created history by becoming the first Wales-born pair to open the batting for England.

Seventeen men and five women England cricketers have been born in Wales but there has never been a duo opening the batting before. 

"It was pretty special doing that with Salty, two proud Welshmen ticking the box for the England and Wales Cricket Board," said Donald.

"We'll mention it a lot more now. It's a pretty unique position. There's not many sports where you play as two nations together."

Wales do not have a professional cricket side with Glamorgan representing the country as one one of 18 first-class counties alongside 17 English teams.

Former Glamorgan and England spinner Robert Croft, a fluent Welsh speaker and proud Welshman, used to use the analogy when asked about representing England.

"Playing for Glamorgan is like representing Wales, playing for England is like playing for the British and Irish Lions," he said.

Donald started his county career with Glamorgan before moving onto Hampshire and now Derbyshire, while Salt has never played for the Welsh county after joining Lancashire.

Salt, 30, is the more experienced international cricketer having made his international debut in 2021.

He was born to a Welsh mother in Bodelwyddan in north Wales and grew up in St Asaph before moving away to Barbados aged 11 because of his dad's job.

Donald, 29, was born and brought up in the Swansea suburb Gorseinon and was playing only his fifth one-day international after making his England debut in September.

"We are from north and south Wales so have got most basis covered," said Donald.

"Wales probably hasn't produced the guys they'd have liked to after having some good youngsters over the years. 

"It's special and it's testament to the system back home.

"Welsh cricket and the support I've had in my career has been incredible and it feels like this is a night where I can give back a little bit."

It was a breakthrough innings for Donald on the international stage as he looks to cement himself on the T20 arena.

Afterwards he was very modest about his cricketing talents with a degree of self-deprecation.

"In the last few years I've realised I'm not great at batting but I'm quite good at slogging," he said.

"In modern cricket I'm not sure there's too much (of a difference), especially in T20, but there might be a career in it now."

Donald says it was about "staying true to himself and trusting his bat swing". 

"I'm sure I'm going to make myself look like an idiot over the next few years batting the way I do, but hopefully it's going to come off more often than not," said Donald.

"I'm not the most skilful, I'm not the most powerful but my intent and the way I play has probably separated me out in the last few months. 

"There's going to be periods where it's not quite firing but it's about staying as true as I can."

Donald has had to show resilience after recovering from two serious knee injuries.

"It is why you spend all your days either recovering from injury or going through bad form," said Donald.

"It was a nice night to show what I can do and take the next step up.

"I feel like I am in a good place now with T20 cricket and it's a feather in my cap I could show it on a big stage."

It was a moment Donald was able to celebrate with parents Andrew and Jocelyn and uncle Paul who travelled to Australia to watch the five-match series.

The television cameras caught the emotional moments afterwards of Donald hugging his parents.

"My dad was a blubbering mess on the way down to see me at the front, but my mother was more composed," he said.

"It's a a special trip for them and a trip of a lifetime for me so far."

He says he is glad he could reward his family after their long trip but also the important people who had not been able to fly to Australia.

"They are glad they have made the flight out and it is the first time they have been in Australia," said Donald.

"I am glad I can do this in front of them and I am sure we will remember this day for a long time.

"My missus [Heather] and brother [Gafyn] are back home and I am sure they feel they have missed out a bit but they are also so important to me."

'He'll win many games' - Vaughan on Donald

So what next for Donald? Former England captain Michael Vaughan has advised him to stick to T20 cricket and predicted he will become a rich man by playing in franchise leagues across the world.

Donald will stay in Australia after the series because he has picked up a contract with Perth Scorchers in the Big Bash league. He will then join Joburg Super Kings in the Betway SA20 in South Africa, which starts in late January.

The Indian Premier League (IPL) remains the mecca for the world's top players.

Salt made his debut in 2023 in the tournament and has been part of three winning sides between 2024 and 2026. 

Speaking before flying to Australia, Donald said: "It's still a premium one and it always will be, not just for the financial element, but for the fanfare.

"You've got a billion fans out there. The biggest games, the biggest pressure with the biggest players and those Indian guys are absolute rock stars.

"It would be an incredible experience if I ever did get a crack out there."

Whether there is enough time for Donald to make the 2027 tournament remains to be seen. 

He is not on the initial 1,200 long list players for the auction that takes place on 16 December.

That long list is made up of 939 Indians and 281 overseas players, and they will compete for a maximum of 66 places across the 10 teams. 

Only 25 of those places are open to overseas players, so competition among the foreign names will be fierce.

Donald still has a limited time to attract the attention of one of the sides who will have witnessed his talents this week.

Whatever happens, Donald will have a taste of India next month. The Perth Scorchers will play against Melbourne Renegades on 12 December in Chennai, which will be the first foreign league to play a regular season fixture in India.

His opening partner that day is likely to be Mitch Marsh, who watched on as Australia's one-day captain on the Gold Coast.
