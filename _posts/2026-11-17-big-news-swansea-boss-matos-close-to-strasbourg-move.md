Vitor Matos is close to leaving Swansea City to take charge of French top-flight club Strasbourg.

Strasbourg have made an official approach for the Swansea head coach, who looks likely to depart even though there is no agreement yet between the clubs over a compensation package.

Matos was at Swansea's training ground on Tuesday morning, but is understood to have told the Championship side he wants to leave. 

Matos is under contract until 2030, meaning Swansea will be due significant compensation should he depart.

But that has not stopped Strasbourg targeting the 38-year-old Portuguese, who had been due to celebrate a year in charge at Swansea later this month.

Current Strasbourg boss Hugo Oliveira, Matos' fellow countryman, is under pressure after a run of five defeats in six matches.

Oliveira has managed only three wins in 10 league games since succeeding Gary O'Neil - who left to take over at Ipswich Town â in July.

O'Neil led Strasbourg to an eighth-place finish last season, but they are currently 13th in Ligue 1, only two points above the relegation zone.

Strasbourg are a sister club of Premier League side Chelsea.

Matos has turned Swansea from potential relegation contenders to promotion hopefuls since taking charge, with the club 20th in the Championship when he took over last November.

The Swans finished 11th last season and have made further strides in the first third of this campaign.

Vitor Matos led Swansea to seven wins in their first 10 league games of the season, though they have since lost four in five 

Matos' team have been among the early-season pacesetters in the second tier, enjoying two spells on top of the table, but their form has dipped in recent weeks.

Swansea have suffered four defeats in their past five matches, though they remain only three points off top spot going into Saturday's home game against West Bromwich Albion.

Matos was touted as a contender to take charge at Crystal Palace earlier this season - the Eagles have since appointed Thomas Frank - while he has also been linked with a return to Portugal on the back of his impact at Swansea.

When asked in October about the Palace links, Matos said: "I'm Swansea manager. I love being here, love my players, love the staff, so my focus is always on the next game and what we can do better."

In an interview last week, Swansea chief executive Tom Gorringe acknowledged that Matos' impact will not have gone unnoticed at other clubs, though he played down the idea of the former Liverpool coach having his head turned.  

"The reality is there'll always be interest in anyone performing well in any role on and off the pitch," Gorringe said. 

"That's one of the outcomes of being successful - we're aware of that.

"[But] we're all quite happy. We've all agreed to go on this journey together and we hope that Vitor is here a long time." 

After West Brom visit, Swansea go to second-placed Middlesbrough next Tuesday before Cardiff City make the short trip west for the first south Wales derby in two seasons on Sunday, 29 November. 

Should Matos depart, it is thought likely that a number of his backroom team will also move on.

Vitor Matos is relatively inexperienced, if you look at his senior management experience alone, but his wealth of footballing experience is evident when taking a wider look at his footballing journey. 

Speaking to BBC Sport earlier this season, Matos explained how his 23 years in football have informed his development - a journey that began when a 15-year-old Matos was inspired by Jose Mourinho's Champions League triumph with Porto. 

So, although he only took his first managerial job at the start of last season in the second division of Portugal, to call Matos an overnight success story would be false.

He developed an understanding of positional play while working as a youth coach at FC Porto, before moving to China. As technical director and an under-16 coach of Shandong Taishan, he added layers of knowledge to his positional game: 

"In China, I understood that to arrive at a more positional game, you could have different steps beforehand," he explained.

"There is a benefit to having more players close to each other, rather than spreading them over space.

"If you spread them over space, you are demanding that, technically, they can solve many situations one-v-one. If they come closer to the ball, you can generate more possibilities. You can create a tempo that allows other players to be better in the big spaces."

The uniqueness of these overloads, around the ball, have been a hallmark of some of Swansea's impressive attacking play this season.

Following stints in Portugal and China, he then found himself working alongside Jurgen Klopp and Pep Lijnders at Liverpool, which provided him a greater understanding of senior football at the elite level. 

"Liverpool influenced me a lot in terms of counter-pressing, pressing, intensity, and what makes you grow as a team," he said.

"The most important thing is how much you believe in your idea, and this style is how I see football. That's why I want this to be the foundation for our team."
