As Marc Marquez ascended a staircase, the silhouette of a goat matched his every step.

It was a nod to his increasing - or solidified, depending on who you ask - status as MotoGP's 'greatest of all time' (GOAT).

In winning the 2026 championship in Portugal on Sunday, Spain's Marquez secured his eighth overall title, the first per son in the modern era to achieve this accolade.

It drew the Ducati rider level with the great Giacomo Agostini, who won his eight titles between 1966-75.

And yet it is Valentino Rossi, who Marquez has now surpassed for World Championship crowns, who most view as his biggest rival for GOAT status. 

"I feel proud when the fans and media say that I am one of the greatest of all time," Marquez told BBC Sport on Monday.

"I do not agree when they say I am the greatest because every athlete in  motorsport has their moments, their career and injuries and I respect all of them, all the legends."

But despite his humility, is 33-year-old Marquez actually the sport's greatest? Have your say at the bottom of this article.

Is Marquez MotoGP's greatest?

In winning his eighth premier class championship on Sunday, Marquez overcame the biggest points deficit in MotoGP history - 102 points - to take the title.

But Marquez is the ultimate comeback king.

Last year, he won his first title in six years, a period in which he overcame 109 crashes, multiple injuries including a broken arm, ankle, ribs and fingers, and a severe concussion.

In 2023 he told BBC Sport he was "not ready to win again" and at the end of that season finished 14th in the standings. He then left Honda, a team where he won six titles, to join Gresini Racing, the satellite Ducati team.

After a solid 2024 season, in which he claimed three feature race wins and two pole positions, he joined the factory Ducati team and now has two titles in two seasons with the Italian outfit.

Aged 33 years and 278 days, Marquez is the oldest MotoGP champion in the modern era

But 2026 was not without its issues. Marquez was without a win when he arrived at the French Grand Prix in May, and things went from bad to worse after he suffered a crash in the sprint race at Le Mans and had to have operations on his foot and shoulder. He then missed the next round in Barcelona.

Marquez made his return at Mugello 20 days later, facing that hefty points deficit, to the then leader Marco Bezzecchi. 

"In that moment after the crash in Le Mans and then the seventh surgery on the right shoulder, [my title charge] was completely over. I was in hospital. I was 102 points behind the leader and I was not the fastest rider on the track," said Marquez.

"Then I say OK you can believe in me, but if you are not the fastest you cannot recover points. Then I tried to be very focused, to work on the mental side and to understand where my level was. I accepted that the level was key during the season and I had to take points and wait for my moment."

Marquez then won seven of the next 13 grand prix races, twice missing out on the chance to seal the title in Qatar and Saturday's sprint race in the Algarve, before finally clinching the win with one race weekend to come in Valencia.

While Sunday marked his eighth MotoGP title, it was Marquez's 10th championship overall after wins in the 125cc and Moto2 categories on his journey to the top of the sport.

Let's look at the case for Rossi.

The Italian is a motorcycle racing legend and still has a cult like status around the world, even after retiring in 2021.

Rossi is a seven-time MotoGP champion, with nine world titles to his name.

The now 47-year-old remains the only rider in history to win World Championships in 125cc, 250cc, 500cc and MotoGP.

Rossi has a record 89 victories in the premier class but Marquez is closing the gap with 80.

Marquez is still the only rider to win a MotoGP championship in his rookie year and after that dominated the series, with six titles in seven seasons between 2013 and 2019.

He and Rossi do share some MotoGP history. They are the only two riders to have won at least two world titles with two different manufacturers.

The rivalry between Rossi and Marquez is legendary.

In 2013 their battle at the Corkscrew at Laguna Seca became infamous, with Marquez eventually taking the win. 

The crashes have been dramatic too. In 2015 in Sepang, the pair collided with Marquez crashing out, resulting in a back-of-the-grid penalty for Rossi which ultimately cost him that year's title. 

Rossi has continually claimed that his younger challenger deliberately sabotaged his championship hopes, admitting in a recent interview on Italian TV that "it still stings".

Two years later, in Argentina, the riders came to blows again, this time with Rossi hitting the tarmac.

At one time Rossi accused Marquez of "destroying" the sport, with Marquez stating their relationship was like a "divorce".

Rossi was larger than life. His unique celebrations made him a fan favourite, from wearing a Robin Hood outfit on the podium, to pretending he was in charge of a chain gang.

Some fans thought Rossi's personality outshone Marquez's. But the Spaniard is also no stranger to making headlines when it comes to race wins, and in 2024 he choreographed a podium dance in Aragon which went viral on social media.

And in a not-so-subtle dig at his old foe in his post-race news conference on Sunday, Marquez was quick to leave a brief reminder of his now superior record of overall World Championship wins.

"This championship is for all of my fans, because when they speak at the table at Christmas, 'blah, blah, blah, blah' - 'yeah, yeah, yeah, but 10'."

Giacomo Agostini surprised Marc Marquez on stage after his title win

When it comes to legends in motorsport, Agostini's name is up there, with 15 world titles - eight in the premier class of motorcycle racing.

There was an emotional moment at the Portuguese Grand Prix when the 84-year-old surprised Marquez on stage and presented him with a golden helmet.

In the greatest of all time argument, Agostini is still the rider with the most world championship titles to his name.

The Italian's last championship win was in 1975, but his successes came in a very different era of the sport. 

"It is a big pleasure to equal Giacomo Agostini in premier class titles," said Marquez.

"He is the greatest of all time."

But in the modern era, does Marquez have the edge?

With a contract at Ducati extended until 2028 you would not rule him out of winning more titles in the future, but for now, Marquez is revelling in the present.

"I want to enjoy the moment, this week," he said. 

"I want to enjoy the next weekend in Valencia. That is a special weekend because it is in a Spanish city and [I will] celebrate the world championship there, and then we will have time to prepare for 2027."
