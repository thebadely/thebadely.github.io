It has been recently reported that Four-time Olympic champion Ben Ainslie split from Sir Jim Ratcliffe's Ineos Britannia America's Cup sailing team in January 2025

Sir Jim Ratcliffe's Ineos group has settled a High Court claim with Sir Ben Ainslie's Athena Racing over a Â£180m boat used in the America's Cup.

Ineos took legal steps against Athena Racing in March to secure the return of the boat - named Britannia - after alleging Ainslie's firm had breached a deal between the two companies. 

Ratcliffe's petrochemicals company funded two America's Cup cycles led by four-time Olympic champion Ainslie in Auckland in 2021 and Barcelona two years ago. 

However, relations between the Manchester United co-owner and Ainslie reportedly turned sour and they split in January 2025, with plans to challenge for the 38th America's Cup in Naples in 2027 abandoned three months later. 

In a statement on Monday, an Ineos spokesperson said they had reached a settlement with Athena Racing and "considers the matter now closed". 

Meanwhile, Athena confirmed the team has ownership of the multimillion-pound boat. "Athena Racing/GB1 have always been, and remain, the owners of their assets," a statement read. 

Ineos blamed the decision to split with Athena on "a protracted negotiation", claiming Ainslie's team had caused a costly six-month delay to settlement talks.

Ainslie became Ineos Britannia's team principal and skipper when he received the backing of Britain's largest privately-owned company in 2018 in a bid to win sailing's oldest prize - something Great Britain had never done before. 

In 2024, the team led by Ainslie, 49, reached the final of the 37th edition in Barcelona for the first time since 1964, but they were beaten 7-2 by defending champions New Zealand. 

Following the fallout with Ratcliffe, Ainslie secured private equity funding for Athena from Oakley Capital and rebranded the team as GB1 in January.
