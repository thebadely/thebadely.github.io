Here’s what you need to know: Luke Littler's title defence is over despite an average of 101.38 against Jermaine Wattimena

Defending champion Luke Littler suffered a shock early exit at the Grand Slam of Darts after a stunning display from Jermaine Wattimena.

The Dutchman was in tears after securing a remarkable, and thoroughly deserved, 10-8 victory in the second round in Wolverhampton.

Having looked imperious as he won six legs on the spin to take a 7-2 lead, 16th seed Wattimena showed signs of nerves as the finish line approached, but dug in to stave off a late Littler comeback and progress to the quarter-finals.

"I think I played really incredible, I played my best game on this stage and I'm really proud of it," an emotional Wattimena told Sky Sports.

"He started playing well at the end. Last leg, [I had] one dart for the match - amazing."

Wattimena finished with an average of 101.70 having hit a staggering 10 180s and, such was his level for the majority of the match, he might feel the margin of victory should have been more comfortable.

The 38-year-old twice threatened nine-darters and had two darts for an 8-2 lead, with Littler rarely even given a look at a double.

However, the 19-year-old world champion sensed an opportunity and showed his class with a 10-darter to break throw as Wattimena finally stuttered a leg from victory.

But a dramatic comeback fell just short as, with Littler waiting to pounce on 65, Wattimena hit tops to take out 77 for the match and reach the last eight of the tournament for a second time.

Wattimena will face compatriot Kevin Doets in the next round after the world number 23 saw off Scotland's Cameron Menzies 10-5.

Doets averaged 101.51 and matched Wattimena's 10 maximums as his relentless scoring made up for an off night on the doubles.

Jermaine Wattimena will face fellow Dutchman Kevin Doets in the next round

Fourth seed Gerwyn Price is also through after a clinical display to beat Englishman Ryan Searle 10-7.

The Welshman averaged 103.31 and hit 45% of his doubles to see off 13th seed Searle, who himself averaged just under 100.

Price will face Niko Springer for a place in the semi-finals after he overcame former world champion Rob Cross 10-8.

Cross averaged 96.05 to Springer's 88.59, but the German's finishing was far superior - with a checkout rate of 48% - and that proved the difference.
