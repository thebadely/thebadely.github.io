Two games, no wins. Two red cards, two goals scored. One goal disallowed, six conceded. Is it possible for Mauricio Pochettino to have learned anything from a chaotic first week as Manchester United boss?

United and Pochettino went on the warpath before the Champions League encounter with Sporting, furious at Leny Yoro's dismissal at Liverpool which they believe was unjust and shaped the eventual 4-0 defeat.

Club officials were seething after the 2-2 draw in Lisbon as well.

This time it was 2022 World Cup final referee Szymon Marciniak in the firing line. Blowing before Amad's first-half header went into the net meant VAR had no chance to assess whether the Polish official was right to rule Youri Tielemans had fouled Goncalo Inacio before prodding the ball through to the Ivorian.

Former United winger Ashley Young didn't think so. He called Marciniak's performance "a disgrace".

That Tielemans picked up a booking that eventually led to his late red card -  meaning the Belgian will miss the home match with RB Leipzig on 8 December just compounded the situation. United have now had four players sent off in their past five games.

Then, in a matter of minutes, Marciniak decided neither Manchester United keeper Senne Lammens nor Sporting counterpart Kaique had been fouled as they conceded. Their respective defences complained bitterly. Marciniak was only asked to go to the pitchside monitor by VAR for the latter incident, which he overturned.

United insiders felt the Tielemans incident was clearly not a foul and Amad's goal should have stood. They also felt any foul on Kaique by Patrick Dorgu was marginal and did not warrant their goal being disallowed.

Pochettino trod a fine path, knowing if he said something too controversial, it could land him with a fine.

A nod of a head in agreement was his initial reaction in his post-match news conference, when asked if he felt Marciniak had got it wrong.

A few minutes earlier, he had been more expansive when he spoke to TNT.

"It's difficult to find a game where you get punished [so much] in one game," he said. "We were punished a lot in key actions."

Not for the first time this season, United controlled extended periods of a game they ended up not winning.

The goals they conceded at the Jose Alvalade were bizarre.

Seventy-five seconds after superbly saving one penalty, goalkeeper Lammens suffered a rush of blood and conceded another, which was scored.

Lammens was at fault for the second, when he dropped the ball into the path of Sporting striker Luis Suarez, who scored with an overhead kick.

"I think we dominated and we deserved to win the game, but we didn't for different circumstances," said Pochettino.

"All the teams make mistakes, but for these mistakes the punishment (for us) is too big in every single mistake. Many other clubs make mistakes or tilt in a different circumstance, in a more positive way. But that is in the way we are. We need to take responsibility."

United are still to win away from home in any competition this season. They have now won once in their past 13 matches. In the Champions League, they are 19th, two points in front of Roma, who are just below the top 24, which guarantees passage to the next phase.

Young said Lammens may be one mistake from being dropped.

The Belgian's performance was not good and errors are beginning to mount.

It is not what Pochettino needs right now. United showed faith in the Belgium international this summer by bringing in 36-year-old Karl Darlow as his understudy.

Darlow did end last season as first choice at Leeds but started just 30 league games in his three seasons with the Elland Road club. Tom Heaton, 40, who was also on the bench against Sporting, has not played in a senior game since February 2023.

Could Pochettino contemplate leaving Lammens out.

"This type of thing happens," he said. "That is football. Football is about making mistakes.

"I always want to support my player. I think we need to talk about responsibility. In this type of action our defensive line needs to cover the keeper better because it was very difficult.

"It's not to blame the defensive line, but it's two things that we need to improve, details that we need to work on. We need to manage his type of situation better."

It has been a whirlwind week for Pochettino, who last Wednesday morning UK time was still United States boss.

There have been a few blows to absorb, and lessons are having to be learned quickly.

But despite the clear frustrations, the 54-year-old is glad he accepted the challenge.

"I am happy to be here," he said. "I'm so excited to be the face representing this club.

"We are here because the situation wasn't the situation all the fans and people expect. We are here to take the responsibility from one week, and we are going to improve for sure.

"It's going to be tough, of course. Nothing is easy. But we have the energy and I think we have the capacity, and, most important, we have the players and the power and the energy from our fans, which is massive."
