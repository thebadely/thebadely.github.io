UK Sport will no longer channel investment in skiing and snowboarding through the sports' UK governing body, GB Snowsport.

The news comes six months after Team GB enjoyed their most successful Winter Olympics at Milan-Cortina.

They won five medals, including an unprecedented three golds, one of which was in the snowboard cross mixed team event - a first ever gold medal on snow.

UK Sport has written to all snowsport athletes who receive funding to make them aware of the decision, also confirming funding grants will continue to be paid directly. An independent review will now be set up into the governance of GBS.

The funding is part of a record investment of more than Â£33m into winter sports for the four-year cycle.

Kate Baker, director of performance and people at UK Sport, said: "Competition across Olympic and Paralympic winter sport is only getting stronger, which is why it's more important than ever that we invest where we can have the biggest impact. 

"It is also why we are not afraid of driving the change needed to give our athletes the very best chance of success."

In a statement, GBS said it was "disappointed" by the decision but will continue to work with UK Sport. However, it said it "reserves the right to keep all options open to ensure that the achievements of the past four years are not lost".

GB Snowsport has changed its chair twice in the past three years and is currently advertising for a new chair.

Michael Oesterlin, who has been a non-executive director at the organisation for four years, is interim chair.

The GBS statement added: "We have recently concluded an independent governance review funded by UK Sport, which has confirmed that our governance standards remain strong and appropriate. 

"We look forward to engaging fully and robustly in any new review, which we are confident will reach the same conclusions."
