It has been recently reported that Verstappen "trusts F1 and the FIA to make the right call" on the Middle East

Max Verstappen says he "trusts" Formula 1 has made the right decision in pressing ahead with the final two races of the season in the Middle East. 

Last week, F1 stood down preparations for a substitute race at Imola in Italy if the events in Qatar and Abu Dhabi could not be held because of ongoing conflicts in the area. 

The sport will now conclude the season in the Middle East, unless the situation deteriorates before the Qatar event on 27-29 November and Abu Dhabi on 4-6 December.

Verstappen said: "I trust F1 and FIA to make the right call on that, right?

"I don't have all the intel. I'm not an officer. I just hope that they make the right call on that. And that's all that you can rely on, really.

"They also send over their findings and understanding. So I trust them."

Verstappen had previously voiced his opposition to ending the season in the Middle East.

"Normally I am happy to go to Qatar and Abu Dhabi, but you know, when there is a war nearby, and if some of the countries are in conflict with each other, then I think we shouldn't go," Verstappen said in September. 

The Red Bull driver's fresh remarks reflect a uniform public stance in F1 on the situation, even though there are widespread misgivings among personnel about travelling to the Middle East in the context of the two conflicts in the area.

Last Sunday Iran targeted a US military base in Bahrain  with a drone attack for the second time in three days, the latest salvo in the war launched against the country by the US and Israel in February. 

And Houthi rebels in Yemen are advancing in their war against the Saudi-backed government of the country.

Missile attacks from the Houthis into Saudi Arabia are threatening that country's chances of hosting the second grand prix of next season, which is scheduled for 19-21 March.

McLaren's Lando Norris said that while he is "fine" with the decision to race, he is concerned about the "big team" behind him.

"I have my friend's wedding in Bahrain next week, so I was planning on going there and then going on to Qatar and then Abu Dhabi," he added.

"But it's not about me. It's a lot more people. It's the fans, it's the team personnel, so the question is for them, more than I would say it is for myself."

Mercedes driver George Russell said he has "no hesitation" in racing and that while he's "sure some people have their reservations" he doesn't "know of anyone who's concerned".

"I know people who live in the region and they said it quite bluntly, if they didn't feel comfortable they wouldn't be there with their families," he said. 

"When you look around the world there are a lot of terrible things going on.

"You've just got to trust in the people. If they say it's safe, then you trust in them. F1 aren't stupid and I'm sure there's plans A, B, C, D, etc. So fingers crossed it'll all be fine."

MotoGP, which is owned by the same US group as F1, held a race in Qatar last weekend without problems.

F1, which has significant commercial links with the Middle East, has been closely monitoring the situation and has been advised by security officials and government figures in the Gulf and Europe that the races can go ahead, and there is no official advice against travelling to Qatar or Abu Dhabi.

F1 said in a statement last week that it was still prepared to cancel the two races in Qatar and Abu Dhabi if the situation deteriorated.

"If the situation develops negatively in the coming weeks before the events in the Middle East, we will not hesitate to do the right thing and the safety of everyone will always be the priority," an F1 spokesperson said on Tuesday.
