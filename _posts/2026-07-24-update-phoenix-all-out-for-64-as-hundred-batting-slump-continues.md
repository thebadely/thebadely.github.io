Trent Rockets make light work of woeful Birmingham Phoenix

The Hundred women's competition, Edgbaston 

Birmingham Phoenix 64 all out (95 balls): Perry 39 (33); Bates 3-10

Trent Rockets 68-1 (31 balls): Dunkley 43* (17); Filer 1-17

Rockets won by nine wickets

Scorecard. Table

Birmingham Phoenix were bowled out for just 64 by Trent Rockets as the bowler-dominated start to The Hundred continued at Edgbaston. 

Ellyse Perry played a lone hand with her 39 as no other Phoenix batter made it to double figures in a dire performance. 

It was the joint second-lowest total in the Women's Hundred, and Phoenix also hold the record for the lowest when they were skittled for 54 against Northern Superchargers (now Sunrisers Leeds) in 2024.

Oval Invincibles (now MI London) also made 64 against Superchargers in the same year.

Debutant Samantha Bates - an uncapped Australian spinner - was the pick of Rockets' bowlers with 3-10 while seamer Georgia Elwiss took 3-13. 

On another bowler-friendly surface, Australia's Kim Garth also bowled a miserly spell of 2-5 and leg-spinner Katie Levick took 1-8. 

England batter Sophia Dunkley dominated the chase with 43 from 17 balls as it took Rockets just 31 balls to reach their target to complete a thoroughly one-sided contest.

It has been a struggle for batters in all four matches so far. London Spirit were bowled out for 80 in their first match on Thursday while the highest total has been Southern Brave's 126, which they successfully defended against Welsh Fire.

Rockets' only blemish was Ash Gardner's drop of Perry on nine, which would likely have resulted in even more humiliation for Phoenix as their captain was the only one to prove that the pitch was not a complete mystery.

It was a horror start to The Hundred for Phoenix which continues a difficult few years. Since 2023, they have finished bottom of the table and then seventh twice.

After the auction earlier this year, they strengthened their batting and though the conditions suited the bowlers and it was a good toss for Rockets to win, it was not a 64 all out pitch. 

Davina Perrin - last year's breakout star - and Tammy Beaumont form the new opening partnership but Perrin was bowled by Elwiss for two off 10 balls and Beaumont chased a wide-ish delivery from Garth and was caught at point for a duck.

Those early wickets allowed Bates, a new name to The Hundred but with 139 matches to her name in Australia's WBBL, to settle into a nerveless spell. 

Alice Capsey, another England player, was bowled missing an ambitious sweep, South Africa's Annerie Dercksen edged behind off a ball that did turn and Jemima Spence inexplicably tried a ramp and was caught at short fine leg.

Perry could only watch the chaos unfold at the other end as she desperately tried to revive the innings, whacking two fours and two sixes in her knock, but her team-mates had no answers to Rockets' consistency.

Dunkley, who has found herself in and out of England's T20 World Cup side, added further embarrassment to poor Phoenix as she made the pitch look like a road and clubbed eight fours and a six to start Rockets' campaign in style with a record-breaking chase in terms of fewest balls faced to complete vicotory.
