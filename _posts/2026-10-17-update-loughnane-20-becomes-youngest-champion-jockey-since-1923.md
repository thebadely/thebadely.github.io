Let's dive into the details: Billy Loughnane became the youngest rider in over a century to be crowned Britain's champion jockey when he collected his trophy at Ascot on Saturday. 

Aged 20, the Irishman beat five-time champion Oisin Murphy to the title, becoming its youngest holder since Charlie Elliott in 1923.

Four years after his racing debut, Loughnane won his first Classic in May, the 2,000 Guineas, and rode to four further Group One victories among more than 220 wins this year.

Loughnane was given a guard of honour as he made his way to the parade ring for the presentation

Loughnane won the 2000 Guineas, St James's Palace and Sussex Stakes on Bow Echo, a horse he called "a dream come true"

Loughnane celebrated with his parents, Mark and Clare

"From the age of six to when I was 12, I used to go racing with my dad [trainer Mark Loughnane] every Saturday to Wolverhampton and I'd be distraught if he didn't have a runner!

"Nearly four years ago when I was going up to Newcastle for my first ride, my aim then was just to have a few winners really. If anyone had said that day that four years later I'd be where I am now I'd have said they were absolutely crazy.

"It's amazing that I've found something that is a passion of mine and to be able to do it as a job as well. Then when you get big success it's even better."

Loughnane's ambitions do not stop here. He is now eyeing Sir Gordon Richards' 1947 record on the Flat of 269 winners in a calendar year and is "not squashing the idea of going for the championship again" in 2027.

Murphy, who went toe-to-toe with Loughnane for a while before the latter strolled to the title, paid generous tribute to his young opponent: "He's got tremendous discipline and work ethic and his results have been outstanding.

"I get on very well with him and I'm delighted to see his success, he's just younger and better looking!"

Meanwhile, it was a memorable afternoon at Ascot for British-Norwegian jockey William Buick who won the Champion Stakes on Ombudsman and the Queen Elizabeth II Stakes on Talk of New York, before completing a 206/1 treble by riding Fifth Column to victory in the Balmoral Handicap.
