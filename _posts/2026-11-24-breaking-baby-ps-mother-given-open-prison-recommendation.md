Experts revealed that Peter Connelly suffered more than 50 injuries, despite being on the at-risk register

The mother of the toddler known as Baby P - who died after months of abuse - has been told she will not be freed from jail, although the Parole Board has recommended she should be moved to an open prison.

Tracey Connelly, now 45, was jailed in 2009 for causing or allowing the death of her 17-month-old son Peter at their home in Tottenham, north London, in August 2007. 

Connelly has been freed twice, in October 2013 and July 2022, and recalled to prison both times, in February 2015 and August 2024.

Public parole hearings took place last October, but the case was adjourned after the panel decided there needed to be a further psychological assessment.

Tracey Connelly has been freed on licence on two previous occasions 

Peter suffered more than 50 injuries, despite being on the at-risk register and receiving 60 visits from social workers, police officers and health professionals over eight months.

Connelly's then-boyfriend Steven Barker and his brother, Jason Owen, were also convicted of causing or allowing the death of 17-month-old Peter.

Giving evidence to the Parole Board review, which was livestreamed from prison, Connelly admitted she had been a "horrendous" mother who "knew deep down" that Barker was abusing her son, but she had wanted her Prince Charming and her children "paid for that".

She said she never intended to live with anyone again or be around other people's children. "I want to be the CEO of my own home and my own life," she told the review.

At the Old Bailey in 2009, Connelly was given an indeterminate sentence for public protection (IPP), which allowed prisoners to be held beyond their minimum term and kept on licence for years after release. 

Such sentences were abolished in 2012, and recent reforms mean licences now end after a set period without recall. The panel said that but for these reforms it would have released her again on strict licence conditions.

The Parole Board said that "somewhat ironically" a measure meant to address the unfairness of IPP sentences had made it harder to free Connelly.

Every professional who gave evidence supported her release, but lawyers for the Justice Secretary Alex Norris opposed it, citing Connelly's history of deception, her failure to disclose relationships and an assessment rating her a high risk of serious harm to children.

A spokesperson for the Parole Board said: "This was a recommendation only and the secretary of state for justice considers the advice before making the final decision on whether a prisoner is suitable for open conditions.

"We will only make a recommendation for open conditions if a Parole Board panel is satisfied that the risk to the public has reduced sufficiently to be manageable in an open prison."
