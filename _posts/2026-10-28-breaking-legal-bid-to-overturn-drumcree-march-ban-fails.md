An attempt to overturn the Northern Ireland secretary's decision to ban the Drumcree parade has failed.

The High Court in Belfast has ruled that Sir Chris Bryant's decision made on 9 October was lawful.

The judicial review was brought by a Portadown Orangeman against Sir Chris and the Police Service of Northern Ireland (PSNI) Chief Constable Jon Boutcher.

The secretary of state's intervention to stop the parade came after he received security advice from Boutcher.

A lawyer for the Orangeman told the court that an appeal of the judgment is "likely".

The Drumcree dispute led to a stand-off between Orangemen and nationalist residents of the Garvaghy Road in Portadown lasting almost two weeks.

Mr Justice Scoffield said: "The secretary of state's decision was lawful and within the margin of discretion properly afforded him, although it is highly regrettable that it was required in the circumstances which arose."

He said those who opposed the march should "take no particular joy in the outcome of this case".

The judge added that "the position which has now been reached has been brought about at very significant cost to the people of Northern Ireland - financially, through the diversion of police resources, and in terms of community relations".

"Depressingly, the threat of serious and widespread disorder has loomed large," he said.

He went on: "Significant concerns have now been expressed about respect for the rule of law in this jurisdiction.

"That includes among some elected representatives, by reason of their participation in the unnotified protest."

The latest flare-up of the Drumcree dispute has featured a number of legal challenges, involving the Parades Commission, Orange Order and Garvaghy Road residents. 

The dispute first came to prominence in the mid-1990s and almost derailed the Northern Ireland peace process at the time.

When the Orange Order was prevented by police from walking down the route in July 1995 there was a tense stand-off. It was the same for the next three years.

The Parades Commission began operating in 1998 to regulate parades and try to defuse tension around marches.

Its decision not to allow a march down the Garvaghy Road that summer has been challenged by the order for the past three decades.
