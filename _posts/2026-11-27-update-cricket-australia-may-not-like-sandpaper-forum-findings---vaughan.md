Cricket Australia (CA) will not like what it finds from a forum into the sandpaper scandal, according to former England captain Michael Vaughan.

CA has invited evidence into the ball-tampering controversy of Australia's Test against South Africa in Cape Town in 2018 following weeks of speculation over which players and coaches were involved.

"It's very clear to me that Cricket Australia are going to find some things out that they're probably not going to want to find out," Vaughan told the Test Match Special podcast.

"If they don't release the truth of what they find in this forum out to the public, we're going to get back to square one."

The row over the ball-tampering scandal was reignited by claims published in the autobiography of former Australia batter Usman Khawaja.

At the time of the controversy, only batter Cameron Bancroft, then captain Steve Smith and his deputy David Warner were punished for the cheating plot.

Khawaja alleged all players and coaches knew of the tampering and should have taken responsibility, a testimony supported by Bancroft, Warner and squad players Chadd Sayers and Jon Holland.

Former coach Darren Lehmann and Australia captain Pat Cummins have been among those who have denied knowledge or involvement.

In an attempt to calm the row that has engulfed Australian sport, CA has established a six-week forum for players, coaches and staff involved in 2018 to give evidence to senior counsel Fiona McLeod.

Although McLeod will publish her findings and CA will give a public response, evidence will remain confidential, there will be no new attribution of responsibility and no further sanctions.

The scope of the forum will extend to the Australian home season prior to the 2018 tour of South Africa, which included their 4-0 Ashes defeat of England.

Writing in his autobiography in 2019, former England opener and captain Sir Alastair Cook said the tourists had suspicions about how Australia were getting the ball to move, and that Warner later admitted to using substances attached to the strapping on his hands in domestic matches.

Asked about what the CA forum could uncover about the 2017-18 Ashes, Vaughan said: "From my cricket experience and a cynical look at the past of what may or may not have happened, I don't think Cricket Australia are going to like what they find.

"Do they make that all public? There's no way in a million years that more people didn't know something was happening to a ball."

Despite the potential implications for a series result against England, the England and Wales Cricket Board has not contacted CA about the forum and has no plans to do so.

Announcing the forum, CA chair David Boon said: "It is very important to understand that it will not seek to sanction or recriminate."

And Vaughan agreed that the governing body should not seek to impose new punishments.

"You can't go back and punish people," said Vaughan, who famously led England to victory in the 2005 Ashes. "The punishment was far too harsh for the three players at the time. Darren Lehmann lost his job as coach for Australia - there's four people who were really punished.

"The tampering of a ball is almost the smallest part of the story now. It's the cover-up. It just seems to me there has been such a big cover-up.

"Some have said that they had no clue sandpaper was used. I understand that, they can have that opinion. The more different aspects of this story that come to light, the more there is that element of do you believe those people? You have to say I'm not too sure about that now."
