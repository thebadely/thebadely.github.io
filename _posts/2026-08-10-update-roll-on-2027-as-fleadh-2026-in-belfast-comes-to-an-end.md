The success of the Fleadh has prompted calls for Belfast city centre to be pedestrianised permanently.

A large zone in the city centre was created over the course of the festival.

Representatives from the Alliance Party from across Belfast have now written to the council and other bodies calling for a "comprehensive review of pedestrianisation" in the city centre.

South Belfast MLA Kate Nicholl said the Fleadh showed "Belfast at its best, open, welcoming and full of life". 

"When we design our streets around people instead of traffic, our city centre becomes somewhere people actually want to spend time, and that's good for our shops, our hospitality sector and as we've seen so clearly this week, our sense of civic pride".

She said accessibility had to be taken seriously, however.

"Any expansion has to be designed with disabled people and accessibility organisations from the start, not bolted on afterwards. That means looking properly at disabled parking, accessible drop-off points, public transport connectivity and wayfinding," she said.

Green Party councillor Brian Smyth said the "bottom line" is that the Fleadh "would not have worked without pedestrianisation in the city centre".

"I think it is starting a conversation with people," he said.

"This isn't some new, radical idea."

However disability campaign groups, such as Disabled People Against Cuts Northern Ireland (DPAC NI), have voiced concern.

On social media they said: "We've seen this played out elsewhere and it's disabled people who pay a price for 'progress'."

DPAC NI spokesperson Dermot Devlin said that "many disabled people can only walk or wheel short distances".

"We need to park or get off public transport close to our destinations."

He added that "further pedestrianisation" of the city centre "without significant mitigation will exclude many of us from our city".

"We have a right to access the city on an equal basis to everyone else. Like everyone else we use the city centre as a place to shop, a place to work and place to go out in.

"Agencies involved in making permanent or temporary changes to the city centre must uphold our rights, not ignore them."
