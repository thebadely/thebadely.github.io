Stones, 32, was always going to be in demand as he was available on a free transfer after a hugely-successful spell at Manchester City, winning the Premier League six times and the Champions League once. 

The only question was over his fitness, but he answered those worries by featuring in five of England's eight World Cup matches, having played in all five in 2022 and all seven in 2018, when the Three Lions again lost in the semi-finals.

He has played 94 times for England and was also an ever-present in their runs to the European Championship finals in 2021 and 2024 and seems to be a risk-free bargain for Inter.

In the past eight years, England have reached two World Cup semi-finals, one quarter-final and the final of two European Championships.

Firstly under Gareth Southgate and then under Thomas Tuchel, the Three Lions have generally been well organised, hard to break down and tough to beat - with defensive players being able to shine.

When they finished fourth in the 2018 World Cup, they conceded only eight goals in seven games and it was the same figures in 2026 in their opening seven matches, before the madness of the 6-4 win over France.

They only conceded twice in seven games in the European Championship in 2021 and six in seven matches at Euro 2024, losing in the final on both occasions.

Under Tuchel, England did not concede a single goal in qualifying for the 2026 World Cup, winning all eight qualifiers and keeping clean sheets in all of them to get to North America with a maximum 24 points.

An attacking and exciting 4-2 win over Croatia in their 2026 opener, led to many thinking there had been a change of philosophy, before England then kept two clean sheets in a row in their other group matches.

England's resolute defending was typified in the last-16 3-2 win over co-hosts Mexico, with Tuchel's side hanging on, despite playing the final 40 minutes with 10 men after Quansah had been sent off.

Similar defensive qualities saw off Norway and star striker Erling Haaland in the quarter-finals, but they could not repeat the trick late on in their semi-final when Argentina, inspired by Lionel Messi, scored twice late on to end English hopes of glory.
