It has been recently reported that Channel 4's chief executive has admitted she doesn't know how many complaints of sexual misconduct were received about Married at First Sight UK, despite a six-month external review into the hit TV show.

When asked by MPs, Priya Dogra said: "I'm not sure I have the details," adding that the review looked specifically at the claims of three women featured in a BBC Panorama.

Married at First Sight UK was cancelled after Panorama revealed rape allegations from two female cast members, while a third woman alleged a non-consensual sex act. The men involved have denied all the allegations.

Separately, Dogra said the broadcaster "cannot apologise enough" to a former employee who twice made complaints about Russell Brand and felt they weren't properly handled.

Dogra said it was "almost unforgivable" that the woman said she complained to Channel 4 in 2009 and again in 2023.

Brand is serving a 10-and-half-year prison sentence after recently being found guilty of one count of rape and three counts of sexual assault.

Married at First Sight UK, known as MAFS UK, had been running for 10 seasons and was one of Channel 4's biggest shows, with audience figures often topping three million.

The Panorama investigation in May heard from three women who made allegations about the men they were partnered with - claims the men deny:

One woman said her on-screen husband raped her and threatened her with an acid attack.

A second woman told both Channel 4 and production company CPL, before broadcast, about being allegedly raped by her on-screen husband. Her episodes were still aired.

A third woman accused her on-screen husband of sexual misconduct.

All three women accused the show of not doing enough to protect them.

A review was commissioned into MAFS UK by Channel 4 shortly after the BBC first contacted the broadcaster with details of the allegations.

Earlier this month, the completed review recommended extra safeguards on the show. But Channel 4 said the programme had "run its course" and apologised to the women involved in the investigation.

Speaking to MPs on the House of Commons culture and media committee on Tuesday, Dogra was repeatedly asked if she knew how many allegations Channel 4 and CPL had received over the years that MAFS UK was on air.

"I looked specifically into those instances [the three women], I did not do a broad sweep of every episode or every season of the show. That is not what the terms of reference for the show were," she responded.

"If there are others, we would take them seriously and look into them on the same basis."

"You're saying your inquiry didn't actually look at how many complaints were made into this show in total?" replied the committee's chair, Dame Caroline Dinenage.

"The inquiry looked at our handling of these specific incidents," responded Dogra.

When pushed about who would know how many allegations of harm had been received over the years, Dogra said that information would be held internally between Channel 4 and CPL.

Dogra, who started as Channel 4 chief executive earlier this year, also said she was "frankly uncomfortable" with some of the changes proposed by the review into MAFS UK - such as 24-hour CCTV and lockable bedrooms - hence her reason for axing the show.

Channel 4's bosses were also questioned at length about Brand by MPs in the hearing.

Brand's work on Channel 4 included hosting Big Brother sister show Big Brother's Big Mouth and its celebrity equivalent from 2004 to 2007, a chat show in 2006, and appearing on Big Fat Quiz of the Year.

Addressing Dogra and Channel 4's chairman Geoff Cooper, MPs said the member of staff who made two complaints about Brand "fell through the cracks".

In response, Dogra said: "I'm not sure we can apologise enough as an organisation for putting her through that twice. We have apologised in public in the past and in private."

She accepted that the woman's complaints were "not handled properly" and added that "it's almost unforgivable that happened twice".

When asked by the committee if junior members of staff could raise complaints against a star without fear of losing their job, Dogra replied she "can't say 100%" that that would be the case.

"What I can do is set the standard, set the tone, make sure that the facilities are available, make sure that my staff and the production companies understand the repercussions of not complying, and understand the kind of culture that I'm trying to set, and we're trying to set as an industry."

She also said she found the "cultural personality" around Brand "horrendous".

"I find it distasteful, and I look at the jokes... that is the lowest form of humour, because these are jokes that are made at the expense of someone else's physical safety.

"I don't believe it should have been acceptable then, and I certainly don't think it's acceptable now."

During the session, Dame Caroline also put to Channel 4 bosses that in both the MAFS UK and Brand cases, the broadcaster appeared like "a child with their hand in the cookie jar".

The committee chairwoman asked: "To what extent can you reassure us that Channel 4's priority is genuinely the welfare of the staff who work there, the staff who have worked there, and the people who take part in your shows - rather than purely when the world is looking at you and you suddenly feel that you've got a reputational reason to take a look at these shows?"

Dogra responded by saying that when Channel 4 commissioned the review, "we didn't even know whether the Panorama programme would be made".

She added: "We had received a right-to-reply letter and the programme came out six weeks later, but it equally it could not have been made. We always intended to do the review."

In the instance of the woman who tried to complain about Brand, she added: "I'm not sure there are more apologies we can give. Because none of those things should've happened."
