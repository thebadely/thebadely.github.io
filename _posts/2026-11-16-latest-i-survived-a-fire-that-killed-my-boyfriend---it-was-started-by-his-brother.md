Bex Williams has told her story nearly 10 years after she was almost killed

After spending the night at a Hogmanay party, Cameron Logan and his girlfriend Bex Williams returned to his family home, in a quiet suburb of Glasgow, in the early hours of New Year's Day 2017.

The young couple fell asleep on an inflatable mattress in the living room.

Within hours, Cameron would be dead and Bex critically injured.

The couple were attacked by Cameron's older brother, who poured petrol over them and set fire to their bed.

Blair Logan was jailed after admitting murder and attempted murder.

Nearly a decade later, Bex is telling her story for the first time as part of BBC Scotland's Murder Case series.

Warning this article contains graphic images.

Bex was 24 and had recently finished university and started what she described as her "dream career" in radio.

"I loved my job. I loved radio reporting and news reading," she added.

"I had dreams of becoming a foreign correspondent at a big organisation like the BBC."

But then came the fire.

"My life completely changed in those 10 seconds," she told BBC Scotland.

"I was just a normal young woman in a new relationship.

"My main worries were what I was going to wear and if I could afford that extra glass of wine."

Instead, Bex woke up in intensive care days after the attack. She was on a ventilator and doctors were unsure if she would survive.

Bex suffered severe burns as well as internal injuries

She was left with severe burns to her face and hands, and internal damage to her throat and lungs. 

Bex had a breathing tube put into her windpipe and underwent 10 surgeries.

But it was damage to her vocal cords that ultimately forced her to give up her radio job. The damage to her voice is still apparent today.

Bex says she has decided to speak about the attack now to highlight the trauma she and her family went through - and make sure Cameron is remembered.

At the time of the attack the young couple were in the process of "building a life"  and had just moved in together. 

Cameron, 23, was at university and had ambitions of becoming a police detective.

Bex said: "There isn't a day that goes by that I don't think about Cameron, sometimes it is happy memories... sometimes it is not.

"I still can't really process what happened to him. 

"It is horrific. When you actually think about what he would have experienced in his final moments."

Bex and Cameron were in the process of moving in together

The attack followed years of tension between the brothers.

The High Court in Glasgow heard that Blair Logan, then 26, had barely spoken to Cameron since their grandmother's death in 2013.

In the weeks before the fire, he had stored petrol in his bedroom and carried out internet searches relating to burn injuries.

On the morning of the fire, Cameron's mother, Catherine, was woken shortly after 07:00 by the family dog. 

When she went downstairs, she saw a figure dressed in dark clothing in the living room.

Moments later, it was engulfed in flames. 

Bex managed to crawl into the kitchen, where she put her head inside the freezer to protect herself from the heat.

Cameron's father and a neighbour helped rescue her, but Cameron could not be saved. The family dog, Gomez, also died in the fire.

Blair told police he was out running when the fire started, but was arrested two weeks later after he could not be found on CCTV on his supposed route.

Officers also found traces of petrol fumes on his clothes and a blue medical glove in his pocket, which he claimed he wore while running. 

Initially, he admitted pouring petrol over the couple but claimed he had not intended to kill his brother - only "maim him".

In July 2017, he pleaded guilty to murder and attempted murder.

He was sentenced to life imprisonment, with a minimum term of 20 years.

Blair Logan started the fire at his own family home in Milngavie 

The consequences of the attack extended far beyond the injuries Bex suffered that morning. 

She has worked hard to get over the "pain, trauma and anger" she was left with. 

She abandoned her ambitions of building a career in broadcasting and is now employed by Police Scotland, in a role that sees her working with family liaison officers and some of the officers involved in her case.

She says her job change is a fitting tribute to Cameron, who himself wanted to join the force.

"I know I can never bring Cameron back, but what happened to us has to mean something. So if I can help other people, that is something," she added.

Bex continues to rebuild her life. Her career has taken a different direction and she has found happiness with a new partner. 

What has changed most, nearly 10 years on, is her perspective on what matters.

"I am still very career driven; my job is very important to me - but some things just feel less important now, like what am I going to wear. 

"Can I afford that glass of wine? Well, tell you what, I'm going to have that glass of wine," she said.
