Let's dive into the details: The all-British heavyweight fight is due to take place on 11 December

Councillors have approved plans allowing December's highly anticipated Tyson Fury v Anthony Joshua fight in Cardiff to finish in the early hours.

Fans in hospitality boxes at the Principality Stadium will be allowed to drink alcohol until 03:00, with the venue closing at 03:30, to align with prime time viewing in the US.

Members of Cardiff Council's Licensing Sub Committee were told 130 police officers would help maintain order at the event on 11 December, with twice the number of stewards present compared to a regular sports event.

A handful of residents had objected to the proposed late finish, raising concerns over noise, antisocial behaviour and public safety, but none came to the meeting.

Councillors were told the event was a "big deal for the city" and would boost the Welsh capital, in terms of "revenue" and "profile."

Huw Thomas, chief inspector with South Wales Police, said the challenge for officers was the length of the programme, compared with other events in the stadium, saying the fight was begin viewed by the force as a "three-day event".

On Wednesday, the council meeting was told 450 out of the 1600 stewards present on the night would be dedicated to fans seated on the pitch area of the stadium.

Mark Williams, Stadium Director at the Principality Stadium, said the ring walk would take place at 01:00 GMT on 12 December, with the fight expected to start at 01:30.

Regarding tickets, he said there were still tickets available, but they were "expecting a capacity of 77,000, if everything sells".

The boxing rivals are expected to contest the WBA 'super' heavyweight title

Councillors heard objectors had raised "legitimate concerns", but measures had been put in place to mitigate them.

Plans for more trains in the early hours of Saturday were outlined, to support spectators getting home after the fight.

In response to concerns over noise, the meeting was told "no external PA system would be used" and noise would be "monitored" on the night.

Councillors heard initial plans to have live music before the fight would not happen but national anthems would be sung.

Regarding alcohol, councillors were told the application was for a "modest two-hour extension on the licence for one night only" and that approximately 10-15 venues were planning to open after hours for fans.

Breathalysers would be used on the gates but only for those, "who look worse for wear", according to Williams.

After a short period of consideration, chair of the committee, Grace Ferguson-Thorne, said the panel had agreed to grant permission, describing the measures taken - including the stewarding levels and the closure of the roof - as "really reassuring".

While early-hour finishes require extensive co-ordination around transport, policing and crowd management in the city centre, the venue already holds a precedent for late-night boxing.

In 2007, Joe Calzaghe's super-middleweight unification victory over Mikkel Kessler did not start until 02:00 to satisfy viewers of the US television network Showtime.

Councillors were told that "not much happened" after the main event.

But other fights in recent years have proved more of a challenge, including Anthony Joshua v Joseph Parker in 2018, which saw the headliners come out for their ring walks at an early time of 22:30.

That fight was attended by more than 78,000 people, which set a record for the largest ever attendance at any Principality Stadium event.

Councillor Sean Driscoll, who declared himself a Joshua fan during the meeting, said he was "sure the Cardiff hospitality businesses" would give Fury "and his fans a warm welcome".
