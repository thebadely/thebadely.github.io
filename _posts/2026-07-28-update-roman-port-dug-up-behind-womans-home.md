Woman discovers 12 Roman buildings beneath her paddock

The remains of what is believed to be a Roman port have been dug up on land behind a woman's home.

Archaeologists said they had found evidence of 12 buildings beneath a paddock close to the Humber estuary, in Brough, East Yorkshire.

Melanie Stanford, who owns the land, said it had come as a complete surprise to learn of the scale of the Roman settlement.

Dr Peter Halkon, site director, said the town was ideally located as a trading point, with access to York and the European continent.

Dr Peter Halkon says Brough was ideally located as a trading point

Brough is thought to be on the site of Petuaria, the tribal capital of the Parisi, who lived in eastern Yorkshire during the Roman period.

Halkon, 70, is leading the community dig for the Petuaria ReVisited Project, external, which was set up in 2018 to shed more light on the town's Roman past.

He said the buildings would likely have been used to store goods taken off ships.

Other artefacts found there included pottery, animal bones and oyster shells.

"The Romans were incredibly fond of oysters," he said.

Roman walls in the area have been known about since the 1930s, but digs in recent years have led to new discoveries, including a Roman forum and a theatre.

Archaeologists made the discovery close to the Humber estuary

Stanford said she was approached for permission to carry out a dig after ground-penetrating radar revealed the buildings.
