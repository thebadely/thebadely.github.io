Andy Taylor, who has died aged 65, was the wild boy of Duran Duran.

In the early 1980s, the pop legends sold over 100 million records and dominated the charts in both Britain and the United States.

But success came at a cost. For Taylor, global superstardom became a cocaine and alcohol fuelled nightmare that left him burnt out and battling clinical depression.

"For sure, we paraded around in our fast cars with beautiful models on yachts in the south of France without needing to pay the bill," he later wrote. 

"But was it worth it?"

Andrew Taylor was born in North Tyneside on 16 February 1961.

Home was Cullercoats, a coastal village near Whitley Bay where many people, including his uncles, depended on the sea for their livelihoods. 

"It was like Coronation Street," he said, "with fish."

At first, it was a happy childhood. There was church on Sunday with his parents, Ronnie and Blanche, followed by games of football on the beach with younger brother Ronnie junior. 

On Christmas Day, his extended family would belt out Bing Crosby hits around the piano.

Andy Taylor's childhood was blighted when his mother abandoned the family without explanation

Everything changed on his first day at grammar school. 

The 11-year-old returned home to find his father in the kitchen, silently weeping. Taylor's mother, it seemed, had simply disappeared. There wasn't even a note. 

"It was like a guillotine had suddenly come rushing down," he recalled. It "sliced everything apart without explanation as to why I was guilty".

They heard nothing for four years. 

After that, occasional meetings with his mother were chilly encounters with no explanation offered. Taylor never discovered why she had left.

Having worked hard to pass his 11-plus exams, Taylor rapidly went off the rails. 

He skipped school and spent many days in remote caves on the beach, drinking cider and smoking dried banana skins. He'd heard absurd rumours this would get him high.

Before long, the school kicked him out. 

Salvation lay in a guitar he found hidden in his father's cupboard and a BBC Two programme that taught him the basic chords.

Andy Taylor's earliest performances were in working men's clubs in the north-east of England

By the age of 16, he was working on a building site and playing in a band. 

Taylor and his friends performed at working men's clubs across the north-east of England, often providing musical accompaniment for strippers.

A contract to provide entertainment for American troops in Germany included a clause stipulating that he did six hours a night on stage. 

In return, the soldiers introduced him to marijuana.

On his return, Taylor saw an advert seeking a "livewire guitarist" for a new band based in the West Midlands. 

He called the number and "made that fateful train journey to Birmingham".

Duran Duran, named after a character in Jane Fonda's space-sex movie Barbarella, were to be based in a club called the Rum Runner.

On arrival, he met bassist John Taylor and drummer Roger Taylor - all unrelated. 

Keyboardist Nick Rhodes followed and, after a short search, Simon Le Bon, the lead singer, appeared "looking like an Elvis who'd been to the chip shop".

Andy Taylor's initial impression of Simon Le Bon was that he looked like Elvis who'd been to a chip shop

They didn't waste time. Within six weeks, the group had pulled together enough material for an album.

Andy Taylor's musical challenge was to work out how his guitar fitted a post-punk age of electronic music, while Le Bon's lyrics and melody skated over the top. 

A record deal with EMI gave the group the production support they needed.

Planet Earth was released on 21 February 1981. It raced towards the top 10, winning them a coveted slot on Top of the Pops.

Six months later, Girls on Film reached number five in the UK charts. 

The New Romantic revolution, as much about fashion as music, was under way. 

The boys discovered that their Antony Price suits and make-up gave them something to talk about with girls in the clubs.

And there were soon girls everywhere, thanks to a marketing campaign that pushed the band heavily in teen magazines.

After one US concert, the band swept the stage and supposedly counted 2,000 teddy bears, 150 pairs of knickers, 300 bras and dozens of hotel room keys that had been thrown from the crowd.

Andy Taylor said life with Duran Duran was like entering Narnia in The Lion, The Witch and The Wardrobe

The music itself was cheerful, catchy and aspirational.

"We were just working-class escapists who wanted to live out our dreams," explained Taylor. "We didn't want to be like UB40 and The Specials singing about unemployment and urban decay."

Duran Duran embarked on exhausting foreign tours to drive sales, and their flamboyant videos, made at huge cost, played constantly on MTV in the US.

Filming came with an element of risk. 

A bandmate pushed Taylor off a yacht while shooting the video for Rio, and he nearly drowned off the Caribbean coast amid a swarm of poisonous jellyfish.

In Sri Lanka, the group was forced to flee when 4,000 furious monks objected to their using a holy site as a backdrop to Hungry Like the Wolf.

Then, having smoked dope and drunk a bottle of Jack Daniel's, Taylor climbed a tree with his guitar for a spectacular shot. 

He fell into a muddy lagoon that was used by local elephants as a latrine and spent four days in hospital recovering from a tropical infection.

He shared a flat in Moseley with Le Bon, took prodigious quantities of cocaine, and worked his way through the flocks of page three girls who frequented the Rum Runner.

Andy Taylor married Tracey Wilson on Hollywood's Sunset Strip in 1982

Then he met Tracey Wilson, a level-headed hair salon manager. 

Initially, she had doubts about becoming romantically involved with a self-confessed womanising, drug-taking guitarist.

She was eventually persuaded and, on his 21st birthday, Taylor proposed. The couple remained together for more than 40 years and had four children.

Famously, Duran Duran were Princess Diana's favourite band. This too, came with risks.

In June 1983, the IRA tried to assassinate the Prince and Princess of Wales while they watched the group perform live at Tottenham Court Road's Dominion Theatre.

The plot, which involved planting a bomb in the toilets, was stopped by British intelligence. 

Fortunately, the IRA man sent to plant the device turned out to be a double agent, who tipped off MI5 and fled to Paris.

The same year, Is There Something I Should Know? became Duran Duran's fastest-selling song to date. 

The Reflex, which their record company initially refused to release, also topped the charts on both sides of the Atlantic.

The American press called them the Fab Five, in comparison to the Beatles, and their vast earnings caused them to become tax exiles.

But Duran Duran were falling apart.

For Andy Taylor, foreign tours had become increasingly drug-fuelled with so-called "rock docs" injecting him with chemicals to get through sets.

The constant use of cocaine made him nervy and irrational. The BBC threatened to ban him from their Saturday Superstore programme when he swore at a caller.

Headlines about Taylor's drug use at the Rum Runner damaged the band's hitherto squeaky-clean image. 

In Birmingham, the club was suddenly served with a compulsory purchase order by the council and bulldozed.

Their Wild Boys video featured Simon Le Bon strapped to a huge waterwheel. But it was Andy Taylor who felt as if he was drowning.

Andy Taylor (sitting in a cardboard box) and Simon Le Bon behind the scenes at Live Aid in 1985

Musically, he was frustrated. 

As a guitarist, he wanted to make records with a harder rock edge, which led to The Power Station - a band he put together with Robert Palmer while killing time between Duran Duran tours.

But it was his lifestyle that was worrying him. After years of living on Big Macs and cocaine, he knew he was heading for a breakdown.

After Live Aid in 1985, the constant bickering between members of the band led to a prolonged separation. 

It was never the same again.

When they ran out of money, Duran Duran's original line-up reformed to work on new projects. 

They continued to sell plenty of tickets for live shows to die-hard fans, but new records had disappointing sales.

By the time the group made Red Carpet Massacre, their 12th album, Taylor had decided to quit. 

In 2006, Duran Duran released a statement saying there was "an unworkable gulf between us and we can no longer effectively function together".

The best Andy Taylor could say about Red Carpet Massacre was that it "sounded as if it had been recorded in a hurry".

Having weaned himself off drugs, he moved to Ibiza and set up a scheme to help unsigned artists release music while retaining their profits.

Andy Taylor performing with Duran Duran in 2004

In 2022, it was announced that he had advanced prostate cancer, the same disease that had killed his father. 

Le Bon spoke for the remaining members of the group. "It's absolutely devastating," he said.

Taylor avoided chemotherapy, believing it would affect his guitar playing by weakening his fingers. Instead, he embarked on an experimental "nuclear therapy", which he hoped would give him five more years of life.

Initially, at least, it seemed successful but, a year later, Le Bon said his former bandmate's disease was a  "very late, fourth-stage metastasised cancer".

In 2025, Taylor managed to release a third solo album, Man's a Wolf to A Man. Urged by Tracey to express himself for a final time, he even provided the vocals.

For Andy Taylor, that first train ride down to Birmingham had been like entering Narnia in the Lion, the Witch, and the Wardrobe.

Overnight, he had gone from serenading strippers in northern clubs to a rock-star lifestyle with the world's most popular band.

So, was the rollercoaster ride worth it? Taylor answered his own question in his autobiography, published in 2008.

"Maybe if there'd been fewer lows there wouldn't have been so many highs," he wrote. "If I had to do it all over again, I don't think I would have changed a thing.

"Though next time I'll give a miss to swallowing all that elephant's piss," he added.
