Two officers who lied about carrying out missing person searches linked to a crash in which three people died have been dismissed after being found guilty at a police misconduct hearing.

PC Richard Hardwick and PC Lauren Livingstone faced gross misconduct allegations over their actions before a car carrying five people was found crashed two days later in St Mellons, Cardiff, in 2023.

The hearing in Newport was told they were "dishonest" in official logs and to a senior officer by claiming searches of the homes of Eve Smith, who died, and Sophie Russon had been carried out.

Panel chairman Ian Arundale said a final written warning was not an option because the level of harm was too high.

Arundale also said there was a lack of mitigating factors. 

He said Hardwick and Livingstone had deliberately misled a missing persons investigation, which may have compromised the investigation, and had undermined public trust in the police in a high-profile case. 

Hardwick faced six allegations, including that he lied about searching the homes of crash victims Smith and Russon, and that he also lied to his supervising officer and the police watchdog.

Livingstone faced four allegations, including that she lied about searching the two houses, as well as making a dishonest entry on the log and lying to her supervising officer.

The police disciplinary hearing at Rodney Parade, which lasted four days, was told that both officers were part of the team assigned to look into reports that Eve Smith, Darcy Ross and Sophie Russon had gone missing after a night out on Friday 3 March 2023.

The three women were reported missing to Gwent Police on 4 March, but were not found until 30 hours after the initial contact by the families.

Five people were found in a car in a wooded area off the A48, 46 hours after the vehicle had crashed. 

Three of the cars occupants, Eve Smith, Rafel Jeanne, and Darcy Ross had died, and two, Sophie Russon and Shane Loughlin, were seriously injured.

Floral tributes were laid out after the crash for the three people that lost their lives 

Hardwick and Livingstone went to Russon's home and then on to Smith's house, and as part of Gwent police's missing person policy were supposed to search the address to look for clues for what might have happened to them.

Hardwick wrote in the official log afterwards "premise search conducted" and told the police sergeant overseeing the case that the search at Russon's house had been carried out. He also said searches had happened when he spoke to the police watchdog, the IOPC, who later investigated.

This was in contrast to what Russon's father, Nathan, told the hearing, that the officers were at the house for a very short period and did not ask to search the bedroom.

Livingstone said she could not remember whether searches had taken place, but wrote what she agreed was a "poorly written" report in the official log, which the panel head heard was "deliberately vague" but suggested a search had taken place.

The panel found Hardwick and Livingstone had failed to conduct a search at Smith and Russon's homes.

Hardwick was found to have provided a false entry into the police log and had provided a dishonest statement when interviewed by the IOPC. 

He had been found, in part, to have given a dishonest account of the search to his supervisor and found in part to have provided a dishonest account in a signed statement to the IOPC.

Livingstone was found to also have provided a false entry into the police log and to have had given a dishonest account of the search to her supervisor.

Panel chairman Ian Arundale said their behaviour had caused harm because of the impact on the family and had undermined public trust in the police in a case which resulted in significant levels of concern from the public.
