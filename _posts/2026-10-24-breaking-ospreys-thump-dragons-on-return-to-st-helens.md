Ospreys enjoyed a happy homecoming with a bonus-point victory over Dragons at St Helen's.

Mark Jones' side returned to the famous Swansea venue in style, scoring five tries in horrendous conditions in front of 5,592 supporters.

Ospreys led 19-7 at half-time after a double by Reuben Morgan-Williams and an effort by captain Sam Parry.

Dragons scored with the final play of the half through Rio Dyer, but Ospreys' driving line-out secured the win by earning a penalty try and a score for Ross Moriarty.

Anzelo Tuitavuki went over for the visitors' second, but they endured an evening to forget.

Dragons had Rhodri Williams, Tinus de Beer and Aneurin Owen sin-binned and Wales were also given a scare before the Nations Championship.

Dyer did not come out for the second half and lock Ben Carter, who established himself in Steve Tandy's starting line-up last season, went off with a head injury. 

More than 5,500 spectators watched the first regional game at St Helen's since 2005

After some pre-match pomp, Ospreys started strongly and got their reward when scrum-half Morgan-Williams burrowed over from close range in the sixth minute, Dan Edwards adding the extras.

The visitors did a good job of playing into the wind and had been the better side, but then conceded a scrappy second on the half hour.

Morgan-Williams was the beneficiary of Dragons' failure to fall on the ball after a grubber through by centre Lalakai Foketi.

Ospreys were over for a third time as half-time approached when fly-half Edwards was brave with a penalty touch-finder and the pack muscled over from a five-metre driving line-out for Parry's score.

A 19-0 lead would have represented a good effort for Ospreys with the wind, but Dragons had the final say of the half when, after pressure and penalties, recalled Wales wing Dyer crossed for his first try of the season.

Those seven points were cancelled out almost straight from the restart when Ospreys were awarded a penalty try for a 20-metre line-out drive being stopped illegally, with scrum-half Rhodri Williams sin-binned.

Dragons then suffered the loss of co-captain Carter and, straight after Williams' return, a yellow card for De Beer.

Edwards pinned them into the corner from the resulting penalty and the pack did the rest, back-row forward Moriarty following Parry in scoring against his former side.

Tuitavuki gave Dragons a smidgen of hope when he sniped at a ruck and powered over with quarter of an hour left, but that was soon followed by Owen's yellow card for a head-on-head tackle.

Ospreys returned to Swansea after playing in Bridgend in 2025-26

After a first block of the season that has seen Ospreys play in Durban, Johannesburg, Tbilisi and Newport, they returned to the famous sporting venue they left in 2005.

They have downsized after leaving the Swansea.com Stadium in 2025 and, while there is a long snagging list and plenty of jobs to be done, this felt like a home.

Like at Bridgend's Brewery Field, their ground for 2025-26, there was a lively atmosphere to make up for the rough edges.

It was Ospreys' first game at St Helen's since moving a game against Benetton during the Covid pandemic in 2020.
