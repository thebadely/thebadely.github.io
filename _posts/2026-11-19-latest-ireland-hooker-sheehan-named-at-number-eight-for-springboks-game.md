Let's dive into the details: Sheehan's only previous game at number eight was Leinster's win over Munster last month

Ireland captain Dan Sheehan has been named at number eight for Saturday's highly anticipated Nations Championship match against world champions South Africa in Dublin.

Having won all 42 of his previous caps at hooker, the 28-year-old returns after recovering from a concussion to partner flankers Ryan Baird and Josh van der Flier in the back row.

Ireland head coach Andy Farrell has faced an injury crisis at number eight with regular skipper Caelan Doris, Jack Conan, Sean Jansen and recent debutant James McNabney all ruled out. 

Nick Timoney, who was handed the jersey for last week's victory over Fiji, drops to the bench. 

With Doris and Conan unavailable, Sheehan anchored the scrum in Leinster's United Rugby Championship win over Munster last month, but Saturday will be his first Test run-out in the back row.

Sheehan's move means that Ronan Kelleher keeps his place at hooker after starting the Fiji game, again joining props Andrew Porter and Tadhg Furlong in the front row. 

After being rested last week, Joe McCarthy returns to the second row to partner Tadhg Beirne, with Van der Flier edging out Timoney for the number seven jersey. 

Sam Prendergast starts at fly-half after being cleared to face the Springboks on Wednesday, with Jamison Gibson-Park retained at scrum-half. 

Prendergast, 23, was cited for his hip-drop tackle on Fiji's Peceli Yato - which was not punished on the pitch - but an independent disciplinary panel ruled the challenge was not worthy of a red card.

Robbie Henshaw and Stuart McCloskey both return to midfield after Farrell opted to pair Jamie Osborne and Bundee Aki in a new-look centre partnership against Fiji. 

However, Osborne was replaced by McCloskey in the third minute after sustaining a pectoral injury that has ruled him out of the South Africa game, while Aki drops to the bench. 

With Hugo Keenan returning to full-back for his 50th cap after sitting out the Fiji game, Mack Hansen switches to the left wing with Robert Baloucoune retained on the right. Tommy O'Brien, who scored two tries from the left wing against Fiji, drops out of the squad.

Farrell has opted for a split of five forwards and three backs on the bench. Tom Stewart, Tom O'Toole, Sam Illo, James Ryan and Timoney provide pack cover with Craig Casey, Ciaran Frawley and Aki named as backline reinforcements. 

Nick Timoney (left) drops to the bench while Sam Prendergast (right) starts at fly-half after being cleared of foul play 

South Africa, who named their team on Monday, make five changes with record cap holder Eben Etzebeth recalled to the second row. 

Cameron Hanekom is named at number eight, replacing Jasper Wiese, who was handed a four-week suspension after being sent off for a dangerous clear-out in last week's defeat by France.

Ox Nche, Malcolm Marx and Wilco Louw are named as the starting front row.

Springboks head coach Rassie Erasmus also named Nche, Marx and Louw to start the France game but swapped them out for Jan-Hendrik Wessels, Gerhard Steenekamp and Thomas du Toit shortly before kick-off at the Stade de France.

Erasmus is not permitted to attend the Aviva Stadium encounter after being handed a six-match ban by World Rugby for questioning the integrity of the match officials in his team's loss to Les Bleus.  

The 54-year-old has since announced he will be hosting an online watchalong of the match while he watches from his team's Dublin hotel. 

South Africa sit second in the Nations Championship southern hemisphere standings, a point adrift of New Zealand.

A South Africa win over Ireland coupled with England beating the All Blacks (Saturday, 14:10 GMT) would be enough for Erasmus' side to seal a place in the 29 November final against the top-ranked northern hemisphere side at Twickenham.

Ireland must beat the Springboks and hope France and England falter against Argentina and New Zealand to finish top. 

The Springboks won 24-13 at Aviva Stadium last year, their first victory over Ireland in Dublin since 2012.
