India captain Shubman Gill says that players should be allowed to use saliva to shine the ball again, six years after it was outlawed.

The ban was first introduced by the International Cricket Council (ICC) as a result of the Covid-19 pandemic, in May 2020, before being made permanent in 2022.

Since then, players have been allowed to use sweat to shine the ball because there was less risk of transmitting the Covid disease.

Bowlers use saliva or sweat to polish one side of the ball while leaving the other side to deteriorate naturally, a process which helps bowlers swing the ball through the air. 

Gill, 27, questioned why the ICC is yet to reverse the ban during his news conference after India's high-scoring draw against New Zealand in the first Test at Wellington.

"I don't know why ball shining [using saliva] is not allowed," said Gill, who scored 98 in India's only innings.

"It's been years since the Covid-19 pandemic passed, I don't know why it's not allowed now." 

The older or more worn a cricket ball gets, the less it will swing and therefore becomes easier for batters to face, which is why bowlers aim to preserve the shine on one side for as long as possible. 

New Zealand had slipped to 70-5 in their first innings before centuries from Henry Nicholls and Glenn Phillips led an incredible recovery to 386 all out.

India then piled on a mammoth 720-9 but were unable to force a victory on the final day as the Black Caps blocked out for a draw, with Gill saying the pitch lacked assistance for his bowlers.

"It's frustrating because we didn't get the result we wanted. Draws are not common, we are not used to draws," Gill added.

"We thought there would be a lot more in the wicket.

"Days three, four and five just got flatter and flatter. There was no impact of the overs being bowled on the wicket, or even the days being played. It just got slower."

The draw hurt both sides in their quest to finish in one of the top two spots in the World Test Championship table to secure a place in next June's final at the Oval in London.

South Africa and Australia are first and second with New Zealand third and India fourth.

The second Test starts on Thursday in Christchurch.
