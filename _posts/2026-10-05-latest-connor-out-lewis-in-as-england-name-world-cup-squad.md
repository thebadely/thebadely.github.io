Let's dive into the details: Jake Connor missed out on an England squad place for last year's Ashes, for which Mikey Lewis was selected

Jake Connor missed out on a Rugby League World Cup spot by a "small margin" as England seek the players who can best stick to their gameplan, head coach Brian McDermott says.

Leeds Rhinos half-back Connor, the 2025 Super League Man of Steel, was left out of the 23-man squad for the tournament - to be held largely in Australia - with Hull KR's Mikey Lewis making the cut instead.

For Connor, it is another England disappointment to follow his controversial omission from the squad for last autumn's home series against Australia.

"Jake Connor missed out on selection by a small margin and it came down to who I think can best stick to the game plan and attack plan," McDermott said.

The 31-year-old had been expected to make the squad after being talked up by McDermott and enjoying a strong domestic season.

But Hull KR's Lewis, who endured an inconsistent year, has done enough to convince McDermott that he should join captain George Williams, the Warrington stand-off, and Wigan's Harry Smith as half-back options.

England begin their World Cup campaign against Tonga in Perth on 17 October, before facing France in the same city on 24 October and then Papua New Guinea in Wollongong on 30 October.

There has been much debate about which half-backs would make the England squad after McDermott said last month that he would probably take three to the World Cup, and that captain Williams - who is heading to Australia to join NRL club Dolphins after the tournament - would be one of them.

McDermott said: "Jake has had a really good year. He had an outstanding year last year and got Man of Steel. 

"What I've got is a squad full of players who I believe will best play to the game style, the attack plan or game model we identified many weeks ago, that I think we need to compete with the leading nations. 

"We can't do the usual things, and I made this a point when I took the job. We've got to play with a point of difference. 

"I've got a squad here who I believe are best suited to the attack plan and game model I think England need to play."

Explaining his decision to select Lewis, McDermott added: "I've picked a group of player that have recent history of playing in finals, winning finals, delivering when it matters. 

"Mikey Lewis won a World Club Challenge at the start of this year and guided his team to a Challenge Cup final. 

"I get he wasn't in the best of shape towards the back of the year and incurred a couple of bans but again, I'll just point people to the attack plan and game model I think England need to have to compete with the leading nations."

Hugo Savala will go into the World Cup having been part of Sydney Roosters' NRL Grand Final-winning set-up

When he replaced Shaun Wane as head coach, McDermott indicated that he was keen to use heritage players - those who qualify for England through family background - to ensure the strongest possible squad.

He has called up two uncapped Australia-born players - versatile back Hugo Savala, part of Sydney Roosters' NRL Grand Final-winning squad, and Brisbane Broncos' front-rower Ben Talty. 

Bulldogs prop Max King, born in Huddersfield while his Australian father David was a player there, also earns a first call-up. Victor Radley and AJ Brimson complete the Australia-born contingent in the squad.

Grand Final winners Wakefield have no players in the squad, with Tom Johnstone, Mike McMeeken, Caleb Hamlin-Uele and Jake Trueman all needing time to recover from injuries.

"It's a real shame that the Wakefield boys aren't available," McDermott said. "There's nothing we or they can do. They're all booked in for operations at the end of the year."

And also injured were Sydney Roosters centre Billy Smith - a scorer in Sunday's NRL Grand Final - Wigan centre Jake Wardle, Leeds winger Ash Handley and prop Mikolaj Oledzki, who is leaving Headingley to join NRL side Perth Bears.

Grand Finalists Warrington have three players in the squad - Matty Ashton and Ben Currie joining captain Williams - while Wigan have seven, including Man of Steel winner Brad O'Neill and uncapped centre Zach Eckersley.

Mikey Lewis over Jake Connor feels like a call made based on pace rather than any other metric, as the Hull KR half is much more of a runner than his opposite number.

He's also perhaps more of an off-the-bench spark than the Leeds playmaker, given George Williams and Harry Smith's blend of run threat and game management will probably get the nod at six and seven.

Herbie Farnworth has been one of the best players in the world for some time and has taken his game to new levels in 2026, and getting the centre involved must be among Brian McDermott's biggest tactical challenges.

Dom Young is another thoroughbred who England will look to out wide, having helped Newcastle to the NRL Grand Final.

Victor Radley's influence and savage tackling makes him a shoo-in for the pack, something that was missed in last year's Ashes series against Australia, and there are other big hitters in the ranks with Morgan Knowles and Morgan Smithies also sharpened by their NRL exploits, and Brad O'Neill back in the international picture after a Man of Steel year.

Staying with the pack, the prospect of a Kai Pearce-Paul and Junior Nsemba second-row pairing whets the appetite.

Elsewhere, the new imports from the NRL have pedigree; Max King has played State of Origin, Hugo Savala can jump into a number of backs spots and Ben Talty is an athletic front-rower who helped Brisbane to success in 2025.

Zach Eckersley has impressed for Wigan this year, and his strong build and athleticism - coupled with an ability to put a winger away - make him a solid inclusion, while Matty Ashton's return after his missed the Ashes with an anterior cruciate knee ligament injury gives England genuine pace out wide.

Everyone will have their opinion on the squad, which features no selections from Super League champions Wakefield - albeit with some contenders unavailable - but there is a balance to the selection.

McDermott has said England might need something different to truly test the best, and it is that conundrum which will again be at the forefront of his thoughts.

Reece Walsh impressed during last autumn's Ashes but has had a testing club season with Brisbane Broncos

Reece Walsh has secured a place in Australia's men's World Cup squad, also named on Monday - although head coach Kevin Walters has refused to confirm whether he will be first-choice full-back.

Walsh has struggled to hit the heights of last season, when he inspired Brisbane Broncos to their first NRL Grand Final triumph in 19 years before helping Australia to a 3-0 Test series win over England.

He missed the end of this season to have ankle surgery, but with Penrith's Dylan Edwards ruled out of the World Cup with a knee injury, Walsh will vie for the full-back spot with James Tedesco - man of the match as Sydney Roosters beat Newcastle Knights 19-18 in Sunday's NRL Grand Final.

Loose forward Isaah Yeo will delay surgery on a torn pectoral muscle to captain Australia.

Winger Mark Nawaqanitawase - who scored two tries in Sydney's win on Sunday - is not in the squad because of his impending code switch to union with Japanese side Saitama Wild Knights. Second-row forward Angus Crichton is absent for the same reason, as he is joining NSW Waratahs.

Star half-back Nathan Cleary is out of the tournament with a broken ankle.

Ali Brigginshaw has missed out on the chance to go for a fourth World Cup winners' medal after being omitted from Australia's women's squad.

Head coach Jess Skinner said the 36-year-old Brisbane half-back - Australia's captain when they won the tournament in Manchester four years ago - was "upset" to be left out.

"That was one of the hardest phone calls I've had to make as a coach to a player and to talk about her and where she was," Skinner said.
