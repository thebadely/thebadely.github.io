Let's dive into the details: Fernando Alonso will take on the Dakar Rally for a second time

Two-time Formula 1 world champion Fernando Alonso will lead Aston Martin's attempt to win the Dakar Rally in January 2027.

The 45-year-old Spaniard, who drives for the British team in Formula 1, previously contested the prestigious rally raid event in 2020 for Toyota.

Aston Martin have struggled in the first year of F1's new regulations in the Adrian Newey-designed AMR26 car.

Alonso sits 17th in the drivers' championship, team-mate Lance Stroll 22nd, and the team 10th in the constructors', only ahead of last-placed Cadillac.

Alonso, who recently signed a new Aston Martin deal and is widely regarded as one of the biggest talents in motorsport history, is joined in the Dreadnought by co-driver Marc Coma.

"After deciding to continue racing in Formula 1 with Aston Martin, I thought carefully about Dakar," said Alonso. "In the end, the decision became simple. I wanted to embrace the challenge completely.

"I have enormous respect for this race and the specialists who have mastered it. Having Marc alongside me again is very important, and with Aston Martin, Prodrive, Sebastien and Lucas we have an exceptional team."

It will be Aston's first involvement in the famously gruelling and dangerous race and they will go up against more established Dakar teams such as Toyota and KTM.

Aston's Dakar machines will be prepared by Prodrive - who had considerable success in rallying with Subaru in the 1990s and 2000s.

Despite challenges on the tarmac in F1, Aston have enjoyed much-improved performances in the World Endurance Championship this year, including at the season finale at Monza last month through the 007 Valkyrie car driven by Tom Gamble and Harry Tincknell.

Alonso could even drive the Valkyrie at the famous Le Mans 24 Hours in 2027 - F1 commitments permitting - where he won with Toyota in 2018 and 2019.

The 2027 Dakar Rally will begin in Portugal on 9 January and after 12 stages end in Morocco on 23 January.

Last month it was announced Dakar would be switched from Saudi Arabia following the unrest in the Middle East because of the Iran war.

The race, which also includes other classifications of cars, as well as lorries and motorbikes, was traditionally held between Paris and Dakar from the late 1970s, but the route has been adjusted over the years for promotional and safety reasons.

Alonso's previous Dakar brought a 13th-place finish. A couple of crashes caused by inexperience of the event delayed him, but a second-place finish on one stage showed his pace and potential. 

"Formula 1 remains my priority," he said. "But I am a racing driver, and challenges like this are why I love competing. I'm looking forward to seeing what we can achieve."
