Here’s what you need to know: Ben Carter and Gareth Thomas are part of Wales' 39-man squad for the Nations Championship

They say rugby is now a game that relies on 23 players.

There was ample evidence of that during Wales' remarkable 35-28 win against New Zealand last Saturday. 

The headline acts were the 346 tackles made by the team, which was a world record for an international match.

The 38 tackles managed by lock Dafydd Jenkins matched the top individual tally, while Jac Morgan (31), James Botham (30), Eddie James (25), captain Dewi Lake (24) and Max Llewellyn (23) were not far behind. 

It was the impact of the bench that also proved crucial in the defensive stand of the final quarter.

Replacement back rower Taine Plumtree popped up with two crucial turnovers, while Gareth Thomas and Ben Carter made a combined 32 tackles in 42 minutes.

Ospreys prop Thomas managed 19 in 26 minutes, while lock Carter's return was 13 in 16 minutes to add to the remarkable statistics and contributions.

Thomas has been Wales' third-choice loose-head in recent times behind Nicky Smith and Rhys Carre.

He found himself promoted to the bench last Saturday, with Smith starting and Carre left out of the squad.

Thomas responded by providing one of the most impressive replacement defensive displays in living memory, especially for a prop.

"I have not made as many tackles in such a short space of time," said Thomas.

"But I kept looking up and they were running at me! It was a wild last 20 minutes, but every single person on the pitch was doing their job. 

"Everybody was doing what they could, emptying every little bit of energy they had to stop them from scoring."

Alongside his tackles, he also held All Blacks number eight Wallace Sititi over the line, one of three vital interventions of its kind, with Botham, Morgan and fly-half Dan Edwards also involved in the efforts that denied New Zealand.

"It was a sketchy moment because there was a lot of space there," said Thomas.

"I knew if I went to hit him hard it could have been a try, easily so I kind of soaked him up over the line. 

"It's something we've been working with, a last resort that Peter Murchie and Dan Lydiate [Wales defence coaches] have brought in. 

"It was nice that transfered to the game. Straight after you're thinking what's my next job and don't think about it. 

"But after the game I've had that video sent to me 50 to 100 times on social media. I've watched it a few times and it's a big moment."

Gareth Thomas has played 45 internationals for Wales

Thomas, 33, is one of the elder statesmen in the group.

"It's a very special feeling," said Thomas.

"I was sat in bed the other night, just watching the videos of reactions in pubs and places around Wales. I love it how it made everyone feel, what it meant to people.

"I had a message saying how special it made their 92-year-old friend and he's happy to go to the grave now after that. So special to make people feel like that.

"We can't stop watching the reactions because it makes you feel like a fan. 

"I would love to have been a fan watching it, it shows what it means to see ex-players with tears in their eyes."

Dragons co-captain Carter, 25, was dropped to being a replacement following the 28-21 win against Japan, with Adam Beard coming into start alongside Jenkins.

Beard made his own telling contribution in an improved Wales line-out and racked up 21 tackles in 64 minutes before Carter came onto help finish the job.

"I probably would have liked to have spent a bit more time with the ball and maybe a bit further up the field," said Carter.

"I do enjoy the defensive aspect of the game, so it was brilliant. 

"They were coming at us very direct and it always felt good in those defensive sets and we were pleased.

"I was tired and I can't imagine how the boys were feeling that they had already played 60 minutes.

"There were some big shifts and tackle numbers from a lot of the boys. 

"The biggest thing was you could feel the the fight for each other on the pitch, and the supporters could see it as well."

Carter, who has played 23 internationals, hopes to be involved this weekend against Australia, while Beard is suffering from a sore shoulder.

"There's a lot of belief in this group anyway and they believe that's the sort of performance we can put in," said Carter.

"Just to live it and to get that result will only help our confidence. 

"There is probably a bit of expectation on us now, and the group looks forward to carrying that into these next games."
