Since the 2018 World Cup, Jordan Pickford has been a fixture in the England team.

He has kept 44 clean sheets in his 91 caps and has made 33 major tournament appearances, including two European Championship finals.

But the Everton goalkeeper will be 34 by the time Euro 2028 begins - so it is reasonable to consider who could eventually take over from him.

The other goalkeepers in England's World Cup squad were Crystal Palace's Dean Henderson - who was probably regarded as Pickford's understudy as he played in the third-place play-off - and James Trafford of Manchester City.

Lauded as "world class" by his then manager Scott Parker, Trafford's 29 clean sheets and 84.47% save ratio were integral to the Clarets' promotion to the Premier League in 2024-25.

Trafford graduated through Manchester City's academy

Trafford, though, is yet to fully establish his reputation at the highest level of club football.

Jordan Pickford has played in five major tournaments for England

Pickford is the man in possession after helping England reach the World Cup semi-finals for only the fourth time this summer.

Thomas Tuchel spoke of his admiration for both Trafford and Henderson after he was appointed England manager in 2024. 

Trafford made his England debut in the 1-1 draw with Uruguay in March but Pickford played every minute of the World Cup until the game against France, when Henderson started.

In doing so he surpassed Peter Shilton's England record of 17 World Cup appearances, ending the tournament on 19.

After England defeated Mexico in the World Cup last 16, former England goalkeeper Paul Robinson said: "I think he's the first name on the team sheet. There is nobody else."

Pickford has made 14 appearances at European Championships, while he has saved four of the 14 penalties he has faced in major tournament shoot-outs, guiding England to wins against Colombia in the 2018 World Cup and against Switzerland in the quarter-finals of Euro 2024. 

The former Sunderland player has been consistent over nine campaigns with Everton, winning the club's player-of-the-season award three times in a row as they battled relegation between 2021 and 2024. 

The 32-year-old has played 362 Premier League games in total, keeping 98 clean sheets and saving almost 70% of the shots he has faced - including 86% from outside the box. 

Over the past two seasons, only Arsenal's David Raya has kept more Premier League clean sheets.

Pickford also won the competition's save-of-the-season award for a second time last season following a remarkable reactionary stop, external from Sandro Tonali's volley to secure Everton's 3-2 win at St James' Park in February.

Comparing Pickford and Trafford directly is not straightforward because of the latter's relative lack of elite-level football. However Leeds' prospective new signing has also starred on the international stage - albeit for England's age-group sides.

He played every minute of the 2023 Under-21 European Championship as England won the title for the first time in 39 years without conceding a single goal.

In Premier League terms, even when discounting the gulf in experience, Pickford boasts an undeniable edge over a comparable period.

Since Trafford made his top-flight debut in 2023-24, Pickford has prevented far more goals - despite facing a higher quality of shot - and has a better save percentage.

Trafford has so far failed to prevent any expected goals in Premier League football as defined by Opta.

So while Trafford will hope his possible high-profile move will help him add to his two England caps, he has work to do succeed Pickford as the Three Lions' first-choice goalkeeper.
