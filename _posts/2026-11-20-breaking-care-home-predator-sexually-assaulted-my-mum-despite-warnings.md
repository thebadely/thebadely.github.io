There is no justice for my mum after care home assault

A man has told BBC News how his elderly mother, who was suffering from dementia, was sexually assaulted by a fellow resident in a care home in Inverclyde.

The man - who we are calling John to protect his mother's identity - said the "horrific" sex attack took place despite concerns previously being raised by staff about the behaviour of the attacker.

John told the BBC he was speaking out after HC-One, which operates the Merino Court Care Home in Greenock, admitted liability and paid damages after a civil action.

He said he was angry that a criminal investigation into the 2023 attack took so long that the man accused of the assault, who was in his 80s, was deemed unfit to stand trial. 

Allistair McAuley died in June, just weeks before a court was due to examine the evidence against him.

John said he was angry that the attack was allowed to happen

Documents seen by the BBC show that safeguarding concerns over McAuley's behaviour were raised by three staff members at the care home in the months before the attack.

The documents show that McAuley was reported for making sexualised remarks and inappropriate conduct. 

He had slapped John's mother, who we are calling Mary, on the bottom as well as similar behaviour with two female staff members. 

McAulay was also reported for giving Mary food that she was not allowed to eat and touching her thighs.

One staff member told management that McAuley was "grooming" John's mum.

Then, on a Saturday night in late 2023, John got a call from the care home to tell him his mother, who was in her 80s, had been seriously sexually assaulted.

"When I got to the home it was full of police cars," he said.

"They called the rape team in and that's when the penny was starting to drop and I was told exactly what happened, and I was shocked."

John said his mum, who had limited speech due to her condition, was very subdued and only able to say "bad man, bad man".

John said that the phrase still haunts him years later.

"I was so angry," he said. 

"I was angered that I put my mum in that home to be safe.

"I just cannot make sense of it. How somebody could even want to do things like that, especially to a vulnerable person."

The care home apologised for the distress caused to the victim

John described the situation as "totally horrific" but said it could have been worse if a carer had not walked into the bedroom and intervened. 

"I wouldn't wish that on my worst enemy because that is horrible, knowing that your mother is vulnerable, can't defend herself or do anything," he said. 

"And to do what that monster has done to her."

McAuley remained at the home under staff supervision for two days following the attack, undergoing assessments on his mental capacity. 

During this time, Mary was confined to her room until McAuley could be removed by police.

After being found to have full capacity, he was charged with four counts of sexual assault. 

Two were in relation to Mary and he was also alleged to have sexually assaulted two carers.

The case took several years to call in court and was due to be heard at Greenock Sheriff Court earlier this year but by that point 86-year-old McAuley was deemed unfit to stand trial. He has since died.

"There was no justice," said John.

"Nobody's got justice. That man did what he did. In those three years nothing happened to him. Where's the justice? There's none."

Michael McKitrick represented John in his case against the care home

A spokesman for the Crown Office and Procurator Fiscal Service (COPFS) said the indictment was served within the relevant statutory timescale but he "understood" John's concern. 

He added that assessments on McAuley's fitness to stand trial affected the progress of the case.

Seeking accountability for his mother's ordeal, John took civil action against HC-One.

In March 2025 the care home provider admitted primary liability for its failure to safeguard Mary and paid damages to the family.

Michael McKitrick, of PBW Law, who represented John in the case, said his client had expected his mother to be taken care of and not subjected to the risk of sexual assault.

"The second assault was certainly avoidable," he said.

John says he has never received a formal apology from the home and, three years on, still "just wants answers".

Merino Court Care Home has been rated good or very good in its most recent inspections by the Care Inspectorate.

The BBC asked an HC-One representative to take part in an interview, but the company declined.

HC-One was asked if it had made any changes to its protocols in the wake of the attack on Mary and why it had not apologised to the family.

A spokesperson for the care home said: "Following this incident [serious sexual assault] we took immediate and appropriate steps to manage the situation. We alerted all relevant authorities including social services, the Care Inspectorate and the Police.

"We also immediately informed the family of the victim and stayed in contact with them throughout the incident and the subsequent investigation. 

"We apologised at the time for the distress caused to the victim and do so again now."

The Care Inspectorate said it was satisfied that the care provider notified it appropriately. 

Richard Robinson said sexual abuse in care homes was a "hidden issue"

The Inverclyde Health and Social Care Partnership (Inverclyde HSCP), which oversees care for the local council and NHS board, told John three years ago that an inquiry would take place but he says has heard nothing since.

The BBC asked Inverclyde HSCP what the outcome of its inquiry was.

A spokesperson said: "Any concerns regarding an individual's welfare are taken seriously and are dealt with through the appropriate safeguarding and professional processes.

"We recognise the importance of families being heard and remain willing to meet with, or speak directly to, family members to understand and discuss any concerns they wish to raise."

Charity Hourglass runs a 24-hour helpline for those suffering or dealing with the abuse of elderly people.

It said calls to its phone line over concerns about abuse in care homes had risen by 125% in Scotland in the past year.

Chief Executive Richard Robinson said sexual abuse in care homes was a "hidden issue". 

"It's more common than anyone would expect, sadly, and I believe we are just seeing the tip of the iceberg in terms of the amount of cases that we're dealing with in Scotland," he said.

"There were a number of missed opportunities here. It's not just the victim survivor here. It's not just the son. It's also the staff themselves that have been let down."

John says his mother "was never the same person" after the attack. 

Her dementia has advanced rapidly in the years since and John says: "I don't think it helped". 

When he visits her now, his focus is on trying to make her smile.

The feeling of guilt has taken a toll on him too, John said.

"I wouldn't wish this on anyone. I don't think there's a time gone by I don't think about it.

"I had to go and seek counselling and things like that because it was affecting me in many ways. It's always on your mind. I'll live with this for the rest of my life."

He hopes speaking out may help others.

"It was never about the money," he said.

"I wanted justice. This happened to my family, and I wanted to stop this happening to anyone else's family. 

"It's the only way we can bring it out into the light to maybe put safeguards in to stop this happening again."
