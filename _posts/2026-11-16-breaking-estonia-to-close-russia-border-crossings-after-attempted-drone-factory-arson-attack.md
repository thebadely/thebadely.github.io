Estonia has said it will close border crossings into Russia from 1 December after accusing Moscow of being behind an attempted arson attack at a drone factory.

Prime Minister Kristen Michal said the break-in on Saturday at a facility north of the capital Tallinn was part of what he called Russia's "broader hybrid war against Europe". 

The alleged attackers were arrested, Michal said, with local media reporting the suspects are Latvian.

They are said to have broken into the factory and attempted to set fire to it using flammable liquids.

On Monday, Moscow said the accusation was "baseless" and accused the Estonian government of "building a wall" between the neighbouring countries.

The facility targeted is operated by Threod Systems and produces attack and reconnaissance drones, with Ukraine and Nato member states listed as customers on its website. 

Foreign minister Margus Tsahkna said on Sunday that "closing the border crossings will reduce Russia's ability to conduct hostile activities in Estonia".

Michal said "extensive damage was averted thanks to heightened security measures" at the factory, while the head of Estonia's internal security service labelled the incident attempted Russian sabotage.

The decision to close border crossings is expected to be finalised next week, according to local media. 

Estonia shares a border of around 220 miles (338 km) with Russia that spans both sea and land. Three road crossings are in operation but are already subject to restrictions. 

One of them is located at Narva, Estonia's third biggest city. It sits on the western bank of the River Narva that flows into the Baltic Sea; on the eastern bank is Russia. Closures will complicate life for families which live either side of the border.

Kremlin spokesman Dimitry Peskov said: "Estonia is building a wall. Many countries, as history demonstrates, have built walls that have brought nothing good to those who erect such walls."

Russia has been repeatedly accused by European leaders of targeting national infrastructure and resources destined for Ukraine since Russia launched its full-scale invasion in 2022.

Baltic region leaders have repeatedly accused Russia of hybrid attacks and raised fears about the Kremlin's territorial ambitions. 

Other major backers of Ukraine in Europe, including France, Germany, the UK and Poland, have also said Russia has orchestrated attacks on their soil in recent months.

Last year, six men were jailed in the UK over an arson attack linked to Russia at a London warehouse where aid bound for Ukraine was being stored.
