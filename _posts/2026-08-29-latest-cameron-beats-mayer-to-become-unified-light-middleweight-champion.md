An engrossing encounter was narrowly won by the British fighter by unanimous decision - 97-93 on two scorecards and 96-94 on the other.

This was one of Cameron's best displays. These fighters were evenly matched in every metric â from punch rate to height and weight, even hairstyle.

But Cameron won out thanks to her unrelenting pressure, which never allowed the usually adaptable Mayer to figure out a winning gameplan.

Mayer was the more imposing figure early on, but Cameron played counterpuncher until she could get onto the front foot.

Cameron forced Mayer against the ropes in round four, and again at the start of the fifth. Increasingly confident to take the initiative, she was roared on by a partisan but disappointingly small home crowd.

Mayer was unable to get in behind her jab for long periods and was bleeding and breathing heavily by round six.

The home favourite was cut in the final rounds but pushed through and was lifted shoulder high by her team before her biggest win since beating Taylor in Dublin three years ago was announced.

For three-weight world champion Mayer, this is the third defeat of her 25-fight career and costs her the WBA and WBC belts, which Cameron now puts with her WBO title.

When this event was announced, it was the prime event of the August bank holiday weekend in British boxing.

However, the elevation of Moses Itauma v Filip Hrgovic to a world title fight left this big-ticket event feeling rather undeservedly like a support act.

That was reflected in the crowd, with scores of empty seats around the arena as the main undercard began. At a generous estimate, BP Pulse Arena was half-full when the fighters made their ring walks for the main event.

Mayer and Cameron did their best to bring the big fight feeling in the build-up. Previously cordial relations boiled over on Friday as they butted heads at Friday's weigh-in at BP Pulse Arena.

Cameron shoved her head into the American, who pushed back, before they exchanged insults side by side.

However, her moxie was eventually worn down by Cameron, with a big right to the chin in round seven the most notable of blows.
