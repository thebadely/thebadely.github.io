Experts revealed that Katie Taylor wrote a letter to Deirdre Gogarty in 1996

Before Deirdre Gogarty became Ireland's first female world boxing champion in 1997, she had already inspired a young Irish girl. 

A 10-year-old Katie Taylor wrote to her hero in 1996 saying she did not "see boxing for girls taking off here", adding "maybe one day they'll even let us box in the Olympics". As Taylor is set to retire on Saturday as one of Ireland's greatest sportspeople, Gogarty writes back to the 40-year-old...

Dear Katie,

As I prepare to come home to Ireland to watch you fight at Croke Park, I keep thinking about just how far we have come.

It has been 39 years since I first walked through the doors of a boxing gym in Drogheda and was finally allowed to train.

Back then, I was told women would never be accepted in boxing. That even if we were allowed to compete, nobody would want to watch.

There was a time when I would have given anything simply to be allowed into a ring. We fought in places with few spectators, little recognition, and almost no opportunities for women.

And now, tens of thousands of people will fill one of Ireland's most iconic sporting venues to watch two women box... wow!!!

What was once considered unacceptable has become something an entire nation celebrates.

Katie, what you have done for women's boxing goes far beyond championships, medals, and records.

You didn't simply succeed in the sport - you helped transform how the world sees it, so that another generation of girls never has to question whether they belong.

When I sit among that enormous crowd at Croke Park, I'll be thinking about all the women who fought when nobody was watching.

I'll be thinking about the girls who were told they couldn't and shouldn't.

And most of all, I'll be thinking about the young girls watching you who will never have to ask permission to dream.

With enormous pride and admiration,

Deirdre Gogarty

Deirdre Gogarty won 16 of her 23 fights as a professional, losing five and drawing two

Deirdre Gogarty (left) beat Bonnie Canino in January 1997 to win the WIBF featherweight world title

Hi Deirde,

Thank you for writing to me and sending me you profile. Sorry I couldn't get back to you sooner as I was doing school exams. I train two nights a week up in my da's club. My da is having his own show in January and he is looking for a fight for me. 

I am fed up not being to get fights especially when I see the lads I spar winning championships. There is the European championships for women being held in Sweden in May and my da is trying to enter me into them. 

There is an open championship being held in England in April and hopefully I'll be entering that as well. I will let you know how I get on. Sorry to hear your fight in January is looking doubtful (Paddy was telling us). 

I hope you can get it on. Hopefully if you get a fight in Ireland, we will all come and see you. My brother Peter recently won the Leinster nine counties and is the reigning champion of the Wicklow/Wexford championship and is fighting in the finals of them again in January on my da's show. 

It will be a tough one as his opponent is the All-Ireland champion. I can't see boxing for girls taking off over here in the near future. I can see why you had to go away to get fights. 

Hopefully things will change over the next few years but maybe one day they'll even let us box in the Olympics. I hope everything goes well with your boxing career. You have achieved so much already, you have done us all proud. I wish you continued success.

Bye for now,

Katie.
