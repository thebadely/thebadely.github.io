Jurors have been sent out to begin considering their verdicts in the trial of a knifeman accused of attempting to murder 10 people on a London-bound train.

Anthony Williams, 33, stabbed 10 passengers between Peterborough and Huntingdon, Cambridgeshire, on 1 November 2025.

He has also pleaded not guilty to the attempted murders of three people in Peterborough and a boy in London prior to the stabbings.

After summing up the evidence in the case at Cambridge Crown Court, the judge, Mr Justice Calver KC, sent the jury out to start its deliberations. It will resume on Tuesday.

"You are under no time pressure whatsoever," he told jurors.

Williams has admitted wounding with intent to cause really serious bodily harm in respect of 12 victims and attempting to do so to two others.

Ten of the victims were stabbed on an LNER service from Doncaster to London King's Cross.

Williams is also accused of trying to murder three people in Peterborough the day before, as well as attempting to murder a boy in the early hours of 1 November on a train in east London.

The judge said jurors needed to ask themselves if they were sure the defendant "intended to kill" each victim.

He said psychiatrists instructed by the prosecution and defence agreed Williams "was suffering from an acute psychosis as a result of his schizophrenia" at the time.

The judge referred to statements allegedly made by Williams to a psychiatrist, including that he believed "people sent by the devil" wanted to attack him.

Calver said the psychiatrist also recalled the defendant saying that "during the events it was all real, he was attacking real people with real knives" and "I just needed to protect myself, it was me or them".

The judge said a "person who is mentally ill, acting psychotically under a delusion, can still form an intent to kill".

Anthony Williams boarded the train at Peterborough railway station

Williams had made comments to people on the train including "do you want to die?" prosecutor Christopher Paxton KC said.

He said the defendant's alleged intention to kill "lay bare not just in his words... but in the slashes and stabs he inflicted not on the devil but on real people".

He noted Williams did not attack people on the platform before boarding the train.

"If there's a conspiracy by the devil, if it was Mr Williams saying, 'It's me or them', it's easier to kill people if they are trapped," Paxton said.

Gillian Jones KC said in her defence closing speech that Williams had been "in the clutches of an acute psychotic episode".

She gave examples of how Williams would start attacking a victim on the LNER train and then stop and walk away, and she said this was "consistent with a delusional belief he was under attack".

She said Williams "didn't intend to kill, he didn't intend to bring about death".

The trial continues.
