Reigning world marathon champion Peres Jepchirchir has been banned for three years for a doping violation.

The 33-year-old, who also won Olympic gold in 2021, was provisionally suspended by the Athletics Integrity Unit (AIU) in October after an out-of-competition sample tested positive for erythropoietin (EPO).

The sample was collected in Kenya prior to Jepchirchir winning the Sydney Marathon on 30 August 2026 and returned an adverse finding on 17 September.

She is one of three high-profile Kenyan athletes to have been sanctioned. Veronica Loleo has also been banned for three years after admitting using EPO, while Ronald Korir has received a six-year ban after failing to explain abnormalities found in his biological passport.

EPO is a natural substance produced within the kidneys that stimulates the creation of new red blood cells.

If injected, it increases the amount of red blood cells, which carry oxygen to the muscles, in the blood stream but is only detectable in urine or blood for a short window of time.

Jepchirchir's other major wins came at the New York Marathon in 2021, the Boston Marathon in 2022 and the London Marathon in 2024.

While she denied using EPO, she signed an admission of violations form and accepted disqualification of all her competitive results since 17 July 2026.

"The Kenyan authorities have made a lot of progress in the fight against doping in the last five years, but these cases highlight that there is still a long way to go," said Brett Clothier, chief executive of the AIU.

"The Kenyan government is aware of the current situation and has committed to further strengthen the Anti-Doping Agency of Kenya and take action to address the criminal networks that facilitate doping within the country.

"We will discuss the details behind these commitments with the Kenyan government in the coming months."

In September, Kenya was awarded the right to host the 2029 World Athletics Championships, with London and Rome missing out.

The decision increased scrutiny of the country's efforts to tackle a long-running doping problem, with more than 140 Kenyan athletes, predominantly distance runners, having been suspended by the AIU since 2017.

Last month Witold Banka, president of the World Anti-Doping Agency (Wada), told BBC Sport that Kenya must demonstrate it can be a "clean" home of athletics if it is to retain its credibility leading up to the 2029 event.

Jepchirchir's ban will deepen scepticism towards Kenyan athletes, including those competing cleanly, and further place the spotlight on athletics administrators and the government. 

She is the second high-profile Kenyan athlete to be banned for doping in the past two years, following 2019 world champion Ruth Chepngetich, who was sanctioned last year.

The timing is particularly damaging. Jepchirchir's ban comes two months after Kenya won the bid to become the first African country to host the World Athletics Championships in 2029.

Kenya's bid was built on its rich history as an athletics powerhouse, producing numerous World and Olympic champions in middle and long-distance running but doping scandals threaten to overshadow that legacy. 

The suspension of another high-profile athlete, who has won Olympic, World and major marathon titles, reinforces concerns about the integrity of Kenyan athletics.

The AIU has previously said the country's cases are isolated with no evidence of systemic violations. Local anti-doping officials say testing, investigations and sanctions have been strengthened in recent years. 

However, recurring cases involving elite athletes raise questions about whether deterrence, education and efforts to prosecute those facilitating doping are keeping pace.

The growing number of positive tests could also reflect more effective testing and intelligence-led investigations, exposing violations that might previously have gone undetected. World Athletics president Sebastian Coe has previously acknowledged Kenya's progress, but says more work remains.

At the moment, Kenya has a proposed anti-doping bill before Parliament, whose passage and effective implementation could help reinforce its commitment to protecting the integrity of sport before 2029. 

But legislation alone will not be enough. The ultimate test will be whether Kenya can effectively detect, sanction and prevent doping as it prepares to host the biggest athletics championships ever staged on African soil.
