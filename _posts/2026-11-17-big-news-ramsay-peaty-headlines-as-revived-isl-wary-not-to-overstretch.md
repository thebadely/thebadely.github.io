Adam Ramsay-Peaty will headline the International Swimming League's comeback All-Star Match, as organisers vowed not to "overstretch" after past financial issues.

The ISL, formed in 2019 as a fast-paced, franchise-based league, has not taken place since 2021 because of the war in Ukraine and its impact on the league's backer, Ukrainian businessman Konstantin Grigorishin.

As a result, prize money totalling about $7m (Â£5.2m) went unpaid to more than 300 swimmers - who did not include Ramsay-Peaty - from its last season, but in July the ISL announced it had set up a payment schedule to repay the amounts owed.

On 19 December, the ISL will return with the All-Star Match at London's Aquatics Centre, with Great Britain's three-time Olympic champion Ramsay-Peaty and South African Pieter Coetze the first swimmers named on the Commonwealth team.

"It's very, very exciting and it's one hit before Christmas, which is something we're always looking for and racing some of the best athletes in the world, which keeps me on my toes," Peaty told BBC Sport.

"I think it's going to be a nice rejuvenation, and just being part of that, I think it's incredibly important that we support that as athletes. For us, making sure that it does well is very important.

"You need people with known names, but also high performers in the sport as well for it to be successful."

The All-Star Match will bring the world's best swimmers to London in four teams - Commonwealth, United States, Europe and Intercontinental.

The format of the competition has had a revamp and will involve 96 swimmers competing in 46 races inside two hours.

On Friday, nine-time Olympic champion Caeleb Dressel and Van Mathias were the first swimmers involved to be named, with both set to compete for the United States.

Further team members for the US and Commonwealth teams, as well as the full rosters for the Europe and Intercontinental teams, will be announced in the coming weeks.

It is hoped the All-Star Match will act as a stepping stone to a full return of the league.

"We would love to see the league format return, but we're also learning from previous experiences not to overstretch," said Ben Allen, commissioner of the ISL, in an interview with BBC Sport.

"I think one of the things which is very important for me, to avoid some of our legacy issues happening again, is to manage expectations and not to jump or run before we can walk.

"We do have plans for 2027 and beyond."

The ISL was launched in 2019 in a bid to shake up swimming by targeting a new audience and growing the sport, while offering athletes substantial prize money and other incentives.

Fina, the sport's governing body, initially indicated it could ban athletes who took part in the ISL, but it backtracked on the threat following a furious reaction from many elite swimmers, including Ramsay-Peaty.

The ISL lasted three seasons before its fourth, due to be held in 2022, was postponed after Russia's invasion of Ukraine.

It announced its comeback with the All-Star Match in August, promising the "largest prize pool ever offered for a single night of competitive swimming", though it has not disclosed the figure.

However, BBC Sport understands there remains some scepticism about the event among the swimming community given the money still owed.

In addition, Rob Woodhouse, the chief executive of Swimming Australia, told BBC Sport he felt "trust building needs to take place in order to build credibility of the league back".

He added: "However, any paid opportunity for swimmers to compete is always a good thing."

Ramsay-Peaty did not compete in the 2021 season of the ISL, despite being named as captain of the London Roar team, and said of the relaunch: "I think we're going into a new era of this league.

"I think the league deserves a little bit of leeway and a little bit of support from the athletes and hopefully everything can be settled."

League commissioner Allen said the ISL had experienced no resistance or reluctance from swimmers in signing up for the All-Star Match, and added it was "ahead of schedule" in the repayments, which it says are being made in four annual instalments.

The ISL says the decision to pay swimmers back in such a way "reflects a careful balance between honouring the prize money owed and ensuring the ISL's return as a sustainable competition".

While Grigorishin remains the league's principal backer as it returns, Allen said the ISL was looking for additional income.

"We're also taking a step back and looking for commercial partners now." he said.

"We've announced to the whole world that we're back, so moving forward we definitely are going to be working together with other parties to make sure that is a sustainable model."
