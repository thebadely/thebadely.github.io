A cost of living roadshow held in a Kent town is the "starting point" and "first step on the ladder" to resolve financial problems, a local councillor has said.

Organisations, including those which help people with health and wellbeing, managing bills and getting back into work, have been in Sheerness for the Swale Borough Council event on Thursday morning. 

This event, funded by the Crisis and Resilience Fund which helps households facing financial crisis, 

Councillor Angela Harrison, deputy mayor of Swale, wants residents to know that "there is help support and guidance for you".

Among those in attendance was Nichola Hermitage from Positively Connected Kent, which helps people manage energy costs and fuel debt.

Reacting to the recent announcement that energy prices are due to go up by 26% in January, Hermitage said there were small measures people can take to mitigate the impact.

She said: "It's about 45p to have a ten minute electric shower; cut that down by in half and you know you can save."

Hermitage stressed that people should not "suffer alone" if they have built up energy debt, with help from the providers themselves and charities.

Charriss Thorne and Alex Emery from Connect To Work

Connect To Work, a 12-month voluntary scheme designed to help people get back into work, offers "individual support" including with travel, clothing and job searching. 

Alex Emery said the roadshow was "about being present in the community to meet people face-to-face for them to understand what we do".

The event comes as Save the Children has released new research into the rising cost of Christmas for families, centred around a survey of 2,000 people including 233 from the South East.

About two thirds of South East respondents expressed concern about being able to afford Christmas.

Harrison said: "It's about time we stopped trying to keep up with the Jones'. 

"Christmas is actually about family. It's not about how much you can spend on your kids and the rest of your family."
