Northern Ireland's Aaron Beggs is one of two runners who have been awarded the International Fair Play award after they stepped in to help a fellow competitor cross the finish line at the Boston Marathon.

Beggs and Robson De Oliveira encountered Ajay Haridasse struggling to stand and put their arms around him to help him down the finishing straight.

The pair became the subject of global attention in April after footage of Haridasse being lifted from the floor was captured by spectators and posted on social media.

"It is so surreal to win this award," said Beggs. "When we first learnt about the nomination, never in a million years did I think we would win it, being up against world-class elite athletes.

"Although Robson and I are the ones receiving the award, credit also needs to go to Ajay. From the moment I went to him, the determination and courage he showed to finish the marathon was incredible.

"When I went to help Ajay, it was natural instinct. Without even thinking about it, I ran over to help him. In that moment I just knew I had to help my fellow runner. 

"Three strangers, three different continents, now friends for life."

Beggs, who runs for North Down Athletic Club, entered the Belfast Marathon in May, only weeks after finishing Boston, and finished in two hours and 48 minutes.

Medical staff provided prompt treatment to the runners at the finish line

De Oliveira, who collapsed after reaching the finish line and required medical assistance, gave up a personal best to help Haridasse.

"During that race, I knew I wouldn't have the strength to do it alone," he said. "But when I saw Aaron, I knew he was the answer to my prayer.

"As for giving up my personal best, I knew that a time was just a number. Showing empathy and helping Ajay was the right thing to do in that moment.

"Ajay deserves just as much credit. He showed incredible strength and resilience that day, fighting to finish the marathon and finding the strength to keep going."

The prize is awarded by the International Fair Play Committee (CIFP) who, in partnership with World Athletics, invited sports fans to nominate athletes or moments and then had them vote on a jury-nominated shortlist via social media.

The CIFP was founded in 1963 by Unesco and leading international sports governing bodies and first collaborated at the 2003 World Athletics Championships, handing out 13 awards since.

"I am delighted our sport continues to recognise these public moments of fair play and camaraderie," added World Athletics president Sebastian Coe said.

"To help another runner in this way, while dealing with the effects of their own endeavours, is the mark of true sportsmanship."

A number of other cases of runners helping their fellow athletes finish have received media attention in the last decade.

In 2016, triathlete Jonny Brownlee was helped over the line by his older brother, fellow Olympic medal winner Alistair, at the end of a race at the Triathlon World Series.

Leading with 700m left, Jonny began to weave over the road in hot and humid conditions in Cozumel, Mexico and was propped up by his sibling, who pushed his brother over the finish for second before taking third place.

At the London Marathon the following year, Swansea Harriers' Matthew Rees helped David Wyeth of Chorlton Runners down the mall, saying "we'll cross the line together."

Meanwhile at this year's event, Gabriella Booth credited Ross Purchase, a fellow member of Porthcawl Runners, and Mo Kachach with ensuring she finished the 26.2-mile (42.2km) route.
