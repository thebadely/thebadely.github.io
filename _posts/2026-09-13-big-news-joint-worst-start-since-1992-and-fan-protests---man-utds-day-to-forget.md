According to new developments, Manchester United minority owner Sir Jim Ratcliffe and co-chair Avram Glazer were at Old Trafford for the 1-0 defeat by Manchester City

Sir Jim Ratcliffe cannot have imagined it would be like this.

When Ratcliffe agreed on Christmas Eve 2023 to spend Â£1.25bn to buy a minority stake in Manchester United, his intention was to restore the ailing club to its past glories. 

Sitting in the directors' box at Old Trafford watching United lose 1-0 to a Manchester City side reduced to 10 men midway through the first half was definitely not part of the plan.

Ratcliffe knew major decisions needed to be made in order to slash the club's losses. And he knew they were bound to be unpopular.

But nearly three years on, he presumably felt the green shoots of recovery would be starting to grow by now. 

After all, the project chief executive Omar Berrada spoke to staff about in September 2024 was an aspiration to win the Premier League title in 2028. In other words, next season.

On the evidence of this 199th Manchester derby, United are a very long shot to achieve Berrada's target. 

At this point, even pacifying the thousands of fans who protested against the club's ownership before the Old Trafford defeat by 10-man City looks beyond them.

United now have four points from their opening four games. It equals their worst start to a Premier League season since 1992-93. Last season under Ruben Amorim, they also had four points from four matches.

In their most recent accounts to 31 March, United had an overall debt of Â£1.3bn, including outstanding transfer fees.

Ratcliffe's involvement promised to change the situation. Yet many fans argue that, if anything, things have got worse.

Ill feeling has spilled over since United launched a ticket-touting investigation in the summer.

Although they have admitted to underestimating the number of fans who shared their account details and toned down some of the threats around season ticket confiscation if supporters failed to respond as requested, United officials defended the investigation.

Last week, they revealed 685 fan accounts had been banned, with a further 464 either sanctioned or warned. 

However, United added that "468 cases were resolved after supporters responded with the relevant information. No further action was taken, as the information provided satisfactorily explained the activity identified".

Many match-going fans felt the investigation was an attack on them and decided to use the City game to protest - inside and outside Old Trafford.

Huge numbers with flares gathered outside the ground, heeding a call from the 1958 Group, before making their way through the Munich tunnel under the stadium.

Inside, The Red Army group of fans opted not to bring in their usual banners - instead display a single banner above the players' tunnel stating: "The way you treat us is a disgrace."

It meant the only banners in the stadium were the ones the club had themselves put up.

"Unless our generational match-going support is given a real voice that is actually listened to, the little that remains of our fan culture will soon be gone forever," said The Red Army group in a statement.

"TRA aren't a protest group, we're an 'atmosphere' group but we've never shied away from making our position clear when we believe not doing so would be to the detriment of the interests of match-going supporters."

It was noticeable many songs were sung in direct protest against Ratcliffe, as well as the Glazers.

"We wanted the Glazers out and that will never change," said Steve Compton of the the 1958 Group.

"Ratcliffe came in and gave us a glimmer, but his idea of putting Manchester back in Man Utd couldn't be any further from the truth.

"The club have made many statements, but one thing they haven't done is actually apologise to anyone. We've had hundreds of messages directly - this has affected people's health.

"These are regular Joes, working-class people who are sent an email telling them their ticket's been suspended with no actual evidence. How do you defend something you don't know what you're defending against?"

The 1958 Group's allegation, which has been voiced by other fan groups, contradicts United's own comments on Friday that state they did advise fans what they were being accused of. The club declined to comment on the protests.
