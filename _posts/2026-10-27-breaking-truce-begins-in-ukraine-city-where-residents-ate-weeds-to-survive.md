According to new developments, A truce has begun in the Russian-occupied Ukrainian city of Oleshky, where residents have been trapped without food, water and electricity.

Ukrainian officials and Russia's defence ministry say the limited ceasefire is being respected. The truce, which the White House said was secured by US First Lady Melania Trump, is meant to allow aid deliveries and a civilian evacuation on Friday. 

Oleshky, along the front line in the Kherson region, is trapped on one side by the Dnipro River, which separates it from Ukrainian positions, and on the other by Russian forces.

Many residents have resorted to cooking garden weeds to survive, telling the BBC they are suffering extreme hunger and want to leave.

There are concerns that truce will not hold, with one resident saying: "I don't believe in Russia's humanity."

Up to 6,000 people including 200 children remain in Oleshky and its surrounding areas, Kyiv believes, with only 1,700 still living in the city itself.

Before the war it was home to 24,000 people, but it was seized by Russia during the first days of its full-scale invasion in February 2022.  

Conditions have become so desperate that not only is getting supplies into the city extremely difficult, but escape is highly dangerous because the routes out of Oleshky have been mined and subject to drone attack.

No shops are open, and the city has had no electricity, gas or water for three years.

Pope Leo XIV appealed earlier this month to Russia's Vladimir Putin to allow food and medical aid into Oleshky as well as a safe evacuation, and urged Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky to co-operate with Moscow over a ceasefire.

The current truce is supposed to allow supplies in and enable roads to be cleared of mines to allow people safe passage out in an evacuation planned to take place on Friday.

One woman told the BBC she had stepped on a land mine and was currently in hospital where she had undergone two operations.

"I don't know how long I will have to spend there," she said, worried about not being able to join the evacuation.

It is hard to make contact with people in Oleshky as communication is patchy and and many are reluctant to speak of the situation in fear of reprisals from the Russians, making it difficult to verify reports the ceasefire is being implemented.

Many details on the evacuation plans are unclear, initially including how and where to register for it. Some people want to know if they can bring their pets. Others are questioning what future they will have if they opt to leave their homes.

"It is critically important that this mission to rescue our people proceeds successfully and safely," Kyrylo Budanov, the head of Zelensky's office, said on Tuesday.

Russia's defence ministry said units from the army's Dnepr group had been conducting "engineering reconnaissance" and clearing main access roads into the village of "explosive and booby-trap devices".

While people are optimistic about leaving, there are also fears that Russia may not allow them to return to Ukraine, or examine their phones and online activity for signs of Ukrainian support, which could see them be taken away and tortured. 

Someone who has been speaking to family in Oleshky said they fear shooting could restart during evacuations and Ukraine will somehow be blamed for not upholding the truce.

These residents were able to get out of Oleshky in spring this year

Last month, the Kremlin acknowledged that Oleshky faced "a critical situation" and "problems with supplies of food and medicine", but added that "Russia's military was working to resolve them" and blamed Kyiv.

In response to the situation, the White House said last week that Melania Trump had secured a ceasefire, with no details for how long it would last.

Russia's defence ministry said on Telegram it had stopped firing toward the Ukrainian city of Kherson - across the river from Oleshky - at 04:00 Moscow time (01:00 GMT) in preparation for the humanitarian operation.

Ukrainian officials say parliament's commissioner for human rights, Dmytro Lubinets, has travelled to the areas to co-ordinate the operation.

"This is part of a humanitarian mission being carried out with the mediation and co-ordination of US First Lady Melania Trump, in co-operation with Yana Lantratova, Russia's Commissioner for Human Rights," Lubinets wrote on social media.

Ukraine's military general staff said that silence was being observed over the operation for humanitarian purposes, and urged people not to trust unverified reports or spread rumours about "the routes, time and place of the movement of people and humanitarian convoys".

Oleshky has been under Russian occupation since 2022 and its people are now trapped

Zelensky has warned that routes out of Oleshky that are demined by Russia will ensure that those civilians leaving Oleshky will "head towards Russia and stay there". However, he said some would get to Ukrainian-controlled territory "one way or another", and ideally anyone trapped would be able to reach their destination and evacuate to Ukraine.

The White House said in the statement last week that "non-military, civilian leaders from both countries would work together to visit Oleshky residents in their homes and determine the level of assistance they require".

While the US first lady is relatively lower profile than some of her predecessors - and only occasionally seen at White House events - she has become involved in addressing humanitarian issues stemming from the Russia-Ukraine war.

In August 2025, she penned a letter to Putin, telling him it was "time to protect children and future generations worldwide".

While a truce is under way in Oleshky, elsewhere strikes continue. 

On Monday evening, attacks on the Ukrainian capital Kyiv hit a university and a bridge, killing one person and injuring 14 others.

In Russia, one person was killed and six others were injured after a Ukrainian drone strike on Bryansk.
