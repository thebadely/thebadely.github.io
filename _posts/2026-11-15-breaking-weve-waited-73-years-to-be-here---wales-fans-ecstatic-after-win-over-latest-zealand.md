Let's dive into the details: Saturday marked the first time Wales had beaten New Zealand in 73 years

Welsh rugby fans were "fizzing off their heads" on Saturday night after ending 73 years of hurt against New Zealand in an epic Nations Championship encounter.

The match at the Principality Stadium ended 35-28 to the hosts as the All Blacks' run of 34 successive victories against Wales - dating back to 1953 - came to a juddering halt in Cardiff.

Wales fans were left stunned by a result that came just a week on from having to grind out a win against Japan.

Prince William, patron of the Welsh Rugby Union (WRU) also congratulated the team on the win, saying it was a "fantastic result".

Three times Wales led by 14 points, and for the first time in history they scored five tries against New Zealand, but ultimately it was heroic defensive work that denied the visitors.

All Blacks head coach Dave Rennie made eight changes from the side that swept past Scotland in their previous Nations Championship game.

But the approach backfired spectacularly after a brilliant performance sparked astonishing scenes in Cardiff.

A crowd of 55,152 could scarcely believe what had unfolded, and there were scenes of wild celebration with players in tears as Wales began taking in arguably the most famous win of their 145-year Test match history.

Wales now holds The Raeburn Shield - a fan-created trophy that traces match-winners in men's rugby union back to the first international rugby union match in March 1871

Prince William shared a post on Instagram saying it was a "fantastic result by the Wales rugby team".

"My congratulations to the players, coaches and everyone who played a part - you have given fans across Wales a day they will never forget".

Celebrities and politicians were also quick to pay tribute to the team for their heroics on the field.

Speaking on 5Live Breakfast on Sunday morning, First Minister Rhun ap Iorwerth said: "Yesterday, meant a heck of a lot to us. 

"We are a pretty sporting mad nation. Rugby is very important to us as a nation.

"The fixture against New Zealand is a particularly important fixture. We are a smallish country but in the rugby world, we are the biggest powers. 

He continued that "73 years we have failed to beat them, we have come close".

"I have watched Wales come close some 20 years or so ago but sport is wonderful and awful at the same time. It became this psychological block.

"Well, no more. That is it, we can go forward now with a new generation knowing that we can beat New Zealand."

He said the crowd on Saturday "wasn't the biggest, but no doubt that changes".

"I full expect the game next week to be a sell out."

Gavin and Stacey star Jones shared her sheer delight in an Instagram video, with the actor looked visibly stunned following the incredible win over New Zealand.

"This has been decades in the making. Huge congratulations to you all," he wrote on Facebook.

"You should all be proud."

Secretary of State for Wales Stephen Kinnock said he managed to watch the final part of the game after speaking at the Welsh Labour conference in Llandudno.

"After 73 years we've finally beaten the All Blacks! Can barely believe what I've just witnessed," he said.

Hannah went to the game with her 81-year-old dad

John Jenkins was eight-years old the last time Wales beat New Zealand, and without a TV, he was not able to watch the game.

Now 81, he has seen his team beat the All Blacks.

John, who now lives in Shropshire but travels for every match, said he has been present at all of the big occasions in Welsh rugby, and Wales' win against New Zealand was the "most memorable".

"I'm not a gambling man, and if I'd known how to place a bet, I would have bet on Wales because I had this gut feeling that we were going to win.

"Everybody in the last 20 minutes was so anxious," he said.

His daughter Hannah, 50, who also travels to every match despite living in Chichester, said "it felt like a party in the first half, we couldn't quite believe what was happening".

John said: "For 10 minutes after the final whistle, Hannah was sobbing on my shoulder."

Hannah said: "For me and dad to be able to go and watch it together, I keep thinking about turning and looking at his face after we scored the first try - we were just kind of incredulous." 

A couple celebrating their wedding day had to delay the speeches by 15 minutes as their guests had to see out the game. 

Photographer Rhian Jones, 32, from Ammanford, who captured the celebrations, said the "entirety of the wedding breakfast were on their phones".

"The atmosphere was just so intense, people were screaming, it was incredible and everyone was on edge.

"The bride actually said she felt such a pressure because she was so scared that they were going to lose on her wedding day and obviously it would have dampened the spirit, but of course we didn't," she said.

"We celebrated the couple, celebrated the victory and it was incredible."

She added none of the guests at the wedding in Carmarthen had seen that in their lifetime.

"I tried to edit during the wedding breakfast but I didn't edit anything and I just sat on there with the saxophonist and videographer watching my phone which is very unprofessional, but what are you going to do when it's making history.

"I can't wait to tell my children, and certainly they will be telling their children this in years to come."

Warren Atkins says "you would have struggled to move", when asked on how packed the pub was

Warren Atkins from The Cottage pub on St Mary's Street in Cardiff said they were already packed but it was "next level" afterwards.

"We were quite busy before the game but then during the end of the game and after the game it was a different kettle of fish.

"The celebrations were wild."

He added that the performance was a "promising start" for Wales, and was getting in more beer for next week's game.

"I think we need to step it up a notch again next week seeing how busy it was this week. 

"We were expecting busy but you can never prepare yourself for what's to come."

Darren Williams went to Caernarfon Rugby Club to watch the match

Darren Williams lives abroad but comes home once a year to visit his family in Wales.

He came to Caernarfon Rugby Club to watch the match and said the result was "excellent".

"I hope they can continue like this and keep moving forward," he said.

"The atmosphere was excellent and very enthusiastic."

Daron Owens said everyone at the rugby club was "ecstatic" after the game

Daron Owens said she was "really glad" to be at the rugby club to witness such a brilliant performance.

"It was great to see everyone so proud. Everyone was cheering so loudly, it was a really positive place to be."

One fan pitch side at Principality Stadium said they had waited "73 years to be at this game", adding that they were in "total shock but absolutely delighted".
