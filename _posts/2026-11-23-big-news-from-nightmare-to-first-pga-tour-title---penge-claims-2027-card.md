England's Marco Penge completed a remarkable comeback from illness and personal struggles to win the RSM Classic in Georgia and secure his PGA Tour card for 2027.

Penge began the week 120th in the FedExCup standings after a year disrupted by health and family issues, but birdied the first extra hole in a four-man play-off to win his first PGA Tour title.

The 28-year-old struggled for form after contracting a virus late last year, before his new-born son spent three weeks in the neonatal intensive care unit (NICU) after being born in February with underdeveloped lungs.

Penge then suffered from a sinus infection and vertigo symptoms, forcing him into a six-week break after May's US PGA Championship.

"Before this week I would have said it was a nightmare. Stressful would be the word," said Penge, who required at least a top-four finish to jump inside the top-100 and avoid losing his card.

"I started the season with seven weeks on the Tour while not being healthy. My son was then born, in the NICU for four weeks, and then while he's in hospital, I'm injured and playing bad golf. It was awful.

"The last four weeks have been a battle. [I told my team] if I'm going to fight, I want to give everything I possibly can in terms of work ethic, and if we don't keep my card, then at least I tried. 

"[I] ended up winning the tournament. It's just how the game is, and it's the wonderful part of golf. I feel like there's an opportunity every week to change your life."

Penge's five-under 65 in a delayed final round at the Sea Island Resort's Seaside Course had left him tied at 16 under overall, with American duo Max Greyserman and Michael Thorbjornsen, and China's Li Haotong. 

However, Penge produced a superb iron shot and drained a birdie putt from no more than five feet to earn the $1.3m (Â£993,000) top prize and a two-year exemption. 

"I proved to myself I can do it under the most immense pressure. I always seem to play my best golf when my back is up against the wall," Penge said.

"I've been through this before on the DP World Tour, keeping my card in the last event and dealing with adversity and coming back from health issues, all that stuff, so I knew I could do it.

"I just wasn't sure I had the game this week to be able to do it. Thankfully I did."

Having picked up four strokes on the back nine, which included a birdie on the 18th to reach the play-off, he achieved his objective in style.

"Of course we're trying to win golf tournaments but the first task for me was to keep my card," Penge said.

"When I got on that 18th tee in the play-off, I was like, 'OK, I can win the golf tournament now'."

"It's a lot of hard work to get back to feeling myself," Penge added. 

With heavy rain forcing Saturday's third round to be postponed until Sunday, it meant the final round on Monday was played without fans being present as the event did not have enough volunteers to manage spectators.
