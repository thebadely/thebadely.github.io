Arsenal boss Mikel Arteta says he was told the club's decision to cut 60 jobs had been made to "achieve the success we want to create".

Gunners chief executive Richard Garlick informed staff this week that Arsenal's workforce would be reduced across a number of departments.

The cuts are set to affect up to seven per cent of Arsenal's 871 permanent staff, but will affect neither coaching nor performance staff in the men's or women's setups and will come from off-the-field roles.

Jobs in the Arsenal Foundation, Arsenal in the Community and within the security staff will also be unaffected.

A review carried out by an external company into the workforce was ongoing before the club won the Premier League.

The decision to make the redundancies comes a week after Arteta was handed a new four-year contract, having guided Arsenal to their first Premier League title since 2004 last season.
