Let's dive into the details: Drivers were limited to 25 laps for each set of tyres in last year's Qatar Grand Prix

Formula 1 drivers will be limited to a maximum of 27 laps on a set of tyres at next weekend's Qatar Grand Prix.

The decision is caused by concern over the integrity of the Pirelli tyres on the high-speed Lusail track, which has a series of demanding, long-duration corners.

It means teams will have to make at least two pit stops during the 57-lap grand prix, as was the case last year.

The limit applies on a single set of tyres whenever it is used across sessions.

So mileage done in practice or qualifying, for example, would limit the number of laps that could be done on a set of tyres in the race.

Pirelli said the limit was longer then last year's 25 laps because of the reduced downforce levels of the 2026 cars compared with 2025.

"The increase has been made possible by the lower load levels recorded so far on the current generation of cars and by a further reinforcement of the tyre construction," a statement said.

Last year, the limit led to a major strategic error by McLaren, who lost a win because they failed to pit under a safety car period when everyone else did.

It is the third time in four years Pirelli has imposed a tyre-duration limit at Qatar, where running wide over flat kerbs in addition to the high-speed nature of the track is especially demanding for the tyres.

Pirelli described the decision as a "precautionary measure" that was "linked to the unique characteristics of the Lusail circuit, which is particularly demanding in terms of energy loads, thermal stress and tread wear".

In terms of the way the limit will be imposed, the statement said: "Laps will be counted cumulatively across all track sessions, including any laps completed under the safety car or virtual safety car.

"As far as the grand prix is concerned, all laps completed from the start signal to the chequered flag will count towards the total.

"Reconnaissance laps, formation laps and any laps completed after the end of the race will not be included.

"In all other track sessions, any lap that has been started but not completed will nevertheless be counted as a full lap.

"Should a team assemble a tyre set using tyres that have covered different mileages, the tyre with the highest lap count will determine the mileage attributed to that set."
