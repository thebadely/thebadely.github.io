Here’s what you need to know: Wales will play Australia for the 50th time at Principality Stadium

The Australia fixture was meant to be Wales' big shot. 

It was the chance to finally claim the scalp of a southern hemisphere big gun. The opportunity to make a statement ahead of the 2027 World Cup that they are capable of beating any of the top teams on their day.

But Steve Tandy's side went seven days early.

Wales will now attempt to claim a hat-trick of Cardiff wins in the Nations Championship on Saturday night after the most unlikely of victories against New Zealand.

It is a success that has changed the November narrative. Rather than "can they do it?", the question is now "can they back it up?".

Wales v Australia

Nations Championship

Saturday, 21 November (20:10 GMT)

Principality Stadium, Cardiff

Wales' win against the All Blacks was arguably the biggest shock in international rugby since Japan beat South Africa in Brighton in the 2015 World Cup.

What is rarely mentioned is that the Brave Blossoms - hindered by a farcical four-day turnaround - failed to back that up when thumped by Scotland in Gloucester in their following game.

Japan caused another surprise on home soil in 2019 when they beat Ireland and that time they did follow up with victories against Samoa and Scotland.

"It's a real test of our mental and physical preparation," admitted head coach Tandy.

"We can't assume that because one scoreboard went our way, everything is fixed.

"We have to double-down on performance, how we recover, how we train and executing the core areas of our game.

"We have to be at our absolute best to compete with the Aussies."

Tandy frequently talks about performance rather than results, yet this is an occasion where an ugly victory would do.

Wales' belief has soared but they remain slight underdogs for the fixture.

The odds are not as generous as last week's 20-1, but Tandy's men are hunting another scalp.

They are ninth in the world rankings and the Wallabies are seventh.

"It's not do or die, but they will want the performance and result," former Wales hooker Scott Baldwin told the Scrum V podcast.

"None of us expected the New Zealand win, it was one for the ages. If they perform anywhere near that then they win quite comfortably.

"After you have beaten the All Blacks, you go into the Six Nations believing you can beat anyone."

Wales enjoyed their moment, dealt with the acclaim, soaked in the social media reactions and fulfilled plenty of extra media requests.

But they were back in for training on Monday afternoon - business as usual(ish).

"I don't think anyone really felt a sore body until Tuesday because of the emotion and riding the weekend," said captain Dewi Lake. 

"I have no doubt the boys will be ready to go.

"There might have been some things the coaches pulled here and there, some minutes off the clock, but the intensity, the challenge, the effort has been exactly the same.

"We've spoken about kicking on from this and to do that you just have to keep pushing."

Wales can go three from three in Cardiff and would then travel to London hunting a perfect November in a fixture likely to be against Japan.

Warren Gatland's class of 2018 are the only team to have enjoyed an autumn clean sweep.

Wales do not want to go from the biggest of highs against New Zealand - something that even the Grand Slam greats could not achieve - to a deflating home loss.

"When you come down off a high like that it is important you get the messaging right," said Gloucester hooker Lake.

"Steve, the coaches and other leaders have really delivered that this week in terms of it being an opportunity for us to build rather than it just being a one-off game we performed in that people will remember forever - but then we didn't turn up the week after.

"There are two ways we can go, we kick on or it's back to how we were. It's an opportunity to kick on."

Kane James is set for his first Wales game in Cardiff while Alex Mann returns to the Wales starting XV

The Wales players have had an extra day of recovery than the Wallabies, who lost to Scotland on Sunday, but Tandy's men did have a considerably bigger defensive effort and high ball-in-play time.

Wales used plenty of emotional and physical energy, leading to the management team freshening up their starting line-up.

They have gone back to the same XV that faced Japan a fortnight ago, and the pressure is on the returnees.

Blind-side flanker Alex Mann has been a favourite of Tandy's â starting in 12 of 14 Tests - but must now respond to Cardiff club-mate James Botham's excellence.

Lock Ben Carter got ahead of Adam Beard in the Six Nations but was tasked with providing energy off the bench last week.

The Dragons co-captain gets back in courtesy of Beard suffering a minor shoulder issue and must deliver when calling at the line-out after an off-day against Japan.

Wing Josh Adams, who was rested, and number eight Aaron Wainwright, back from a leg injury, have nothing to prove on the Test stage but would not be human if they were not a little disappointed to have missed out on making history.

Back row Taine Basham and wing Gabriel Hamer-Webb go from the starting line-up to missing out, with Kane James given the role of impact sub.

The promising Exeter flanker is set for a first international appearance in Cardiff after winning his first cap in Argentina in July.

"We have fresh and hungry players coming in, which will add the energy that we need," said Tandy.

Eleven Wales players are set for their third start on the spin, with the coaching staff poised to rotate for Twickenham.

While Wales are hunting an autumn clean sweep, Australia are at risk of another November whitewash.

The last time that the Wallabies won in Europe was the 52-20 win in Cardiff two years ago.

They have suffered eight successive losses on this continent since that eight-try victory and travel to Cardiff fresh from a heavy defeat by England and a loss to Scotland in a drab encounter.

"Australia are there for the taking," said former Wales centre Shanklin on the Scrum V podcast.

"I have been a little bit disappointed with how they are playing. They haven't looked that good, they look lost in attack."

The Wallabies could drop to ninth in the world rankings on Monday, although that would take a heavy loss at Principality Stadium.

Les Kiss' side have enjoyed some notable results this year - a superb win against South Africa in Perth plus a win and a draw in Argentina - and remain slight favourites.

"A hurting team is a dangerous team. We've been in that position, and probably still are to an extent," said Lake.

"We know they're a dangerous team and they've got dangerous players. Look back over the last 12 months and they've had some big results and big performances."

Wales have won three games on the bounce at Principality Stadium

An unfriendly kick-off time for the Wallabies fixture presented a challenge to the Welsh Rugby Union (WRU), but then Lake and his team ensured a ticket surge.

A crowd of 55,152 witnessed history against New Zealand at the 74,000-capacity Principality Stadium - but the 62,000 mark has already been passed for Australia.

"Our performance drives people to come and watch us and get behind us," said Lake.

"We saw that in spades last Saturday for every try and the defensive shifts. I think the loudest cheer was for a held-up ball or Eddie James and Louis Rees-Zammit's tackle [of a New Zealander] into touch in the 77th minute.

"That's what drives us on the field as well. The energy that we get from the crowd does help massively and our performance drives that, it's not shock that the two go hand in hand.

"We've seen how it's kind of given the nation a bit of hope. We've seen loads of videos rolling in, and that hope drives us as well."

A win against Australia would not prompt tears of joy in the stands, but Wales want to celebrate again after so much post-World Cup pain.

Wales will not mathematically know their Twickenham opponents before kick-off against the Wallabies, but they could be 99% certain to finish fifth.

If Scotland win with a bonus point against Japan then Tandy's side would need to do likewise, and win by 20 points more than the Scots.

Wales cannot drop to sixth and are likely to finish fifth, and that will mean another meeting with Japan unless Fiji win in Italy with a bonus point.

The Fijians would also need to turn around a hefty points difference gap to the Brave Blossoms.

The game between the fifth-placed teams is on Saturday, 28 November (13:10 GMT), followed in the double-header by the nations that finish second in the respective southern and northern tables.
