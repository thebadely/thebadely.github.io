Experts revealed that George Davies says he feels resentful towards the insurance companies

For 17 years, George Davies made payments to an insurance company to cover the cost of his funeral.

The 82-year-old, who has stage 4 cancer, thought he had prepared and provided for his wife Marion.

But now he isn't entitled to a penny.

George took the policy out through his local credit union, but it was cancelled after the company backing the product pulled out of the UK.

Credit unions say the closure of the plan has left thousands of people like George in limbo, with many unable to arrange an affordable alternative because they are elderly or unwell.

A coalition of 17 credit unions across Scotland and the north of England is calling for those affected to receive fair compensation.

George, from Bellshill in North Lanarkshire, is currently undergoing palliative care after being diagnosed with oesophageal cancer in 2023.

He says he can't sleep at night because of worrying about how his wife of 50 years will cope when he's gone.

"I needed something to leave to Marion so that she didn't have to worry about funeral expenses," he says.

"The only consolation is that when I die, I stop worrying about it. I just worry about it all the time."

George had been paying into a policy called the Family Protection Plan (FPP), which he took out through Mosshill Credit Union.

He initially thought it was "excellent", promising a cash lump sum on death to policy holders and up to five named family members.

But as his health deteriorates, George says he feels resentful towards the insurance companies involved.

"How can these people do these things?" he asks. "I'm going to need this money sooner than later."

George said he had not wanted his wife Marion to worry about funeral expenses

The FFP was a renewable life insurance product which did not build up a cash value over time and could be cancelled with 30 days' notice.

It closed in November 2025, leaving about 5,000 people across the UK without cover.

The Family Protection Plan was first offered in 1999 and closed to new business in 2009, with cover continuing for those already on the scheme.

Although the money could be used for any purpose, BBC Scotland News understands that it was initially advertised to help cover the cost of funerals.

An early leaflet, provided to us by the credit unions, says the FPP could be used for "any of the costs associated with the funeral" â including the wake and travel expenses for relatives.

It was sold exclusively through credit unions to their members.

Credit unions are not-for-profit financial institutions where members pool their savings to lend to one another. They are often community-based and offer an alternative to traditional banking.

FPP was offered by insurance broker CMutual, which was formerly known as CUNA Mutual Group.

The underwriters on the policy changed over the years, with Maiden Life covering the plan between 2022 and 2025.

Maiden Life announced it would leave the UK market in 2024 and gave notice of termination.

CMutual was unable to find another suitable insurer and the plan closed in November 2025.

The coalition of credit unions group says it represents about 1,500 people who have been affected by the closure. Some have paid premiums for up to 25 years.

Julie Reddin is the chief executive of Mosshill credit union and co-chairs the coalition, which is campaigning for members to receive a fair pay out.

She says it is a "scandal" that so many people have lost out on what they regarded as funeral cover.

"We would just like the people that have made money out of the policies to actually stand up and be accountable and not step back and say no that wasn't us," she says.

"It may not have been illegal what they did, but it was immoral."

Julie Reddin says legislation needs to be changed

Credit unions in the coalition have agreed to help with the cost of funerals for affected members as a goodwill gesture as they feel a level of responsibility.

"If we had to pay that out straight away, the credit union would go into liquidation. We just don't have that type of money to pay out," she said.

Julie says the Financial Conduct Authority should have stepped in sooner and wants financial redress for the most vulnerable customers to be split between credit unions, CMutual and Maiden Life.

"Legislation needs to be changed - this should never be able to happen again," she added.

Credit union trade body Altogether Money receives payment from CMutual for offering products through credit unions.

A spokesperson for Altogether Money said the family protection plan had been an optional product that credit unions could choose to offer.

"The decision as to whether or not to offer such products to credit union members has always been one for individual credit union boards to determine and they are responsible for their regulatory compliance and due diligence in relation to these products," it said.

"Some have taken decisions to provide financial ex-gratia payments as a goodwill gesture but in doing so are only concerned to support their members as co-operative, member-owned organisations and do not admit any liability.

"Each credit union should consider its own financial, legal and regulatory position in offering such support and we are providing advice and guidance to credit unions that wish to do so."

The Financial Conduct Authority investigated the cancellation of the plan, and acknowledged the closure had caused "real worry" to those affected.

"We have looked carefully at the circumstances. Based on this evidence, we have decided against taking further action in relation to historical conduct at this time," said a spokesperson.

"We know this is disappointing. We have strongly encouraged the firms involved to help impacted consumers and will support them to achieve this."

The coalition of credit unions will hold a meeting on Wednesday with the UK Treasury, which previously said it was holding talks with the industry and encouraging those involved to "explore all options" to support those affected.

In a statement issued in 2025, Maiden Life said the interests of CMutual's clients and policyholders had been "foremost in our plans" during its dealings with the insurance broker.

"As part of closing our life business we ensured we gave CMutual considerable notice to enable them to inform and provide their customers with alternative arrangements," it said.

A spokesperson for CMutual said the FPP had been "much valued" by credit unions and their members.

It said more than 70% of those holding a policy had claimed at least once, and over 25% had claimed more than once. The FPP enabled the policyholder to claim after the death of other people named on the policy.

CMutual also said the FCA had concluded that many people aged 80 and under were likely to be able to secure other insurance cover.

"Credit unions across the United Kingdom have a tremendous record of supporting and safeguarding their members, and we remain instrumental in helping them achieve this," it said.

"For those members and insureds most affected by the withdrawal of FPP, including those aged over 80 years for whom alternative cover may not be widely available, we remain supportive of both individual credit union initiatives already implemented and sector wide discussions to support such members."
