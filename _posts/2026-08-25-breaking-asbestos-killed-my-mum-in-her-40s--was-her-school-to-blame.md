'Mum died young from asbestos - now my family want justice'

When Caroline Bryan was enjoying high school with friends in the early 90s, she would never have imagined replaying those memories in her final weeks, while searching for answers about her terminal condition.

The mum was 46 when she died from mesothelioma, an incurable cancer linked to asbestos exposure.

As she came to terms with her difficult diagnosis, Caroline's belief was the only place she could have encountered asbestos was during her time at school decades earlier.

Her family blame the local authority that ran Caroline's school for her death and are taking legal action. Cardiff council said it would be inappropriate to comment on ongoing legal proceedings.

"She never had the sort of career that would have exposed her to asbestos," said Caroline's daughter Brooklyn.

"We never lived in any houses that would have had asbestos in."

Caroline was mostly a stay at home mum and the family hadn't heard of mesothelioma before, but quickly learned it likely came from asbestos fibres she will have unknowingly breathed in many years before.

Brooklyn said she remembered her mum as a "kind and caring person" who would go "above and beyond for anybody"

"Before she passed, she contacted a solicitor," added Brooklyn.

"She wanted justice - not just for herself, but to stop anyone else going through the same thing."

The 26-year-old said her world "turned upside down" when she found out her mum had incurable cancer. 

Brooklyn said after looking back through her mum's life, they began to believe she was exposed to asbestos at her old high school, the old Fitzalan site in Cardiff.

The family's legal team helped to uncover council documents which they say show asbestos was used "quite extensively" at the site during Caroline's time there in the 1990s.

"Mum mentioned that the building wasn't in good condition," said Brooklyn.

Caroline was 45 years old when she noticed she was having breathing difficulties 

"Things like ceiling tiles were broken. There was dust and debris when things were disturbed, though at the time she didn't know what it was."

Caroline had only gone to the doctors because she'd noticed a shortness of breath. In her mid 40s, she was far younger than most mesothelioma patients.

"I was 23," said Brooklyn.

"Suddenly, I had to take over everythingârunning the house, paying bills, cooking, cleaning. She had always looked after me, so I wasn't used to doing those things. I was essentially forced into adulthood overnight. It was very difficult.

"Losing your mum, who is your main support system, at that age is incredibly hard. Now she's gone, I feel I have to continue this [fight] for her."

Caroline's family are bringing a High Court claim against Cardiff council over her death, with the case set to be heard in early 2027.

"The council's own documents show that asbestos was used quite extensively in that school," said Phillip Gower from Redkite Solicitors, who are representing the family.

"The buildings were in pretty poor condition, with damage to the walls around the doors, the floor tiles, the ceiling tiles."

Cardiff Council said it would "not be appropriate" to comment on Caroline's case but it recognised the "devastating impact" of mesothelioma on patients and families. 

"We would like to reassure parents, staff and the wider public that the management of asbestos in Cardiff council buildings, including schools, is taken extremely seriously," added a spokesperson. 

Many primary and secondary schools were built using asbestos, a common building material until its dangers became clear and it was banned in the UK in 1999.

Most schools still contain some asbestos and authorities must regularly inspect it to check it has not been damaged and at risk of releasing tiny fibres into the air, which can cause cancers like mesothelioma.

A Freedom of Information request by BBC Wales suggested more than three quarters of schools in Wales still contained asbestos.

Responses from 20 of Wales' 22 local authorities showed 76% of primary schools and 81% of secondary schools had asbestos containing materials (ACMs).

Fitzalan High School pupils were taught at the same site Caroline attended from 1964 until the 1,800-pupil school moved to new premises in 2023

Mesothelioma is the 20th most common type of cancer in the UK, responsible for 2,200 deaths per year, according to Cancer Research UK.

It can develop from a single asbestos fibre lodged in the lungs, often unknowingly breathed in many decades earlier.

It is more common in men, particularly those who have worked in heavy industry or trades where they would have regular contact with asbestos. Most deaths from the disease happen when people are in their 80s.

However, an epidemiologist who has spent decades studying mesothelioma rates, said there were two "concerning epidemiological observations" coming from more recent data.

"First, the number of mesothelioma cases per year [in people] under the age of 40 in Britain has increased over the last 5 or 6 years - it' increased from 2.5 cases per year to 5 cases per year," said Professor Julian Peto. 

"The second thing is that there is a slightly but significantly increased mesothelioma risk in female teachers."

Peto said the findings were "not conclusive" but did "raise concern".

"What we are also starting to see is a number of younger people who are suffering with mesothelioma. In the last 12 months or so, we've definitely seen an increase in the number of people coming forward who are under 50."
