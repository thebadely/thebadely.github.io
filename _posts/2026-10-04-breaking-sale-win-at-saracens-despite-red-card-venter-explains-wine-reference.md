Sale overcame Jacques Vermeulen's red card to defeat Saracens and pick up their first win of The Prem campaign.

Sarries struggled to break down a stubborn Sharks defence despite having numerical superiority for 27 minutes of the first half after Alex Lozowski's yellow and Vermeulen's 20-minute red card.

Incredibly, Sale took a 7-5 lead into half-time thanks to Nathan Jibulu's early converted try, despite Eroni Mawi's reply, and then went further ahead soon after the break as Tom O'Flaherty raced in.

Ben Bamber stretched Sale's lead to 11 points after Sione Va'enuku had cut the gap, and though Charlie Bracken's late score again put the game in the balance, the hosts held on.

After shipping 150 points to Saracens in two Prem hammerings last season, Sale's victory at the Stone X was built on a huge defensive effort.

Their forwards laid down an impressive early marker when a driving maul carved through Sarries with relative ease to sweep Jibulu over the line.

But the visitors were soon down to 13 as former Sarrie Alex Lozowski, on his first Sale start, was yellow-carded for a high shot on Alfie Barbeary, before Vermeulen was red-carded for putting a shoulder into Tomos Williams' head.

And only a TMO intervention prevented the visitors going down to 12 when Hamish Smales overturned Courtney Lawes' yellow card for a deliberate knock on.

Smales was involved again to overturn Nick Tompkins' smart close-range finish for a Nick Isiekwe's obstruction in the build-up, but Mawi finally turned Sarries' red-zone pressure into points when he powered over.

Yet Sale were heartened to head into half-time with a two-point lead and back up to full strength as Elia Canakaivata replaced Vermeulen.

Saracens responded with three changes at half-time, including a debut for England lock George Martin, but Sale soon went nine points clear when O'Flaherty collected George Ford's up and under on halfway and raced away to score.

The hosts finally found their cutting edge when a quick switch of play while camped on the Sale line created an overlap and gave Va'enuku a walk-in on debut.

Sale's defence continued to impress before a quick change of pace in attack sent them 21-10 up as Ford teed up Bamber to run a hard line through a gap and race in.

Once again the Saracens response was swift as Jack Bracken cut in off the right wing to give brother Charlie a clear run to the line to set up a tense finale.

Brendan Venter (right) with Saracens head coach Joe Shaw who will continue to take pre-match and post-match interviews

Despite opening the season with an eventful and impressive 40-38 win over Leicester, Brendan Venter made most of the headlines in its aftermath.

The former South Africa international was reported to have changed his job title to head of rugby culture and wine consultant after failing to follow media commitments at the league's season launch and opening match.

New Saracens chief executive Ed Coetzee ended the confusion on Friday by confirming Venter as head of culture and technical director, a role which will include "significant media duties".

Venter duly spoke to TNT Sports before the match with Sale and said he refused the director of rugby job title because he had "absolutely nothing" to do with other sections of the club such as the academy and women's section.

The 56-year-old also insisted the wine consultant reference was not a joke aimed at the media.

"Our whole theme this year was all about wine," Venter told TNT Sports. "Everything in our theme was about a blend and getting the blend right.

"There's nobody in life that is successful by doing one thing the whole time - it doesn't work like that. As you grow older you have to blend things, so that's where it came from, and it's very relevant."

Chris Jones, BBC rugby union correspondent

The Prem has been all about free-flowing attack so far this season, but this was much more of an old school dogfight.

A stop-start game dominated by the referee's whistle, Sale came up with two big plays in the second half: Tom O'Flaherty with a stunning individual score, while Ben Bamber's try was also a beauty after excellent build-up play.

The big names stepped up for Sale. Courtney Lawes looked his old self in the lineout and on the floor, while Tom Curry was magnificent in the kind of physical scrap he relishes. George Ford managed the game beautifully in the closing stages.

Sarries director of rugby turned wine consultant turned technical director Brendan Venter again handed media duties over to head coach Joe Shaw post-match, but he will talk this week in a bid to avoid further sanctions from Prem Rugby.

Saracens head coach Joe Shaw:

"We couldn't really get into our flow, that's the truth of it. Credit to Sale, they came and fronted up.

"They had adversity, which we had last weekend, but they kept coming. We have to take it on the chin and move on."

Sale director of rugby Alex Sanderson:

"Any time you watch Nathan [Jibulu] play, he makes an impact.

"He was a wrecking ball and caused havoc around the field. He nailed his line-outs and showed he's of international class.

"You can get away with being fast and skilful in The Prem, but it's another level again in terms of the power that's required at international level and he's got that ability. He's immensely strong.

"This could be the season when he explodes on to the international scene."
