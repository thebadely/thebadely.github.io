Ireland's return to Twickenham may not have produced more viral moments like Stuart McCloskey barrelling Marcus Smith into touch, but it did yield the victory needed to close the book on what head coach Andy Farrell considers a successful year. 

Farrell has not shied away from his intention to use the November window as a World Cup test run: four weekends, four games, heavy-hitting opposition, six-day turnarounds. 

Friday's Nations Championship fifth-place play-off at a half-empty Allianz Stadium, however, felt like a comedown in the wake of a nail-biting win over world champions South Africa.

But winning ugly is an important feature for the best teams and carving out a gritty 38-14 success over Australia is a reflection of the progress Farrell has been demanding to see.

Casting his eye over the campaign as a whole, he sounded upbeat as he spoke about a noticeable uptick in his squad's desire to grow with the World Cup coming into view. 

"We've certainly seen a shift in our appetite to want to grow and get better," said Farrell. 

"That's been very evident in the camp. It transfers sometimes to games, sometimes it doesn't, but we've seen the step up as far as that's concerned.

"It's been a very successful campaign for that alone, but we've got to keep that going now and taking big steps forward."

Replicating the punchy, spirited display that brought down South Africa in Dublin last week was always going to be a big ask, and Ireland certainly showed signs of weariness during a clunky first-half display that a more accurate team than Australia would have punished. 

But by hook or by crook, Ireland went in at half-time with a 10-point lead before an improved second-half showing saw them dispatch Australia for the seventh time in a row. 

Tries by Robbie Henshaw, Nick Timoney, Rob Baloucoune, and a double from breakout back-rower James McNabney ultimately sealed Ireland's 10th win in 12 games in 2026.

It was also enough to secure fifth place in the inaugural Nations Championship. Farrell, however, was more concerned about how his side adapted during a sticky Test match when neither side were at their best.

"It was a stop-start game where things weren't really going our way," he added.

"That was very similar [to the South Africa game] in the sense that we had no field position, no ball, a lot of that down to Australia, but most of it was down to ourselves as well, giving penalties away and not winning the ball we wanted to win. 

"But we still found a way to be clinical when we had an opportunity in their 22."

Farrell praised Sam Prendergast's performances after the fly-half started all four November games

Having ended the July window with a deeply disappointing performance in a 40-21 defeat by New Zealand, Ireland felt like they needed to make an autumnal statement. 

That chastening night in Auckland spelled Ireland's sixth successive double-digit loss to New Zealand, South Africa or France. 

Having stewed on it for three months, they spoke about maintaining "new standards" at the start of this month. And while the squad would be quick to point out how far away they remain from their best, the returns have been largely satisfactory. 

They comfortably saw off Argentina and Fiji without top-drawer performances and landed a telling blow on the world champions 10 months before boarding the World Cup plane. 

The squad look in reasonable nick too with four straight wins achieved without influential figures such as Caelan Doris, Jack Conan, Garry Ringrose and Jack Crowley. 

Farrell has been able to get valuable minutes into Ryan Baird and Andrew Porter, who have missed a considerable portion of the Test calendar over the past 12 months, while Rob Baloucoune's stock as an international-level winger rose further with five tries in four outings this month.

But perhaps there is no better symbol of Ireland's improving recent form than Sam Prendergast. 

Having come through a bruising first two years in Test rugby, the Leinster fly-half - who was dropped for Ireland's Twickenham triumph over England - capped his best campaign in green with a player-of-the-match performance against the Wallabies, his evolution not going unnoticed by Farrell. 

"Coming into camp I saw a shift in how he's demanding of himself and others as well.

"It felt like, this campaign, it was his side he was driving forward so that's a massive step in the right direction. We all know when you're comfortable in doing that, you're able to bring your own game to the fore as well. 

"You can see the confidence in him, not just from his ball playing but I think that shows up in his running game as well, he's backing himself so he's certainly getting confidence, that's for sure."
