Leeds dominate Chelsea for first Stamford Bridge win since 1999

Chelsea manager Xabi Alonso is already in firefighting mode.

His team were booed by their own supporters at several points during a dominant 4-1 Leeds victory at Stamford Bridge, their first win there since 1999.

The first negative reaction came at half-time while the score was still 1-1. The ground then began to empty after the third and fourth Leeds goals, and the fans remaining voiced their dissent once more at the final whistle.

Although there were mitigating circumstances, including a potentially harsh penalty decision against them and the loss of midfielder Moises Caicedo to injury, joining a five-strong list of players on the treatment table, the defeat was thoroughly deserved.

It leaves Chelsea with three losses in their past four Premier League matches and they have slipped from fifth place to eighth, already 14 points behind league leaders Arsenal.

In October, Tottenham won at Stamford Bridge for only the second time in the Premier League era, while the 1-0 defeat at bottom club Sunderland before the international break was another alarm bell.

It is the first time in 67 years that Chelsea have lost at home to both Leeds and Spurs in the same season. 

"We need to find solutions, for sure, because there will be setbacks during any game," Alonso said after the match.

"To be able to go through that and to know that it's still so long to play, it's something mandatory in this competition and today I felt that probably we are in a fragile moment mentally.

"When we received a punch we didn't recover that strongly."

Xabi Alonso was appointed Chelsea manager on a four-year deal in May

In Alonso's defence, Chelsea had problems before his arrival.

They finished 10th last season, having been outrun in 37 of their 38 matches, and had three managers during a chaotic campaign.

Remarkably, Chelsea have seven more points from corresponding fixtures this season and last, but it mostly serves to show how low the bar was set.

Champions League qualification was again the club's pre-season target and Alonso's appointment was designed to bring stability and authority. 

The former Real Madrid and Liverpool midfielder had an impressive playing career and earned much early admiration as a manager, guiding Bayer Leverkusen to a league and cup double in 2024.

However, the Spaniard, who was sacked by Real Madrid last season, has yet to establish any real consistency - beyond his defence leaking goals regularly.

Only Crystal Palace, Coventry City and Ipswich Town have conceded more goals than Chelsea this season.

Chelsea are also now averaging fewer than a goal per game since Joao Pedro suffered a thigh injury in training. Alongside Pedro and now Caicedo, Jorrel Hato, Maxence Lacroix, Reece James and Jamie Gittens are also injured.

It highlights how they have gained little benefit of having a season without European football. 

Alonso is also beginning to face criticism from sections of the fanbase for both his team selections and substitutions.

Chief among the reasons is the constant chopping and changing of the starting XI, having now made 28 line-up changes (behind only Tottenham) while also switching between back-four and back-three systems. 

In addition, Alonso is being criticised for his use of personnel, having deployed Pedro Neto as a striker on two occasions and, some would argue, not made enough use of exciting young winger Estevao.

Against Leeds he gave striker Emmanuel Emegha his first start alongside Estevao and, although the Dutch striker scored, the pair were the first players he substituted while Chelsea were chasing the game at 3-1 down.

Similarly, his late in-game interventions are under scrutiny, including the decision to bring on striker Welbeck only in the 90th minute of the defeat by Spurs.

He took until the 64th minute to make changes against Leeds when Chelsea were already 3-1 down. Only three clubs had made more substitutions in the last 15 minutes than Chelsea in the league this season. 

Given Leeds' strengths, Chelsea should have been better prepared for what was coming. Before kick-off, Leeds had applied more high-intensity pressures in the opposition half than any other team in the Premier League.

They had also generated the most expected goals from set-pieces and won the most duels. Leeds maintained those trends at Stamford Bridge against a Chelsea side often accused of being soft.

Chelsea have had problems in recent seasons, but this was still their worst home defeat since a 4-1 loss to Brentford in 2022.

Likewise, it is almost uncharted territory in Alonso's managerial career, having only previously lost at home by a three-goal margin once.

The stadium emptied and supporters again left early.

Players displayed negative body language and argued with one another both during the match and after the final whistle. 

As a result, Alonso has gone from needing to fix defensive issues to dealing with problems that appear to be spreading throughout the squad.
