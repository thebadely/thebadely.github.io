England centre Seb Atkinson says he is living a childhood dream as he prepares to face New Zealand in Twickenham on Saturday.

The Gloucester pivot has recovered from a hip knock and is set to return to the starting XV at number 12 for the visit of the wounded All Blacks.

For Atkinson, this weekend's game comes a fortnight after he made his first Allianz Stadium appearance with England in the 45-22 thumping of Australia.

"It's sort of ticking off wishes and dreams that you have as a young aspiring rugby player," Atkinson told BBC Sport.

"Watching [England against New Zealand in 2012] when Manu [Tuilagi] scored that try and set up that try, the big performance in the semi-final of the World Cup a few years ago [in 2019].

"And obviously I was frustrated to not be available last year when the team put in a good performance.

"So a lot of things spring to mind and to be able to be out there and show what I can do on that stage will be really exciting for me personally."

New Zealand - beaten 33-19 by England on the same stage last November - will recall several big-name players this weekend.

They are looking to make amends for a shock defeat by Wales in Cardiff, with Atkinson set to line up against the heralded Jordie Barrett in midfield.

Atkinson has observed and admired Barrett at his best, but he is determined to be his own man.

"I love rugby and I love watching rugby and I love watching the likes of Jordie and [South Africa World Cup winner] Damian de Allende and try and take bits from their game," Atkinson said.

"But I know that fundamentally for me to get better as a player I need to be myself. I can't just replicate other people.

"You're trying to take away what they're good at - but also show what you're good at too."

New Zealand head coach Dave Rennie said his group would "poke each other in the chest" at the start of this week as they pick the bones out of a chastening first loss to Wales in 73 years.

Rennie said the All Blacks "let the country down" against the Welsh, but with an expected 500 caps of experience to come into the New Zealand matchday squad, England senior coach Richard Wigglesworth says the home side are preparing for the highest standard of opposition regardless of what happened in Cardiff.

"It changes nothing," Wigglesworth said.

"We get a different team. It is a massive challenge for us. England against New Zealand is an historic game. We just want to bring the best of us and give it a good rip."
