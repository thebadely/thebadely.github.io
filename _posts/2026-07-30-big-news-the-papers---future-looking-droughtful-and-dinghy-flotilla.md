Here’s what you need to know: Several papers lead with declarations of drought across the UK, the third in five years. "Future looking droughtful for baking Britain", says the Metro, which reports that dry weather has impacted "more than half of the country", with millions more warned this is a "new normal". It also cites forecasts that it could be the "driest July on record", with England having had "just 7% of its expected rainfall" for the month.

The i Paper leads with official advice to "take a shorter shower to save water in drought" under a new Environment Agency plea to "save every drop". The agency warns that households are "using water faster than nature can replenish it", as experts caution "another year of dry weather may trigger formal water rationing measures".  

Parched brown fields cover the front page of the Independent under the headline "Scorched earth Britain", with the paper noting that Wisely in Surrey has seen "43 days with no rain".

The Sun leads with reports of a "dinghy flotilla in Channel", writing that the boats carried "up to 1,000 migrants headed for Britain". Next to a photo of Prime Minister Andy Burnham, the paper asks: "How's that plan going, PM?" It adds that French authorities "admit waving migrants through", despite receiving "more than Â£1billion from UK taxpayers to stop them". 

"Still say your boats plan is working, Andy?" continues the Daily Mail, after Burnham says the government's plan to stop small boats is "working".

"Campaigners devastated after Burnham says law on assisted deaths must wait," is the lead for the Daily Express. Those impacted by the hold up to assisted dying legislation say: "We don't have time for these delays." Elsewhere, "Zendaya wows with her Spider-Man" is written next to a photo of the actress on the red carpet for the new Marvel movie.

"Get tax rebate from your mayor," leads the Times, which reports that "local leaders may hand revenue back to residents under PM's radical devolution plans". It quotes Burnham previously describing the UK as "one of the most overcentralised countries in the world", as data shows that local authorities "receive just 5 per cent of the tax revenue raised in their areas, while 95 per cent goes directly to the Treasury".

"Fed holds rates despite fears of price pressures from renewed Iran conflict" writes the Financial Times. The move "defies markets", although it says the "central bank hinted that rate rises could come as soon as September as a rapid rise in energy prices threatens to morph into a broader bout of inflation". 

"PM Andy cheers up Fiona" writes the Daily Star, featuring a new photo of the retired broadcaster Fiona Phillips, who has Alzheimer's, shared by her husband Martin Frizzell. The paper quotes Frizzell saying the prime minister's social care speech "cheered up our morning".

The paper writes that it comes after the remains of their children were treated with "callous disregard" at the hands of funeral home director Robert Bush, who admitted to 67 offences, including failing to bury and cremate people, and giving out wrong ashes.
