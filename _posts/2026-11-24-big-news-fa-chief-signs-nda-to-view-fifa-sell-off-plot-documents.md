Football Association chair Debbie Hewitt has signed a non-disclosure agreement (NDA) as a condition of viewing documents related to Fifa president Gianni Infantino's failed World Cup sell-off plot.

Hewitt was granted access to the information at the world governing body's headquarters in Zurich.

Earlier this month, Fifa said "key documents" on the abandoned plan to sell stakes in its tournaments to private investors would be made available to members of its council, but only in person and under "strict review protocols to preserve confidentiality".

Uefa had demanded Fifa release details about the controversial Fifa Forward Enterprise (FFE) proposal, and answer questions relating to its decision-making.

Hewitt was among a group of council members who wrote to Fifa to say they were "concerned that any review would seemingly be limited to certain 'key documents'â¦ which in turn would only be available for review under unknown 'strict review protocols'".

They also asked for clarity on what the protocols were, and "an explanation as to why Fifa considers such restrictions necessary since it is not clear why our rights are being limited in such a way".

The NDA means Hewitt cannot reveal what she has read, or comment about it publicly.

Fair Square, which campaigns for greater accountability in sport, told BBC Sport: "Debbie Hewitt and the FA didn't need to see the details of FFE to know that it was a gross abuse of presidential power.

"As a member association, the FA is entitled to hold the Fifa president accountable for his actions, and as a Fifa Council vice-president, Hewitt should have seen these plans as soon as they were hatched.

"They had the right to demand to see these documents, and should have exercised that right forcefully and publicly, rather than signing a gagging clause that puts Fifa and Infantino back in control."

Hewitt has previously criticised Infantino for a lack of transparency.

However, she embraced the Fifa president at a ceremony in Zurich on Monday when the UK bid to host the 2035 Women's World Cup was ratified at a special ceremony.

It is unclear whether she viewed the files on the same day, and which documents have been made available.

Last week, BBC Sport revealed the FA had considered challenging Infantino's eligibility to run for a fourth term as Fifa president, and that it is backing French candidate Philippe Diallo in next year's presidential election.

Fifa has been approached for comment. Sources have previously indicated Fifa's ability to answer some questions is limited by legal action that Uefa launched in October in the US, requesting disclosure of evidence and documents, warning it could lodge a criminal complaint in Switzerland.
