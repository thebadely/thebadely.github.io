According to new developments, The States of Deliberation is due to sit next week

The States is to debate cutting 2027 social security contributions by 0.5%.

Deputies Gavin St Pier and Tom Rylatt, from Forward Guernsey, said they would be bringing an amendment to the Social Insurance (Rates of Contributions and Benefits etc.) Ordinance, 2026, external.

The States of Deliberation will decide next week whether to temporarily cut 2027 employee (primary Class 1) and self-employed (Class 2) contributions by 0.5%, to 7.1% and 12.1%.

St Pier said global events including the Russia-Ukraine and US-Iran wars had had knock-on effects on the island's economy and "created significant pressures for the whole community".

"Social security contributions have been increased each year as part of a 10-year programme of increases," St Pier said.

"Without this amendment, since 2021, employee contributions will have increased by 1% and self-employed contributions will have increased by 1.6%."

Rylatt described the amendment would give "breathing room for working islanders".

He said: "ESS's [Employment and Social Security] 10-year plan emphasised a deliberately flexible approach that could take account of the economic circumstances of the day, alongside the outcome of the long-debated tax reform.

"With little sign of cost-of-living pressures easing in 2027, and with tax reform now decided, we believe this coming year is the right time to use that flexibility for the benefit of islanders."
