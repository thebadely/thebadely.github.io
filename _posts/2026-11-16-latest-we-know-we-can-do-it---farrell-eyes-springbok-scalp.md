Here’s what you need to know: Farrell has won three out of five matches against South Africa as Ireland boss

Head coach Andy Farrell says Ireland will look to draw inspiration from previous big wins under his watch when they renew their rivalry with world champions South Africa this weekend. 

Having beaten Argentina and Fiji, Ireland hope to avenge last year's 24-13 defeat by the Springboks when they host Rassie Erasmus' side at Aviva Stadium on Saturday (16:40 GMT). 

South Africa arrive in Dublin with a point to prove after being edged out by France in an enthralling heavyweight tussle in Paris on Friday night.

Ireland, who were unconvincing at times in Saturday's 43-15 victory over Fiji, have lost their past six contests against New Zealand, France and South Africa - the three teams above them in the world rankings - by double-digit margins.

However, Farrell has guided Ireland to three wins over the Springboks, including a 2023 Rugby World Cup pool stage success at Stade de France and a last-gasp 25-24 triumph in Durban the following year.

"We know we can do it because we've done it," said Farrell. 

"History tells us that as well. We know that when it really matters, we've been able to stand up - not always has that turned out to be a winning result, but we've always showed our mettle against the best and are always up for a challenge. 

"I hope we can compete like the game was on Friday night and it could go either way because you'd expect the world champions to be at their best, so we draw on the big wins that we've had and everyone knows about those."

Farrell admitted his side looked "suppressed" at times against Fiji. A disjointed first-half display left Ireland just 17-15 ahead, but they scored 26 unanswered points after the break to secure a bonus-point win. 

Fly-half Sam Prendergast was among those to endure mixed fortunes in a green jersey against the Pacific Islanders. 

While he produced some decisive moments in attack, including an inch-perfect grubber kick for Tommy O'Brien's second try, his defending was targeted by the Fijians, with Josua Tuisova barging past him for the away side's first try and Ben Volavola charging down a kick that almost led to another.

"He got hit off the ball once or twice, didn't he, and nothing came of that, but it's good for him," added Farrell. 

"He's been kicking excellently the past three or four weeks and missed a few there, that's good for him as well, to be able to get over that and get on with his game and understand how he can have more impacts within that.

"Albeit he had some nice parts of his game."

Kelleher came off the bench against South Africa last year

Last year, Ireland were shown five cards as South Africa dominated the scrum to win an incident-packed Test.

On that occasion, Ireland conceded six scrum penalties and a penalty try, while props Andrew Porter and Paddy McCarthy were sin-binned for infringements against the relentless Springbok set-piece.

Hooker Ronan Kelleher, who came off the bench against South Africa last year, said he is using the chastening experience as fuel heading into Saturday's contest with the Springboks, who lost 21-19 to France despite winning nine scrum penalties off the Les Bleus pack. 

"Obviously it was a very tough day, particularly at scrum time and for us in the front row," said Kelleher, who scored the fourth of Ireland's seven tries against Fiji. 

"It's something that kind of drives you on a bit. You kind of had to take it on the chin.

"There hasn't been a drastic overhaul (of Ireland's scrum) or anything like that, it's just been us focusing on what we can do.

"I think we're just gonna have to wait and see next week and please God we'll have enough in the tank and we'll do a job."

Ireland must beat South Africa and hope France and England slip up against Argentina and New Zealand to seal a place in the inaugural competition's final at Twickenham on 29 November.
