It had been some run for Everton captain James Tarkowski.

Prior to Monday night, the defender had received 74 yellow cards in the Premier League but had never been sent off - a top-flight record.

But that run came to an end at Brentford as he was given his first red card in his 351st Premier League appearance, although the decision has attracted plenty of debate.

His dismissal crucially means he will miss Sunday's Merseyside derby against Liverpool and also ruined David Moyes' record-breaking night as he took charge of his 593rd game as Everton boss - more than anyone else in the club's history.

The 4-0 loss was also the heaviest defeat Everton have had since Moyes returned to the club at the start of last year.

Tarkowski's dismissal proved a big talking point during and after the match, with opinions split on whether he should have gone. Even Moyes wasn't sure.

He told BBC Match of the Day: "My thoughts at the time, I didn't think it was but when I've looked at it again I think his foot is high. Is there enough force for it to be a red card? Not sure about that."

James Tarkowski received his first Premier League red card

So what exactly happened?

Just 15 minutes after Keane Lewis-Potter scored his second deflected strike of the evening, Everton skipper Tarkowski thundered into a challenge on Brentford's Yehor Yarmoliuk.

He won the ball but also followed through with the challenge forcefully. Initially, referee Craig Pawson deemed a foul and a yellow card was enough.

But, after an intervention from the video assistant referee (VAR) and studying the replay, Pawson changed the yellow for a red.

The Premier League match centre said on X: "After VAR review, the referee overturned the original decision of yellow card for Tarkowski for the challenge on Yarmoliuk and issued a red card for serious foul play. 

"VAR: 'Point of contact is on the shin. He's got speed and intensity. Studs are high.'

"REF: 'After review, Everton No.6 commits a challenge that endangers his opponent's safety, making contact with the studs onto the shin. Cancelling the yellow card - and a red card.'"

Referees are asked to take into account several considerations for what counts as serious foul play including the speed of the challenge, its intensity, force and what degree of control there was.

But, as always, there was plenty of debate about whether it was the correct decision.

BBC Sport's football issues correspondent Dale Johnson, said: "Really not sure about this as a VAR red card. 

"Yes, Tarkowski absolutely ticks the boxes for speed and intensity and leading high with studs showing. 

"But the question marks are that Tarkowski made only small contact and didn't have a straight leg when he did so.

"Maybe his reputation played a part? Remember, he wrongly escaped a VAR red card against Liverpool for a challenge, which was along the same lines. He won the ball and then followed through into Alexis Mac Allister. Though that was a far stronger challenge."

BBC Radio 5 Live's - and former Blackburn Rovers striker - Chris Sutton, said on X: "Come on that's not a redâ¦ the game's gone."

Former Arsenal striker Alan Smith agreed the decision was harsh. He told Sky Sports: "Ten years ago we wouldn't have looked at it once and now we still [frame] it on the follow-through. I just can't see it.

"Only Tarkowski knows if he wanted to leave it high or if it was natural. The follow-through was just natural momentum.

"There's a bit of anger in there from Tarkowski and it's a little high but only he knows if he wanted to leave a little more on Yarmoliuk there."

Opinion was far from unanimous though, with both pundits in the Sky Sports studio adamant it was a clear red.

Former Liverpool centre-back Jamie Carragher said: "It's a red card. Tarkowski has got previous for tackles like that. He is a 90s defender, he tackles like that.

"He follows through with such force, it is excessive. You want to give him the benefit of the doubt but it's a red card."

He added: "You could argue was it a big enough mistake for VAR to get involved but I think we are splitting hairs. He doesn't need to do this."

While ex-Tottenham defender Toby Alderweireld added: "It's a red card, he knows what he is doing. 100%."
