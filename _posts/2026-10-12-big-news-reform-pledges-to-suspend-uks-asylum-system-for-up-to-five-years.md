Reform UK has announced plans to effectively close down the UK's asylum system for a full parliamentary term if elected.

The party said all new asylum applications would be rejected and existing applications voided, with all claimants marked for deportation. It said no grants of humanitarian protection or refugee status would be made, while legal refugee routes and resettlement schemes would end.

Reform said it would also scrap the asylum tribunal system and deport 90,000 people currently with appeal cases, with home affairs spokesman Zia Yusuf saying "Britain is full".

Labour called the proposals a "clear sign of desperation and weakness" and the Refugee Council branded them "unworkable".

Reform said its existing plans to make migrants who have entered the UK illegally, overstayed a visa or originated from a safe country ineligible for asylum would effectively prevent any current asylum claimant "from ever having their case heard". 

The party said it would therefore "suspend the asylum system entirely for a full parliamentary term".

A parliamentary term usually lasts five years unless an early UK general election is called.

Under further proposals, Reform said it would pull the UK out of the European Convention on Human Rights (ECHR), repeal the Human Rights Act 1998 and disapply the 1951 Refugee Convention to prevent legal challenges to its plans.

The party said it would ban British visas for countries such as Pakistan, Somalia, Eritrea, Syria, Afghanistan and Sudan if they refuse to allow deportation flights to land in their country.

Nigel Farage and Zia Yusuf gave speeches in front of a replica "mega dinghy" involved in Channel crossings - which Farage said showed it was no longer "a small boats crisis"

For countries without a significant legal foreign national presence in the UK, the party said it would consider asset freezes and potential tariffs on goods and services for non-compliance.

Announcing the plans in a speech in Hythe, Kent on Monday, party leader Nigel Farage said the UK is "now living in a national security emergency".

"We want to get back to being a normal country, a country that puts national security at the top of the agenda, a country that does everything it can to protect its citizens."

He claimed the plans were "going to save us a fortune" but declined to say how many civil servants would be required to process the deportations, other than that Home Office staff currently overseeing asylum claims would be reprioritised.

In a speech, Yusuf said he would "have to embark on the biggest mass deportation programme this country has ever seen" if made home secretary but "we will not be returning people to harm".

"We've got to prioritise the British people," Yusuf said, pointing to Britons facing long waits for emergency care and people in their 30s and 40s having to live at home because of rent prices.

He claimed the millions of unauthorised immigrants deported under former US President Barack Obama's administration showed there was "precedent" for the party's plans.

Reform also said it would create a specialised agency focusing on the detention and deportation of those in the UK illegally, with 24,000 to 28,000 detention spaces needed - but is unclear how this would provide enough capacity.

Between 1 January and 5 October 2026, a total of 20,478 people crossed the English Channel by small boat from France. This was down by 40% on the same period the previous year.

These crossings have become the most common way for people to be detected entering the UK illegally since 2020.

Nearly all those who arrive by small boat claim asylum. Under international law, this means they are allowed to stay in the country while their asylum application is considered.

Small boat arrivals made up 40% of asylum applications between July 2025 to June 2026.

Boats that arrived in the UK from 6 October 2025 to 5 October 2026 carried an average of 68 people. This has more than doubled since 2021.

Experts say overcrowding in boats makes crossings riskier. At least 84 people died while attempting to cross the Channel in 2024, according to the United Nations (UN).

When looking at the scale of small boats crossings, the number of these arrivals is about 5% of the size of total immigration into the UK from January 2025 to December 2025.

People from Eritrea accounted for 18% of all arrivals from July 2025 to June 2026.

In the latest figures covering January 2025 to December 2025, at least 2,335 people who arrived by small boat were found to be potential victims of human trafficking or other forms of modern slavery, according to the Home Office.

Another 4,712 people were detected entering the UK without permission via other methods including hiding in vehicles, travelling on ferries or through airports from July 2025 to June 2026. This was down by 17% on the same period the previous year.

There are also individuals who arrive legally, for example via a work or study visa, and then overstay their limit.

The total number of people who live in the UK illegally after arriving legally is not known.

Previous pledges from Reform include a review of all asylum claims from the last five years and returning all small boat arrivals.

In response to the latest announcement, Jon Featonby, chief policy analyst at the Refugee Council, said "closing our asylum system would cause chaos". 

"Desperate people would still arrive on our shores, but instead of entering an orderly system, they would be driven underground and vulnerable to exploitation and abuse," he told the BBC.

All the main political parties should focus on speeding up asylum decisions "so that instead of languishing in accommodation paid for by the taxpayer, those found to be refugees can move into proper housing, start work or training, and begin contributing to their communities, while those with no right to stay can be helped to return with dignity," he added.

Bridget Phillipson said Farage was "casting around for more and more extreme policies" as he deals with questions about finances and rival party Restore Britain.

The Labour chair said her party is showing "we can control immigration and get numbers down with serious action, not by abandoning the asylum system altogether", adding the "right approach" is "not giving up or caving to the most extreme voices, but ensuring that the rules we have are properly enforced".

In March, Home Secretary Shabana Mahmood said any asylum seeker given refugee status will now only be offered temporary protection, reviewed every 30 months.

In September, she also reopened a scheme to resettle refugees in the UK, saying she hoped that offering "safe and legal" routes would deter small boat crossings.

Reform has questioned if safe routes for Afghans and Palestinians to the UK should stay, but said it would keep a temporary settlement scheme for Ukrainians fleeing the war against Russia, as well as EU settled status with benefit cuts.

Conservative shadow home secretary Chris Philp said the proposals "largely copy" his party's policies, arguing: "Only the Conservatives have a serious and credible plan to fix our borders."

The Tories previously said they would task officials with removing 750,000 illegal immigrants within five years, under Trump-style deportation plans.

Lib Dems home affairs spokesperson Max Wilkinson said the "UK can only restore order, control and fairness by joining the EU migration and asylum pact", which increases the number of irregular migrants returned to their home countries and spreads responsibility for asylum seekers across the EU.
