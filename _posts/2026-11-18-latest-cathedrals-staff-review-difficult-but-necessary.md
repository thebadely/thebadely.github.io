Zihni said that visitors to the cathedral are unlikely to notice any changes. 

Cathedral chiefs said the restructure involved a review of staff roles and responsibilities, as well as how the organisation is run. 

A small number of staff had been made voluntarily redundant as a result of the changes.

In a statement, cathedral leaders said that these had been "very difficult decisions," but that these changes will ensure it will continue to serve the "city, county and wider community for generations to come".

Speaking to BBC Radio Gloucestershire, Zihni said the cathedral had a number of costs, including heating, maintenance and staffing, as well as safeguarding, education, worship, music and heritage.

"We have to raise the money year on year through donations, visitors, grants, fundraising and other income because we don't have any automatic funding either from the national church or the government," he said.

Gloucester Cathedral is not the only one in the country with high maintenance costs.

"The scale of what cathedrals offer is considerable. They employ 6,000 people across the sector, welcome nearly 10 million visitors each year and are open 365 days a year," said Pitt.

Zihni said the Chapter, who are responsible for running the cathedral, are also looking at ways to "diversify their income stream and develop commercial angles".

He said: "People may have seen some of it already with the concerts we've been hosting in the cathedral, but there isn't a separation between the commercial activity and the regular activity," he said.
