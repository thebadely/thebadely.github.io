Notts County beat Altrincham in incredible fog-filled spectacle

One of the most important attributes for a commentator is to be able to see the action you are trying to describe but that became a luxury for BBC Sport's Steve Bower when Altrincham hosted Notts County in an FA Cup tie on Saturday.

As thick fog enveloped the Moss Lane ground, he could barely see what was going on in a first-round game being televised live on BBC Two.

"One of the more challenging of my long career, that's about 35 years now," said Bower about the experience.

"I was reminded by a colleague - maybe I'm a jinx - that we had a similar thing on the BBC about three years ago when we did York against Wigan in the second round and the fog came in.

"Tonight was worse. From where we were, opposite the dugouts, once the play went to the centre circle and beyond, we couldn't see a thing. 

"We couldn't see which team had the ball, let alone which player had the ball after about the 60th minute. Bearing in mind it went to extra-time, it was challenging.

"I just kept looking down at my phone and it was text message after text message from people saying, 'You're sounding great, but I can't see a thing'. In the end, you have to roll with it and do the best you can."

'It's like a fever dream' - commentators struggle through fog

National League Altrincham were on for an upset when they took a 2-1 lead against the League One side but a late Callum Roberts equaliser for County took the game to extra-time before the visiting side won on penalties.

"The frustrating thing is that for an hour it was a really good FA Cup tie, it had everything," said Bower.

"It was on for another giant-killing script but then the frustrating thing is that it becomes a non-event because, without stating the obvious, people can't see half of it. 

"Also, it's not then about the tactics, or the shape of it, or the aggression, or the effort because you just can't see it. As a spectacle in trying to describe a game like you normally would, it's impossible."

Bower's radio colleagues were not faring much better as they tried to work out what was going on.

"I can just about see the bright white numbers on their shirts, but I am struggling to see what is happening on the far side of the pitch," said BBC Radio Nottingham commentator Adam Hassell.

Former Notts County striker Mark Stallard, who was also speaking on BBC Radio Nottingham, said: "We're looking around, and here in the press box no-one knows where the ball is! It's like a spot-the-ball competition.

"I played in the Notts County cup side against Wimbledon when it was abandoned - they equalised in the second half and I didn't see any of it!"

Stallard later said: "We genuinely cannot see the linesman or the players on the far side. It's slightly farcical having to commentate for radio listeners."

Altrincham have a rich history of causing FA Cup upsets but could not add to that in the fog against Notts County

The managers of both teams were on the touchline but even they were struggling as tactics went out of the window. 

"[Notts County boss] Martin Paterson said credit to Altrincham, they were the better team in the first half and, as for the second, I couldn't see it. He was laughing," said Bower. 

"Neil Gibson, the Altrincham manager, was saying, 'We were trying to get down to pitchside to look tactically at what they had done after those four substitutions. Have they changed shape? Is it a back four? Have they gone to a box midfield? But we just couldn't see'. 

"So even from a tactical point of view, the players had to stop playing long diagonal passes and things like that because they couldn't see, they were just having to play short five, 10 yard passes so the game changed in that sense as well. 

"It was very, very close to getting called off."

Bower said some of the players told him they couldn't see both goals or the corner flags from the centre circle.

"It will live long in the memory, that's for sure," said Bower. "Even on the penalties, you were having to rely on the crowd's reaction before committing to the tale. 

"You couldn't see the goalkeeper's save, for instance, until the first replay came in."

The penalties took place in front of Altrincham fans and, while Bower had replays to call on, the Notts County fans "couldn't have seen anything" from the other end as they had to go on "crowd reaction".

"Obviously, the Altrincham fans all had their phones on with the lights trying to put the Notts County players off as well in the fog. A unique evening all around."
