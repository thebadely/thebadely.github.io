Angry players urge ATP rethink over doubles cuts

It is crystal clear why Patten decided he needed to dig out ATP bosses Gaudenzi and Polo.

Patten, Heliovaara and the rest of the men's doubles cohort believe their governing body simply does not care about their discipline. 

And, as Patten barely concealed in his pointed speech, the non-appearance of Gaudenzi and Polo underlined what the players believe is a lack of respect.

In July, the ATP made proposals to halve doubles draws at its flagship Masters 1000 events to 16 teams. Smaller tournaments would be reduced to eight teams.

That would see the percentage of prize money devoted to doubles events falling from 20% to 10%, with the difference redistributed to singles players.

The plans drew the fury of doubles players, who believe the "drastic" changes could kill the format. Patten also used his Wimbledon victory speech to passionately point out the importance of doubles. 

The ATP says it wants to create "a more sustainable long-term model" for the men's tour, insisting it wants to "maintain the important role" of doubles.

The tour's board - comprised of chairman Gaudenzi, four tournament representatives and four player representatives - will vote on the plans in Turin this week. 

Gaudenzi and Polo are spending much of their week in those meetings.

The decision will shape the future of men's doubles, along with the careers and lives of its players.

By speaking out again now, Patten's barbed comments were interpreted to be timely, in an attempt to apply more pressure before the ATP board meets on Tuesday and Wednesday.
